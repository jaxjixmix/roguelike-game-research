##### Praise

- Body-swapping gimmick is fresh and the game pushes you to keep switching, rewarding fast swaps
- Hard but feels fair; deaths are your own fault for not switching
- Each robot has distinct, tricky mechanics with good tutorials
- Level-up upgrades can radically change a robot (e.g. flamethrower becomes explosive fumes, shooter gains fire)
- Fantastic visuals, electronic rock music and a beautiful living hub
- Early access with unlockable content (new robot, final area) gives something to look forward to

##### Criticism

- Levels are beautiful but always the same, so runs feel repetitive
- Wants more unique things to do during runs
- Same enemies are encountered constantly, limited enemy variety
- Not beginner friendly; every robot is difficult to get comfortable with
- Fishing mini game in the hub is frustrating; meta progression is fairly small

##### Unique Points

- You only control enemies, so enemy variety is inherently capped by design
- Fuel system ties switching and kills with different bodies to staying alive/invincible
- Skill-heavy core gameplay paired with genuinely good roguelike upgrades is surprising

##### Design Criteria

- Core gimmick/novelty: enemy possession and constant switching is the hook
- Difficulty/fairness: very hard but feels fair
- Skill ceiling: each robot needs mastery
- Roguelike upgrades: build-altering changes to robots
- Enemy variety: limited by premise
- Level design: beautiful but repetitive, same layouts
- Visuals/audio/vibe: standout music, hub and art
- Meta progression: small, mostly skill-based
- Run variety: focus on two robot types per run gives flexibility

#### Best Things

- The core host-swapping loop: every enemy type is a playable body with its own moveset, and using them against each other feels creative and rewarding
- High skill ceiling and emergent 'tech' (momentum, cancels, juggling, trick shots) that players compare to ULTRAKILL-style mastery design
- Each bot feels weighty and distinct when it clicks, with real risk-reward in deciding when to swap
- Two-layer progression: Hades-style meta-progression (Hyperparameters, Adversarial Learning) plus genuine player-skill growth, which reviewers find very addictive
- Soundtrack is widely praised, and a separate OST exists on Steam
- Funny writing, charming characters and surprisingly creative worldbuilding for a robot-apocalypse setting, delivered through a hub town
- Per-bot tutorials/training regimens are described as essential and well made
- Great value at $15, with daily challenge runs and modifiers adding replayability

#### Worst Things

- Very steep learning curve and high cognitive load: many bots, each with their own tech, all expected to be mastered because you keep swapping
- Early hours before the game 'clicks' are rough; reviewers describe the start as punishing and a real time investment
- Some bots control jankier or more awkwardly than others (Tachi and Epitaph are called intentionally unwieldy)
- Swap/health rules (global health loss when a host dies, penalties for swapping back to the same robot, mite/aphid forms) are confusing and feel inconsistent to some players
- Limited upgrade variety is mentioned even by fans, and certain upgrade paths (e.g. Deadlift) feel underpowered
- Technical issues reported in a minority of reviews and on the Steam forum: crashes after a few minutes, performance problems (devs suggest running the OpenGL renderer), audio bugs, Linux-related bugs

#### Improvements

- Better in-game explanation of swap mechanics, the two juice/backup bars and death-to-mite rules, rather than relying on tutorials alone
- Buff weak upgrade trees and add more upgrade variety
- Add alternate areas or paths and more content beyond the current loop, which fans say they would love
- Reduce friction between runs: one critic wants an instant-restart option instead of several screens
- Give town quests more meaningful rewards, as they currently feel like a diversion
- Fix crash and performance issues; a lower-pressure or easier-onboarding mode was implied by reviewers who found the pressure overwhelming
- Trim some of the dialogue so the narrative flows more smoothly (raised by both a fan and a detractor)

#### Unique Things

- You have no player character of your own: you are a parasitic AI hopping between enemy robots, each of which is your moveset
- Dying is part of the flow: the game pushes you to swap before a body breaks, and spare hosts are a resource
- Style-based currency system inspired by Devil May Cry and ULTRAKILL rewards flashy play
- Turning enemies against each other (friendly-fire exploitation) is the main tactic rather than a side gimmick
- Hub-town narrative and dry comedic writing wrapped around a bullet-hell-adjacent action roguelite, made by a very small student-origin team (IGF 2024 Audience Choice winner)

#### Tedious Or Disliked

- Too much dialogue and humor for some: one reviewer found the writing self-satisfied and obstructive to gameplay
- Several screens to click through before starting another run
- Needing to relearn each bot's moveset, timing and combos after a break; the game punishes time away
- Mandatory-feeling tutorials for each bot create an upfront time cost (one reviewer spent about an hour on tutorials)
- Being forced into under-upgraded hosts because not every host type spawns in every room, so you must spread upgrades across all bots

#### Divisive Points

- Difficulty and 'no chill' pressure: fans see a fair, skill-based challenge where every death is your fault, while others find it overwhelming and stressful
- Mandatory mastery of all bots: praised as depth and variety, criticised as a punishing, inconsistent rule set
- Humor and narrative tone: charming and funny to most reviewers, insufferable to a few
