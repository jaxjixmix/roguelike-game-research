### Community

- **One Liner:** A turn-based tactics roguelite from Subset Games (makers of FTL) where you pilot a squad of three mechs on tiny 8x8 grids to protect city buildings from insectoid Vek, with every enemy attack telegraphed in advance.
- **Consensus Summary:** Into the Breach is treated as a modern classic of the tactics and roguelite space. Critics gave it a Metacritic score of 90 on PC (89 on Switch), and it won Best Strategy Game at The Game Awards 2018, among other awards. Steam players agree, with about 94% positive across roughly 22,400 reviews (Very Positive). The praise centres on perfect-information combat, compact puzzle-like turns and squad variety. The main dissent is from players who find it more of a rigid puzzle game than a tactics game, and from those who still feel unavoidable spawn RNG can doom a run. It is not early access or abandoned. The free Advanced Edition update shipped in July 2022 alongside the Netflix mobile release, and players still ask for more content or a sequel.
- **Reception Notes:** Launched Feb 27, 2018 on Windows to acclaim and became Metacritic's best-reviewed PC game of 2018. It came to macOS and Switch in August 2018 and Linux in April 2020. The free Advanced Edition (July 19, 2022) added squads, pilots, enemies and an Unfair difficulty, and Netflix released iOS and Android versions the same day. Awards include Best Strategy Game at The Game Awards 2018, Best Design at the 2019 GDC Awards and Best Original Property at the BAFTA Games Awards. #### Best Things

- Enemy attacks are telegraphed, so there are no hit-chance rolls. Losses feel like your own mistakes, which reviewers often contrast with XCOM.
- Each turn is a self-contained micro-puzzle: pushing, pulling, blocking spawns and redirecting attacks, with depth coming from very simple rules.
- Protecting the Power Grid (city buildings) rather than just killing everything is a fresh objective that changes how you evaluate every move.
- Large variety of mech squads, pilots and weapon upgrades. Each squad teaches a different way to think about the game, and squad achievements hint at how to play them.
- Short runs and short battles (roughly 10-15 minutes each) make it easy to pick up and put down, and it works well on Steam Deck and with a gamepad.
- Economy of design: small maps, no filler, clean pixel art and a well-regarded soundtrack by Ben Prunty.
- Difficulty ramps gradually, with Easy through Unfair settings, so players report steadily improving on squads that once beat them.
- Runs are short enough that a blunder costs about 3 hours rather than a 15-hour XCOM-style campaign.

#### Worst Things

- Some players say it plays like a rigid puzzle game rather than a tactical one, with the board state mattering more than player choice.
- Enemy spawn positions and targeting can create situations that feel unwinnable without grid damage, especially on the first turn of a mission or in the final island.
- Losing a run discards upgrades. Some players find the penalty harsh when a poor upgrade choice only shows up an hour later.
- Missions share the same core puzzle despite varied objectives, and some players report repetitiveness after a while.
- The tutorial is thin and the legibility of threats and solutions is hard for newcomers, who often need external guides.
- The unlock system (squads and pilots tied to achievements) is criticised by some as an FTL-style chore.
- Steep difficulty: several negative reviewers say even Normal wipes them out repeatedly and the game is not for players who dislike punishing roguelikes.
- Legibility of puzzles is hard: players struggle to tell whether a bad board needs a tradeoff or was set up wrong at the start.

#### Improvements

- Players keep asking for more content or a sequel, and several recent Steam reviewers say they return hoping for an update.
- Better onboarding or in-game explanation of mechanics and enemy behaviour.
- Some want more flexible undo, for example allowing a turn reset when it is the last unit's action.
- More build and upgrade variety, and more map or mission variety, to reduce repetition.
- Reduced luck in spawn placement, or more tools to recover from a bad spawn.

#### Unique Things

- Fully deterministic combat with visible enemy intentions: a tactics game built like a chess puzzle.
- Health bar is the city Power Grid, so victory means damage mitigation rather than total elimination.
- Time-travel framing: losing a run sends a chosen pilot back into a new timeline with their experience, giving a light meta-progression.
- Enemy and environmental interactions such as water, ice, fire and mountains are central, and enemies can kill or block each other.
- 8x8 boards and five-turn missions give it a compactness unlike XCOM-style or Fire Emblem-style tactics games.

#### Tedious Or Disliked

- Restarting entire runs after a late failure, with upgrades lost.
- Unlocking squads and pilots via achievements, which some find grindy.
- Having to spend minutes planning single turns, which can feel like homework to people expecting a relaxed strategy game.
- Learning curve where players feel they are being punished for not knowing enemy behaviour yet.
- Once mechanics are 'solved', some reviewers feel runs become a repeated puzzle with the same base and little novelty.

#### Divisive Points

- Whether the RNG is a problem: fans say the game is essentially deterministic, while critics say spawns and targeting can make puzzles unsolvable.
- Puzzle versus tactics: some see it as a perfect blend, others feel it lacks the tactical freedom they wanted.
- Difficulty: some beat Unfair, others report never finishing Normal.
- Run length and permadeath: fans value short restarts, detractors dislike losing progress.
