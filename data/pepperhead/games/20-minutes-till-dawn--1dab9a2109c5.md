### Community

- **One Liner:** A Lovecraftian twin-stick survival roguelite by solo dev flanne where you actively aim and shoot hordes for twenty minutes, stacking runes/upgrades into wild builds (a Vampire Survivors-like with manual aiming).
- **Consensus Summary:** Players like it a lot: Steam sits at roughly 90% positive (about 29k reviews, Very Positive) at a $4.99 price, and it is widely described as great value and a fun, snappy, build-driven survivor-like for short sessions. Critics are thin but positive (OpenCritic 77 from 6 reviews, scores 7.5-8/10 from most, one 5/10), praising weapon/upgrade variety and complaining about limited content. Steam early access began June 2022, 1.0 landed June 2023, and a Switch port followed in Dec 2023. The dominant modern sentiment is frustration rather than hatred of the game itself: the PC version appears abandoned (promised updates and a beta build never shipped) while the mobile versions keep receiving content, and many negative Steam reviews are about that rather than gameplay.
- **Reception Notes:** Early access launched June 8, 2022 and reportedly earned over half a million dollars in its first week; mobile versions (a free ad-supported one and a premium one) arrived Sept 2022, Steam 1.0 on June 8, 2023, Switch Dec 21, 2023. Since roughly 2024 the Steam build has had no content updates despite a posted promise of big reworks and new characters/weapons, with a beta build available for a long time; mobile keeps getting updates and events, leading to a steady stream of 'abandoned' negative reviews (top negative reviews are nearly all about this). Overall Steam score remains about 90% positive. Metacritic has no page/score; OpenCritic has 6 reviews (77 top critic average, 50% of critics recommend). Mac and Windows only on Steam, no Linux.

#### Best Things

- Active manual aiming and shooting makes positioning and accuracy matter, unlike auto-attack survivor-likes
- Rune/upgrade system with no cap on picks allows wild, sometimes broken synergies; called a sandbox for build crafting
- Large roster of characters each with distinct abilities, plus weapons that evolve mid-run and change how they play
- Darkness difficulty levels add different modifiers (faster/tankier enemies etc.) and give a reason to keep winning
- Dark, minimalist pixel art with a Lovecraftian mood; cute character designs
- Very good value at about $3-5, often on sale; many players report 10-60+ hours
- Short, snappy runs suit quick sessions and the game runs smoothly for most players
- Nothing important is locked behind achievements; unlocks are bought with gems so experimentation is free

#### Worst Things

- Only three maps, which are barren with few gimmicks; same muted palette throughout
- Limited enemy variety and repeating bosses make runs feel samey after roughly 10 hours
- Balance problems: summons and elemental builds are overtuned at top difficulty while pure gun-ability builds and some characters/weapons (e.g. Hastur) are weak or unviable
- Endless mode becomes easy and repetitive quickly and is not sustained by enough content
- Performance: builds that trigger many procs can cause lag or stutter late in runs
- Single repeating music loop and weak soundtrack per several reviewers
- Difficulty spike past about 15 minutes that is not well telegraphed, and RNG can produce unwinnable runs
- PC version abandoned; promised updates and reworks never arrived

#### Improvements

- Add XP magnet/vacuum effect so collecting gems across the map is less tedious
- More maps, enemy types and bosses, plus a rebuilt Endless mode (previously promised)
- Rebalance builds so summons do not dominate and more characters/weapons are viable at high darkness levels
- Ship the long-promised update (Emberpath/beta content) and communicate with the community
- Accessibility: colorblind options and better visual clarity during dense fights
- Achievements that reward creative play rather than repetitive high-difficulty clears
- Surface more stat/info detail in-game; a reviewer who 100%ed the game says it hides too much information about how stats work

#### Unique Things

- Survivor-like with twin-stick manual aiming and reloading rather than auto-attacks
- Fixed 20-minute standard run (plus 10-minute Quickplay and Endless with adjustable difficulty up to 15 levels)
- Each character has a unique ability and weapons evolve into stronger forms mid-run
- Runs are built from unlimited rune/upgrade picks, enabling very broken combinations
- Near-monochrome Lovecraftian pixel aesthetic
- Solo-developer, very cheap, with parallel PC, Switch and freemium mobile versions

#### Tedious Or Disliked

- Gem collection without a magnet and long unlock grind for characters, weapons and runes
- Achievement hunting (reported ~60h) requires repetitive runs at highest difficulty with many characters
- A full 20-minute run plus rising darkness levels that mostly just inflate enemy HP/speed feels like numbers rather than new challenge
- Repeated bosses and three maps make later runs feel rote; some call the clicking monotonous
- Dying repeatedly while testing builds is slow because every choice starts to funnel into the same few picks

#### Divisive Points

- Abandonment: many reviewers refuse to recommend it on PC; others say a cheap solo-dev game is fine 'as is' and expect no endless updates
- Depth vs repetition: fans call it a build sandbox with huge replay value; others say it runs its course in about 10 hours
- Art and palette: praised as moody and stylish by many, criticised for eye strain and low visual clarity by some
- Difficulty: some find it easy and unbalanced, others hit a hard wall late in runs
