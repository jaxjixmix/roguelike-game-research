### Commentary


#### Entry 1

- **Part:** 2
- **Label:** Tier list Part 2 (2024)
- **Summary:** Death Must Die is a top-down horde-survivor (Vampire Survivors-style) game that openly borrows Hades' structure, with gods offering boons and duo combinations. He considers it one of the better entries in a flood of low-quality clones. He likes the boon synergies, the explorable map and the readable art. He is let down by bloated, boring stat-stick loot, near-identical characters, and runs that repeat the same enemies and bosses. He places it in high B tier.
- **Quote:** it is literally just Hades but Vampire Survivors, which is fine
- **Rerank Note:**

##### Praise

- Hades-style gods offering boons with keywords that combine, including duo boons, are unique and change how you play
- Combining gods for different effects is enjoyable
- Effectively infinite map with random events and upgrades rewards exploring instead of only killing for XP
- Art style is simple but fits the theme, and screen clutter stays readable (e.g. big red Nova attacks are clearly visible)
- Good humor and personality in character dialogue
- Loot/equipment layer persisting between runs is a cool roguelite element

##### Criticism

- Most equipment is boring stat bonuses; 20+ items per run and a stash of 60 makes sorting annoying
- Characters feel nearly identical (swipe vs stab vs swirl), with only one minor unique attribute each; Mage is the exception
- Runs are repetitive: same enemies, waves and bosses at the same times in a roughly 20-minute run
- Exploration events are hard to find and not unique enough

##### Unique Points

- Hades-like boon system grafted onto the horde-survivor formula
- Exploration of a huge open level yields character upgrades, an alternative to pure grinding for XP
- Loot layer with per-character equipment persisting between runs

##### Comparisons

- Hades
- Vampire Survivors
- 20 Minutes Till Dawn

##### Design Criteria

- Synergies: boon keywords and duo boons combining across gods
- Level design: large explorable map with events versus minimalistic single levels
- Character diversity: characters too similar mechanically
- Loot/meta-progression: too much boring stat equipment
- Run variety: identical enemies, waves and bosses each run
- Visual readability/art: clear, fitting style amid chaos
- Humor/charm: character personality and dialogue
- Genre context: quality relative to many low-effort clones

### Community

- **One Liner:** A pixel-art roguelite action RPG 'bullet heaven' from Realm Archive that mixes Vampire Survivors-style horde survival with Hades-style god blessings and a Diablo-style loot and talent system, in Steam Early Access since November 2023.
- **Consensus Summary:** Players rate it highly overall: Steam shows about 90% positive (roughly 21.8k reviews, Very Positive) at a low price (about $7), and the art, combat feel and build crafting get consistent praise. Recent sentiment is noticeably worse, because the last substantial content update (Act 3, April 2025) is more than a year old, developer communication is sparse, and the team announced a large rebuild of core systems. Many negative reviews come from players with many hours who say they enjoyed it but cannot recommend it until updates resume, while positive reviewers keep defending it as not abandoned. Professional coverage is mostly promotional or news-style (Act 3 and roadmap pieces), and no Metacritic or OpenCritic scores exist.
- **Reception Notes:** Launched into Early Access on 14 Nov 2023 at roughly $7 with Mostly Positive reviews, which rose to Very Positive (around 90-94% in press coverage). Roadmap after launch promised Act II (Q1 2024), more heroes, gods, duo blessings, loadouts, localisation and Act III; Act 3 (The Darkmoor, with the Druid, Aegir and summons) shipped 27 April 2025. Since then Steam reviewers report little content, a rebuild of core systems and an announced Act 4, and recent reviews have turned mixed on abandonment concerns, though the lifetime score stays high. Platforms: Windows and Mac on Steam (no Linux build, though a reviewer reports it running smoothly on Linux); Steam Deck plays well; Metacritic page shows scores as unavailable, and no OpenCritic entry was found.

#### Best Things

- Polished, detailed pixel art and strong sound design, praised as remarkable for a very small team
- Smooth, responsive combat ('smooth as butter'); reviewers also report it running well on low-end PCs and on Linux
- Hades-style god blessings let runs snowball into distinct synergy builds (lightning storms, crit executions, etc.)
- Diablo-like gear with affixes plus per-character talent trees gives a satisfying loot and build-crafting loop
- Heroes play differently (Druid shapeshifting, summoner-style options), giving replay value across characters
- Short, intense runs that deliver an empowered 'high-level ARPG character' feeling quickly, good for limited play time
- Strong value: many reviewers report 50-150+ hours for about $7
- Hub with unlockable characters, vendor and skill trees gives a sense of long-term progression

#### Worst Things

- Content drought: last big update (Act 3, The Darkmoor) came in April 2025, and content is limited to three acts/maps with repeating enemy variety
- Poor and infrequent developer communication; many reviews cite six months or more of silence and abandonment fears
- Missed original release window; Early Access has run since 2023 with updates taking a year or more each
- Heavily RNG-dependent loot and god-blessing rolls, so runs can feel luck-gated
- Balance issues: between patches dominant god combos emerge while other gods are nearly useless, and the game gets too easy after the early stages
- Terrain and map problems, with reports of getting stuck on terrain while taking attacks that are hard to avoid
- Some players say the game does neither survivors nor ARPG well and lacks a clear creative direction
- Loss of identity: several reviewers say recent updates made it feel like 'yet another survivors-like' and forgettable, and object to the announced rebuild/about-face of the gameplay

#### Improvements

- Regular progress updates and roadmap communication (devs have said they dislike weekly posts, but fans want more than silence)
- More maps, acts, enemies and unique items to reduce repetition
- A talent tree preview or calculator so players can plan without paying the reset cost each time
- Better in-game tutorials and clearer progression explanations, since many players rely on wikis
- Loot and progression tweaks such as reroll or more deterministic options to reduce RNG frustration; balance pass on overpowered gods and builds

#### Unique Things

- Hybrid of Vampire Survivors-style auto-fire horde survival, Hades-style gods and Diablo-style gear and talent trees
- Persistent hub that upgrades over time with new characters, vendor trading and skill trees
- Item affix and loadout system deeper than typical survivors-likes, letting gear define the build
- Olympian-style pantheon with personality-filled gods and a light, optional lore layer
- Planned five-act structure, with Act 4 announced alongside a rework of core systems such as terrain generation and sprites

#### Tedious Or Disliked

- Loot-hunting for affixes can mean collecting lots of useless items, and reviewers call the RNG loot system bloated
- Repetition sets in once the three acts are cleared (about 10 hours to beat the final act per one reviewer), because enemy variety does not change with difficulty
- Paying a reset cost to re-spec talents after a misplaced point
- Some players find a few gods' voice lines cringey
- Getting stuck on terrain while being hit by attacks that are hard to avoid (one of the most upvoted negative reviews)

#### Divisive Points

- Whether the game is abandoned or just slow: positive reviewers point to blog posts and a rebuild in progress, negatives say the last year showed almost nothing
- The planned rebuild toward more handmade, less procedural content: some see ambition and a fix for staleness, others see scrapping the game's identity mid Early Access
- Whether it succeeds as a blend of survivors-like and ARPG, or is unfocused
- Difficulty and balance: some find it easy after early stages, others hit walls at high darkness levels
