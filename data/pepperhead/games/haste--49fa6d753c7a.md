- **Summary:** Haste is a fast-paced 3D running roguelite where you race from the start to a portal while a void chases you, chaining dives and perfect landings to keep energy up for your active ability. He finds it skill-intensive but deeply satisfying once it clicks, with excellent visuals and standout music. The roguelite layer is light but impactful, deliberately so as not to disturb the core movement. Updates since his earlier review fixed many of his complaints. He enjoyed it enough to keep playing well past what the tier list required and places it high on the board, though the exact tier letter is not spoken.
- **Quote:** What you see is what you get.
- **Rerank Note:** He reviewed the game previously in a standalone video; since then it has been updated and many earlier issues were fixed, so this is a re-evaluation.

##### Praise

- Core movement and dive/perfect-landing system feels great and rewards mastery
- Excellent visuals and a large, high-quality soundtrack he listens to outside the game
- Roguelite elements (passives, actives, gems) blend well without straying from the core running
- Active abilities (board, wings, jump) enable distinct playstyles
- Post-launch additions: per-level modifiers, harder modes, better keyword and item explanations
- Extremely satisfying and fun; he kept playing beyond what he needed for the ranking

##### Criticism

- Losing all health costs a life and restarts the same level layout or boss, which feels tiring
- Active abilities are too expensive, discouraging experimentation with builds
- Loop mode gives few items, making an endless mode less appealing
- Bosses can be frustrating and are hard to make work in a running game
- Hub area could use more secrets and things to do; runs do not differ drastically from each other

##### Unique Points

- Momentum-and-angle based movement with perfect landings feeding an energy system
- Roguelite layer kept intentionally minor to protect the core mechanic
- Four biomes repeated at higher intensity with randomized levels

##### Comparisons

- Cave (Cave of Qud-style pick unclear; he says he prefers Haste)

##### Design Criteria

- Core gameplay feel: movement and skill ceiling dominate the evaluation
- Roguelite integration: elements should support, not disrupt, the core mechanic
- Visuals and audio: art and music are major strengths
- Meta-progression: stats and unlocks acceptable but active abilities overpriced
- Run variety: modest differences between runs, deemed acceptable
- Failure/retry design: restarting the level on life loss is tedious

#### Best Things

- Movement and sense of speed: chaining perfect landings, reading terrain and weaving obstacles feels fluid, tactile and arcade-like; repeatedly compared to what a good 3D Sonic or Tiny Wings in 3D would feel like
- Soundtrack: drum-and-bass style tracks with smooth blending between levels are called out by players and by critics (e.g. But Why Tho) as a highlight
- Art style and character design are widely described as charming and gorgeous
- Skill expression: no jump button, high skill ceiling, and S-rank chasing gives mastery payoff; flow state when it clicks
- Late-game and endless-mode item snowballing (RoR2-style) lets runs go absurdly fast, which fans find hilarious and satisfying
- Runs great, including 60fps on Steam Deck, and DualSense haptics are praised
- Good value and broad appeal: cheap-ish at $19.99, Sonic-fan catharsis, works as a chill or background game
- Critics (But Why Tho 9/10, Vandal 82, The Games Machine 80) call it a refined arcade experience

#### Worst Things

- Levels are procedurally generated and, per many negative reviews, blur together with mostly palette and obstacle changes across the 10 shards
- Only three (some say four) bosses, recycled and made tougher in later shards; the final boss is called underwhelming by some
- Roguelite layer is shallow: many items are small stat or condition bonuses, few change how you play, and runs feel similar
- Story and dialogue are frequently criticised as wordy, flat or badly paced for a game about going fast; many players skip it
- Procedural generation can produce bad terrain or no-win stretches, with obstacle clusters forcing hits and some boss setups depending on luck
- Bugs and performance issues reported by some: crashes, freezes, black screen after Continue, stuttering, lag in extreme endless runs
- Multiplayer is thin: players share a world but barely interact beyond gifting lives and items, so buying extra copies disappoints some
- Repetition after the early shards (novelty wears off around shard 3-4) leads several reviewers to a thumbs-down despite loving the core

#### Improvements

- Hand-crafted or curated levels (Clustertruck, Neon White and Cyber Hook are cited as better comparisons), possibly with time-trial and leaderboard support
- Items that change playstyle or act as permanent mechanics rather than small stat boosts; more build variety
- More bosses and more distinct biomes and level variety per shard
- Stronger, better-paced story or an option to skip or trim dialogue; UI readability (speed indicator is easy to miss)
- Fixes for procedural-generation edge cases (no-win landings, luck-dependent bosses) plus accessibility options such as a screen shake toggle and the ability to decline challenge rewards
- More content or DLC and mod support; players were disappointed the devs said no more content updates are planned

#### Unique Things

- Sonic-like speed and momentum fantasy in a roguelite with no jump button; speed is built by timing landings on downslopes
- Landing-quality mechanic (perfect, good, bad landings) that drives both speed and score
- Sparks economy and item snowballing that can let endless mode reach absurd speeds
- Ten-shard campaign with a node map, characters and story wrapped around a runner, plus an endless mode and later time trials
- Drop-in online co-op with crossplay on consoles, and a Workshop mod scene (e.g. character swaps)
- Built by Landfall (Totally Accurate Battle Simulator, Clustertruck) with a playful tone and Steam Deck-friendly performance

#### Tedious Or Disliked

- Shards can run around 20 minutes and feel long and samey once novelty fades
- Forced dialogue and story beats between levels; many skip them
- Grindy achievements: one reviewer cites the 5000 km achievement as requiring a long endless slog, and shard 10 achievements as annoying
- Permanent upgrades (anti-spark and similar) feel like plain stat bumps, and some players need repeated runs for them
- Collision or terrain trapping in ditches, unavoidable obstacle clusters, and luck-driven level layouts that can ruin a no-hit attempt
- Challenge nodes and the map feel tacked on, and several reviewers say items and map choices can be ignored while still getting S ranks

#### Divisive Points

- Whether the roguelite structure belongs: many negative reviewers say a hand-crafted level game would be better, while positive ones say procedural runs and item snowballing drive replayability
- Story and characters: some find Zoe and the cast cute and delightful, others find the writing boring and overlong
- Difficulty: some call it too easy (items and speed trivialise it) while others hit boss and level-generation spikes they find unfair
- Multiplayer: fine as a casual drop-in for some, disappointing as true co-op for others
