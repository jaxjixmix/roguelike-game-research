### Community

- **One Liner:** A hand-drawn Lovecraftian Metroidvania from Thunder Lotus (Jotun) with procedurally assembled maps, endless enemy horde rooms, and a corruption-based power system.
- **Consensus Summary:** Sundered is well liked overall: Steam shows Very Positive (about 82%, roughly 5,200 reviews), and Metacritic sits at a Mixed-or-Average 74 from 39 critics, with user score 7.1. Nearly everyone praises the hand-drawn art, animation, atmosphere, boss designs and the fluid movement and abilities. The split is over the core design: the endless horde spawns, semi-procedural maps and stat-driven progression delight some players and feel repetitive and tedious to others. It is a finished 2017 release (Eldritch Edition added the free Magnate of the Gong update with new areas and local co-op); it is not early access. Some recent Steam reviewers mention a dip in recent ratings, and the top positive reviews push back on it.
- **Reception Notes:** Launched July 2017 to generally strong reviews from outlets such as GameSpot (8), Eurogamer and Rock, Paper, Shotgun per the Steam page, while the Metacritic aggregate is a more modest 74 because of mid-range scores tied to repetition. The Eldritch Edition added the free Magnate of the Gong update (new areas, quest chains, a boss, local co-op). Platforms: Windows, Mac and Linux on Steam; Steam Deck and controller support noted in reviews (keyboard play is weaker). #### Best Things

- Gorgeous hand-drawn art and fluid animation, the most universally praised element (Thunder Lotus signature, compared with Jotun and Spiritfarer)
- Large, memorable boss fights that reward learning patterns
- Movement and traversal feel great, and new abilities (especially corrupted versions via eldritch shards) make you feel powerful
- Atmospheric Lovecraftian world, soundtrack and sparse, evocative lore with a narrator voice and multiple endings
- Sprawling central skill tree and build variety give a real sense of progression after each death
- Procedural layout keeps exploration fresh and makes a blend of Metroidvania and roguelite that some call the most meaningful one they have played
- Non-linear exploration and currency collection, plus difficulty options and easy 100% achievements for completionists

#### Worst Things

- Endless horde rooms and waves of weak, repeated enemy types (palette swaps) feel overwhelming, spammy and screen-covering
- Combat is described as button mashing or dodging, with little depth outside bosses
- Long stretches of empty or samey rooms; the map is not very helpful and the start is confusing with little explanation
- Some boss fights are considered poorly designed (either trivial with the right build or slow and tanky)
- Local co-op is widely called a letdown: the second player cannot die, collect items or meaningfully interact
- Keyboard play suffers, notably the grappling hook section (works far better on a gamepad)
- Story is told obliquely and is easy to miss or forget; it is also short (roughly 10 to 20 hours)

#### Improvements

- Clearer onboarding and tutorials, including explaining shard destruction and corruption mechanics
- More enemy variety and less reliance on palette-swapped stronger variants
- Better map and navigation clarity in a procedurally assembled world
- A meaningful co-op design where player two has health and can interact with the world
- More flexible save slots, since players say the three slots limit getting all endings without replaying
- Clearer storytelling and more content or length

#### Unique Things

- Resist or Embrace system: corrupt abilities with eldritch shards for power at the cost of humanity, leading to different endings
- Procedurally assembled Metroidvania map with handcrafted elements rather than a fixed hand-built world
- Horde-based combat where huge numbers of enemies spawn continuously in some rooms
- Roguelite-style permanent upgrades via a central skill tree that let you come back stronger after death
- Lovecraftian setting in an abandoned underground military base with huge boss sprites

#### Tedious Or Disliked

- Endless enemy respawns that force you to stop and fight while chipping away at your health, then chase you back through rooms
- Grinding perks and shards; guides reportedly recommend AFK or boss farming for money, which players read as a flawed progression loop
- Three endings requiring separate playthroughs, repeating the same path and looting
- Running through many empty rooms to get from A to B
- Overly complex skill tree with few exciting unlocks for some players
- Early game frustration before upgrades arrive

#### Divisive Points

- Procedural generation: some love the replayability, others say it undercuts what makes a Metroidvania (authored level design)
- Horde mechanic: seen as fitting chaotic eldritch atmosphere by fans and as an annoying pacing killer by detractors
- Difficulty and feel of combat: empowering and smooth once upgraded vs. shallow and sloppy
- Value and length: worth it at the price for many, bare-bones and only decent on sale for others
