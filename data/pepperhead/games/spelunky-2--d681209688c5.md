### Community

- **One Liner:** Derek Yu's 2D procedurally generated platformer-roguelike sequel in which Ana Spelunky, daughter of the original hero, descends through a much larger, more interconnected set of caves on the Moon, with one-life runs, physics-driven chaos and optional 4-player co-op or arena play.
- **Consensus Summary:** Spelunky 2 is received as a superb, more-of-the-same-but-bigger sequel: critics gave it near-universal acclaim (PC Metacritic 91, IGN 10/10) and Steam players rate it Very Positive (about 93% of roughly 22k reviews). Praise centres on its deep systems, secrets, mounts, branching paths and hilarious emergent deaths. The recurring caveats are brutal difficulty with hidden one-hit-kill hazards, a no-save/no-checkpoint structure that turns the true ending (Cosmic Ocean) into a multi-hour endurance test, and, in recent Steam reviews, long-running online multiplayer/crossplay bugs. Some long-time fans feel it is slightly less tightly balanced than Spelunky HD. It is a finished, shipped game (PC/PS4 2020, Switch 2021, Xbox 2022, PS5 2025) rather than early access, though developer updates appear to have been sparse after the initial patches.
- **Reception Notes:** Launched on PS4 (Sept 15, 2020) and PC (Sept 29, 2020) to universal acclaim and a Best Indie nomination at The Game Awards 2020; later ports came to Switch (Aug 2021), Xbox One/Series (Jan 2022) and PS5 (Mar 2025). Launch reception was about difficulty and refinement; the current Steam conversation (recent negatives) is dominated by online co-op and crossplay breakage, with reviewers saying a crossplay update made friend sessions unreliable and that developer communication is scarce. #### Best Things

- Systems-driven chaos: liquids, fire, explosives, thrown items and enemy interactions create funny, memorable emergent stories (players describe deaths as punchlines)
- Huge, dense secret and unlock content with branching paths (Jungle/Volcana, Tide Pool/Temple), alternate routes, and shortcuts that reward exploration
- Mounts (turkeys, rock dogs, axolotls, qilins) and new environments add real tactical variety while keeping the classic feel
- Strong mastery loop: reviewers and players describe progress as learning to read threats, with winning a run being unusually satisfying
- Polished art, animation and soundtrack, more expressive visuals than the original, plus a coherent world with NPCs and a family-themed story
- Excellent couch and online co-op for up to four players, plus a customizable arena/deathmatch mode with 40 maps and bots, daily challenges and seeded runs
- Enormous replay value; many Steam reviewers have 100 to 1000+ hours and call it a near-perfect or desert-island game
- Quality-of-life improvements over the first game (e.g. more consistent shopkeeper balance, expanded exploration) that some players think make it more approachable
- Best with friends: reviewers repeatedly say the chaos and deaths are funniest in co-op, and a few call it a calming, even cozy, game once you learn its rules

#### Worst Things

- Many instant-death or combo-death situations, including hidden hazards such as bear traps concealed by terrain, which critics (e.g. Destructoid) called frustrating and players call cheap or trolling
- Online multiplayer problems widely reported on Steam: stuck on the loading screen, inability to join, crossplay-related breakage, and a bug requiring workaround input to load the next area; some reviewers say it has been broken for months or over a year
- Perceived lack of developer communication/support after launch; Steam complaints mention no word from the developers for a long stretch
- The Dwelling, Jungle and Volcana are called disproportionately hard for early worlds by some veterans, and certain levels feel specially designed to ruin a run
- Some dedicated players feel the added mechanics (and the 'Sunken City'/Cosmic Ocean path requirements) dilute the tight risk-reward balance of Spelunky HD
- Movement/controls friction: no dedicated pickup/drop/throw/climb buttons, leading to accidental climbs or item handling mistakes that kill the run
- Short-range whip and thrown-item ricochet feel punishing to newcomers, who complain about taking damage just to hit a stationary enemy
- Gameplay randomness (seed quality, needing specific items to spawn for shortcut unlocks) is cited as unfair by players who wrote negative reviews

#### Improvements

- Fix online co-op stability and crossplay bugs, and communicate about fixes on the Steam forums
- Offer optional accessibility or difficulty options (e.g. extra health, easier modes, checkpoints/continues); one reviewer called their absence sadistic, though the developers deliberately preserve the no-continue design
- Reduce reliance on concealed or unreadable hazards so deaths feel more telegraphed and learnable
- Add dedicated buttons or rebind options for pick up/drop/throw/climb to reduce accidental actions
- Smooth the early-game difficulty ramp (Dwelling and Jungle in particular) and make unlock requirements less dependent on random spawns
- More post-launch communication and updates; reviewers complain of over a year of silence while plush merchandise was announced

#### Unique Things

- Every level is a hand-tuned procedural layout built from templates, so runs feel random but readable and fair at a systems level; the series is the reference for this style
- Permadeath with literally no meta-progression in power: only shortcuts, unlocks and knowledge carry over, so skill is the main upgrade
- Shared physics and item interactions where almost anything can kill you, including your own thrown items, enabling speedrun and trick strategies
- Branching world structure with hidden areas, alternate paths and a secret true ending (Cosmic Ocean) requiring a long perfect run
- Integrated multiplayer modes: 4-player couch and online co-op over the same campaign, and a full arena deathmatch mode with map variety
- Distinct personalities for shopkeepers, NPC family members and the ghost mechanic that punishes slow play

#### Tedious Or Disliked

- Cosmic Ocean: a many-hour endurance sequence with no saves that many call the main grind; Steam reviewers joke about not being able to spare four hours and about few reaching it
- Starting over from 1-1 after a sudden death, which newcomers and some critics (Gameblog.fr at 60/100) find unpedagogical and demoralizing
- Repeated tedious retries to unlock shortcuts and characters, with rewards like a victory screen and a new character considered underwhelming by some
- Status effects such as poison and curse described as run-ending annoyances
- Fighting the interface and inputs (accidentally climbing or not being able to drop items) rather than the game
- Bug-related restarts in online mode, such as getting stuck on the 'walls are shifting' screen

#### Divisive Points

- Difficulty and fairness: fans call it a masterclass, others call it a troll game; critics at Destructoid and GameSpot (8/10) noted excess one-hit-kill traps while IGN scored it 10/10
- Versus Spelunky HD: some find 2 the better, more expansive game, while others think HD's risk-reward balance was tighter and that 2 is merely 'more Spelunky' with added complexity
- Randomness vs skill: some players love the unpredictability, others argue too many deaths come from unfair spawns rather than player error
- No-continue philosophy: praised as the source of the game's tension and reward, criticised as disrespectful of player time and a barrier that steers casuals away
