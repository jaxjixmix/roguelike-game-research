##### Praise

- Very unique and interesting blend of Tetris-like placement with strategy and combat
- Distinct factions (now a third via DLC) that play so differently they feel like new games
- Multiple build paths such as river, mountain/military and religion/enemy-stealing
- Bosses act as mini puzzle mechanics you must solve with your build
- Map pathing requires real thought depending on your build, which he loves in deckbuilders
- Simple rock-paper-scissors unit combat becomes surprisingly strategic and mathematical late game
- Update added the constellation cooldown ability and many QoL fixes, like an option to stop pieces auto-falling

##### Criticism

- Still no rewind button, despite frequent small mistakes
- Always the same three bosses; wants variety within them
- Meta-progression mostly unlocks more options, which can clutter the reward pool
- Wants more Slay the Spire-style narrative events with choices on the map
- Runs are very long (hours), which is a caution even if pacing is good
- Early on the game felt overwhelming, boring and repetitive until more was unlocked

##### Unique Points

- Tetris piece placement as the core of a strategy roguelike, with placement location changing building effects
- Factions each change the game drastically, making the game feel new
- Bosses designed as individual miniature puzzle mechanics
- Rerolling map path choices tie terrain and unit presence to boss strength, creating a risk/strategy tradeoff

#### Best Things

- Genuinely fresh twist on Tetris: terrain tiles (plains, forest, river) and building tiles interact, so placement matters more than clearing lines; completing a row triggers an 'exploration' that pays out resources by terrain
- Satisfying synergy building: farms, lumberjacks and military buildings reshape nearby terrain and combo with each other; finding combos is repeatedly called rewarding
- Rock-paper-scissors unit counters and enemy buildings that you are forced to place add a second layer of tactical thinking to block placement
- Forgiving and relaxed: unlimited single-turn undo and quick reload of a battle, no time pressure on placement, good for short sessions and runs well on Steam Deck
- Lots of content in the base game: 110+ cards, 100+ achievement-style challenges driving unlocks, multiple very different factions, branching paths and selectable boss order
- Hooks grow over time: several players say it is gimmicky for the first hours and becomes addictive around 10 to 30 hours as depth appears
- Polished, cute presentation with snappy actions and a pleasant evolving soundtrack; critics (Guardian, Vandal, Games Machine) singled out the clever design and clean interface
- Good value at $14.99 with post-launch faction DLC and ascension/endless modes; Steam reviews at 89% positive across ~3,300 reviews

#### Worst Things

- Heavy RNG: with roughly 20 pieces per round, a few bad draws can ruin a run, and several reviewers say good play cannot always outskill a bad hand; GameCritics called it RNG-dominated
- Unlock tree dilutes runs: as you unlock more tiles and passives the draft pool gets watered down, making later runs feel unfocused and harder to get the pieces you need
- Combat math and information are opaque: players say enemy vs player buildings are hard to tell apart (poor contrast, small flags), and the UI lacks the info needed for informed placement; the dev reportedly acknowledged the explanation is confusing
- Balance is uneven: some factions and cards feel far stronger than others, later-unlocked factions are more tile-order sensitive, and military buildings are often the obvious pick over resource ones
- Some find it more of a deckbuilder with Tetris flavor than a real Tetris game; Tetris skill helps but the game offers no tutorial or training for newcomers
- Boss and late-game design feel like mechanical checkpoints to some, and late-game reroll abundance makes builds samey
- Minor technical issues reported: Steam Deck button dropouts, controls or keyboard layout resetting, resolution setting not applying, occasional stuck combat prep and visual flicker

#### Improvements

- Reduce RNG frustration with more draw control, better piece smoothing, or a fast retry that keeps the same seed
- Rework the progression tree so unlocking more does not dilute the pool, and allow presets or loadouts for the progress tree (a requested Steam feature)
- Clearer combat UI and tutorials: show unit math, make enemy buildings visually distinct, and add a Tetris primer or training mode
- Rebalance factions and weak cards, and make peaceful/resource paths competitive with military
- More varied bosses and a fresher late game (one reviewer asked for a different Act 4), plus more run-to-run variance
- Fix reported platform bugs (Steam Deck input, controls resetting, French DLC translation error) and consider a GOG release that players have asked about

#### Unique Things

- Hybrid of falling-block puzzle, roguelite deckbuilder and light tactics; compared by players to Slay the Spire mixed with Tetris, or a single-player Catan
- Line clears are 'explorations' that harvest resources from terrain rather than just clearing space, and you are sometimes better leaving gaps than clearing
- Enemy buildings are shuffled into your deck and must be placed on your board, turning defense and counters into a placement puzzle
- Faction design: Duchy, Republic and Order in the base game, plus Tribe (druid, constellation/combo mechanics, endless mode) and North (Viking, individual unit battles, ascension mode) DLC
- Branching map with selectable order of the boss fights and a large achievement-driven unlock tree
- Won Excellence in Game Design at the 2025 DevGAMM Awards and got D.I.C.E. and Pegases nominations; Todd Howard publicly praised it

#### Tedious Or Disliked

- Progression is grindy and opaque early on: other clans are gated behind crowns and tree unlocks, and one reviewer was 8 hours in before understanding how crowns are earned
- Achievement-gated unlocks can stall progress (a reviewer stuck waiting on a rare card tied to a windmill achievement), and forcing you to play weaker starting cards for unlocks feels punishing
- Choosing between a winning run on higher difficulty and an 'unlock run' is a disliked tension
- Repetitive loop after roughly 20 hours: several positive reviews say they finished most achievements and hit the end of engagement
- Combat often feels pointless or slow by the end of each round to some, and pace is too slow for those expecting fast Tetris
- Late-game reroll and sell cycles feel like chore work when you can simply reroll until you get your build

#### Divisive Points

- RNG: fans see it as roguelite spice and part of adapting, while detractors say it removes the sense of skill and cleverness
- Tetris identity: people who love Tetris techniques (T-spins, holding for key pieces) feel rewarded, others feel it is really a deckbuilder and Tetris newcomers feel lost
- Depth vs overwhelm: some call the synergy depth addictive, others call the deck and combat systems overwhelming or over-complex
- Longevity: 50 to 60 hour reviewers love the ongoing discovery while others feel done after 20 hours
