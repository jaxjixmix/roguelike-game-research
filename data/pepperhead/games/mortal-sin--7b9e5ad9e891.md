- **Rerank Note:**

##### Praise

- Core first-person combat is very satisfying; dismembering enemies changes how they behave
- Combo system with many class-specific combos adds depth beyond simple swinging
- Auto-combo feature makes the game beginner friendly and suits the fast pace, with a slight penalty
- Weapon durability forces constant weapon and equipment switching, keeping combat fresh
- Very unique visuals with adjustable filters so players can tone down the intensity
- Many classes and hidden content, plus signs of a true ending, encourage replay
- Three-stage run structure with hub upgrades gives a sense of building a character

##### Criticism

- Hub has too little to do given you return to it between biomes
- Enemies and environments are visually repetitive
- Classes feel different but not dramatically so; mostly combos and killing next to you, little ranged play
- Roguelike elements found in runs are enjoyable but not game-changing
- Default visuals can be overwhelming, and dark levels make it hard to see what is going on
- Has been in early access for 4+ years; full run is long at roughly an hour and a half

##### Unique Points

- Combo-scoring system where chaining attack types (dash, kick, swing, charge) triggers extra effects
- Auto-combo option that shuffles combos automatically, while manual play avoids unwanted effects like the mage freeze
- Three separate runs per character with a hub in between, like a very long run with soft permadeath
- Limb-loss enemy behavior and weapon durability drive the moment-to-moment variety

- Combat feel: core first-person combat is the main draw and very satisfying

- Accessibility: auto-combo lowers the barrier in a fast-paced multi-class game
- Class diversity: many classes but only moderately different in play
- Run structure and meta-progression: three biome runs with hub upgrades, long total run length
- Visual identity and options: unique, dark, adjustable filters, but repetitive enemies

- Replayability: hidden content and a likely true ending

#### Best Things

- Fast, visceral first-person melee with combo chains, kicks, dodges and heavy dismemberment that many reviewers call addictive and 'crack-like' once the systems click
- Distinctive comic-book / manga-like art direction in red, black and white tones with dark-fantasy iconography, praised by 3DJuegos and Steam users
- Large roster of classes (16 per one review, including Berserker, mage, pirate, gun users) each with their own combos and unlockables that drive replay
- Very responsive solo developer: adjustable graphics/eye-strain settings were added within about a week of a forum post; Berserker was made the starting class after feedback
- Steady stream of free content and reworks through Early Access and after 1.0 (new languages, unique items, the Blitz Ascension mode with daily challenges)
- Good pick-up-and-play feel for short adrenaline sessions, and many players with hundreds of hours say it holds value for the price
- Helpful onboarding tools once you find them: combo list on Esc that can be pinned on screen, a practice dummy in the hub, and an auto-combo option for newcomers

#### Worst Things

- The combo system is a core mechanic that some describe as DDR/Guitar Hero-style input; players who dislike it say the game cannot be enjoyed without it, since unused combos leave you underpowered
- Combat can feel weightless, mashy and chaotic: enemies spawn behind you, floor effects kill with little warning, and hits lack impact according to several negative reviews
- Enemy variety looks large but enemies feel alike, and fights become 'build a setup and spam your best combo' with few situational decisions
- Run upgrades and randomized loot are often described as not impactful enough, making later runs feel samey
- Opaque systems and confusing UI: multiple mana bars, inventory/RPG screens and unexplained mechanics force players to watch videos or read a wiki
- Some traps and the secret platforming portals are disliked, especially the forest branches/hands
- Limited content per critics: one review counted only six areas with three gated behind story progression, and the first three zones repeat over 15 levels
- Reports of bugs or performance regressions after updates, such as a mouse cursor locking and FPS drops on camera movement

#### Improvements

- Better in-game explanation of combos, stats, mana bars and inventory so new players do not need outside guides
- More impactful and build-defining run upgrades and loot so runs diverge more
- Slower, more readable encounters with more distinct enemy behaviours that require different approaches (a common wish from players who want parry/pattern-learning depth)
- Stronger hit feedback and weight in melee combat, and tighter integration of block/parry with the combo and power-up systems
- Rebalancing of classes (a monk is called underpowered in one review; a Steam thread calls Gladiator broken) and more varied levels
- Options to reduce combo dependence, e.g. richer auto-combo, and quality-of-life options such as turning off the leaderboard or fixing cursor issues

#### Unique Things

- First-person melee roguelite with a per-class combo-input system rather than simple attack spam, combined with a Doom-like pace
- Comic-book, sketch-like dark-fantasy look with PS2-style textures, volumetric fog and gory dismemberment that changes how enemies behave
- Over a dozen classes spanning greatswords, guns, claws and magic, each with distinct combos
- Hundreds of hand-crafted rooms stitched into procedural maps, plus randomized quests and secret platforming zones
- Blitz Ascension mode with time pressure and daily challenges, plus a true ending in the 1.0 release

#### Tedious Or Disliked

- Memorizing and executing combo inputs while hordes surround you; some feel they spend all their attention on controls
- Repetition: several reviewers say runs get repetitive or exhausting after a couple of hours because enemies and upgrades feel alike
- The final boss, called tedious and unbalanced by some players: long health pool and one-shot moves
- Unreadable deaths and ambush spawns or floor hazards that kill without clear warning
- Platforming portal sections and certain environmental traps
- Cluttered HUD and inventory UI that some never get used to

#### Divisive Points

- The combo system: praised by many as the crux of the game's depth, and called the thing that ruins it for others
- The combat reworks (class-specific combos replacing universal ones, faster weapons): some long-time Early Access players feel the game got monotonous and worse, while many consider the patched version better
- Price at $24.99: some call it great value, others say it is overpriced or fine only on sale
- Chaos vs readability: the frantic Doom-like pace is a selling point for some and a mess for others
