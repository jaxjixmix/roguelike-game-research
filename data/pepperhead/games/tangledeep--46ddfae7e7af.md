### Community

- **One Liner:** A 16-bit-styled, turn-based traditional roguelike/dungeon crawler by Impact Gameworks with a job system, pet taming and a town hub, with an optional no-permadeath Adventure Mode.
- **Consensus Summary:** Tangledeep is well regarded: Steam shows Very Positive (about 82% of ~1,490 reviews positive), Metacritic critics average 79 from 7 Switch reviews, and GOG lists about 78 on OpenCritic with 100% critic recommend. #### Best Things

- Polished SNES-era pixel art and an acclaimed soundtrack (composer Andrew Aversa with guest composers) that reviewers repeatedly call captivating
- Job/class system with about 12 base jobs where you can mix and match abilities and passives via multiclassing, giving lots of build freedom
- Very customizable difficulty: four difficulty tiers including a no-permadeath Adventure Mode plus 10+ optional modifiers; praised by GameCritics as something more games should copy
- Approachable for roguelike newcomers while still offering depth; several players call it a cozy, chill traditional roguelike
- Large amount of content: side areas, quests, item dreams (mini-dungeons to upgrade gear), a town hub, farming, pet taming and breeding; one player logged 192 hours without seeing everything
- Good controller support plus fully rebindable keyboard controls; players say it plays well on gamepad and the Switch version gets long playtime

#### Worst Things

- Top-voted negative review (12 votes): beautiful art and good music but 'a whole lot of unmemorable stuff in between'; never reaches its potential
- Combat and build depth felt shallow to veterans: few enemy types, skills maxed out early so tactics stop changing, equipment rarely changes how you play
- Generally too easy for experienced roguelike players, who say a no-death clear in one attempt is plausible (while Nintendo Life cautioned difficulty spikes for newcomers)
- Clunky interface and controls per several players: unhelpful default keymapping, menus described as convoluted, and movement described as clunky
- Tutorial is thin and mostly leaves players to figure mechanics out
- Late-game gear selection becomes a slog, with strategies dictated by RNG loot and lots of unusable drops for your class
- Some feel it does nothing better than its genre predecessors and lacks the creativity and humor of games like Dungeons of Dredmor
- Music cannot be turned off until after character creation, cited as a UX annoyance
- No character customization beyond class choice (4 votes)

#### Improvements

- A better, more informative tutorial and clearer default control/keymap labelling (controller Start/Select confusion, WASD vs arrow keys mismatch reported)
- More enemy variety and skills/equipment that change tactics rather than giving flat passive boosts
- Cleaner menus and inventory UI; Steam reviewers called the interface clunky despite an unlimited text inventory
- Fewer rough edges for ranged/melee play such as automatic weapon switching back to melee (one reviewer complaint)
- Mitigations for run length and RNG punishment, since full runs are reported at 6-14 hours and a late death is demoralizing
- More build-defining synergies so the multiclass system feels necessary rather than optional

#### Unique Things

- Capture-and-breed pet system: catch monsters, keep one fighting beside you and breed them in a Monster Corral
- Job system with free class switching (for a fee or free) and cross-class ability mixing, reminiscent of JRPGs
- Item Dreams: random mini-dungeons you enter inside an item to strengthen it, a Disgaea-like idea
- SNES/JRPG-flavored presentation with town hub, farming, banking and shared progression between characters, blending roguelike with Mystery Dungeon and classic RPG feel
- Adventure Mode without permadeath alongside a hardcore mode, plus a heavy modifier menu and challenge leaderboards

#### Tedious Or Disliked

- Full runs are very long (one player reported 6-7 hours at best, up to 9-14 hours), so a late death feels painful
- Catching pets is called a drag (GameCritics) and pets feel almost necessary for some players
- Grinding for gear upgrades and sifting through lots of junk loot late in the game
- Some players say winning favors patient defensive play rather than tactical skill, making long runs feel repetitive
- Turn-based movement and menu navigation felt slow or unintuitive to some new players

#### Divisive Points

- Difficulty: newcomers and some critics report spikes, while veterans find it easy and never close to dying
- Depth: fans see great build freedom via multiclassing, detractors say abilities are basic and multiclassing is unnecessary
- JRPG-adjacent style and pet/farming features read as charming to some and as distraction from a pure roguelike to others
- Permadeath pacing: long hardcore runs are praised for stakes by some and called unfulfilling by others
