- **Summary:** Gambonanza is a chess-based roguelike often described as a Balatro-like chess game, with blind-style boards, a shop for jokers-equivalents (gambits), new pieces and board upgrades. He feels the roguelike layer dominates and the chess itself gets lost quickly, leaving him with a muddled in-between design. ##### Praise

- Tutorial is great and explains everything clearly
- Bosses are unnecessarily cool and impressive
- Music copies Balatro's layered, iconic-feeling soundtrack successfully
- Unique concept with a great vibe that many people enjoy
- Some builds where everything clicks feel great
- Board upgrades are a cool substitute for Balatro's tarot cards

##### Criticism

- Lots of randomness, including the board crumbling, which can help or hurt a lot
- Losing pieces permanently makes strategy and build-paths feel costly and annoying
- Builds and gambits are too specific and hard to synergize, especially early in shop
- Chess thinking is lost quickly; fast pacing conflicts with chess, caught between being chess and being a roguelike
- AI moves randomly and unpredictably, so you can't outplay it; you often just wait for a safe capture
- Queen is always best, undermining piece diversity despite gambits

##### Unique Points

- Balatro-style blinds and shop pacing applied to chess, with gambits as jokers
- Board itself is upgradable via special spots
- Crumbling board pressures you to clear fast, unusual for chess
- Design lesson: he would rather it fully lean into roguelike and drop chess purism

##### Comparisons

- Balatro
- Below the Crown
- Chess

- Synergies: builds too specific and hard to assemble
- Randomness: crumbling and AI randomness reduce strategic control
- Risk vs reward: permanent piece loss discourages creative play
- Core mechanic integration: chess and roguelike elements do not mesh
- Enemy/AI design: random, unpredictable AI feels dumb
- Boss design: flashy and memorable
- Audio: Balatro-like layered music praised
- Onboarding: tutorial clear

#### Best Things

- Strong high-concept hook: Balatro-style run building applied to chess, with Gambits that change piece and board rules (150+ advertised)
- Slick, polished presentation: CRT pixel-art look, smooth animations, catchy music and a clean interface
- Addictive 'one more run' synergy building when a combo lands, e.g. mass-producing pieces or chaining skipped enemy turns
- Easy to pick up for people who only know basic chess moves, and some players say it quietly teaches chess tactics
- Unlockable Gambits and higher difficulties extend replay beyond the first win
- Tiles, enemy modifiers and boss fights add tactical puzzle variety; some reviewers call bosses and tile upgrades the highlight
- Short-session friendly, especially on mobile and Steam Deck
- Mobile and PC versions exist, and several players like the low mobile price

#### Worst Things

- Enemy AI is weak or effectively random unless it can capture, so the optimal play is often to burn turns and wait for it to blunder a piece
- Permanent loss of captured pieces across the run means trades and sacrifices, the heart of chess, are punished and the economy drains on replacing pieces
- Balance: queens outclass other pieces, duplication and skip-turn Gambits are overpowered, phantom-to-gold tile combos dominate, while many Gambits are weak or rely on 1/3 to 1/5 random triggers
- Crumble mechanic (tiles falling away to end stalling) and some enemy modifiers feel random and can decide or ruin a run
- Unskippable animations and boss cutscenes with no speed-up option make the game feel slow on PC
- Repetition: identical starting board layouts per stage, builds locking in early, low variety in pieces and tiles
- Heavily derivative of Balatro in art, UI, sound and loop, which some reviewers saw as near copy-paste
- Bugs and rough edges reported: achievement and Gambit interaction bugs, lost progress in one review, resolution shared across devices, no cross-save
- Price: several top-voted reviews (85 and 161 helpful votes) say $14.99 on Steam is too high for the content, especially since the mobile version is about $6 and includes a PC copy

#### Improvements

- Add an animation and game speed option or skippable boss cutscenes
- Make the enemy AI play to win and rework the stalling meta (for example make piece loss partial or chance-based, or restore pieces between stages)
- Rebalance the economy, especially strain 2 price and income changes and queen-strain perishable Gambits
- Buff or redesign weak Gambits, reduce low-probability triggers, and nerf the dominant queen/duplication/tile combos
- Add more pieces (e.g. fairy pieces), upgrades, tiles, board layouts and music for replay variety
- Add an undo or confirm-move option, larger or clearer text for tooltips, and cross-save between platforms

#### Unique Things

- Win condition is capturing every enemy piece, not checkmate, which removes check and pin logic
- Gambits rewrite chess rules and piece behavior, and a reserve system lets you deploy pieces from off the board
- Pieces are a permanent run resource: losing one removes it for the whole run, tying chess tactics to a shop economy
- Tiles on the board (trap, gold, ghost, blessed) plus a crumbling-board timer that forces action
- Gacha, pachinko and slot-style mini-games for acquiring pieces and items
- Mobile-first origin (simultaneous iOS/Android release with a port by Ateo) with a Steam version that has controller and Steam Deck support

#### Tedious Or Disliked

- Waiting moves and passing turns until the AI hangs a piece, which some players describe as effectively AFK play
- Long unskippable animations, especially the roughly 15-second boss intros, plus a gacha opening delay
- Rerolling the shop and chasing specific pieces or Gambits via gacha RNG to complete a build
- Repetitive runs because stage layouts repeat and the same strong Gambits get picked every time
- Higher difficulties (queen strain and above) that feel more like RNG or forced resets than tactical depth, with Gambits expiring
- Text is small and hard to read on Gambit tooltips

#### Divisive Points

- Whether a chess game should lean on chance: fans say it's a chaotic synergy builder, not Stockfish chess, while others say randomness removes skill
- The Balatro comparison: some love it as 'Balatro but chess', others say it is only a thin clone or that it is a different game entirely
- AI difficulty: some call it easy and dumb, others say reviewers did not get past stage 3 and later stages punish mistakes hard
- Price: $14.99 Steam feels too high to some given a cheaper mobile version and Balatro's price, while others call it a fair buy, and Steam-specific discussion notes mobile is cheaper
