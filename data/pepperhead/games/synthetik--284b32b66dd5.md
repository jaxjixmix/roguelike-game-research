##### Praise

- Slow, deliberate shooter pacing that differs from typical fast-paced shooter roguelikes
- Huge variety of unique guns with modifiers and upgrades, which is where most of the depth lies
- Mechanics like jamming, reloading and limited ammo force awareness of build and gun behavior
- Precise, satisfying gunplay; headshots feel great
- Sound design and VFX are fantastic
- Pre-run class choice with distinct mechanics and different starting weapons

##### Criticism

- Levels are repetitive and feel like one big room, reducing immersion
- So much detail and complexity that bosses can kill him with no clear cause
- Unlocking classes and starting weapons is slow, so runs begin with the same setup
- Leveling is per class, so trying another class means starting from zero
- Could be intimidating for newcomers

##### Unique Points

- Approaches the shooter genre realistically and slowly, rewarding stealth and careful aim instead of constant firing
- Gun mechanics (jams, reloads, scarce bullets) are central to the roguelike build rather than a side detail

##### Design Criteria

- Gameplay depth: complex gun mechanics and a slow pace are what make it distinct
- Build variety: many unique guns with modifiers and upgrades drive the roguelike layer
- Level design: repetitive, big-room feel counts against it
- Readability: dense stats and details make boss deaths hard to understand
- Meta-progression: class-locked leveling and slow unlocks limit starting variety
- Audio/VFX: sound design and effects make shooting feel great
- Accessibility: complexity may intimidate newcomers

### Community

- **One Liner:** A top-down tactical shooter roguelite from Flow Fire Games where hands-on gun handling (active reload, jams, recoil, ammo types) meets randomized weapons, eight classes and a punishing five-floor run against the Machine Legion.

A sequel, Synthetik 2, has been in Early Access since 2021. The core complaint is a steep, poorly explained learning curve plus heavy RNG, and some players report crashes and glitches in recent years.

#### Best Things

- Gunplay feel: active reload, magazine ejecting, jams/overheating and recoil make every gun feel physical and skill-dependent
- Huge weapon pool (70+ guns with manufacturer variants and randomized stats) plus 140+ items (turrets, mines, mind control, orbital lasers) enabling very different builds
- Eight classes/subclasses with long progression and prestige arcs, so new unlocks keep coming 30-40+ hours in
- Very high replayability and skill ceiling; runs feel like mastery of a build rather than a fixed meta
- Sound design and soundtrack are repeatedly called out as excellent
- Modular difficulty (about 11 independent modifiers) lets players tune the challenge, including 220%+ for veterans
- Two-player online co-op with shared loot is described as great fun by those who get it working
- Cheap price for the depth, and a free Arena version works as a demo

#### Worst Things

- Poor onboarding: the game drops you into a run with a weak tutorial and items/weapons/mods whose effects are not explained, so reviewers say many mechanics must be discovered or looked up
- Harsh RNG can starve a run of weapon crates or upgrade shops for several floors, and runs can end from a single small mistake
- Crashes and game-ending bugs, including runs wiped by crashes and getting stuck on obstacles; co-op lobbies sometimes fail to connect
- Unfinished or broken interactions (for example the Modular weapon mod is reported as unusable)
- Display and UI problems: no real ultrawide or multi-monitor support, minimalistic UI that some find awkward, no item descriptions in shops
- Late game spikes hard: floors 4-5, back-to-back gauntlet rooms and the final boss Armageddon are seen as a DPS check that only a handful of weapons can handle
- Some features feel gated (a loadout tied to a friend referral code, locked DLC content) which irritated a few reviewers

#### Improvements

- A proper tutorial and in-game tooltips/item descriptions, including in shops
- Stability fixes for crashes and run-ending bugs, and more reliable co-op lobby/connection
- Better balance across the weapon pool so more than a small subset is viable into floors 4-5 and the final boss
- Less punishing or more forgiving RNG for crate and upgrade-shop spawns
- Display options: ultrawide/multi-monitor support, an earlier settings menu, and a way to disable screen flash
- Finish or fix half-implemented content such as the Modular mod

#### Unique Things

- Hands-on gun mechanics in a roguelite: manual magazine eject, timed active reload, jams and overheating
- Weapons generate with manufacturer-specific variants and randomized stats instead of fixed items
- Eight specialist classes that change playstyle entirely, from sneaking assassin to turret engineer to heavy breacher
- Engineer-style gambling dynamics (double or nothing with credits) and devil-offer red crates that trade health for power
- Modular difficulty sliders and a prestige system rather than a single fixed difficulty
- Free companion game Synthetik: Arena shares character and achievement unlocks with the main game

#### Tedious Or Disliked

- Long losing streaks while learning: only a small share of players beat the final encounter (one reviewer cites about 7% with the achievement)
- Trial-and-error discovery of what items and weapons do, often costing a whole run
- Late-floor gauntlets (three hard rooms in a row) and the final boss feel like a slog after a long run
- Overwhelming enemy spam and explosive threats such as Doombots in solo play
- Grinding class levels and unlocks, and repeating the long runs (8-hour sessions reported)
- Screen flash on explosions with no option to turn it off

#### Divisive Points

- RNG: fans see it as brutal but fair chaos that creates stories; detractors call it unfair and run-deciding
- Difficulty: some treat it as a mastery game and eventually love it, while others bounce within an hour or two
- Solo vs co-op: co-op is praised but some find the game too complex or enemy-heavy for a casual co-op session
- Synthetik 1 vs 2: at least one 870-hour reviewer says neither the Early Access sequel nor any other game grabbed them like the original
