##### Praise

- Two distinct loops (digging and defending) that create tension when you must return from deep to defend
- Huge variety from many gadgets, different domes (sword, turrets, guns, lasers) each with unique upgrade paths, and a new character with a different playstyle
- Selective upgrading: you cannot max everything in a run, so choices depend on what you find and the challenge at hand
- Unlocking new gadgets and starting gadgets drives replayability; every run feels like its own game
- Minimalistic, peaceful audio with sparse music, plus satisfying visual effects and ore sounds that make the loop relaxing
- Exciting discoveries underground, like unique interactables that reward figuring them out

##### Criticism

- Early game is slow and repetitive since you always start very weak
- Digging can get repetitive and runs are long, especially when luck keeps the relic hidden (an hour and a half for some runs)
- Excitement may fade once all gadgets have been seen
- Permanent progression is not held onto as strongly as in other roguelikes
- Personal gripe with the hard 1% health remaining achievement

##### Unique Points

- Challenge runs, not random runs, are the core and they unlock the permanent progression, which is rare for roguelikes
- Defense is deliberately simple so the digging and discovery remain the focus
- Hybrid of digging exploration and base defense with two very different paces

##### Comparisons

- Steamworld Dig
- Blueprints (audio approach)

##### Design Criteria

- Replayability: new gadgets, domes and characters make each run feel different
- Meta-progression: unlocks tied to challenge runs, light permanent progression
- Build variety and choice: cannot max all upgrades, so selective upgrading matters
- Pacing and run length: slow start and long runs when the relic is hard to find
- Audio/visual feel: minimalistic music and satisfying effects support the chill vibe
- Gameplay loop design: dig vs defend with intentionally simple defense
- Discovery and exploration: unique finds underground are exciting
- Risk vs tension: going deep while needing to return to defend

### Community

- **One Liner:** A 2D roguelite from Bippinbits (published by Raw Fury) where you dig underground for iron, water and cobalt between monster waves, then rush back to a dome to upgrade it and personally shoot down attackers.
- **Consensus Summary:** Players like it a lot: about 92% positive on Steam across roughly 22.5k reviews (Very Positive), with a long tail of players logging hundreds of hours. Critics were warmer than lukewarm but more reserved, landing at Metacritic 76 and OpenCritic 78, with a 71% critic-recommend rate and Eurogamer as the one outright no-recommend. The shared verdict is a tight, addictive short-session loop with great atmosphere that is repetitive and thin on content, particularly solo after roughly 10 hours. It is a finished 1.0 game that has kept receiving free updates: a Godot 4 migration with many new gadgets, later co-op multiplayer (local and online), a paid character/content DLC line (Engineer Gear Pack, The Lost Keepers), and an Xbox Series X/S release in December 2025.
- **Reception Notes:** Released Sept 27, 2022 (Steam, Win/Mac/Linux) after an itch.io origin; launch reviews were favourable but not glowing, mostly praising the addictive simple loop and criticising content depth. The 2024 announcement of a major content update (new gadgets roughly doubling the set, Godot 3 to 4 migration) was followed by free multiplayer, which Steam reviewers credit with lifting the game from average to top-notch for groups. Additional paid content (Engineer Gear Pack, The Lost Keepers) exists, and some reviewers dislike characters sitting behind DLC. Xbox Series X/S version launched Dec 9, 2025 and mobile versions are listed as in development; Metacritic also lists an iOS entry. #### Best Things

- Risk/reward tension of mining: every second underground is a second away from defending the dome, and players say that pull never goes away
- Simple, readable, low-friction design that works for a quiet 30-minute run and is praised as a Steam Deck fit (clean UI, stable 60 FPS)
- Atmosphere: pixel art, monster design and a sparse, crystalline sound design and soundtrack that reviewers and players both single out
- Replayability from modes, relics, gadgets, multiple domes and keepers, map sizes and modifiers, plus meta-progression and challenges
- Multiplayer/co-op added later is frequently described as a big upgrade and a great family or friends game
- Approachable difficulty that scales, so newcomers and veterans can both find a setting; runs are short
- Runs well on modest hardware and Steam Deck, with native Linux support
- Ongoing developer support with free content updates

#### Worst Things

- Repetition: runs play out near-identically (dig, return, upgrade, fight), and the same optimal upgrade paths get picked each time (Shacknews, many Steam reviews)
- Limited content at launch price: few unlocks, only two base keepers with more behind paid DLC, and some players report everything unlocked in 10 to 40 hours
- Mining/defense split can feel like each half interrupts the other; Eurogamer said it struggles to make either a success in its own right
- Late-game automation (drones, automated turrets) can make runs trivial, reducing the need to defend manually even on hard difficulties
- Sparse tutorial; many mechanics and early challenges are opaque and players turn to the wiki
- Technical complaints in Steam reviews: crashes on some setups, language-change bugs, a Steam Deck launch failure report, and frame-rate drops in Endless Prestige past about wave 35
- Reports of RNG cave generation producing dense blocks with few resources, which can soft-lock a run or challenge
- Some consider full price ($17.99) high for the content depth

#### Improvements

- Bigger late game: larger maps, more resource types, more weapons, base mods and artifacts (repeated by critics and testers)
- A way to shorten or vary the opening, such as modifiers or unlocks that start you with basic upgrades or random early tech, since the opening is identical every run
- More run variety and surprises from mining (more randomness, more enemy types) and some balance work on essential-upgrade builds
- Better multiplayer progression: a shared token or upgrade pool and cheaper second gun so one player does not hog resources or babysit the tower
- Free reroll on gadget choices and more forgiving resource generation to cut unlucky runs
- Performance optimization for long Endless/late-wave runs and better onboarding for new players

#### Unique Things

- Reverses the tower defense formula: no building dozens of towers, you personally operate the dome's laser between digging trips
- Comparisons to Motherload-style mining combined with wave survival and a roguelite meta layer
- Three-resource economy (iron, water, cobalt) with transport by carrying, so logistics and depth decisions drive every run
- Win conditions beyond survival, including relic hunts, scenario modes, guild challenges and endless modes
- Multiple keepers, domes and worlds that each add a twist, plus up to 4 local or 8 online co-op players and cross-platform multiplayer

#### Tedious Or Disliked

- The early game of each run: slow hauling of resources, no meaningful upgrades yet, and a travel-heavy return trip for each wave, described as a chore
- Having to go back to the dome constantly because waves come quickly, which some call more chores than stress
- Progression gating: inter-run progress tied to guild challenges, some of them considered nearly impossible early and frustrating when a loss yields nothing
- Searching blindly for the relic or specific ore; challenge runs like mazes or iron quotas can drag
- Multiplayer tends to split into one tower babysitter and everyone else mining, with chores visible and little variation

#### Divisive Points

- Whether the dig/defend switch is the charm or the flaw: some love the tension, others find the fun part constantly interrupted by the boring part
- Difficulty and depth: many praise scalable challenge, while others say as long as you keep busy you always win and automation trivialises later runs
- Value: players who log hundreds of hours call it a bargain, while ones who finished in under 10 to 40 hours call it thin for the price
- Repetition is seen by fans as a cozy loop with hidden depth and by critics as predictable
