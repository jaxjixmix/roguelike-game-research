#### Entry 1

- **Label:** Tier list Part 4 (2025)
- **Summary:** Sephiria is a top-down action roguelike dungeon crawler from the makers of Dungreed, currently in early access. Its hook is an inventory-placement system where artifacts and item positions power up your build rather than traditional leveling. He loves the adventure feel, story, weapon variety, pre-run customization and music. He calls it a hidden gem deserving more attention, placing it at the top of A tier with S potential if balance and content improve.
- **Quote:** This is another absolute hidden gem.
- **Rerank Note:**

##### Praise

- Inventory management as the core progression: artifact placement buffs items, and level-6 items do drastically different things
- Each weapon feels distinct, with special abilities, dash attacks, dashes and combos; gameplay more satisfying than Dungreed
- Strong adventure, story and hub side-quest feel (even a creepy horror-tinged quest); each early-access update adds content
- Lots of pre-run customization via multiple upgradeable 'mirrors' giving permanent stats, plus a newly added starting-item pool
- Heat-style difficulty system that rewards permanent progression
- Every run feels unique, with varied bosses and mini-bosses per biome; killer music

##### Criticism

- Elemental builds (fire, lightning) feel much stronger than anything else and are too easy to just collect into
- Melee/stat builds require juggling many item types while elemental items all just work, so items need to be more flexible
- Mana/spell system feels weak, unnecessary and not loved; spells not impactful
- Same biomes and few secrets per run, and the true final boss is still missing in early access
- Wants more secrets and alternate biomes like Dungreed had

##### Unique Points

- Inventory layout and artifact placement replace stat-based leveling as the way you get stronger
- Many different mirror-style permanent upgrade tracks that you can keep switching between each run
- Tablet imprint mechanic that embeds tablets without using an inventory slot; he wishes it could be a core, occasional option
- Multiplayer support, which he has not tried

##### Comparisons

- Dungreed
- Hades (mirror and heat system)

##### Design Criteria

- Build/inventory synergies: placement strategy and artifacts are the core, but elemental builds dominate
- Combat feel: weapon diversity and satisfying moveset
- Meta-progression: many mirrors, hub unlocks, starting-item pool
- Replayability/run variety: unique runs despite repeated biomes
- Difficulty: heat-style system with permanent rewards
- Story/adventure and immersion: hub quests and ongoing content updates
- Balance: elemental versus non-elemental and mana/spells
- Audio: praised music
- Content/boss variety: two boss and mini-boss variants per biome, missing final boss

### Community

- **One Liner:** A top-down pixel-art action roguelite from Team Horay (makers of Dungreed) where you play a bunny defending Bunnyville, building power by fitting artifacts and tablets into a Tetris-like inventory grid, with up to 4-player online co-op.
- **Consensus Summary:** Sephiria is received as one of the best recent action roguelites: the local Steam snapshot shows about 12,970 reviews at 94.5% positive (Very Positive), and the Steam store page showed an Overwhelmingly Positive rating earlier (97% of roughly 2.9k reviews), so sentiment has cooled slightly as the audience widened at 1.0. Players and critics praise the inventory-puzzle build system, big weapon/costume variety, pixel art and co-op. The two Metacritic critic reviews are 90 (TheSixthAxis) and 83 (This Is Game). Complaints cluster around netcode and disconnect-loses-the-run in multiplayer, artificial pacing gates, boss design that pushes evasion builds, and repetitiveness after many hours. #### Best Things

- The inventory-management build system (placing artifacts/relics around tablets and talismans in a grid) is called unique and addictive; several reviewers describe optimizing the grid as the core puzzle of the game
- Large variety of weapons (6 main weapon types, each with 50+ upgrades that change how it plays), costumes with unique stats and set bonuses, and artifacts gives many viable, distinct builds
- Snappy, responsive combat with a wide range of archetypes (tank, greatsword, bomber, frost, lightning, meteor mage)
- Clean, expressive pixel art and cute character designs; boss music and chill soundtrack are singled out
- Smooth progression curve: weapons, artifacts and unlocks are introduced gradually, with very wide meta-progression and plenty of secrets to unlock
- Co-op for up to 4 is seen as well balanced and a standout feature for playing with a partner or friends; you can leave a co-op run and return to your own village
- Strong quality-of-life features such as loadout preferences and control over favored item spawn rates, plus multiple difficulty levels
- Large amount of content and polish with a charming story and lots of care from a small studio; players report hundreds of hours

#### Worst Things

- Multiplayer netcode: delay-based and server-priority hit registration makes dodging unreliable for clients, especially at 100+ ping; many negative reviews are purely about this
- A disconnect or host drop ends the run for everyone with no rejoin and no mid-run save, which can waste an hour or more; it also happens on LAN for some players
- Progress can be lost on game crashes, reported by some players including on Linux
- Boss design: large unavoidable-feeling attacks, unclear telegraphs, long invulnerable phases, and one boss with a screen-wide tentacle barrage that some find annoying or unfair
- No epilepsy or photosensitivity warning and no flashing-reduction toggle despite a newer chapter 3 boss with map-wide flashing attacks; raised repeatedly by affected players and their friends
- Some reviewers find combat shallow or hollow (one-button combos) and enemy rooms either too easy early or visual damage-soup late
- Story is called over-the-top or cliche by some, and cutscenes/dialogue get skipped; boss cutscenes are repetitive when retrying
- Localization quality issues (for example a Russian reviewer rates the current translation about 4/10)

#### Improvements

- Add rejoin/reconnect and a mid-run save or checkpoint for multiplayer and crashes; several reviewers suggest a save point after the first boss
- Move to client-side hit detection or rollback-style netcode so non-hosts are not penalised
- Add an epilepsy warning and a flashing-effects toggle, plus other accessibility options such as OLED-friendly text colors and a way to disable hold-to-continue prompts
- Rebalance evasion/defense builds and healing scarcity so tanking and offensive builds are viable; tune boss telegraphs and invulnerability phases
- Reduce the artificial progression gates (some chapters require extra runs for small lore rewards) and speed up meta-progression and rewards for winning runs
- Improve translations and add more post-1.0 content (players noted little changed between Early Access and release); a voting system for which floor to take in co-op was also suggested

#### Unique Things

- The Tetris-like inventory grid where artifact placement next to tablets and talismans is the main source of power, comparable by one reviewer to a Materia or Attache Case system
- Deep roguelite meta-loop with a village (Bunnyville) and story chapters gated behind repeated runs
- Weapon upgrade system where anvil upgrades make the same weapon play like a different one
- Costumes that act as class-like set bonuses (evasive cat, big-inventory fox, fast white wolf, scholar lizard) with trade-offs
- Co-op where main story progression belongs to the session host while you can still return to your own village
- Dungreed-style synergy ideas expanded into the central build mechanic, presented in cute bunny-themed pixel art

#### Tedious Or Disliked

- Runs get long (an hour or more for some) and the constant inventory shuffling plus fast-travel backtracking through non-continuous map paths pads the time
- Repetition after roughly 20+ hours: players settle into one build and have little reason to vary it because the game is easy once core synergies click
- Padding in progression: extra runs demanded per chapter and slow meta unlocks feel like a grind
- Late-game boss fights described as bosses spamming hard-to-avoid attacks; the final boss can kill in one attack and delete an hour or two of progress
- Evasion is widely seen as mandatory, which flattens build diversity at the top end

#### Divisive Points

- Difficulty: some call it very difficult and hectic, others say it becomes trivial after a few talents and that evasion is overpowered
- Inventory management: praised as the best feature by most, but described as 50% of the game and a time sink by a few (even a joking positive review calls it a puzzle game)
- Build variety: praised as huge by many, but others feel builds are a checklist and that costume set bonuses lock you into specific routes
- Story: sweet and charming for some, cliche and skippable for others
