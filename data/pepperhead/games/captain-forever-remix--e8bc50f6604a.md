##### Praise

- The core concept of building your own ship from salvaged parts is unique and fun
- Collecting parts from defeated ships is an appealing loop
- Progressing through different universes gives a sense of journey
- He thinks it might be interesting as a multiplayer game

##### Criticism

- No pause, so you can be attacked while trying to grab dropped parts
- Runs are long, up to around 45 minutes
- Losing parts or a favorite weapon leaves you at a big disadvantage for the rest of the run
- No real progression system within a run, so losing parts returns you to starting strength
- Level layouts are basically empty space with random ship encounters, which gets tiring quickly
- Pacing feels odd

##### Unique Points

- Building a ship out of parts taken from enemy ships is an unusual roguelike build mechanism
- Design lesson: if power lives only in collectible parts, losing them with no fallback progression feels punishing

##### Design Criteria

- Concept originality: the ship-building idea is the main draw
- Run length: 45-minute runs are too long given the punishment of setbacks
- Progression within a run: none, so losing parts resets your power
- Risk vs reward: losing parts is a severe, hard-to-recover penalty
- Level design: empty space with random ship encounters is tiring
- Controls/pacing: no pause makes part collection stressful
- Single-player fun: judged solo, where it did not hold up

#### Best Things

- Build-while-fighting ship construction feels improvisational and creative; every piece you attach changes movement, armor and firepower
- Scavenging parts from randomly generated enemy ships keeps each encounter a new puzzle (PC Gamer: improvisational, gleeful and brutal; Retromation: action-FTL meets Legos)
- Weapon variety (ScatterBlasters, MegaBoom rockets, RipSaw blades, lasers, a healing module) and unlockable Power Bulkheads give room to experiment
- Bright 90s Saturday-morning-cartoon art style and sibling-rivalry framing (girl as Captain Forever vs little brother King Kevin) are charming
- Mellow electropop soundtrack is singled out by reviewers and sold separately as an OST
- Easy to pick up, strangely addictive, with a sandbox mode and Steam Workshop support for free-play ships

#### Worst Things

- Timed build phase after a fight: leftover parts vanish when the teleport timer ends, so you arrive at the next sector half built
- Brutal difficulty spikes: a much stronger enemy can shred your ship in seconds, making effort feel pointless (reviewers describe one-tap deaths even with a shield)
- Dying loses everything from the run, with no resource saving or meta progression to soften it
- Combat reads as shallow: mostly orbiting an enemy with clunky controls; many weapons just get outranged by lasers
- Enemy parts are always a tier above what you can build, so farming and scaling feels futile
- Seen by fans of the original as more restrictive than the seamless Captain Forever; some say to buy the Trilogy or Reassembly instead
- Reported as needing keyboard and mouse for best control
- Builds feel pointless: after a long build, a random much stronger enemy can wreck the ship, so the creativity is rarely rewarded

#### Improvements

- Option to shorten, lengthen or disable the build timer
- Let players store higher-tier parts or carry some resources between runs so the start is less punishing
- Selectable difficulty or a balance pass on enemy tier jumps and one-shot deaths
- More content and goals (quests, objectives) because killing ships and grabbing parts gets old by the time all characters are unlocked
- Multiplayer, requested by a long-time player
- Better controls and less obstructive dialogue

#### Unique Things

- Real-time modular ship building on the fly in the middle of a roguelike run, rather than menu-based upgrades
- Licensed re-imagining of a cult indie classic by a different developer (Pixelsaurus with Future Crayon) than the original Farbs trilogy
- Ten-sector trip across the Solar System with a sibling-rivalry story wrapper and cartoon tone
- Teleport after killing the highest-tier enemy makes each sector a fight-then-scavenge-and-rebuild cycle
- Steam Workshop and sandbox mode let you reuse constructed ships outside the campaign
- Physical Collector's Edition on USB cartridge with manual, soundtrack CD, watch and stickers (reviewed by SLUG Magazine)

#### Tedious Or Disliked

- Rushing to attach parts before the timer expires, often with little time to build anything usable
- Repeatedly restarting after losing a long run with nothing carried over
- Fine-motor precision needed to attach parts while under fire (Common Sense Media notes this and later-level frustration)
- Harvesting parts from the same kinds of enemies becomes repetitive over a long session (RPS: can feel like a grind, best in short bursts)
- Devs' standard advice to find time-extension components is seen by some as not addressing the complaint

#### Divisive Points

- The build timer: some see it as the tension that defines the game, others call it the main flaw
- Art and humor: praised as nostalgic and charming by some, criticized as plain immature by others
- Remix vs the original trilogy: Remix is more structured and accessible, while purists prefer the seamless, slower original ship-building
