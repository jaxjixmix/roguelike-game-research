##### Praise

- Huge amount of content and permanent upgrades, including equipment that can be put on different characters
- Always something to do in a run: digging for minerals, a mission objective, swarms, and a payload
- Runs are short, which suits the heavy roguelite focus
- Weapon evolution system adds mechanical upgrades and some strategy in choosing upgrades
- Fantastic visuals, befitting the IP
- Four characters with subclasses that play very differently (effectively ~16)

##### Criticism

- Gameplay is fairly simple and generic; nothing crazy unique beyond the digging
- Upgrades are mostly numbers rather than mechanical changes
- Unlucky low-value upgrade rolls can feel really small
- Some weapons feel unsatisfying or not crisp early on despite great art and animation
- Levels sometimes feel too short
- Prefers the original mode over the newer payload campaign

##### Unique Points

- Digging for different minerals that gate permanent upgrades
- Per-level mission with a reward adds an extra goal within a run
- Advocates rarity tiers (common to legendary) on level-up upgrades, tying into luck

##### Comparisons

- Death Must Die
- Repetendium

- Content volume: rates the amount of unlockables and modes highly
- Meta-progression: mineral-gated permanent upgrades and equipment
- Upgrade design: numbers-heavy versus mechanical, rarity and luck, weapon evolutions
- Weapon feel: guns must be crisp and satisfying
- Run pacing: short runs with constant activity and objectives
- Uniqueness: weighed heavily, and the game loses points for being generic
- Visuals and charm: strong art from the IP

#### Best Things

- Polish and presentation: colorful, glowing minerals and enemies, satisfying explosions, runs well on Steam Deck up to Hazard 4
- Mining adds real risk/reward: standing still to mine valuable loot while swarmed, and digging new paths through maps
- Weapon variety with overclocks that change how a weapon behaves, plus 12 subclasses that play differently
- Optional side objectives alongside the main escort/return-to-ship goal make each run more strategic than a pure survive-the-timer loop
- Good value at $12.99; many reviewers report 50-100+ hours of play
- Carries the DRG humor and feel, so fans of the main game feel at home without needing prior experience
- Deep meta progression and lots of unlocks (weapons, classes, biomes, hazard levels) for completionists
- Hazard levels give a clear difficulty ladder; higher hazards feel tense and punish greedy decisions

#### Worst Things

- Weapon balance: a large share of weapons are described as weak or even harmful to have, while a few carry whole runs
- Visibility problems: isometric camera hides enemies behind terrain, the escort vehicle and crystals, and weapon effects draw over enemies, causing unseen contact damage
- Permanent upgrades are mostly small flat percentage bonuses that players feel little impact from
- Shallow in-run build variety and few synergies: many upgrades are just more damage, and many weapons (drones, beams) feel alike
- Large gaps in artifact strength push players to pick the same few upgrades every run
- Hazard 4-5 difficulty spike leans heavily on luck (the Luck stat, gear drops) and on a narrow set of meta builds
- Some bugs reported: supply pod getting stuck, freezes between levels or at launch
- Single-player only, which many reviewers (and Game8/GamingBolt) call a missed opportunity given the original's co-op identity
- Run-to-run variance is thin: the variety is pushed into the meta-progression, so a run feels much like the last (top-upvoted negative reviews, e.g. 'extracted all the run-to-run variance into its over-long meta progression')
- Steep difficulty walls between hazard levels that are cleared by hours of grinding 1% stat bumps rather than skill or strategy

#### Improvements

- Add co-op multiplayer, the most frequent request in Steam reviews
- Rebalance weapons, artifacts and underperforming classes (the Driller is named as weak)
- Streamline or reduce the meta-progression grind, e.g. let progress accrue on more than one weapon/class/biome at once
- Fix visibility: show enemies behind obstacles and stop weapon effects obscuring them
- Add tooltips for character/weapon attributes and make upgrades more build-defining with real synergies
- Add an endless mode and more late-game content or sinks for excess resources; allow separate move and aim direction (requested by one reviewer)

#### Unique Things

- Mining is integrated into a survivor-like: you must stand still to extract resources while enemies close in
- Escort/extraction structure: reach a randomly located ship or drop pod, with a timed escape after boss kills, rather than a fixed 30-minute survive timer
- Level-capped runs with a capped weapon loadout (max 4 weapons) and choose-one-of-three upgrades
- Overclocks that modify weapon behavior, borrowed from the main DRG game
- Gear system with randomized stats and per-subclass loadouts (12 subclasses, each with its own gear slots) layered on top of mastery progression
- Tunnel-digging that reshapes the map, which can rescue you or backfire

#### Tedious Or Disliked

- Late-game grind: one reviewer counts roughly 42 weapons x 12 classes x 5 biomes each with 5 mastery levels, meaning up to ~295 repeated missions
- Gear management chore: 12 subclasses each needing 8 items, limited inventory, risk of selling needed gear if not favorited, and mostly boring stat-stick gear
- Escort missions and the 30-second escape timer after a boss are cited as stressful or unfair
- Cheap-feeling deaths: chip damage from environmental hazards, exploding-enemy waves after bosses, enemies tuned to be just faster than mining speed
- Rare gear drops and luck-gated achievements make completion feel like rinse-and-repeat
- Early menus require clicking through several screens of locked modes and characters before the first run

#### Divisive Points

- The 1.0 gear system: long-time Early Access players say it cut their rating (some from 8/10 to 5/10), while others enjoy the extra customization and loot chase
- Difficulty: some call it too hard and unfair at Hazard 5 or on escort missions, others say the right gear makes it easy and that it is a fair challenge
- Depth vs relaxation: some call it the most polished but shallow and grind-driven survivor-like, others find it both intense and chill and their second-favorite in the genre
- Critics (86 Metascore, PC Gamer 90) are notably warmer than Metacritic users (7.2) and the more negative Steam reviewers
