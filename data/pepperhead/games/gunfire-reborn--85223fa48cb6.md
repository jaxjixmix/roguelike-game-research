#### Best Things

- Gunplay is fun and satisfying, often compared to Borderlands and Immortal Redneck, with an addictive run loop
- Large roster of characters, each with two skills and an ultimate, that change how a run plays, especially in co-op
- Wide variety of weapons and scrolls allows many build experiments, including lucky-shot and elemental builds
- Bosses are distinct and fluid, each with readable mechanics
- Great character designs, from cool to cute, in a colourful art style
- Co-op networking is smooth, and each player gets their own drops that can be traded
- Seasonal mechanics are all kept available so players avoid FOMO, and support continues years after release

#### Worst Things

- English localization is widely described as machine-translated, with confusing or wrong descriptions and some text left in Chinese
- DLC characters are much stronger than base ones, making runs on the highest difficulty very easy and creating power creep
- Build variety becomes stale, with each character reduced to a few optimal weapon and skill combos, and seasonal mechanics feel similar
- Online co-op matchmaking is described as dead, so co-op needs friends
- Balance swings in high difficulty, where enemies either die in one hit or barely take damage
- Recent reviews cite undisclosed AI-generated art in official artwork and emotes, which drove a wave of negative reviews
- Some players report worsening performance, crashes and lost save data over time
- New EULA added forced arbitration and deletion of data after 365 days of inactivity, drawing the most-upvoted recent negative reviews even from fans

#### Improvements

- Add more bosses per stage and new stage areas, since current boss variety is limited
- Make updates meatier, because long-term players say a year of patches can be seen in a few hours and maxing meta progression is quick
- Improve English localization and tooltips so item and scroll interactions are clear
- Rebalance DLC versus base characters and give hard difficulties a real challenge ceiling
- Fix matchmaking or add a lobby or social hub for co-op players
- Offer a clearer dodge (short distance and unclear i-frames) and possible third-person or mod support, both requested by players

#### Unique Things

- Hades-and-Borderlands-style mix: FPS shooting with hero abilities, scroll (passive) builds and Chinese mythology-inspired anthropomorphic characters
- Scroll system lets players assemble synergies, with each character having a different preferred approach (cat for elements, owl for scrolls, fox for frontal assault)
- Seasons as switchable, permanently available mechanics with a free battle pass rather than limited-time FOMO
- Each co-op player has individual drops, guns and scrolls that can still be exchanged
- Runs are long, skill-based dungeon crawls where player skill and enemy knowledge matter more than level-ups

#### Tedious Or Disliked

- Runs can feel repetitive in the short term, and veterans say restarting becomes a chore once builds feel samey
- Waiting for co-op matches, or never finding one
- Getting key scrolls matters for some builds, so a run can be lost to luck of drops
- Reading unclear tooltips and untranslated text to understand mechanics
- Meta-progression is quickly maxed, leaving little to chase between updates
- Dodge feels awkward and the game can feel laggy even solo for some players

#### Divisive Points

- DLC model: some see it as fair funding for a live-service game with no FOMO, others see paid characters as power creep and pay-to-win-feeling
- Difficulty: early runs feel unfair and punishing, yet experienced players find builds trivial on the hardest difficulty
- Comparison to Roboquest, Deadzone Rogue and Abyssus: some say gunplay is weaker but content is larger, others feel its builds are shallower
- Update cadence and nerfs: fans praise ongoing support while others call updates thin and dislike character nerfs
