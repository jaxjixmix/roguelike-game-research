##### Praise

- Gameplay is very satisfying and entertaining once you are comfortable with it
- Surprising variety of unique passive abilities and builds (pig, chicken, bee) that change how you farm and fight
- Many weapons and marriageable NPCs that grant unique bonuses, giving run choices
- Late game is super cool, with a variety of bosses and many ways to approach each run
- Huge content volume keeps long runs from feeling the same
- Would be a pick for hundreds of hours on a desert island, even over some S-tier games
- Simple, straightforward shooting is a good choice since extra mechanics would clutter an action game

##### Criticism

- Tutorial is poor and jokey, leaving new players overwhelmed and confused
- No pause: farming and combat happen simultaneously, which is chaotic and discouraging at first
- Planted items (like trees) cannot be removed, so mispositioned placement under fire feels bad
- Information presented is confusing, making the game hard to get into
- Reading and choosing between passives while being shot with seconds before sundown is punishing for new players
- Roguelite unlocks take very long because runs are slow

##### Unique Points

- Fusing a farming sim with a bullet-hell shooter in real time creates a chaotic multitasking design
- Passive abilities that themed around animals reshape both farming and combat strategy
- Suggests a limited time-stop for farming or a paused choice menu would make it more approachable without losing the chaos
- Long runs make each run feel important and unique

##### Design Criteria

- Onboarding/tutorial: judged poor and overwhelming
- Build diversity: many unique passives and weapons across runs
- Mechanical complexity: straightforward shooting is seen as appropriate for an action game
- Pacing and pressure: real-time farming plus combat with a sundown timer is stressful
- Replayability: lots of content and approaches per run
- Run length: long runs make roguelite unlocks slow
- Difficulty: eight difficulty levels ramp up very quickly
- Accessibility/player agency: lack of pause or time-stop hurts approachability
- Boss and late-game content: variety and payoff praised

### Community

- **One Liner:** A twin-stick bullet-hell roguelite by Bird Bath Games (published by Raw Fury) where you farm mutated crops by day and defend the farm from mutants and bandits by night, across a 12-day year with bosses every third night.
- **Consensus Summary:** Atomicrops is well loved by the players who stick with it (Steam: Very Positive, about 92% of roughly 6,350 reviews) and received a solid-but-not-glowing critic reception (Metacritic 78 from 9 reviews, OpenCritic top-critic average 75 with 69% recommending). Critics and players agree the farming-plus-bullet-hell juggling act is original and addictive, with great pixel art and music. The recurring caveats are a brutal, poorly explained first few hours, visual clutter, thin meta-progression, and a fixed map that repeats. It left early access on Epic and launched on consoles in May 2020 (Steam release listed Sep 2020), got a free crop-system update and the Halloween Deerly Beloved DLC in 2022, and several 2025-26 Steam reviews say the game has gone roughly two years without a patch while fans ask for a sequel or co-op.
- **Reception Notes:** Metacritic's 78 rests on only 9 critic reviews and 27 user ratings, so treat the user 7.1 as low-sample. OpenCritic lists 30 critic reviews (top-critic average 75, 69% recommend) across PC, Xbox, PlayStation and Switch; Eurogamer recommended it after the launch build improved health management, IGN called it less relaxing than farming sims but recommended it to fans of both genres, and PlayStation Universe flagged framerate issues. Early access ran on the Epic Store before the May 28 2020 full launch on consoles and PC (Steam page lists Sep 17 2020, Windows only). Post-launch support included a free Steam update with a revamped crop progression system and the $4.99 Halloween DLC Deerly Beloved (Oct 2022) plus other DLC packs; a mid-2026 Steam reviewer says there has been no patch in about two years, and fans keep asking for co-op and a sequel. Third-party estimates (steam-revenue-calculator.com) put Steam gross revenue near $2.6M and net about $765k with SteamSpy owners 200k-500k, which are order-of-magnitude guesses.

#### Best Things

- The core hook: juggling planting, watering, harvesting and shooting at the same time works as a tower-defense/bullet-hell/farming hybrid, which many reviewers say should not work but does
- Very addictive one-more-run loop; Steam reviewers report hundreds of hours and repeat returns years later
- Pixel art by Toby Dixon and the quirky, catchy soundtrack are widely praised (though music is a minority complaint)
- Build variety and item synergies, such as weed-cutting, slow-bullet and farm-animal combinations that turn a bullet hell into bullet time
- Distinct playable characters (Robusta and the DLC characters are named favorites) that change pace and difficulty
- Humor, marriage/polygamy mechanic and memorable bosses give the wasteland a lot of personality
- Lots to unlock and high replayability with late challenges like deeper years and cat/kitty modifiers
- Tight controls in the Binding of Isaac / Enter the Gungeon mold, with a plant-defense feel compared to Plants vs Zombies

#### Worst Things

- Poor onboarding: the tutorial and item descriptions explain very little, so early runs feel confusing and unfair
- Visual clutter and low readability in later years, making it hard to tell friend from foe or what hit you
- Weak or slow meta-progression; critics and players call the unlock tree (ant colony) close to pointless or just a list you eventually exhaust
- Economy and shop balance: weapon shop prices and weapon upgrade costs feel punishing, and runs can hinge on affordability luck
- Fixed repeating map layout (the same handful of fields) with little randomness, which some feel is not level design
- Difficulty spikes in late years (around year 9) with off-screen charges and poorly telegraphed attacks
- Performance and framerate problems reported at console launch, and some scrolling judder complaints on PC
- Permadeath clashes somewhat with the theme of growing things, per one critic

#### Improvements

- A proper tutorial and clearer in-game explanation of items, economy and farming rules
- Better visual clarity and enemy/projectile readability, especially late in a run
- More meaningful meta-progression and more weapon and shop variety
- Rebalancing of characters, weapon pricing and upgrade cost scaling
- More map or level variety (or randomization) plus an endless mode, which some users request
- Co-op multiplayer and a sequel or further DLC, frequently asked for in recent Steam reviews; ongoing patch support

#### Unique Things

- Farming sim time management fused with nightly tower-defense bullet-hell waves, all in one loop
- A seasonal calendar structure: a 12-day year across four seasons, bosses every third night, and runs continuing through successive years
- Crops double as currency and health economy, so farming decisions directly affect survival and upgrades
- Marrying townsfolk (including multiple spouses) gives run-altering perks
- Farm animals, cats and other allies, plus tool and turret placement, change how you defend the farm
- Health is scarce and hard to replenish, pushing a risk-management style unlike typical twin-stick roguelites

#### Tedious Or Disliked

- Constant multitasking under a timer feels stressful or chore-like to some players, who expected a chill Stardew-style farming game
- Accidentally uprooting turrets or using the wrong tool in hectic combat, and losing items when airlifted away
- Grinding for unlocks across the same five fields and same music tracks; a few players say full unlock takes very long
- Repeated early restarts while learning the game, since little carries over between runs early on
- Sameness of runs for some, with two dominant build styles and shops that restrict purchases
- Some dislike the music, art style or camera scrolling and feel the game is too chaotic visually

#### Divisive Points

- Difficulty and learning curve: many call it addictive and rewarding once it clicks, others refund or quit within hours
- Fixed map: praised by fans as working well for mastery, criticized by others as repetitive with no level design
- Farming versus combat balance: Rock Paper Shotgun-style takes felt farming was weaker than the shooting, while fans see the multitasking as the whole charm
- Meta-progression: some see useful carry-over like the time capsule, others see it as negligible
