#### Best Things

- Fast, responsive Elden Ring combat plus a much-improved movement and traversal system that Steam reviewers say fixes the base game's clunkiness
- Ten-ish distinct Nightfarer classes (8 at launch, Undertaker and Scholar added by DLC) that play very differently and support many build styles
- Nightlord bosses, their stronger Everdark versions, and the soundtrack and arena presentation are frequently singled out as highlights
- Co-op with friends is the core strength; Metacritic users and Steam reviewers call it addictive and very replayable
- Condensed run length (around 40-45 minutes) gives a bite-sized, replayable take on Souls combat with randomized bosses, points of interest and Shifting Earth map variants
- No PvP, no microtransactions and no difficulty-locked paywalls; the roguelite loop drives players to hundreds or even 1000+ hours
- Good value perceived by fans at $39.99 given the hours logged, with long-term players reporting the game is in a better state than at launch

#### Worst Things

- Heavy reuse of enemies, weapons, bosses and environments from base Elden Ring; some call it a mode that should have been free or a mini-game for $40
- Solo play is widely described as frustrating because many bosses and the Deep of Night update feel tuned for a party
- Playing with randoms is chaotic: teammates quit after going down, and Deep of Night leaver penalties punish the remaining players
- Cheated or modded relics reportedly appear in higher-depth lobbies, with no ban or reporting system that players trust
- Loot is a roulette: players describe 40-minute runs with mostly irrelevant drops (boost incantations, weapon skills), making progression luck over skill
- Content drought: Steam reviews in 2026 say months passed with only two new characters, two new Nightlords and one new map, and the developer is accused of abandoning the game
- Deep of Night update drew complaints about the win/loss point ratio, enemy stat scaling at high Depths, and reskinned mutated enemies that mostly one-shot players

#### Improvements

- More regular content: additional Nightlords, maps, characters and unused Elden Ring assets (fans explicitly ask for Shadow of the Erdtree weapons and the Everdark versions of remaining bosses)
- A real matchmaking and leaver system: penalties for quitting, reporting/bans for cheating, and fixes to the Deep of Night point loss when teammates leave
- In-game communication (voice or text chat) and better tools for playing with randoms
- Reworked loot so runs feel less RNG-dependent, with more varied and meaningful drops
- Solo balancing and better scaling for duo/solo, plus pausing or a safer way to handle interrupted runs (players note no pause in online runs)
- Broader map variety and event frequency, since enemy spawns and POIs become predictable

#### Unique Things

- Souls-like combat reframed as a session-based co-op run: three in-game days, each ending in a boss, with a shrinking storm-like circle ('the Night') that forces movement
- Fixed roster of heroes with unique passives and ultimates rather than a blank-slate character, giving it a hero-shooter feel inside an action RPG
- Extraction-style pressure: limited time to level and gather gear before each night, no shopping or long exploration like Elden Ring
- Shifting Earth map variants and randomized points of interest/bosses layered onto a recognizable Limveld map
- Two distinct versions of each Nightlord (Everdark Sovereign), with a difficulty-escalating Deep of Night ranked mode added later
- Meta progression through relics and a hub (Roundtable Hold) instead of the usual Souls-like level-and-weapon grind

#### Tedious Or Disliked

- Runs are long (up to about 40-60 minutes) and cannot be paused online, which is hard for players with limited time, especially in duo or trio
- Deep of Night penalties and leavers can force players to grind matches solo to advance
- Searching for a useful weapon or relic across a run is seen as repetitive and luck-driven
- Rushing to the next objective prevents reading abilities or comparing items, so the game feels hectic rather than tactical
- Content repetition: once the roster and map pool are exhausted, long-time players say runs blur together
- No in-game voice chat or cross-play make it harder to coordinate with friends or randoms (duo mode was missing at launch)

#### Divisive Points

- Whether it is a good roguelite: some reviewers call it a good spinoff but a weak roguelite compared with indie genre peers, while others love the loop
- Solo play: some players say the base game was workable solo, others say recent updates made it effectively unviable
- Design direction: fans who love combat praise the focus, while fans who want world-building and exploration find it stripped down
- Whether the price and reused assets are acceptable for a $40 standalone title
