### Community

- **One Liner:** A top-down roguelite shooter from solo-ish indie studio Bearlex Games (Steam Early Access since Nov 2022) where you pick a character, dig through large cave stages, mine crystals and fight swarms and bosses while stacking weapons, mods and perks.
- **Consensus Summary:** Steam players rate it Very Positive (roughly 90-92% positive across about 220-300 reviews), but it is a tiny-audience title (Steam estimate of 20k-50k owners, only a handful of concurrent players) with no Metacritic or OpenCritic critic coverage at all. The sampled top-helpful English reviews skew heavily negative and are remarkably consistent: a great-feeling base (pixel art, music, humor, gameplay loop) that runs out of variety quickly, with the same maps, enemies and bosses repeating and very sharp difficulty spikes. The game is still in Early Access almost four years after launch. The developer kept shipping updates through mid-2025 and published a revised roadmap in Feb 2026, but has slipped the full-release target more than once. Critic-style validation is absent, so the verdict rests on players and on the developer's own update trail.
- **Reception Notes:** Launched into Steam Early Access on 4 Nov 2022 at a very low price with Windows only and no Mac/Linux. The store page says price will rise about 50% through Early Access. Launch-era reviews already flagged repetition, one-shot deaths, broken or badly designed leaderboard achievements, and a balance spike after the first level; the developer responded by changing the first-place achievements to top-5. #### Best Things

- Strong pixel-art look, music and humor that land in the first runs (a reviewer who still gave a negative verdict praised all three)
- Fast, satisfying core loop of shooting swarms, leveling up and stacking perks, in the survivors-like / twin-stick tradition
- Low price (about $5.99) makes it feel like low risk; several reviewers say the price softens the disappointment and that it is worth trying for genre fans
- Weapon modding and a perk/gem system give room for theorycrafting and min-maxing builds (one 15-hour reviewer says it is fun for chasing a specific build)
- Several playable characters with distinct mechanics, e.g. a bear with a tank companion, Team Mischief as a group you drag around, and an auto-aim/auto-fire character for less precise players
- Local co-op, full controller support, global leaderboards and a competitive scoring angle for players who like chasing ranks
- Developer is visibly active and communicative: regular patch notes, new biomes (Enveloping Rime), new characters and a public roadmap
- Random events add some variety inside runs (called fun even by a critical reviewer)

#### Worst Things

- Repetition: same large cave-style maps, same enemy set and same bosses recur across runs and even within a run, so runs feel alike after a few hours
- Content is thin: reviewers cite about 3 characters and one repeated map at the time they wrote; several reviewers say it feels like a demo or tech demo at the current price
- Upgrades feel flat: mostly plain stat boosts (damage, attack speed, accuracy) without real trade-offs or interesting interactions between weapons and skills
- Steep difficulty spikes: enemy power jumps after the first level, elites are bullet sponges, and late-game enemies or modifiers (e.g. exploding enemies) can nearly one-shot even heavily defended builds
- No real ending or goal to finish a run, so the game is scores and upgrades with no payoff; one reviewer contrasts it with Binding of Isaac for this reason
- Stability complaints: reports of crashes during runs (including losing a long run while chasing an achievement), and one reviewer says it heavily taxes their PC
- Levels look bland (walls, floors and crystal veins) and rooms can feel empty
- Early Access has dragged on since Nov 2022; the full-release estimate of 2025 has passed (store page, not reviews)

#### Improvements

- More enemy and boss variety, including biome-specific enemy sets, so deeper floors do not just mean more of the same enemies
- A real ending or win condition (and more biomes) so that runs and meta-progression have a finish line
- Upgrade and build design with trade-offs and synergies rather than plain stat bumps; make weapon and ability choices feel meaningfully different
- Rebalance difficulty (early ramp, ice biome, boss timer that fills too fast, elite damage) and make defense stats stay useful late
- Fix crashes and add run recovery; make the no-upgrade score mode's rules and lack of unlocks clearer on the store page
- Clearer communication on development pace and release timing (the Feb 2026 roadmap moves in this direction with a town hub, reworked upgrades, gear system and more biomes)

#### Unique Things

- Digging and crystal mining are built into level traversal; the crystal-wall mining is the one thing reviewers say sets it apart from other shooters (though some find it pointless or annoying)
- Weapon modding plus a gem system on top of perks, rather than only passive pickups
- Team Mischief character, a squad you lead and drag with a distance slider, and Orso with a tank companion and HQ assistance
- Two distinct modes at launch: a roguelike mode with upgrades and unlocks and a score-focused mode with leaderboards, weekly/daily challenges (weekly mode slated for removal in the Feb 2026 roadmap) and a planned speedrun mode
- Rover-style mining vehicle abilities and industrial weapons such as a sawblade launcher; Steam Inventory cosmetic drops

#### Tedious Or Disliked

- Meta-progression reads as grindy: one reviewer says grinding upgrades pushed the point of being overwhelmed back but added little new fun
- Slow start and boring early perks; first few minutes of a run lack excitement
- Being one-shot or killed by a single hit in an otherwise slow-paced run
- Leaderboard-first-place achievements (later changed to top 5) were seen as unattainable and as a sign of priorities; suspected cheating on the boards was also mentioned
- Digging through walls is called annoying by some players
- Steam community threads complain about an ice biome that slows and punishes players and a boss timer that fills too fast

#### Divisive Points

- Auto-aim/auto-fire versus manual aiming: one reviewer found aiming too hard without auto-aim and auto-fire unsatisfying, others enjoy the survivors-like feel
- Mining/digging: a signature feature to some, pointless busywork to others
- Price vs content: praised as a cheap fun roguelite by some, called not worth it (compared unfavorably to Vampire Survivors at similar price) by others
- Difficulty: some want a harder roguelike challenge, others find the spikes and one-shot deaths unfair
