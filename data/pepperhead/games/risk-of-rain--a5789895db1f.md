### Community

- **One Liner:** A 2D pixel-art action platformer roguelite from Hopoo Games where up to four players fight through escalating, time-scaled waves on a hostile planet while stacking passive items.
- **Consensus Summary:** Risk of Rain is regarded as a genre classic: Steam shows Very Positive (about 93% of roughly 29.8k reviews) and Metacritic has a 77 critic score (PC) with an 8.0 user score. Critics called it addictive and worth the struggle, while noting dated presentation and rough controls. Today many players recommend it as an important, still fun game but point newcomers to the 2023 remake Risk of Rain Returns, which fixes many of the original's rough edges. Recent Steam sentiment has been dented by a 2025-2026 EULA change (forced arbitration and a class action waiver under Take-Two/2K ownership), plus reports of crashes and poor controller support. It is a finished game, not early access.
- **Reception Notes:** Launched on Windows in Nov 2013 after a 2013 Kickstarter; Mac/Linux followed in Oct 2014, PlayStation in 2016, Switch in Sep 2018 and Xbox One in Aug 2019; it passed 3 million copies sold by 2019 (Wikipedia). Steam-revenue-calculator estimates about $2.0M gross for the Steam PC version alone (Boxleiter-style estimate), and SteamSpy lists 500k-1M owners. Gearbox acquired the franchise in 2022 and released the Returns remake in Nov 2023, after which the original became the cheaper, rougher option. The 2025-2026 EULA/arbitration change and the Take-Two/2K ownership have triggered a wave of negative Steam reviews from otherwise fond players. #### Best Things

- Difficulty-equals-time design creates constant risk/reward tension between looting for items and rushing the teleporter
- Item stacking: dozens of passive pickups combine until a run turns from barely surviving to screen-clearing chaos
- Distinct survivors with different abilities, unlocked through challenges, that change how each run plays
- Chris Christodoulou's soundtrack and lonely sci-fi atmosphere are near-universally praised
- Gritty pixel art that is readable once you adjust to the visual noise
- Fun online/local co-op for up to 4 players
- Good value: very cheap, frequently on sale, and with a strong modding community
- Runs escalate into a satisfying power fantasy by the late stages

#### Worst Things

- Dated presentation: PC Gamer (via Metacritic summary) criticised awful maximum resolution and tiny sprites
- Poor controller support, especially in menus and on Steam Deck; many keyboard-rebinding complaints, including awkward diagonal jumping
- Crashes and stutter reported by recent reviewers, including crashes during the final boss
- Combat feels repetitive: for many survivors it is run away, fire abilities, run again, which gets old in 2D and makes melee survivors and the sniper feel weak
- Difficulty can feel artificially inflated by the timer, with enemies that take ages to kill once you linger
- Enemies can spawn very close to the player, and some elites (e.g. Overloading Magma Worm) kill suddenly
- Opaque item effects and little onboarding make the first hours unintuitive
- Recent review bombing over the EULA/arbitration terms and distrust of Take-Two

#### Improvements

- Proper controller support and menu navigation, including Steam Deck
- Higher-resolution graphics and UI scaling (addressed by Returns)
- Better netcode and co-op stability (Returns reworked networking)
- Stability fixes for crashes and periodic freezes on the Steam version
- Clearer tutorialisation and item descriptions for newcomers
- Reversal of the forced-arbitration EULA that players have to accept to keep playing

#### Unique Things

- Difficulty scales with elapsed time rather than stage count, so the player controls pacing and risk
- Enemy spawning uses a director point system that also creates elite variants when the player gets strong
- Near-total reliance on stackable passive items over permanent upgrades, so each run is about emergent builds
- Roguelite platformer blending 2D movement/platforming with bullet-hell chaos and boss-rush teleporter events
- Born as a two-student University of Washington project that raised over $30,000 on Kickstarter (goal $7,000) and won the 2014 IGF Student Prize
- Launched the franchise that became Risk of Rain 2 and the 2023 remake Returns

#### Tedious Or Disliked

- Repetitive levels and a grindy unlock structure, cited by some as getting boring after a while
- Kiting-based play that some find unengaging (run, shoot, repeat) especially with melee survivors
- Lack of permanent upgrades is a dealbreaker for some players who want meta progression
- Stressful, steep difficulty for casual players, one negative reviewer compared it to The Binding of Isaac and did not enjoy it
- Late-run slowdowns and frame rate drops on weak hardware as the screen fills
- Having to accept a new EULA to keep launching a game already owned

#### Divisive Points

- Original versus remake: many say Returns is strictly better, others say the original has more enemies and items, better exploits/cheese and a rawer indie charm
- Original versus Risk of Rain 2: some call the sequel superior in nearly every way, while others prefer the 2D game's levels and feel that it has less punishing RNG
- Difficulty: celebrated as fair and learnable by some, called unfair and artificially inflated by others
- Simple mechanics: loved as tight and approachable by some, dismissed as shallow by others
