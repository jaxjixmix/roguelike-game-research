##### Praise

- Adorable art style and visuals that carried him through the game
- Great music
- Nice sense of adventure and strong overall vibe
- Being an underpowered shopkeeper rather than an overpowered adventurer fits the theme
- Split between dungeon crawling and shopkeeping is an interesting structure

##### Criticism

- Shop management is repetitive, near-automatic, and could be removed without much loss
- Loop of selling similar materials at higher prices to buy better weapons is rinse-and-repeat
- Gameplay and action are minimalistic, not amazing
- Roguelike elements are mild and runs do not differ much from each other
- Campaign-based: once finished there is little reason to replay

##### Unique Points

- Dual structure of dungeon roguelike plus shopkeeping, with dungeon crawling clearly dominant
- Loot-to-sell economy gates progression into better gear and deeper dungeons
- Shopkeeper fantasy deliberately makes the player feel not overpowered

### Community

- **One Liner:** A top-down action RPG with rogue-lite dungeons where you loot randomized-layout dungeons by night and run your own shop in the town of Rynoka by day (Digital Sun / 11 bit studios, 2018).
- **Consensus Summary:** Moonlighter is broadly liked: Steam shows Very Positive (about 81% of roughly 24.6k reviews) and Metacritic critic scores sit in the mid-70s to low-80s depending on platform (PC 74, PS4 81, Switch 83). Players and critics love the pixel art, soundtrack and the addictive loot-sell-upgrade loop, but a recurring verdict is that both halves (combat and shopkeeping) are shallow, the loop gets repetitive after several hours, and the novelty fades. The game is fully released (not early access) and has had post-launch content (a New Game+ mode, companions, extra room layouts, and the Between Dimensions DLC, July 2019), and is now often free or deeply discounted on Steam. Its sequel Moonlighter 2: The Endless Vault went into early access in November 2025, which has revived attention. Persistent complaints on Steam are controller-first design with poor keyboard and mouse support and a few long-standing bugs.
- **Reception Notes:** Launched May 2018 on PC, PS4 and Xbox One to generally favorable critic reviews, with the PC version scoring lower on Metacritic (74) than consoles (81-84), consistent with player complaints about the keyboard and mouse port. Switch followed Nov 2018, iOS Nov 2020, Android 2021/2022 (via Netflix Games). The Between Dimensions DLC arrived July 2019. Steam user sentiment has drifted slightly lower over time (81% all-time, 79% recent), helped along by reviews from people who got the game free or cheap, and by recurring complaints of unfixed control and bug issues dating to 2018. Wikipedia lists SXSW Gaming and Gamers' Choice nominations. The sequel Moonlighter 2: The Endless Vault entered early access Nov 19, 2025 (PC Metacritic 80, OpenCritic 83% recommend) with 1.0 planned for Sept 2, 2026. Sales data is not officially published; third-party Steam estimates (2-5M owners, ~$1.6M gross Steam-only via SteamSpy and steam-revenue-calculator) are rough and likely undercount cross-platform revenue and overcount free-giveaway copies. #### Best Things

- Pixel art and animation are widely called gorgeous and charming, and the soundtrack is praised as cozy and atmospheric
- The core loop of dungeon looting, selling in your own shop, then upgrading gear and the shop is described as addictive and 'one more run' compelling
- The day/night split gives a satisfying rhythm: shop management is a relaxing break from dungeon crawling
- Smart inventory risk/reward: loot has curses and takes up limited backpack space, forcing choices about what to carry out
- Steady, readable power curve; upgraded armor and weapons make you feel stronger, with five weapon types offering different playstyles
- Dungeon bosses are well designed and memorable; several players say combat reaches a good feel once you have a favorite weapon and armor set
- Good value and accessible: family friendly, runs well on Steam Deck, roughly 15-25 hours to finish and 40+ for completionists, often on deep sale or given away free

#### Worst Things

- Combat is frequently called clunky and stiff: slow weapon animations with animation lock, awkward dodge roll, and unreliable hitboxes
- Shopkeeping is seen as undercooked: pricing boils down to trial-and-error micro-tuning with little strategic depth or consequence
- Dungeon design is shallow: rooms are enclosed arenas where you clear every enemy, and with only a handful of dungeons the layouts and encounters repeat
- PC port problems: built for gamepad, no mouse support even in menus, some keybinds cannot be rebound, and menu keys are not explained in game
- Long-standing bugs reported years after launch, such as the interact button also switching weapons and a sporadic bug where the day cannot be ended after opening the shop
- Late game sputters: the final dungeon and final boss are called anticlimactic by some, or a sudden difficulty spike by others
- Inventory management becomes a chore because loot is so plentiful and bags fill quickly
- No colorblind mode; red-green colorblind players report being unable to see slimes

#### Improvements

- Proper keyboard and mouse support and fully rebindable controls, including menu navigation, plus a colorblind mode
- Deeper shop simulation: more meaningful customer behavior, pricing gambles or risk, and stronger links between shop choices and dungeon play
- More dungeon variety and stronger randomization (more rooms, enemy mixes and mechanics) so runs stop feeling memorized
- More responsive, varied combat: better hitboxes, more fluid dodge and attacks, more weapon depth
- A more satisfying endgame and final dungeon, and meaningful persistent progression (Game Informer felt the persistent elements had too little impact)
- Fix old bugs and skippable cutscenes and quality-of-life items such as better inventory handling

#### Unique Things

- The hybrid of dungeon crawler and shop sim: the loot you risk your life for is literally the stock you price and sell, which also funds town growth and gear
- You price items yourself and watch customer reactions (from overjoyed to overpriced) to learn each item's value
- Retail-style upgrades to the shop (chests, display stands, hired staff like a cashier, and a town of shops to expand) tie into the dungeon-diving economy
- Cursed items and a backpack-as-puzzle inventory where item placement and curses matter during a run
- Fixed hand-built dungeon rooms shuffled per run rather than fully procedural rooms; comparisons often cite Zelda and Binding of Isaac for dungeons and Recettear and Stardew Valley for the town loop
- Gear is crafted and enchanted using dungeon loot, and dungeon gates act as story-driven progression

#### Tedious Or Disliked

- Price-tuning every item in the shop becomes repetitive, and some players call the shop a gimmick that stops being interesting after a couple of hours
- Looting, selling and upgrading turns into a monotonous grind to afford the next gear tier before you can push the next dungeon
- Re-clearing the same rooms and enemies with identical patterns; some players say they saw everything within an hour or two
- Managing a cluttered inventory and dealing with cursed items during runs
- Unskippable, lengthy cutscenes and thin story
- Fighting menus and control schemes on keyboard; some spent around 20 minutes remapping

#### Divisive Points

- Whether the shop is a charming relaxing counterbalance or a shallow gimmick
- Combat feel: praised as fair and satisfying with the right weapon versus clunky and sluggish
- Difficulty: some find dungeons sufficiently challenging and bosses fair, others find the final boss a spike or the early game grindy and punishing
- Replay value: some enjoy 40+ hours and New Game+, others feel the game peaks early and gets worse the longer they play
