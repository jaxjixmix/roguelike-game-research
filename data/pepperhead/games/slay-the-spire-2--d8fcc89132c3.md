#### Entry 1

- **Summary:** Slay the Spire 2 is the sequel to the game that popularized roguelike deckbuilders, reviewed here while still in early access. He has cleared Ascension 10 with every character and sees it as a proper sequel that refines the original rather than reinventing it. He calls the card design a master class and praises the variety, visuals and multiplayer. His complaints are thin content in places, unfinished art and a disliked meta-progression system. He places it in S, slightly above the first game.
- **Quote:** This is how you do a sequel in my eyes.
- **Rerank Note:** Places Slay the Spire 2 above Slay the Spire 1 in S tier, saying both are game of the year worthy.

##### Praise

- Card design is a master class: cards work across many decks and no single card is mandatory
- Huge variety across relics, characters and cards makes every run feel very different
- Kept what players loved from the first game and cut disliked characters and cards (the Watcher)
- Big visual upgrade, with unique animations for many cards and every enemy, making it very satisfying to watch
- Ancients replace boss relics, and the new relics are more niche and polarizing, with less guaranteed energy
- Multiplayer is well integrated and fun, with real-time visibility and map drawing
- Developers balance actively and listen to community feedback, such as cutting early infinites

##### Criticism

- Early access content is a bit light: the final boss lacks animation and much art is unfinished
- Meta-progression (the epoch system) is visually messy, confusing and uninvolving; he wants a full revamp
- Acts 2, 3 and 4 still need work, and he worries about a very long early access
- Multiplayer lacks teammate deck visibility, relic exchange and unique multiplayer events
- Harder and more complex for casual players because energy is scarcer

##### Unique Points

- Deliberately a straightforward sequel with no new gimmick mechanic, which he sees as the right call for the genre's originator
- Two complete biomes on the first act, each with distinct events, enemies and bosses
- Multiplayer chest-picking mechanic and drawing on the map
- Builds are not locked to one archetype, unlike the poison-focused approach in the first game

##### Design Criteria

- Card design and balance: cards viable in many decks, no mandatory cards
- Variety and replayability: characters, relics and cards producing different runs
- Synergies: multiple build paths per character rather than one archetype
- Visuals and animation: unique enemy and card animations matter in a low-motion genre
- Meta-progression: epoch system judged confusing and unfinished
- Difficulty and complexity: scarcer energy and polarizing boss relics add depth
- Content completeness: early access gaps in acts, art and animation
- Multiplayer design: cooperative features and missing unique interactions
- Developer responsiveness: balance patches and community feedback

Since then Steam sentiment has slid to Mixed (about 61% positive across roughly 203k reviews), driven by a long run of balance patches that many players feel made enemies, elites and the Act 3 bosses more punishing and less varied, plus review-bombing waves (including heavily negative Simplified Chinese reviews). #### Best Things

- The core deckbuilding loop stays as compelling as the original, with many reviewers saying it feels even deeper and more polished
- New art style, animations and game feel are widely called a clear upgrade over Slay the Spire 1
- Up to 4-player co-op that translates the single-player experience well, including multiplayer-specific cards; players use it to play with friends and family
- Large amount of content already in Early Access: two new characters (Regent, Necrobinder), new enemies, bosses, events, lore and music
- Ancients replacing boss relics give three-way choices each act and add build variety
- Epochs/unlock progression and richer events with real risk make runs feel more rewarding and story-connected
- Fair pricing ($24.99, same as the first game) with content updates promised as free rather than DLC
- Steam Workshop mod support, Bestiary and ongoing developer communication

#### Worst Things

- Repeated patches that nerf strong cards and buff enemies; many long-time fans say each update made runs harder and less fun
- Act 3 third-boss slot has been a flashpoint: the boss was redesigned several times then replaced (Doormaker removed, Aeonglass added), with reviewers saying the fights counter whole archetypes or feel unfinished
- Elites and bosses often feel like raw number checks (block X now, huge HP pools, regen, strength scaling) with less identity than in the first game
- Difficulty is front-loaded and punishing at high Ascension, with little margin for error so runs can end without an obvious lesson learned
- Balance is uneven across characters; some builds feel almost irrelevant against the third boss
- Accessibility gaps: incomplete keyboard navigation, which a disabled player cited as a reason for a negative review
- Some negative reviewers question Mega Crit's Early Access management and direction after rapid boss changes

#### Improvements

- Finish Act 4 and the remaining act variants (only Act 1 had multiple variants early on)
- Make elite and boss fights more varied and puzzle-like instead of damage races, and stabilise the Act 3 boss pool
- Rebalance difficulty curve and Ascension scaling so it does not hinge on a strong Act 1 or a 'god run'
- Full keyboard and controller-style navigation/accessibility support

#### Unique Things

- Co-op deckbuilding for up to 4 players inside a Slay the Spire framework
- Ancients: a choice of three rewards at each act's end instead of a single boss relic
- Epochs meta-progression and a stronger lore/story layer than the original
- Two new characters, The Regent and The Necrobinder, alongside returning Ironclad, Silent and Defect
- Rebuilt RNG system meant to decorrelate random elements, and a Bestiary showing enemy moves and animations
- Shift from Unity to Godot after Unity's fee controversy, costing the team around two years of rework

#### Tedious Or Disliked

- Long, high-hour grinds through Ascension where build options get narrowed by enemy checks and infinite/engine decks feel forced
- Enemies that chip through block or slam 30+ damage, making many fights feel like the same damage race
- Frequent balance churn means good strategies can be invalidated between patches
- Solo play feeling less fun to some players than co-op after recent changes
- Perceived developer responsiveness to a few high-level streamers rather than casual players

#### Divisive Points

- Difficulty: some critics and players find it easier than the original (PCGamesN noted easier infinites and dangers), while a large share of patched-era Steam reviewers find it harshly difficult
- Whether the balance patches are necessary corrections to infinites and homogenised play or an over-correction that removed fun builds
- Geographic split: English Reddit and reviews skew positive while Simplified Chinese Steam reviews skew much more negative
- Whether the sequel is a big leap or feels like an expansion of the original (noted by some critic reviews)
