### Community

- **One Liner:** A sci-fi roguelike deckbuilder by Rocket Rat Games in which you pilot a spaceship with a three-character crew through a time loop, dodging enemy fire by moving left and right to line up cannons.
- **Consensus Summary:** Cobalt Core is very well received by both critics and players: 96% positive on Steam (Overwhelmingly Positive, about 4,400 reviews), Metacritic 94 and an OpenCritic top critic average of 89 with 100% of critics recommending. Reviewers present it as a compact, tactical take on the Slay the Spire formula, with the lateral-movement mechanic, crew-mixing and charming writing as the standouts. The recurring caveats are content volume and difficulty: several players and critics say it is easy even on top difficulty, runs repeat the same events and bosses, and the unlock arc is short. #### Best Things

- Movement is the core hook: dodging incoming missiles while lining up your own cannons adds a tactical puzzle layer absent from other deckbuilders (FTL / Into the Breach flavour).
- Crew mixing: picking three of the characters to form your starting card pool gives a lot of combinations and distinct archetypes (drones, heat, corrosion, shields, infinites).
- Characters and writing: crew members banter reactively based on cards played, damage taken and who is paired together; widely called charming, funny and endearing.
- Enemies telegraph their next shots, so outcomes feel driven by player choices and planning rather than pure luck.
- Snappy, polished feel: UI, sound design and animation make runs fast and satisfying.
- Time-loop story told through unlockable memories ties neatly into the roguelike structure and pays off; some reviewers say the story is better than typical for the genre.
- Multiple ships with their own rules and playstyles, none strictly better than the starter, plus daily challenges and a codex that encourages completionism.
- Minimal meta-progression: no grinding for power, you win by getting better; a small team (three people) delivered an unusually polished game.

#### Worst Things

- Limited content: the same events, enemies and bosses recur every run, card and artifact pools are smaller than Slay the Spire's, and players report seeing everything in roughly 10-20 hours.
- Too easy: multiple reviewers beat hard and harder difficulties within a few runs and say they must self-handicap with weaker crews or ships to be challenged.
- Same-y builds: all of a character's cards unlock immediately, so the best cards become obvious and runs converge.
- Map traversal and pathing are considered uninteresting compared with other genre entries.
- Shield feels underpowered relative to movement/dodging, narrowing viable defensive strategies.
- Critics noted boss fights lack randomness and the total unlock arc is short compared with peers.
- Some players find the core loop of shifting left or right with hit-or-miss draws unengaging and feel cards/effects are underwhelming.

#### Improvements

- Add much more content: players ask for roughly double or triple the cards, enemies, events, bosses and gimmicks (events and ship count were singled out in reviews).
- Offer harder difficulty tiers or modifiers so experienced players are challenged without self-handicapping.
- Add randomness or variation to boss encounters and more alternate bosses per zone.
- Make map traversal more meaningful (more interesting route decisions).
- Add an undo option and show card upgrade outcomes without having to click into every card.
- Rebalance shield versus movement and further distinguish the crew so fewer cards are obvious picks.

#### Unique Things

- Single-axis tactical combat: you slide left or right to dodge and line up shots, rather than just playing cards against a health bar.
- Your deck is built from the three crew members you choose, making crew selection a pre-run strategic layer.
- Dynamic crew dialogue that reacts to specific game state, inspired by Star Trek and Reigns.
- Time-loop framing with story memories unlocked through runs, inspired in part by Inscryption's meta-awareness.
- Ships change the ruleset of the run (different meta rules), and the game is compact rather than sprawling.
- Distinct cobalt-blue pixel-art look with a light-hearted tone and serious undercurrents.

#### Tedious Or Disliked

- Repetition: seeing the same events and enemies every run becomes tedious after the first dozen runs.
- Feeling underpowered in fights and chipping away at enemies, with no Balatro/Monster Train-style moment of unlocking an overpowered build (one 16-hour reviewer).
- No undo for misclicks and having to inspect each card to see upgrade results.
- Some find the unlocks spaced both too far apart and too thin, and the story not compelling enough to motivate unlocking.
- RNG-driven draws can leave you without the movement or range card you need, which some reviewers find frustrating.
- Short playtime-to-content ratio: several negative reviewers (8-10 hours) beat hard or harder difficulty within a few runs and call it thin for the price, wanting a steep sale.

#### Divisive Points

- Difficulty: some celebrate that skill reliably wins, others find even the hardest mode too easy and then feel there is little left to do.
- Movement mechanic: loved as fresh and tactical by most, but a minority finds moving left/right and hoping for the right card dull or RNG-heavy.
- Value: fans put hundreds of hours in (one reviewer 520 hours, another calls it their favorite roguelike), while others call it thin for about $20 and recommend waiting for a sale.
- Versus Slay the Spire: some see it as a mini StS clone, others rank it above StS.
