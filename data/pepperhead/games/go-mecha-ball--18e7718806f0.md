##### Praise

- Core gameplay of dashing and rolling into enemies feels good and enjoyable once comfortable
- Visuals and VFX are really solid
- Enemy and boss designs are great
- Launch pads and boosts hint at cool movement options
- Some players will connect with the crazy gameplay much more than he did

##### Criticism

- Little roguelike depth: guns have no upgrades or variance, character and grenades stay the same every run
- Upgrade choices after levels are generic (more damage, more health) and don't change play; new abilities can be useless with a two-ability cap
- Bosses give no reward beyond a bit of health; not enough rewards throughout runs
- Very hard and punishing with no healing outside the shop; some enemies, like a homing dashing ball, are hard to evade
- Movement focus is underused since fights happen in single rooms with little exploration
- Severe frame drops by the third world, down to 4-5 fps
- Flying up feels awkward since you can't descend quickly

##### Unique Points

- Transforming into a ball to dash and roll between areas is the core gimmick
- The slam-down area attack ability should have been a core mechanic to make vertical movement feel complete
- Design lesson: a strong movement hook needs level design and roguelike variety that make use of it

##### Design Criteria

- Core movement/feel: dashing and rolling is fun but underused
- Visuals/VFX: strong and chaotic
- Roguelike mechanics: too little variety in guns, upgrades, character
- Upgrade quality: generic stat bumps that don't change play
- Rewards: bosses and runs give too little
- Difficulty: punishing, no healing, unkind to new players
- Enemy/boss design: great but some hard to evade
- Level design: rooms too confined to exploit movement
- Performance: frame drops hurt the experience
- Meta-progression: only permanent additions, nothing interesting

### Community

- **One Liner:** A neon arcade twin-stick shooter roguelite by Whale Peak Games (published by Super Rare Originals) where you switch between a shooting mech and a fast rolling pinball-style ball to smash, bounce and blast enemy bots.
- **Consensus Summary:** Players like it: Steam sits at Very Positive (about 86% of roughly 460 reviews) and critics landed at a Metacritic 70 from 10 reviews (range 60-85), so the split is 'superb feel, thin roguelike'. Nearly everyone praises the movement, physics-driven collision combat, bosses and presentation, while the recurring complaint is that levels and enemy spawns are fixed, so runs feel samey and content runs out fast (4 zones, 4 bosses, 4 mechs). Metacritic users are noticeably cooler (6.6 from 25 ratings). #### Best Things

- Movement and game feel: rolling, bouncing, boosting and transforming mid-air feels weighty, snappy and 'juicy' rather than floaty, and is the most-praised element across Steam and critics
- Collision combat: building speed to deal contact damage (which also drops ammo) and launching enemies into each other or off the map is widely called satisfying
- Fusion of genres: pinball physics plus twin-stick shooting plus roguelike progression is described by critics as a combination nothing else quite matches
- Bosses: multiple reviewers (Game Rant, Inverse, Steam players) single out the boss fights as exhilarating pattern-learning set pieces
- Presentation: bright neon art style, fluid animations and a dynamic, interactive soundtrack draw frequent compliments
- Level furniture (bounce pads, boost pads, fans, tubes) feeds into mobility and gives arenas pinball-table character
- Easy to pick up and play: Steam reviewers note the simple core loop and good controller and handheld PC play; runs are short arcade-style sessions
- Solid enemy variety in the moment-to-moment sense that forces use of ball dodging, abilities and weapons together

#### Worst Things

- Levels, enemy spawns and boss order are the same every run, so as a 'roguelike' it lacks the randomness the genre is known for (the most repeated Steam negative and echoed by Inverse, GameCritics and Game Rant)
- Thin content: only 4 zones, 4 bosses and 4 mechs; unlock lists are long on paper (25+ weapons, 20+ abilities, 50+ upgrades) but many weapons and upgrades feel incremental or same-y
- Higher difficulties mostly add enemy health/armor rather than new enemy types or layout changes, so progression feels static
- Stability and bugs reported on Steam: pause menu that cannot be closed mid-run, key rebinding not working, and lag attributed to a memory leak after 30-60 minutes
- Seemingly abandoned: players report the developers said no further work would be done, so known bugs persist
- Some players find the visuals and audio fatiguing, with reports of eye strain and watering eyes and an 'overstimulating' soundtrack over long sessions
- Steam reviewers also cite little meta-progression beyond unlocks, limited power-up choice (perfect builds feel rare) and burnout after a few hours

#### Improvements

- Add procedural or shuffled level layouts and enemy spawns (a 'true randomizer') so runs differ
- Clearer upgrade descriptions showing actual stat changes, since players often cannot tell how an option will improve a build
- Higher difficulty tiers that change enemies, bosses and layouts, not just health and armor
- More content: additional zones, bosses, characters and more build-altering weapons and upgrades rather than small stat tweaks
- Deeper meta-progression than the gacha-style unlock system that critics say feels disconnected from progress
- Fix pause menu, rebinding and memory-leak issues, and add accessibility options (GameCritics noted no colorblind mode; some players want a clearer crosshair and less screen strain)

#### Unique Things

- Instant transformation between an on-foot twin-stick mech and a rolling ball, where ball speed is itself an offensive weapon and ammo source
- Pinball-style arenas with ramps, bumpers, fans and boost pads that turn level geometry into a movement and damage tool
- Arcade structure of three themed levels then a boss, repeated across four worlds, with short runs
- Dynamic, interactive soundtrack and a saturated neon aesthetic; one Steam reviewer likens the hectic pace to an easier ULTRAKILL
- Hub that functions as a small playground with permanent upgrades and unlocks in a gacha-like system

#### Tedious Or Disliked

- Repeating identical level layouts and spawn patterns every run, which some call a slog after 2-3 hours
- Unlock-only meta progression: once you finish unlocking everything there is little to do
- Repetitive tutorial prompts (noted by Checkpoint Gaming per Metacritic's summary)
- Chaotic, visually busy screens where it can be hard to read what is happening or track the crosshair, which put some players off entirely
- Difficulty jumps between normal and harder tiers that mainly inflate enemy health
- Slog after the first 2-3 hours once the main activity is just unlocking everything (Steam reviewer, with complaints the base price, about 19.49 EUR, is too high for the content)

#### Divisive Points

- Whether it counts as a true roguelike: fans accept it as an arcade run-based game, others feel misled by fixed levels
- Price vs content: Steam lists $7.99 (may be a sale price; one reviewer cites a 19.49 EUR base price), and some call that overpriced for the content while others call the core loop worth it
- Sensory intensity: praised as thrilling by some and exhausting or eye-straining by others
- Metacritic users (6.6) vs Steam users (86% positive) disagree noticeably on overall quality
