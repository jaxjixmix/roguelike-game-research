#### Entry 1

- **Part:** 5
- **Label:** Tier list Part 5 (2026)
- **Summary:** Breachway is a deck-building roguelike that mixes FTL-style ship management and galaxy travel with Slay the Spire-style card combat. Cards are split across ship slots, you can target parts of enemy ships, and you manage fuel and energy. He enjoys ship building, the map and the visuals, but finds the combat loops repetitive, the card design uninspiring and synergies limited. Bugs, frame drops, long loads and crashes after about two years in early access hurt it further. He places it in low C, with potential to rise to high B or low A after polish and a full release.
- **Quote:** I'm going to put Bridgeway as a low C tier.
- **Rerank Note:** Not a rerank, but he says it could rise to high B or low A after full release and fixes; it has been in early access about two years.

##### Praise

- Ship building and customization is enjoyable
- Travelling the galaxy map with events and fuel management is cool
- Visuals and art style look great, with a 3D space environment and changing background
- Mixing FTL and Slay the Spire is a concept he likes
- Targeting different parts of the enemy ship adds interest
- Lots to love, and he may return after polish

##### Criticism

- Core combat is repetitive: enemies run short fixed loops (missile, block, recharge; or burn, block, burn)
- Once the build is set, battle outcomes depend on the build rather than on play
- Used cards go on cooldown and back to the draw pile, so key passive or power cards can get stuck undrawn
- Card design is basic and uninspiring (lasers that deal a bit more damage), with no crazy scaling
- Synergies are limited because energy types split play across three or four decks
- Early access bugs, frame drops, long loading and crashes
- Small, boring font and wordy dialogue in a reading-heavy game
- Little meta progression; unlocks mostly add new things to play with

##### Unique Points

- Cards are attached to different ship slots, each with its own energy type, which adds complexity but fragments deck building
- Cooldown cards return to the draw pile instead of discard, creating an unusual draw dynamic
- A 3D animated space backdrop makes a deck-builder feel more alive than static screens

##### Comparisons

- FTL: Faster Than Light
- Slay the Spire

##### Design Criteria

- Combat variety: enemy loops are too repetitive
- Card design and synergies: basic cards, limited synergy across split decks
- Build customization: ship building is fun but outcomes depend mainly on the build
- Complexity vs depth: complex energy and slot systems yet simple card effects
- Visuals and art style: 3D space setting is a strong point
- UI and readability: small, boring font in a text-heavy game
- Meta progression: minimal, only unlocks of new options
- Polish and stability: bugs, frame drops and crashes in early access
- Exploration and map: galaxy travel, fuel and events are appealing

#### Best Things

- Distinctive take on deckbuilding: each weapon and module acts as its own mini-deck, and played cards must cool down in the discard pile before they can be drawn again, so burning through your deck is a real risk
- Strong FTL plus Slay the Spire feel; several reviewers call it a better spiritual successor to FTL than Into the Breach
- Power allocation, heat management and crew abilities add a layer of decision-making beyond plain card play
- Weapon types feel different from one another (for example pulse lasers and railguns that snipe components, flak against missiles or drones), and loadout variety supports different builds
- Sci-fi presentation, Expanse-like tone, ship design and music get frequent praise from positive reviewers (though some negative reviews call the ship designs generic)
- Developer has been responsive and patched frequently during Early Access, and many reviewers say the game improved a lot since launch
- Good value and replayability for $19.99 according to positive reviewers, with ascension-style difficulty levels unlocked by winning

#### Worst Things

- Progress has stalled: the run still ends after the second sector with no resolution of the story, and the long-promised third map has not shipped, so some players think the game is abandoned
- Stability and performance: crashes after combat or on load, lag spikes and input lag even on high-end hardware (RTX 5070 Ti/5080 and similar), with reports that save progress is lost after a crash
- Many bugs around card interactions, tooltips and heat, and some players cannot tell what is a bug and what is a badly explained mechanic
- Combat becomes repetitive: shoot, shield, shoot, repeat, with long fights against high-health, high-shield enemies
- Shallow content: little enemy variety, repetitive bosses, few ships, four factions that barely differ, and very thin story or motivation
- Balance problems that cut both ways: easy for most of a run, then spikes from the final boss and the second boss (overheat, heavy shielding, hacks); raw damage often beats the hacking, brittle and overheat systems
- UI and clarity problems: tooltips that overlap and block equipping items, hard-to-read health and shields, and a tutorial that does not explain the fuel and yellow-path movement costs
- Weak metaprogression: reviewers say you carry little between runs, and most runs are a slight variation of the starting gear

#### Improvements

- Ship the promised additional sectors and an ending; one reviewer also suggests an endless or procedural mode
- Fix the crashes, save issues and performance stutter before adding content
- Add more enemy, event, quest and boss variety so the map stops feeling sparse
- Improve tooltips, card descriptions and the tutorial so mechanics such as heat, fuel and movement costs are clear
- Rebalance difficulty and resource scarcity (starting fuel, deck-thinning costs, heat builds, the second and final bosses) and make the hacking, brittle and overheat options competitive with raw damage
- Strengthen metaprogression and add more ships and weapon variety; some players also ask for ship visual customization

#### Unique Things

- Per-module mini-decks that combine into your hand, with a cooldown in the discard pile, unlike Slay the Spire's shuffle-the-discard loop
- Three resource colors (power types) plus heat and overheating as a core tradeoff
- Node-map navigation where path colors spend different resources, and fuel that is scarce and only found at stations
- Crew and subsystem management with enemy component targeting and hacking, plus faction relations and 4X-flavoured space trade
- Hard sci-fi tone in the style of The Expanse rather than space-fantasy

#### Tedious Or Disliked

- Long, drawn-out early fights where damage per turn is low (roughly 4-8) and enemies carry about 50 HP plus shields
- Fuel scarcity (only 2 starting fuel, refuel only at stations) that limits exploration and pushes grinding sectors
- Hard-to-predict negative node debuffs and enemy hacks with no counter unless a station offers security systems
- Calculating multi-card damage combos with shred, brittle and other modifiers is cumbersome
- Deleting or upgrading cards is expensive, and weapon decks can be full before you get a chance to upgrade
- Reinstalling or restarting after crashes and the general sense of being railroaded toward a loss

#### Divisive Points

- Difficulty: some players find runs and bosses too easy, others find the later bosses and scarce resources punishing
- Whether the roguelite loop is engaging or a slog: a minority call the combat great, while highly upvoted negatives (150 hours, 6 hours, 30 hours) say decisions feel low-impact
- Whether the dev is still committed: earlier reviews praise frequent updates, while 2026 reviews cite two years with no new map
- Deck size strategy: Breachway rewards bigger decks than most deckbuilders, which some see as innovative and others as unintuitive
- Visuals and ship design: positive reviewers praise ship design and the sci-fi look, while a 14-upvote negative review calls the ships generic and the battle visuals pointless
