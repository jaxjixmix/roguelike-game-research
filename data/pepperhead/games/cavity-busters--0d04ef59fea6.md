##### Praise

- Boss fights are really cool and the boss designs are interesting
- Bullet hell gameplay feels great
- Many viable ways to approach any fight
- Huge amount of content and plenty of secrets
- Funny, fun story with lots of humor
- Passives that radically change how the character plays, with five to six found per floor

##### Criticism

- Too many mechanics; takes forever to get comfortable and is overwhelming
- Because all options are viable and usable constantly, it is unclear what to do
- Overall chaos across gameplay, mechanics and run structure is too much
- Health-based digging can make a single floor last around 40 minutes
- Passives with massive downsides that you are pressured to take, and removing them is slow and not guaranteed

##### Unique Points

- Tooth throw, recall, and mid-air recall with special effect, alongside jump, wall-dash and slow-mo
- Spending health to dig new rooms lets you expand floors nearly infinitely
- Items have upsides and heavy downsides, and skipping them triggers other negatives, echoing Blazing Beaks

#### Best Things

- Movement and skill expression: dash with i-frames, bullet parry, wall-running and sliding, stomping enemies from above, all chainable
- Tooth-as-weapon boomerang: shooting the tooth and recalling it deals damage both ways, making gunplay feel inventive and satisfying
- Large item pool (Steam lists 317 upgrades; console listings say 280) that can make a run wildly strong, weak or both, with diseased upgrades as risk/reward
- Playable characters feel mechanically different rather than like stat-modifier reskins
- Wall-digging mechanic adds risk/reward and is seen as well balanced: helpful but not a cheat button
- Reviewers praise boss and enemy design and the amount of content for the price ($14.99)
- Music and gross-but-funny audio/art style praised, including many tooth puns
- Steady post-launch content updates; reviewers say each update brings new content; runs well on Steam Deck

#### Worst Things

- Overwhelming onboarding: many mechanics are thrown at the player at once, and key information gets lost in rapid-fire tutorials
- Item descriptions are hidden or vague; some items are unidentified and some set HP to 1, which can unknowingly end a run
- Stability complaints: crashes, freezes and audio bugs reported on Windows and under Proton/Linux (Windows-only on Steam)
- Clunky feel to some early weapons: one requires picking up the ammo you fire, the other auto-aims
- Samey colour palette makes enemies, items and props hard to tell apart (IndieGameFans review)
- No autosave mid-run reported by a player, so a crash costs the whole run
- Critics and some players see it as a close Isaac/Gungeon derivative that does not surpass them

#### Improvements

- Introduce mechanics gradually with a better tutorial or pacing, so runs start simple like Isaac and complexity builds through items
- Make item descriptions clear and show what items do, especially HP-altering ones, instead of hiding them
- Improve visual clarity and colour differentiation between enemies, pickups and environment
- Fix crashes and freezes and improve Proton/Linux compatibility (no native Mac or Linux build)
- Add a mid-run save or resume safety for crash cases

#### Unique Things

- You are a tooth: a boomerang-style shoot-and-recall attack that damages enemies on the way out and back
- Wall-run and wall-dig mechanic adds a second traversal layer unusual for top-down shooters
- Disease upgrades you can contract and cure to keep the good effects, as a risk/reward item twist
- Mouse-and-keyboard-first design with bullet parry and i-frame dash, more fast-paced than tank or twin-stick Isaac clones
- Dental/body-horror toothpunk theme with pun-heavy humour and a 'bloated body kingdom' that changes each run

#### Tedious Or Disliked

- Steep learning curve and a punishing early game; one reviewer took several attempts to beat the first boss
- Too many systems to juggle at once (parry, roll, wall-run, ammo pickup, stomp) feels cumbersome to some players
- Constant dodging, rolling and wall-running reads as more bullet hell than roguelike to players who expected an item-driven game
- Decoding unidentified items by trial and error is frustrating
- Technical crashes interrupt long runs

#### Divisive Points

- Mechanical density: some see layered systems as deep skill expression, others say less would have been more
- Bullet-hell vs roguelike identity: praised as fast and skill-based by some, disliked as too dodge-focused by others
- Isaac/Gungeon comparison: several players say it beats Isaac, while one critic says it fails to meet the legend that inspired it
- Difficulty: some find it hard to master but rewarding, others find it tough and cruel, though accessibility options soften it
