##### Praise

- Core tactical combat is satisfying, enjoyable and fairly strategic
- Timeline/time-bar mechanic, where you manipulate enemy turn order and crits, is unique and strategic
- Art style and animations are excellent, with unique animations per enemy and attack
- Characters play drastically differently and there are many party combinations
- Solid story with attention to characters and camp relationship systems
- Items add roguelike variety so runs feel different

##### Criticism

- UI is a mess: no quick inventory, information is unclear, and enemy intentions are hard to see
- Basic quality-of-life gaps, e.g. needing to press a button then click to inspect an enemy instead of hovering
- Overwhelming and confusing to start; unclear why some attacks are automatic and turn order can trigger accidentally
- Runs can be very long, with fights taking many turns of repeating the same few abilities
- Starting a new run feels like a drag; characters have no roguelike progression and level identically

##### Unique Points

- The timeline bar, where positioning before enemies and pushing them back enables crits, is its most unique part
- Turn-based RPG roguelikes are rare despite the genre's roots, making this a notable entry
- Gameplay is more complicated than Darkest Dungeon, which he is unsure is good or bad

- UI/information clarity: his biggest issue, poor presentation of enemy intent and menus
- Strategic depth: timeline mechanic and turn-based tactics are the highlight
- Visuals and animation: strong art and per-attack animations

- Roguelike variation: items provide variety but characters lack roguelike progression

#### Best Things

- Timeline-based turn combat: chain attacks, delay enemies, and 'break' them so they forfeit their turn and take crits; reviewers call it innovative and puzzle-like, often comparing the feel to Into the Breach
- Very strong pixel art with modern lighting and effects, plus an energetic synth soundtrack, highlighted by critics (Digital Chumps, GamingTrend) and players alike
- Deep build variety via classes, weapons, perks and skills, with lots of synergies to discover
- Nemesis-style system where escaped or victorious mini-bosses return stronger with new modifiers, giving runs memorable loops
- Camp and affinity system between party members produces cutscenes and unlocks alternate appearances and movesets; progeny system unlocks new characters
- Escalating difficulty ladder (Harsh up to Entropy levels) and new-game-plus style restarts where unlocks carry over give strong replay depth for dedicated players
- Great value on sale; several players report 200-700+ hours played
- Runs on Steam Deck and is praised as a handheld game despite minor lag when fast-forwarding

#### Worst Things

- Runs are very long (commonly 2-4 hours, some report 6+), so a death late in a run feels punishing
- Heavy RNG layering (rewards, enemy backline placement, nemesis modifiers) can invalidate a build through no fault of the player
- Timeline predictions are sometimes inaccurate or ignore certain moves, which can cause run-ending surprises; mechanics are poorly explained in tooltips
- Writing and tone are a frequent complaint: irreverent, cliched, fourth-wall-breaking dialogue that clashes with the world; thin story with no Hades-style narrative progression across runs
- Bugs: soft locks when returning from the map view, chests or looting ceasing to work, stuck end-of-run states, and autosaves capturing broken states; heavy CPU use reported
- Weak tutorial and steep learning curve; players often need outside guides to understand time manipulation and statuses
- Meta-progression is slow or uninspiring and many players unlock the important items within a few runs, while higher difficulties effectively force a narrow set of builds
- Too few regions, so repeated runs feel samey; map navigation is point-and-click and slow, especially during night backtracking
- Full price ($24.99) is widely called hard to justify: several top reviews say it feels unfinished, like early access, and recommend buying only on sale

#### Improvements

- Shorter or more modular runs, or mid-run save points and healing options, to reduce the cost of dying
- Reroll or refresh systems for rewards to mitigate bad RNG
- More accurate and complete timeline predictions and better in-game explanation of mechanics and statuses
- More regions, bosses and content variety so later runs differ more, plus richer meta-progression
- Rebalance difficulty tiers and weak synergies or combo skills so more than a handful of builds remain viable at high difficulty
- Bug fixes and stability work (soft locks, looting, performance); players also ask for a sequel that addresses the feedback

#### Unique Things

- Timeline combat where pushing enemies off the timeline breaks them and forces crits, mixing tactical-RPG puzzle logic with roguelite runs
- Nemesis-system-inspired boss and mini-boss memory across attempts, with special dialogue on revisits
- Dimension-hopping sci-fi JRPG setting (Star Wars meets cyberpunk flavour) with mech and pixel-art aesthetics
- Progeny and relationship system that generates variant heroes and unlocks characters through affinity rather than simple currency
- Alternating male or female sibling protagonist between campaigns, and a true-ending chain that restarts you at higher difficulty with all systems scaling

#### Tedious Or Disliked

- Multi-hour runs that must be restarted from scratch after failure; several reviewers say it would be better as a linear JRPG with saves
- Night-time backtracking and clicking through empty map segments
- Grindy, repetitive structure: similar fights and few regions across runs, with small incremental upgrades between runs
- Learning phase of reading about elements and statuses, and dying to unexplained mechanics
- Cringe character banter, and a perceived janky UX and controls that several reviewers say bury a good core game

#### Divisive Points

- Roguelite structure: some love the long, high-stakes runs and depth while others say the game would be better as a standard JRPG
- Story and humour: seen as charming and funny by some, unbearably cliched or off-tone by others
- Difficulty and RNG: 'perfect information' and cerebral puzzle-solving to fans vs unfair and luck-driven to detractors
- Value: a steal at under $5-10 on sale, but many feel it is unfinished at the $25 full price
