### Community

- **One Liner:** A 2D pixel-art action roguelite dungeon crawler by Lamina Studios (Dec 2016) that mixes Nuclear Throne-style shooting/melee, Risk of Rain-style item stacking and snowballing power, with 10 classes and local co-op.
- **Consensus Summary:** Dungeon Souls is received as a decent but derivative budget roguelite. Steam players rate it Mostly Positive (about 74% of ~2,200 reviews by the local data; the store page shows 77% of 1,310), and Metacritic sits at a Mixed 67 from 8 critic reviews with a 6.0 user score. Critics called it a fun, casual tribute to older dungeon crawlers that borrows heavily from its inspirations and is quickly forgotten. Long-time players say it gets addictive once the between-run upgrades start compounding. It is a finished 2016 release (not early access); the Steam forum has threads asking whether the game is dead, and recent negative reviews cite unresolved controller and crash problems on modern systems. #### Best Things

- Ten distinct hero classes with different abilities (e.g. Barbarian with health and area attacks, Cleric with bouncing projectiles and a shield) that change how a run plays
- Items and passives (50+) provide a snowballing, overpowered-build feeling in the Risk of Rain mould; one 40-hour reviewer says runs start short but become an avalanche once meta upgrades kick in
- Addictive, replayable loop with lots of RNG and unlocks; one reviewer calls it among the most addictive dungeon crawlers they have played in a while
- Bite-sized, turn-your-brain-off action that suits short sessions; Hardcore Gamer said practice and learning the characters eventually pays off
- Pleasant pixel-art presentation and simple fitting audio, plus local split-screen co-op
- Nostalgic old-school dungeon crawler feel; one reviewer feels it set a standard later games improved on

#### Worst Things

- Heavily derivative of Nuclear Throne, Risk of Rain and Spelunky without its own identity (Destructoid, LEVEL, High-Def Digest: forgettable)
- Bland, repetitive procedurally generated environments with poor visibility; traps blend into the dark
- Melee characters are poorly balanced in a bullet-heavy game with no dodge mechanic; enemies feel like bullet sponges
- Unfair hazards: floor traps in narrow corridors, ritual-marker rooms, and enemies that spawn directly on the player
- Confusing interface and item placement (chests look like shop items, accidental warps to secret levels)
- Technical issues: stated Xbox controller support reportedly does not register input (negative reviewers say unresolved since at least 2018), mouse is not captured on multi-monitor setups, black screen and crash reports on newer Windows
- Low-sophistication story and visuals; the plot is described as merely present

#### Improvements

- Fix controller detection and make the advertised Xbox controller support actually work
- Capture the mouse cursor and fix window/taskbar handling for multi-monitor play
- Add a dodge or mobility option and rebalance melee classes against projectile-heavy enemies
- Make traps, chests and shop items readable against dark backgrounds; clarify interface and warp triggers
- Stabilise the game on modern Windows 11 to stop crashes and black screens
- Clearer explanation of how to unlock characters (Brawler, Redeemer) and find crafting recipes such as Nimby's Grace

#### Unique Things

- Cross-breed of Nuclear Throne action, Risk of Rain item scaling and Overture-style dungeon crawling, according to its own store description
- Activating marks/altars on a level to open the escape portal rather than simply reaching an exit
- Crafting system with recipes that players must discover, plus permanent meta upgrades between runs
- Ten hero classes with unlockable characters such as the Brawler and Redeemer
- Shared/split-screen local co-op and Remote Play Together support in a pixel-art roguelite at a low price

#### Tedious Or Disliked

- Early runs feel short with little progress until meta upgrades accumulate; one reviewer calls the meta progression 'dark' and monotonous
- Slow walking speed makes fleeing traps and enemies hard
- Random monster spawns punish stopping to level up, so the pace forces constant movement
- Crafting reportedly disables gameplay while it is active (one negative review)
- Losing a run's progress by exiting to the main menu (Steam forum PSA) and unclear save behaviour
- Limited variety between characters according to a negative reviewer, with monotonous gameplay over time

#### Divisive Points

- Simplicity: some enjoy mindless, easy-to-pick-up action; others find it monotonous and shallow
- Difficulty and balance: Hardcore Gamer says mastery pays off, while Destructoid found melee classes badly outmatched by bullets
- Derivativeness: some see a loving tribute to the genre's greats, others an unoriginal copy
- Class variety: praised by critics and many players, but one negative reviewer says characters offer little gameplay variety
