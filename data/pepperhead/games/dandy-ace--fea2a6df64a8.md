### Commentary


#### Entry 1

- **Part:** 5
- **Label:** Tier list Part 5 (2026)
- **Summary:** Dandy Ace is a top-down action roguelike that is essentially Dead Cells with a magician theme, where you escape imprisonment by a jealous rival magician. Its one distinctive hook is card fusion, letting you slot a spare ability as a passive on another piece of equipment. He feels it copied the surface elements of Dead Cells without the depth that makes that game work. Combat is okay but clunky and repetitive, biomes look alike, and there is little left to do after the first win. He still had a good time, beat it on his third attempt, and recommends it to people wanting a different Dead Cells-style experience, placing it in B tier.
- **Quote:** they're just trying to copy something and I think they copied kind of all the wrong things
- **Rerank Note:**

##### Praise

- Card fusion mechanic (using an ability as a passive on another) allows customization and is the game's unique twist
- Enjoyable and satisfying once you get comfortable with dashing and learning enemy movesets
- Funny story and opening cinematic with a magician antagonist narrating
- Strong meta-progression with gems given to NPCs for permanent unlocks and a sense of growing stronger
- Distinct biomes with different bosses and a fitting magician theme with magic references

##### Criticism

- Copied most of Dead Cells' elements without doing much new with them
- Fusion passives are too simple, static, and do not improve during the run; abilities lack randomized passives, only level differences
- Gameplay is clunky, slower than Dead Cells, and stiff animation; basic attack gets spammed with no combos
- Biomes look too similar and rooms are all just enemy-filled rooms; little sense of adventure
- Metroidvania gating is shallow (coloured keys and gates opening similar-looking areas)
- Narrator voice lines in every room get to be too much; little content or secrets after first win; off-screen projectiles in late game feel unfair

##### Unique Points

- Card fusion: slot an equipped ability as a passive onto a new item instead of discarding it
- Magician theme could have been pushed further into distinct environments like a circus or Las Vegas world
- Design lesson: cloning a successful game's parts fails without the blend that makes the original work

##### Comparisons

- Dead Cells
- Hades

##### Design Criteria

- Build customization and synergies: fusion system is interesting but too shallow
- Item/ability variety: abilities lack randomized passives, reducing loot excitement
- Combat feel: clunky, slower, repetitive basic attack
- Level and biome design: biomes and rooms too samey, lacking adventure
- Visual theme and charm: magic theme underused, biomes not differentiated
- Meta-progression: gem-for-unlocks system is solid
- Post-game content and secrets: little to do after first win
- Difficulty: challenging, with unseen off-screen projectiles late
- Narrative/tone: funny narrator but overused

#### Best Things

- Card-slot combat: cards fill dash, main attack and special slots in any combination, and each card also grants a secondary modifier effect, which produces a large number of build options
- Art style and colour use are the most consistently praised element (Shacknews, Steam reviewers, Metacritic critics)
- Fast, responsive movement and fluid action feel once a build comes together
- Humour, personality and a memorable cast of characters (Cultured Vultures, PC Invasion)
- Branching palace routes lead to different boss fights so runs are not identical
- Short runs plus three extra difficulty tiers above Normal give replay value for those who like a challenge
- Full voice acting and stylish music that add charm for many players
- Good value when discounted; several players say it is worth picking up on sale

#### Worst Things

- Low enemy variety, with many recolors and variations, and only around four bosses in total
- Rooms and scenarios feel samey; route choices make little practical difference
- Narrator has a small pool of voice lines, repeats often, and some find the delivery grating (many mute him)
- Combat can feel underwhelming or clunky: ability cooldowns make chaining moves hard and enemy density in small arenas is high
- Some cards, trinkets and skills are weak or trap picks, so experimenting feels stale and poisons the unlock pool
- Progression pacing: players can finish the game before unlocking half the cards and trinkets
- Mac release problems reported: poor controller support on an M1 Mac and a generic OS cursor instead of the in-game cursor
- Reviewers compare it unfavourably to Hades for depth, variety and presentation polish
- Active skills and passives are unevenly balanced, with some players saying most of them are not worth using and enemies feel strong for the balance

#### Improvements

- Make dash a baseline ability instead of consuming a card slot
- Let players keep shards between runs, or otherwise speed up unlocks, so progression is not lost on death
- Add more enemy types, bosses and distinct room/scenario types; make path choices more meaningful
- Rebalance weak cards and trinkets and reduce cooldown friction so combos chain more easily
- Widen the narrator's line pool or make him less intrusive; better voice direction for secondary characters
- Improve controller support and cursor handling on macOS; Steam forum threads also mention achievement bugs and crashes

#### Unique Things

- Weapons are replaced by a four-slot card system where any card can go in any slot and each slot also takes a secondary modifier, rather than a fixed weapon plus upgrades
- Even movement (dash) is a card you equip, so you can build around unusual kit such as all dashes
- Bright, colourful, comedic magician theatre aesthetic, lighter in tone than typical dark roguelites
- A live narrator comments on your performance, which is a Supergiant-style device with a comedic bent

#### Tedious Or Disliked

- Repetitive narrator lines wear out quickly, with multiple reviewers muting him
- Clearing near-identical rooms of the same small enemy roster becomes a slog, with some levels feeling like a dash to the boss
- Losing shards on death makes unlocking cards and trinkets feel slow, while weak unlocks dilute the random pool
- Cooldown-gated abilities make crowded fights feel restrictive
- Normal difficulty is seen as tough by some, with new players needing several attempts for a first win (about 10 runs for one reviewer), though a later update eased the early game

#### Divisive Points

- Difficulty: some call it brutally hard even on Normal, others find it fair and like the higher tiers
- Voice acting and narration: praised as charming and fully voiced by some, described as stiff, nasal or cringe by others
- Hades comparison: seen as a solid budget alternative by some and a weaker, shallower copy by others
- Build depth: some find limitless card combinations, others find no real strategy beyond making initial cards' numbers bigger
