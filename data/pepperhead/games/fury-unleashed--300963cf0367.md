#### Best Things

- Tight, responsive controls: dash, double jump, stomp, shoot and melee feel smooth and precise, which reviewers call the game's strongest asset
- Combo/heat system rewards aggressive, damage-free play with escalating rewards such as health and shield drops, and is described as the freshest mechanic
- Comic-book framing: chapters are individual comics and stages are laid out as comic panels, which players find creative and cohesive
- Big arsenal and many power-ups with trade-offs, giving varied runs; cited as a source of replayability, plus about 40 bosses
- Very customisable: adjustable difficulty sliders, full button remapping and character/face customisation
- Couch and online co-op make it far more fun with a friend, and two controllers even work in single-player for a silly shared-character mode
- Great value: often heavily discounted, with many reviewers saying it is worth more than they paid
- Strong soundtrack and a fast, chaotic, adrenaline-heavy feel that several players call an underrated gem

#### Worst Things

- Online co-op is unreliable: players report failing to connect over half the time, desyncs, and in 2026 reviews it is described as hard to get working
- Co-op design gripes: in online lobbies only the host can customise a character while the guest waits
- Repetition: only four level themes, rooms, weapons and bosses can feel alike over long sessions
- Enemies are spongy on Hard, slowing the pace; some find enemies spawning underneath them cheap
- Progression is seen as minor and mostly stat upgrades (health, etc.) rather than changing how you play; some upgrades are barely noticeable, like the blink invulnerability
- Weapon drops are random, and some runs go long stretches with nothing good
- Stability issues reported by some: a top-upvoted negative review reports consistent crashes shortly after launch; Steam forum topics mention black-screen crashes, Steam Deck and Linux audio problems
- Some reviewers find it short and, for solo play, outclassed by better action roguelites

#### Improvements

- Fix and modernise online co-op connectivity and sync, and let guests customise their own characters
- Add more level themes, enemy types and boss variety to reduce sameness
- Deeper meta-progression: unlocks that change playstyle rather than small stat bumps
- Rebalance enemy health on the default Hard difficulty to keep pacing brisk
- Smoother Switch performance (Nintendo Life cited framerate issues) and better Steam Deck/Linux support
- More reliable weapon availability per run so a run is not dead for long stretches

#### Unique Things

- Every stage is a page of a comic book, with rooms as panels and chapters as issues, tied into a story about a creator who has lost faith in his hero
- Ink is both the currency dropped by enemies and the thing you spend on upgrades
- Combo meter tied to survival: a whole run on one continuous combo is a stated challenge, and heat increases damage and healing drops
- Full customisation: players can change every difficulty setting, remap every button and design their character's look
- Hard is the default, with Easy mode locking leaderboards and achievements, plus harder modes and a secret final level unlocked later
- Roguelite with light grinding until the late secret final level, blended with old-school Contra-style run-and-gun

#### Tedious Or Disliked

- Hard-mode enemy sponginess and tiptoeing around damage makes some runs feel slow and tedious
- Early-game low-level runs feel very hard before upgrades make it easy (one reviewer likens it to Destiny: the game starts at full level)
- Upgrade grinding is called a grind by some, and the secret final level surprised and frustrated a reviewer
- Repetitive bosses, which one reviewer summarises as jumping around and shooting
- Connecting to online co-op is a hassle, sometimes harder than playing the game
- Basic for genre veterans: one top negative review says anyone who plays a lot of roguelikes will get bored of it

#### Divisive Points

- Difficulty: Hard default is praised as challenging-but-fair by some and as spongy, tedious and unfair by others
- Art style: some enjoy the personality of the paper cut-out comic look; Gameblog.fr called the art direction insipid, and one reviewer admits he dislikes games that look like flash games (though he ended up recommending it)
- Value: many call it a steal on sale, while a couple of reviewers say it is not worth full price and is better with a friend than solo
- Progression depth: some find upgrades motivating and enough to keep playing; others find them too minor to matter
