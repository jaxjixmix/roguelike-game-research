##### Praise

- Surprising scope: keeps expanding until it feels like a massive adventure game
- Intentionally slow, simple, clunky gameplay that supports the vulnerable start
- Progression from helpless hiding to becoming a true fighter or wizard
- Hardcore death system that sends you back to chapter start, making combat risky and frightening
- Remembrance shrines let you pay to permanently keep items, an interesting player decision
- Curse rot mechanic changes visuals, audio and gameplay, with fantastic effort put into the changes
- Strong sense of adventure and unique monsters

##### Criticism

- Map and inventory UI could use better filters and player feedback
- Late-game fast travel is lacking
- Gets repetitive by the end despite its size
- Player became so overpowered as to be near invincible
- Many bosses look cool but are mechanically uninteresting, and infinite lives in boss fights reduce the threat

##### Unique Points

- Permadeath treated as a hardcore chapter-reset death system rather than a full run reset, keeping levels and permanent items
- Curse meter that rises with horrifying events and transforms the world and mechanics
- Roguelike elements (random loot, rings with randomized stats) serve an action horror adventure

##### Design Criteria

- Risk vs reward: hardcore death and loot loss make combat frightening
- Meta-progression: remembrance shrines to permanently keep items
- Atmosphere/visuals/audio: curse-driven changes to the world
- Boss design: visually cool but mechanically shallow, infinite lives reduce threat
- UI/usability: map and inventory filtering and feedback
- Pacing/repetition: large game that gets repetitive late
- Difficulty/balance: becomes overpowered late
- Exploration/adventure feel: sense of expanding world
