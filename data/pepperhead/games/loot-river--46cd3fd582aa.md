##### Praise

- Unique gimmick of controlling both your character and the water platforms you stand on
- Art style and strong adventure vibe
- Unique events and bosses are exciting to discover
- Skill-heavy; learning to control character plus platform is initially confusing but rewarding

##### Criticism

- First floor has few, repeating enemies and few unique spells, so early builds and maps feel identical for hours

#### Best Things

- The tile-sliding mechanic: pushing floating landmasses to bridge gaps, isolate enemies, create chokepoints or escape is praised as a genuinely fresh twist (God is a Geek, Hardcore Gamer, many Steam reviews)
- Dark, atmospheric pixel art and an intense soundtrack are the most consistently complimented production elements
- Combat, once learned, is described as engaging and well-paced, with parrying and dodge timing rewarding skill
- Bosses are said to have distinct mechanics instead of reskinned patterns
- Build variety: many weapons, spells and armour pieces plus multiple routes through levels encourage experimentation
- An easy/accessibility mode lets players learn the unusual controls and difficulty curve, and modifiers can raise difficulty back up
- Short, replayable runs (roughly 5-10 hours to beat, 10-15 for 100%) and a low sale price make it easy to recommend as a change of pace

#### Worst Things

- Combat is frequently called clunky, with janky collisions and sloppy hitboxes; one Steam reviewer called it some of the worst combat they had played
- Bugs can ruin runs: enemies that lock platform movement can stay in effect after they die, and a boss was knocked outside the arena barrier, forcing restarts
- Loot and weapons feel samey (many weapons differ mainly by element and scaling) and meta-progression is very shallow for a game named Loot River
- Not much content: several players felt they had seen nearly everything in one or two runs, and Screen Rant scored it 40 saying it feels like an Early Access product
- Story and lore are described as boring or forgettable, and endings unfulfilling
- Visual clutter and poor visibility during fights make it hard to read the action and spot drops (LEVEL, Metacritic user reviews)
- Stamina and parry-heavy design irritates players who wanted fast, fluid roguelike combat
- Floating-block mechanic sometimes leaves no route forward, and most weapons are slow, so players feel pushed to abuse parry or die often (Steam negative reviews)

#### Improvements

- Fix the soft-locks and bugs (persisting platform locks, bosses leaving arenas, controller remap double-inputs) and keep polishing; players ask whether development is still active
- Make loot more interesting and weapons more distinct, with deeper meta-progression
- Add more content and gameplay variety, especially mid-run, which GRYOnline.pl and Steam users both allude to
- Improve combat readability, hitbox cleanliness and enemy attack/parry timing; some parry-only enemies are said to be nearly impossible to hit
- Fix level layouts where the floating mechanic leaves no route forward, and reduce time lost to block-puzzle stalls
- Better regional pricing and storefront reach (Steam users ask about Argentina pricing and a GOG release)

#### Unique Things

- Tetris-like shifting floor: the map itself is an active weapon and puzzle that you rearrange in real time while fighting
- Marketed and reviewed as Dark Souls meets Diablo meets Tetris (The Games Machine, Hardcore Gamer)
- You control character movement, attacks and platform pushing at once, which makes controller play close to mandatory
- Flooded-ruin setting with procedurally generated labyrinths, a narrative thread and multiple routes
- Soulslike stamina/parry combat inside a fast roguelite loop, with easy mode and modifiers to tune difficulty

#### Tedious Or Disliked

- Block-shifting puzzles can stall the pace when you are stuck trying to progress, especially when repeated across runs
- Stamina management and the delayed parry window frustrate players who find it punishing for any misstep
- Unlock/meta loop feels thin, so repeated runs can feel repetitive once the hook wears off
- Needing five keys to open gates and other progression gates feel grindy to some players (Steam community thread)
- Keyboard and mouse play is widely called awkward, so a controller is effectively required

#### Divisive Points

- Is it a souls-like or a roguelite? Players who expected fluid roguelike combat bounced off; those expecting deliberate soulslike combat loved it
- Difficulty and easy mode: some say easy mode is essential to learn it, others say hard mode is only frustrating, not rewarding
- Length and price: fine at $1-4 on sale, but many say too little content at the $20-25 launch price
- Critics scored it far higher (74 average) than Metacritic users (5.1, just 13 ratings) and Steam players (about 67% positive)
