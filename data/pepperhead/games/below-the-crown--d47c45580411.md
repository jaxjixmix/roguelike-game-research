He feels it is the first chess roguelike that truly captures real chess, since good chess play matters more than wild mechanics. Roguelike elements are tiny per-encounter boosts that never overshadow the core chess thinking. ##### Praise

- Captures the feel of real chess better than other chess roguelikes; chess skill genuinely makes you better
- Tutorials teach basic chess tactics like pinning, so players learn something
- Smart AI, with gold rewards for strategic plays like checkmates and pins rather than just clearing pieces
- Small, simple roguelike elements (easier placement, one-time freeze, one-time transform) fit the game's goals
- Limited pieces and a sparse board keep turns slow and deliberate
- Unexpected story with fourth-wall-breaking elements
- Rooks look like frogs

##### Criticism

- Gameplay can feel repetitive, like chess itself
- Slow turns and long individual encounters due to heavy thinking
- Not a game for everyone; requires careful step-by-step play
- He would not personally play it a ton since he is not a strong chess player

##### Unique Points

- Roguelike upgrades stay modest so the run is still about playing chess well, even at the final boss
- Rewards strategic chess play (checkmate, pins) with gold
- Avoids the common trap of piling on crazy rules that erase the chess feel

#### Best Things

- Approachable even for people who are bad at or indifferent to chess: moves are explained, and a danger-tile button shows which squares are threatened
- Fair, well-designed puzzles: failures usually feel like your own mistake rather than cheap unfairness (echoed by reviewers and players)
- Fresh take on chess roguelikes: you start with no pieces, must move the wizard before placing pieces or casting spells, forcing long-term planning instead of just building an overpowered board
- Pieces stay mostly standard with a few restrictive variants, so the game feels 'not too chess but enough chess', with spells, hazard tiles and non-chess pieces adding variety
- Retro neon vector/scanline presentation, hum of old electronics and a tense-but-relaxing atmosphere inherited from Duskers
- Daily challenge leaderboards, shareable replays and community challenges give short-session replayability
- Short runs that can be resumed anytime and multiple win conditions (capture everything or checkmate, board shrinks over time) keep pace brisk
- Interesting meta layer: odd psychological-assessment questionnaires and a mysterious emperor narrative that some compare to Inscryption

#### Worst Things

- Some players find it shallow or just 'worse than playing chess with generic roguelike mechanics bolted on' (mostly short-playtime negative reviews)
- Technical problems at launch/EA: a Linux crash with heavy memory use, invalid game states, a hopper capture not ending a level, a boss turn that would not end, and achievement tracking glitches
- Board can feel chaotic once more than about five pieces are in play
- Performance issues mentioned in an early negative review
- No mid-puzzle restart: failing means leaving the room without bonuses or upgrades (per GameGrin)
- Minimal animation; the presentation is stylish but sparse
- Narrative is light, especially in the early access build

#### Improvements

- Controller support was requested on Steam discussions
- A campaign mode built around the Hopper King character was suggested
- A way to restart a puzzle mid-room without forfeiting rewards
- Bug fixes for stuck boss turns, level-completion triggers and achievements
- More depth or content so it holds attention beyond short sessions
- Linux stability: early Linux crash with heavy memory use (reported fixed later), although the store page lists Windows only

#### Unique Things

- You are a wizard who starts each level with zero pieces and summons them within the wizard's line of sight, unlike drop-anywhere chess roguelikes such as The Rookery
- Shrinking board and alternative win conditions remove the need to actually orchestrate a checkmate
- Psychological-test questionnaires interleaved with play that affect the runs
- Undo tokens, a good-move hint and saved/shared replays make it puzzle-forward and forgiving
- Dungeon-crawler framing with RPG-style piece upgrades and spells layered on real chess tactics (forks, pins, skewers)

#### Tedious Or Disliked

- Losing a room means losing the run's upgrades from that room with no mid-puzzle restart
- Crowded boards with many pieces can become hard to read and calculate
- Some situations are perceived as unfair, particularly at the highest difficulty
- Players who dislike chess-like thinking find the core loop not engaging
- Not a game for long single sittings; better as a second-monitor or between-chores game

#### Divisive Points

- Chess as foundation: fans love that chess knowledge is optional, while a few critics say it is simply worse than playing chess
- Depth vs shallowness: long-playtime players find deep synergies and builds, short-playtime negative reviewers call it shallow
- Session length: praised as bite-sized and flexible, but criticised by the critic as unable to hold focus for a full session
