#### Best Things

- Fluid, punchy combat with light/heavy chains, arcana specials and three defensive tools (dodge, deflect, clash) that reward timing; often compared favourably to Streets of Rage 4
- Hand-drawn art and animation (Supamonks) widely called among the best 2D visuals of the year, with a soundtrack praised as memorable
- Four heroes (Karl, Galandra, Cider, Brome) with distinct movesets and six arcanas each, so runs feel different and builds can get absurd and overpowered
- Boon-like Ritual system with real synergies (water/tornado combos, thrown coins, thorn daggers, summons) that encourages experimentation
- Excellent two-player couch co-op, repeatedly described as the best way to play, including parents playing with kids
- Branching routes, secret areas, side quests and NPC stories give exploration and narrative texture beyond a plain arcade brawler
- Boss fights described as deliberate and not just damage sponges; assist and difficulty options make it accessible to older or casual players
- Good value at $24.99 for most reviewers, and runs well on Steam Deck

#### Worst Things

- Online co-op reliability: many negative Steam reviews cite disconnects, crashes mid-run and broken matchmaking, some saying it got worse after the crossplay update; no rejoin once dropped
- Late-game and Corrupted Biome encounters flood the screen with enemies, teleporting-in attackers and projectiles hidden behind big enemies, plus bloated enemy HP pools in co-op, which many find unfair rather than hard
- Hitbox and verticality complaints: enemy and boss attacks seem to reach past their animations or hit across the whole depth plane while the player must align precisely
- Elite and super-armored enemies and shield enemies are seen as unengaging and punish defensive play; dodge is widely called weak compared with deflect and clash
- Visual clutter and unclear telegraphs, plus a few reports of poor PC performance and softlock/stuck bugs
- Some quests make the game harder with little or no reward (for example extra enemies or boss changes added permanently)
- Co-op UI quirks: both players' inputs can hit the same NPC dialogue and trigger unwanted map changes; co-op lacks the solo difficulty sliders

#### Improvements

- Fix online co-op stability, allow reconnecting to a run, and only spend revive perks once both players are down
- Balance rituals so weak or junky options and dominant elements (water, chaos) are more even and fewer boons feel like filler
- Make dodge and defensive options more reliable against wide AoE, super-armor and shield enemies, and tidy hitboxes and telegraphs
- Offer difficulty adjustment in multiplayer and reduce stacked late-game enemy spawns
- More content and variety: levels feel repetitive across runs to some, the game is short for completionists (under 10 hours to clear everything for one reviewer), and fans ask for DLC, sequels and maybe four-player co-op
- Stronger narrative integration; some critics found the story thin compared to the worldbuilding

#### Unique Things

- One of the few roguelites built on a true arcade beat 'em up foundation, from the Streets of Rage 4 co-developer, with fixed level layouts but randomised rituals and branching paths
- Mounted combat: riding and taming creatures adds a different combat mode and is upgraded with elite mounts in 1.1
- Clash mechanic (strong attack just before being hit) alongside dodge and deflect gives a parry-flavoured defence layer uncommon in brawlers
- Persistent world with quests, NPC side stories and secret routes across runs, tied to a soul-tree meta progression and a harder true ending
- Mystic Ordeals (custom run modifiers with shareable codes), Boss Rush and Horde modes added post-launch
- Character-locked quirks: each hero's arcana and pickups (for example thrown objects as grenades, air-dash builds) can reshape how they play

#### Tedious Or Disliked

- Meta-progression grind and repeated runs through the same stages and bosses, especially for people who would prefer a one-shot arcade mode
- Getting the true ending takes many runs and was called stretched out by some reviewers
- Linear, mandatory stat upgrades in the meta tree rather than new options
- Corrupted Biomes and elite enemies turning late runs into cheese-build checks
- Having to wait for startup frames to clash or dodge instead of freely cancelling out of combos
- Some players say the roguelite layer is weak: in-run upgrades mostly gate moves and meta upgrades are flat stat bumps (the most-upvoted negative reviews call it a great beat 'em up but a poor roguelike)

#### Divisive Points

- Roguelite structure itself: praised as what keeps it fresh and deep, but IGN and some players say it dilutes the beat 'em up and leans on numbers over skill
- Combat depth: some call it simple but fun, others find the defensive system clunky or the combo limiter and spacing shallow
- Art style and character design: near-universal praise, though a minority of Metacritic user reviews dislike the character designs
- Difficulty: older or casual players love the assist options, while veterans complain about balance in corrupted late-game content
