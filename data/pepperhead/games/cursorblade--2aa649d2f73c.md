#### Entry 1

- **Summary:** Cursorblade is a cute, very simple top-down bullet-hell-ish game where your cursor is both hitbox and weapon; you damage enemies by touching them and dodge by not getting hit. He finds it satisfying and charming but basic, with a game-jam feel. Upgrades arrive once per wave but barely change how you play. - **Rerank Note:**

##### Praise

- Cute and satisfying to play
- Very straightforward concept that does what it sets out to do well
- Does not over-reach; restrained content suits the game
- Could be a fun, easy intro to basic roguelike action for a younger audience

##### Criticism

- Upgrades (rockets, spinners, fire damage) are not impactful, so runs play the same regardless of picks
- Gameplay loop of hitting then evading is very repetitive
- Pace is so fast it feels random, like swiping without thinking
- Would be more interesting slower, leaving room for more attacks and mechanics
- Feels like a game jam project; he would not play it again

##### Unique Points

- The cursor is both hitbox and weapon, dealing damage by touching enemies
- One upgrade per completed wave keeps the structure minimal
- Design lesson: slowing the pace would allow richer enemy attacks and more thoughtful bullet-hell play

##### Comparisons

- Bullet hell games
- Game jam games

##### Design Criteria

- Upgrade impact: upgrades should meaningfully change gameplay; here they do not
- Pacing: too fast makes it feel random and leaves no room for depth
- Repetition: same hit-and-evade loop throughout
- Scope/simplicity: straightforward design is appropriate but shallow
- Charm: cute presentation
- Enemy attack variety: wants more attack patterns via slower pace

### Community

- **One Liner:** A cheap Windows-only arcade bullet-hell roguelite by solo-ish studio RealPeach in which you are the mouse cursor, slashing waves of cute enemies and picking weapons and passives between waves.
- **Consensus Summary:** Steam players rate it Very Positive (about 85% of roughly 1,045 reviews) and treat it as a short, satisfying, cheap ($4.99) arcade score-chase with an original mouse-only hook. The most common complaint, even in positive reviews, is thin content: few weapons, enemies and upgrades, one boss, one map, and most people see everything in 1-3 hours. There are no professional critic reviews; Metacritic shows no critic score and too few user ratings for a score. #### Best Things

- Novel concept: the mouse cursor is both character and weapon, with one-to-one mouse-only control that players say they have rarely seen in a bullet hell
- Fluid, responsive, fair arcade feel; difficulty is described as challenging but fair by many, and as a good nice timekiller or relaxing pause
- Wave structure with a choice of one of three random weapons after each wave and a passive every few waves, giving simple survivors-like build decisions
- Cute art and animation (goofy-eyed monster sprites) and satisfying sound and visual effects
- Very cheap, short, easy to pick up and addictive for quick runs; some players lost track of time
- Achievement hunting and the wave-50 boss give a clear goal; some players pushed to wave 57 and beyond chasing high scores

#### Worst Things

- Very little content: roughly 6 weapons, about 12 upgrades, fewer than 10 enemy types, one map and a single boss at wave 50, after which waves continue endlessly with the same enemies
- Builds feel bland and a handful of options dominate, so runs feel identical; many upgrades are just 'X appears more often'
- Almost no meta progression: currency only unlocks new swords/cursors, so there is little reason to keep playing after unlocking everything (several say you have seen it all by the third game)
- Late waves become cluttered, so skilled dodging matters less and play turns into slowly picking off stragglers
- Looping, short and generic music gets grating; a few players mute it, though others praise the soundtrack
- Technical bareness: no Steam Cloud saves, essentially no graphics options (no resolution or FPS cap), some reports of bugs and slowness
- Some unlockable cursors/characters are seen as weak or useless, and right-click abilities have a slight activation delay that makes them unappealing
- Shallow, barebones feel: several negative reviews call it unfinished and Flash-game-like, with no saving and little build diversity

#### Improvements

- More weapons, items, enemy types, bosses, maps and characters; players note little has been added since the demo
- Real meta progression or build-diversity systems (and a save feature) so unlocks matter beyond new swords
- Make cursor-specific active abilities more useful and responsive
- Options: FPS unlock, resolution/graphics settings, Steam Cloud, and more music tracks
- Modding or Steam Workshop support (repeatedly requested in Steam discussions), plus community translations
- Clearer developer communication about the project's future and fixes for balance issues such as a blade bypassing invincibility frames and concern about the round-80 boss

#### Unique Things

- You play literally as a mouse cursor, using mouse motion alone to move, attack and dodge
- Bullet hell blended with survivors-like level-up choices but with no persistent stat meta-progression, closer to an endless arcade high-score chase
- Cursor-themed characters/blades each with a right-click active ability
- Combo meter and score-driven unlock points, where raising wave difficulty earns unlocks faster than perfecting combos
- Tiny runs and a playable free demo on itch.io (v0.27f), making it a pick-up-and-play desktop toy

#### Tedious Or Disliked

- Content exhausted within 30 minutes to 3 hours, making repeat runs feel padded and monotonous
- Some players say runs feel like chore-like work, one comparing it to cleaning windows
- Reliance on RNG weapon offers to carry runs
- Difficulty spikes: a minority find it too hard from the start, and demo players say it gets annoying past wave 10
- Endless post-boss waves repeat the same enemies with nothing new
- Looping music and lack of settings options

#### Divisive Points

- Difficulty: some call it fair and even easy (100% in a couple of hours), others call it punishing and unfun
- Value at $4.99: fun short game for the price versus not worth it for the content, with some recommending alternatives like 20 Minutes Till Dawn
- Roguelike versus roguelite label: debate over whether the lack of meta progression makes it a true roguelite
- Music: praised as awesome by some, called repetitive and grating by others
