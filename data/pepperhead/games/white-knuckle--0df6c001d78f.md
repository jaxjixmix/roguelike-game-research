#### Best Things

- Climbing feel: holding ledges with separate left and right mouse buttons feels tactile and physical, and players compare the immersion to Shadow of the Colossus-style grabbing
- Very high skill ceiling; a first campaign clear can take an hour or more while top players finish in under 10 to 20 minutes, which drives heavy speedrun/competitive engagement
- Sweaty-palm tension: horror atmosphere, sound design and the constant risk of falling combine into genuine anxiety, with no easy respawn or checkpoint
- Environments become more colorful and varied as you ascend, and a full-scale structure with no loading screens gives a strong sense of place
- Depth layered under a simple premise: secret rooms, item caches, facility upgrades, artifacts, and lore people keep uncovering after the first clear
- Frequent substantial content updates during Early Access (new regions, Endless mode, new perks/artifacts) keep long-time players returning
- Distinctive dark humor and community in-jokes (roach currency at vending machines, a joke-review culture around the elevator), plus controller support although keyboard and mouse is widely considered best
- Critics who covered early builds noted it was nearly bug-free for Early Access and had a motivating faster-and-longer loop
- Streamer-driven word of mouth: several top reviews say they bought it after watching Jerma struggle with it, and many joke-reviews celebrate the clenching tension

#### Worst Things

- Permadeath sends you back to the start of a long run; many negative reviewers say progress loss is soul-crushing and time-wasting, especially for new players
- No cheap mid-run save: saving and quitting is costly (bugs currency) or awkward, and floppy-disk backups only respawn you at the computer where you inserted them
- Late-game areas (the Abyss, Hanging Gardens, later regions) are called brutally hard, with enemies like the flying head and harpoon barnacles criticised as frustrating or unfair on precarious terrain
- Heavy trial-and-error: several reviewers describe the campaign as a series of knowledge checks, and deaths can feel like they come from RNG or ragdoll/enemy randomness rather than player error
- Physically demanding for long sessions; one older reviewer found the continuous intense mouse-and-keyboard input exhausting
- Grab detection is seen by some as finicky (crosshair must sit on a hold for a moment), making recoveries inconsistent
- Recent regions (the Nest) were criticised by a few high-hour players as repetitive, visually hard to read and thin on tile variety
- Roguelike layer feels thin to some: perks are rarely offered and lackluster, so there is little power-growth excitement compared with other roguelikes

#### Improvements

- Add a casual/campaign mode with checkpoints or persistent progress so players can reach new areas without replaying old ones
- Make saving and quitting mid-run cheaper and more accessible, or let floppy-disk backups be banked or transferred between computers
- Rebalance late-game enemies (Abyss and Hanging Gardens threats, harpoon barnacles) and reconsider the gun-driven last section
- Clean up or merge the perk pool so weak perks do not dilute runs, and make perk offers feel more impactful
- Improve transparency of mechanics such as inventory weight and its effect on movement speed, and add clearer on-screen tutorials
- Commonly requested extras: a relaxed zen/no-enemy mode, a level editor or workshop, and more cosmetic/collectible content

#### Unique Things

- A first-person climbing roguelite where your two hands are literally the core input, which almost no other game matches
- Runs are a vertical ascent through a single continuous megastructure (SUB-STRUCTURE 17) rather than generic room-to-room levels
- Horror that comes from physical exertion and fear of falling, not jump scares; chasing threats like the Mass force you to keep moving fast
- Speedrun-first design: a built-in timer, a competitive mode that trims the perk pool, and a community that clears the campaign in minutes
- Endless mode with an Abyss section where players compete for altitude (one reviewer reports steady runs around 5000 m)
- Roach/bug currency, vending machines, floppy-disk lives and computer respawn points as a quirky meta-layer

#### Tedious Or Disliked

- Replaying the same early regions every run because there are no real checkpoints is the most repeated complaint
- Long time per run before reaching the next section, which punishes players with limited play time
- Low-value or rarely offered perks can make early runs feel empty when RNG gives nothing useful
- Rage-inducing hazards in the Abyss and Hanging Gardens where enemies block or harpoon you mid-traverse
- Repeated tile reuse in newer regions makes Endless mode feel like a slog to some veterans

#### Divisive Points

- Roguelite structure: fans see permadeath and randomness as the tension that makes it special, while several reviewers call it a great climbing game held back by being a roguelike
- Difficulty of late-game and new content updates: hardcore players welcome the challenge, but some high-hour players say recent regions are overtuned or less fun than earlier builds
- Input method and stress: some call it relaxing, others find it physically and mentally exhausting, and there is debate whether permanent progression and perks smooth or distort the skill challenge
