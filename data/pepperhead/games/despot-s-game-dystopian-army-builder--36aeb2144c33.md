##### Praise

- Satisfying auto-battler loop of preparing a team and watching the chaos play out
- Funny, unserious tone with lots of humor
- Cute art style
- Passive effects are plentiful and impactful
- Enemy/ally AI is pretty good despite many triggering abilities
- Great overall vibe that makes it fun to watch

##### Criticism

- Far fewer classes and characters than first expected (about five per class, fixed abilities)
- You collect all the classes and characters you want by roughly mid-run, so there are no rare build-defining finds
- Passives make you stronger but do not change how you play; unit placement stays about the same
- No interaction during battles, which feels frustrating with so many abilities
- Strategy stays shallow; builds like fighter just mean collecting fighters and their passives
- Gets repetitive and he grew bored after six to seven hours

##### Unique Points

- Pure watch-and-see auto-battler format where all decisions happen before the fight
- Fixed-ability characters (e.g. the fridge guy) tied to classes instead of customizable units
- Humor and vibe carry a mechanically minimal game

##### Design Criteria

- Build diversity: fewer classes/characters than promised limits variety
- Player agency: no control during battles reduces engagement
- Synergies/passives: impactful but do not alter play patterns
- Strategic depth: minimalistic mechanics cap strategy
- Charm/visuals/humor: cute art and funny tone are strong points
- Replayability/run length: becomes repetitive, bored after ~6-7 hours
- AI quality: pretty good given chaotic ability triggers

### Community

- **One Liner:** A pixel-art roguelite auto-battler by Konfa Games (tinyBuild) where you recruit and equip an army of up to 49 puny humans, march through procedural dungeons managing food and shops, then test your build against other players' teams asynchronously.
- **Consensus Summary:** Players rate it Very Positive on Steam (about 84% of roughly 3,360 reviews), and critics are lukewarm-positive (Metacritic 73 from only 4 reviews, OpenCritic 73 with 55% recommending). The core loop of drafting weird synergies, min-maxing and watching tiny units fight is widely called addictive and charming. The recurring complaint is that it is shallow and short: once you have won a run there is little reason to continue beyond PvP leaderboards and mutation grinding. Many reviewers say it is worth buying only on sale. It launched in Early Access in 2021 and went to 1.0 on Sept 29, 2022 on PC and consoles; it still receives player discussion and some seasonal content as of late 2026, but this is a finished game, not abandoned.

#### Best Things

- Gorgeous, detailed pixel art and weapon animations, plus a well-liked soundtrack
- Addictive core loop: drafting classes and weapon synergies and trying a different build after each failed run
- Asynchronous PvP (fighting ghosts of other players' builds, leaderboards, King of the Hill/Brawl modes) is the most-loved part for many
- Dark, cheerful-cruelty humor and pop-culture references in the writing and unit designs
- Accessible and streamlined: easy to pick up, hands-off battles are quick to read, runs are short
- Progression unlocks give new options rather than raw power, so you can still lose to your own bad decisions
- Food-meter mechanic stops aimless wandering and limits army size in an interesting way
- Good value when bought on sale; several reviewers say they got their money's worth (e.g. 41 hours in)

#### Worst Things

- Little replayability after the first victory; many players feel the game ends too early and call it a long demo
- Shallow strategic depth and one-note items/classes; PvE gets boring quickly
- PvP is dominated by a few meta builds and RNG rolls, and PvP balance concerns spill into PvE
- Levels and biomes feel samey; minimap icons and some rules (magic damage) are confusing
- Heavy RNG and sudden difficulty spikes in later floors, including shop availability that can leave you unable to refill ranks
- Mutation/achievement completion becomes a grind
- Bugs reported in places (loading-circle bug in Brawl runs, mantrap enemies seen as unfairly tanky)
- Many reviewers will not recommend it at the $19.99 full price: 'wait for a sale', and one calls it a very long demo

#### Improvements

- More content and variety in biomes, enemies, classes and items to extend replay value after the first win
- Better PvP balance and anti-meta measures so non-meta builds can compete
- Fairer, more reliable shop/market access so runs are less RNG-gated
- Clearer tutorialisation and UI (mutation reminders, minimap icons, magic damage explanation)
- Make it more of a true roguelite with lasting meta-progression rather than a one-win-then-PvP structure
- Bigger, more explosive death feedback so losses register (early reviewer suggestion)

#### Unique Things

- Roguelike dungeon crawl combined with auto-battler army building at up to 49 units, rather than a small party
- Food meter as a run-length and army-size constraint
- Asynchronous PvP where your winning run becomes a ghost team for others, with a leaderboard
- Class synergy by equipping a number of unique items from the same class, unlocking powerful abilities
- Part of the Despot universe (Despotism 3k, Slime 3K) with a shared puny-humans pixel-art style and dystopian dark comedy

#### Tedious Or Disliked

- Grinding all mutations and achievements (one 79-hour reviewer enjoyed the first 20-30 hours, then found it a slog)
- Replaying runs until RNG hands you the meta build for PvP
- Early PvE repeated across runs feels brain-dead or easy once solved
- Hands-off combat leaves some players feeling it is fun to watch rather than play
- Late-floor traps/bosses such as mantraps and repeated first-biome bosses that can wipe high-XP units

#### Divisive Points

- Value: many call it great on sale and a ripoff at full price
- Hands-off auto-battle: some love the streamlined fights, others want more control or interaction
- PvP emphasis: loved as the endgame by some, called pointless (bragging rights only) or meta-driven by others
- Roguelike vs roguelite identity: some wish it were more of a roguelite with persistent progression
