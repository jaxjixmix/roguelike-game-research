#### Best Things

- Steep but rewarding learning curve: players say runs feel fairer and more controllable as they learn items, spells and boss patterns
- Class and build variety: each class plays differently, any class can use any item (a mage can wield a knife), and most weapons and stats are described as viable
- Co-op with up to 4 players locally or online is repeatedly called the best way to play, producing chaotic, funny moments (reviews mention reviving friends, shared trap deaths)
- Tight, responsive movement and combat; one reviewer notes they rarely felt a death was out of their control, and jeuxvideo.com praised 'millimeter-tuned' gameplay
- Atmospheric pixel art, oppressive dungeon mood (catacombs named) and a well-liked soundtrack
- Depth from item identification, shrines/offerings, magic versus burning books for healing, and gambling on unidentified potions
- Replayability and completionist content: Book of Secrets unlocks, hard achievements, Steam Workshop and custom levels, plus a community Discord
- Reminds players of Spelunky and UnEpic (Steam reviewers), with RPG-style classes layered on a platformer

#### Worst Things

- Controls draw repeated complaints on keyboard/mouse and especially controller; some players report needing Steam Input settings or a restart to get a pad recognized
- Trap deaths (spikes, arrow traps, boulders) and instant kills feel cheap to some players, who say traps rather than enemies dominate the difficulty
- Combat is called simple or repetitive by some (Hobby Consolas, and a Steam reviewer who says the RPG layer is a facade over platforming)
- Weak onboarding: Nintendo Life and Metacritic users cite minimal tutorials and unexplained mechanics
- Hard-to-see hazards, including traps that blend into the environment, make some deaths feel unfair
- Online multiplayer is reported as a bit buggy, with sparse public servers per Metacritic users
- Limited content scope for some critics: jeuxvideo.com wanted more biomes and Nintendo Life found the adventure short with no story
- Dev support appears to have ended, with Steam forum users asking if the game still receives updates

#### Improvements

- Better, rebindable and more reliable controller support and general control feel
- Real tutorials or in-game explanation of hazards, items and systems
- Difficulty or accessibility options, for example ways to tone down traps, since one reviewer found the devs offered none and modding them out is hard
- More biomes and content variety, plus some narrative or goals beyond the dungeon crawl (critic feedback)
- More varied and complex enemy mechanics so combat carries more of the challenge than traps
- More stable online co-op and matchmaking, and continued post-launch updates or a sequel (Steam forum users want one)

#### Unique Things

- RPG-style classes, skins and backgrounds with free item mixing inside a Spelunky-like platformer
- Item identification, curses, shrines and offerings that make item use a risk-reward gamble, close to classic ASCII roguelike design
- Up to 4-player local and online co-op built into a hardcore permadeath platformer
- Steam Workshop custom levels plus the Vagante Remastered community mod
- Hard mode and Rift bosses give high-end endgame challenges for veterans

#### Tedious Or Disliked

- Repeated early deaths, sometimes within seconds of starting a run, from traps or bad RNG
- Item roulette: unidentified items and randomized loot can make or break a run
- Losing long runs (an hour or more) to a single spike or boulder
- Learning mechanics by dying because the game explains little
- Achievement hunting is described as extremely difficult though fair, with 100% completion taking 180+ hours for one reviewer

#### Divisive Points

- Difficulty: Steam reviewers call it brutal yet fair and rewarding, while Metacritic users and some Steam negatives call it punishing for its own sake
- Combat: praised as good on every class by some players, but called simple and repetitive by some critics and a co-op reviewer
- Trap-heavy design: a feature that demands awareness to fans, a cheap source of deaths to detractors
- Critic scores vary widely by outlet, from 80 (TouchArcade, Hobby Consolas, jeuxvideo.com) to 40 (Nintendo Life), with Pure Nintendo and Gamezebo at 70 and Nintenderos at 60
