#### Best Things

- Breakcore/hardbass soundtrack is repeatedly called top tier; some players say they found the game through the OST
- Cohesive psychedelic webcore / early-internet aesthetic, with weapons, upgrades and death screens presented as code and damage numbers doubling as XP pickups
- Blend of bullet hell dodging with Vampire Survivors-style build knowledge, so skill matters more than a pure knowledge check
- Screen-filling, dopamine-heavy chaos where your bullets outnumber the enemies
- Story with heavy Yume Nikki / Evangelion-style dreamlike feel that some players say made them care about the characters
- Bosses with bullet hell patterns that are approachable for newcomers but still challenging
- Game speed options (including 2x) and other quality-of-life features that let players tune the pace
- Very low price and a free demo; many reviewers call it badly underpriced, and the major post-launch update was described as huge
- Absurd, funny, meme-heavy tone (throwing dynamite at Geocities fish gifs, getting attacked by burgers in space) that reviewers clearly enjoy

#### Worst Things

- Runs feel structurally identical to some players: same bosses, map layout and backdrops each run, little difference between weapons
- Upgrades and powerups are poorly explained, and many permanent upgrades feel minor
- Difficulty falls off once you have a decent build; one 80-hour reviewer says it becomes trivially easy and that higher overdrive levels only add FPS drops rather than challenge
- Performance problems at high difficulty and a known FPS-drop issue during the final boss
- Limited roster (one playable character and a handful of weapons) compared with Vampire Survivors or Halls of Torment
- Some players feel underpowered early on and never reach the screen-filling power fantasy shown in screenshots
- Enemies and some weapons read as bland to a few reviewers; one disliked a specific boss (the biker) as boring
- Flashing strobing visuals with no obvious photosensitivity/epilepsy option, per at least one reviewer

#### Improvements

- Add an epilepsy/photosensitivity setting to tone down flashing effects
- Explain upgrades, weapons and meta-progression more clearly in-game
- Give later difficulty tiers real challenge instead of just heavier load, and fix FPS drops at high overdrive and in the final boss fight
- Vary runs more: more stages, backdrops, characters or build-defining choices
- Rebalance weapons and some boss fights that players call questionable
- Small UX requests from the community, such as making the four choosable paths clickable for faster selection, plus a Russian localization request

#### Unique Things

- Fuses Touhou-style bullet hell with Vampire Survivors-style auto-fire, with reviewers also citing Cruelty Squad and Yume Nikki as touchstones
- Internet-nostalgia webcore presentation (Geocities-style gifs, hardbass, breakcore) rather than generic fantasy or sci-fi
- Dream-memory story structure with lore-rich, surreal and transhumanism themes delivered through flashbacks between runs
- Gameplay information presented as in-world code, and damage numbers that act as XP pickups
- Daily and monthly challenge runs plus an overdrive difficulty ladder and a customizable skill tree

#### Tedious Or Disliked

- Meta-progression described by critics of the game as a grind of replaying to earn skill points rather than a meaningful loop
- Repetitive runs with the same bosses and maps, and short runs that blur together
- The edgy or tumblr-esque writing and rabbit emo character art put some players off, and they skip story scenes
- Trial and error needed to understand builds because upgrade text is unclear
- Dodging-dots focus leaves less room for the power-fantasy build crafting survivor fans expect

#### Divisive Points

- Story and tone: some find it moving and well written, others find it cringe, edgy or too internet-flavoured
- Difficulty: newcomers find it hard at first, veterans report it gets easy once builds come together
- Weapon balance and variety: praised as fun and chaotic by most, called samey or questionably balanced by a minority
- Heavy flashing psychedelic visuals are a selling point for most but a health barrier for photosensitive players
