### Commentary


#### Entry 1

- **Part:** 4
- **Label:** Tier list Part 4 (2025)
- **Summary:** Keeper's Toll is a Vampire Survivors-style auto-attacker that wraps its runs in an adventure campaign with quests, NPC events and quest-specific bosses. He loves the discovery and secrets and the attention paid to enemies, visuals, music and Souls references. The roguelike layer is thin: each character has about two build paths, items are bland, and meta-progression is heavy and tied to per-character XP. He had a good time finishing the campaign but would not start fresh runs, and places it in low mid B.
- **Quote:** I'm going to put this as a as a low midb tier.
- **Rerank Note:**

##### Praise

- Campaign structure with quests, NPC events and quest-dependent bosses gives a real adventure feel
- Discovery of secrets and puzzle-like mysteries, which he feels most survivor-likes fail to capture
- Characters feel drastically different from one another
- Lots of attention to enemy variety per level and quest progress, with fun Souls references (bonfire, Dark Souls mimic)
- Visuals and music are very solid

##### Criticism

- Replayability is weak: each character has only two build paths (e.g. punches or kicks for the monk)
- Passive items are few and mostly unremarkable; level-up upgrades are always the same with no rarity and few synergies
- Meta-progression is very impactful and feels unfair; characters are weak at zero upgrades, and it comes from per-character XP rather than a spendable currency
- Bosses are single big creatures with no adds, making horde-clearing and on-kill/bounce abilities useless and fights drag on

##### Unique Points

- Quest-driven campaign layered on the survivor-like formula, with bosses depending on the active quest
- Design lesson: survivor-like bosses should come with adds, since horde-oriented abilities are otherwise dead
- Idea: bosses could trigger on-kill effects when hitting certain HP thresholds

##### Comparisons

- Vampire Survivors
- Dark Souls
- Dungeon/Soul Slayer-style survivor-likes (the game covered just before)

##### Design Criteria

- Discovery/secrets: quest-based campaign and mysteries are the strongest aspect
- Replayability/build diversity: only two paths per character, weak
- Synergies and items: few synergies, bland passives, no rarity
- Meta-progression: powerful but per-character XP feels unfair and unflexible
- Enemy design: good variety and attention, Souls references
- Boss design: single big creatures with no adds hurt horde-focused builds
- Character diversity: characters feel drastically different
- Visuals and audio: solid art and music

#### Best Things

- Six classes that play very differently (fighting-game-style combo inputs for the Shadow Monk, minion-based Necromancer, tanky dodge-based Bogatyr, etc.), and the cast is praised as unusual versus the standard fighter/mage/assassin set
- Detailed pixel art and a well-liked dark fantasy soundtrack
- Boss fights and enemy design with Souls-like flavor; bosses are more than plain damage sponges (though some call them not especially intricate)
- Cheap price (about $7) and a 10-20 hour campaign make it an easy casual recommendation, described as a relaxing shut-your-brain-off game that suits short 20-30 minute sessions
- ARPG touches: limited healing potions, in-run merchants, a minor quest system, per-character skill trees and a global skill tree that forces choices
- Curses that impose a penalty but can reward you if you survive them, which is a twist on the genre formula
- Skill tree and progression are called fun by some finishers, and quick early power growth suits casual play
- Challenging for the genre, which some players welcome

#### Worst Things

- Severe class and skill imbalance: Blood Maiden, and to a lesser extent Archer/Pyromancer/Summoner, are described as far stronger, while Monk and Bogatyr are called weak; melee classes can take tens of minutes to kill bosses
- Poor run variety: only 4 maps (one a small square arena), a limited merchant pool, and upgrade options that let you pick up nearly everything, so runs with the same class feel identical
- The power fantasy fades or never arrives; there is a clear ceiling on how strong you become, and several reviewers said they never felt powerful even with talents maxed
- Stat and mechanic opacity: curses, defense value, pickups such as yellow crystals and XP sources are poorly or never explained, and there is no codex or visible list of active curses
- Clunky movement and dodging, and only left/right aiming, which is an odd fit for a genre where enemies surround you
- Visual clutter late in a run hides dodgeable hazards; some players turn effects off to see traps, and certain curse hazards look like background effects
- Seen as derivative: several reviewers say enemies and bosses are very close copies of Dark Souls designs, and that it is a weaker Halls of Torment
- Endgame and story disappoint: the final boss is called underwhelming compared with the third boss, and the story is called nonsensical or almost non-existent
- Frustrating RNG: too few rerolls and upgrade choices that are often poor or junk actives, which reviewers cite alongside balance problems

#### Improvements

- Rebalance classes so weaker ones (Monk, Bogatyr, Archer, Necromancer) and melee builds are viable, and so one or two classes do not dominate
- Add more maps, stages and a bigger item/merchant/upgrade pool so runs differ from each other; more levels was a direct request from finishers
- Add in-game information: a codex or index for curses, clearer defense/stat effects, a detailed character sheet with damage and bonus breakdowns, and visual indicators for XP sources and elites
- More rerolls or better upgrade choice control, and reduce randomness that hands out junk actives
- More meta-progression depth and unlockables (only six characters, all unlocked from the start, reduces the chase); players also ask for more XP sinks late-game and more tome drops
- Polish controls and sight lines: dodge/aim improvements and clearer hazard telegraphing; one player also requested co-op on the Steam forum

#### Unique Things

- Hybrid of survivors-like and Diablo-style ARPG: each character has its own full weapon/skill set rather than a single starting weapon
- Souls-lite tone and boss homages layered on a bullet-heaven loop, with bullet-hell style bosses
- Class mechanics that break from the norm, such as the Shadow Monk's directional combo inputs and the Necromancer's corpse/minion play
- Curse system that gives both debuffs and potential boons, plus a minor quest system and limited healing potions
- Individual per-character talent trees on top of a global skill tree where you cannot unlock everything
- Kindled (harder) runs that grant extra XP and are used to advance character talent trees

#### Tedious Or Disliked

- Grinding the same four maps repeatedly to level each character's talent XP and to see late-game bosses, then restarting the loop with every new class
- Achievement grind: some achievements (for example 1 million kills) are called tedious, and several reviewers say most of their hours after the first 4-15 were achievement grinding
- Hard kindled runs on fresh characters are described as near-impossible until perks are unlocked, which forces the XP grind
- Repetition sets in after roughly 10-15 hours; one reviewer says only about 3-4 hours felt like discovery
- Learning curses and unexplained pickups by trial and error
- Three of the four maps are infinite in all directions, which one reviewer found disorienting and wished had a constrained layout or map
- Character talent XP is slow to earn, which negative reviewers call a drag on progression

#### Divisive Points

- Difficulty and challenge: some enjoy it as more challenging than most survivors-likes, others find bosses and kindled runs frustrating, especially with weak classes
- Power fantasy: dad-gamer/chill reviewers love quickly getting powerful with AoE and big numbers (especially maxed Blood Maiden), while others never feel powerful
- Value: a low price and roughly 10-hour campaign make it a fair buy for some, while others say wait for a deep sale and buy Halls of Torment or Vampire Survivors instead
- Souls homages: praised as flavor by some and criticized as too close to copying by others
