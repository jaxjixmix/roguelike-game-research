##### Praise

- Real-time strategy plus roguelike structure is fairly unique and few games do it
- The genre itself is distinctive and he respects the attempt
- Easy to play quickly in short sessions

##### Criticism

- Little strategy in combat; mostly click a spot and have vehicles shoot

- Outside world is boring, lonely and empty
- Exploration events and signals are not very engaging

### Community

- **One Liner:** A pixel-art tactical roguelike from Convoy Games that mixes FTL's overworld of random events with real-time, Mad Max-style vehicle combat as you cross a wasteland hunting for parts to repair your ship.
- **Consensus Summary:** Convoy is received as a solid but flawed FTL-inspired indie. Steam users rate it Mostly Positive (about 78% of roughly 1,470 reviews), and critics land in the mixed-to-good range (Metacritic 72, OpenCritic top-critic average around 76). The concept and the vehicle-customisation loop are widely liked, but many reviewers and players say the moment-to-moment combat becomes repetitive and the difficulty can feel unfair, especially early ambushes. It was a 2015 PC release from Indietopia Games that later got console ports (PS4 at least); I found no sign of a sequel or recent major updates, and some recent Steam reviews ask for one. The developer's follow-up title Landinar: Into the Void is bundled with it on Steam.
- **Reception Notes:** Launched April 21, 2015 on PC (Windows, Mac, Linux) via Indietopia Games at $14.99. Critic reaction was mixed to positive with the recurring repetition complaint; Metacritic lists 17 critic reviews (7 positive, 9 mixed, 1 negative), while OpenCritic shows only 38% of critics recommending it despite a mid-70s average. Steam sits at Mostly Positive, and estimates suggest 200k-500k owners and roughly $599k gross revenue (Boxleiter-style estimate, treat as order-of-magnitude). It later came to consoles; PS4 reviewers liked the game but found the controller scheme clumsy and recommended the PC version. The game has Steam Workshop support. I found no evidence of ongoing development, and recent Steam reviews (as late as 2026) are still asking for an update or sequel.

#### Best Things

- The FTL meets Mad Max pitch is delivered faithfully; several Steam reviewers call it the only FTL-like that really feels like FTL
- Upgrading and customising your vehicles and mobile base gives a strong sense of progression during a run
- Random radio-signal encounters can turn into combat, text dialogue or chance-based role-play, which keeps runs varied
- Tense risk/reward decisions around fuel, repairs and whether to fight, with permadeath adding stakes
- Runs can be short (15 minutes) or long (2 hours), so players can tell their own hardship stories (Twinfinite)
- Good replay value: extra starting vehicles unlock by completing specific tasks, and harder difficulty modes exist
- Controls are easy to learn; a new player has the concepts down after a game or two (Game Informer)
- A 'chill' run-after-run feel that long-time players return to years later, often pairing it with FTL
- Fits its $14.99 price point; Steam reviewers call it simple but lots of fun

#### Worst Things

- Combat is the core loop and repeats so much that critics call it a slog or chore (Game Informer, Critical Hit, Gamers FTW)
- Some critics say there is little real tactics beyond focus-firing and attrition, and that it lacks FTL's depth (Riot Pixels scored it 40)
- Early game can feel brutally unfair: enemies outnumber you, hit hard, and ambushes appear suddenly and wipe out vehicles
- Visuals and music get called threadbare, uninspired or repetitive, and the soundtrack wears out its welcome
- Story and world-building are thin or annoying; narrative ambitions go unfulfilled (Games.cz)
- Quality-of-life gaps and the odd bug; one mistake can ruin an entire run
- Console ports suffer from controls that are too slow for commanding several vehicles and from a cluttered UI with hard-to-read fonts

#### Improvements

- More variety in encounters and enemy types so fights stop feeling like the same exchange
- Deeper tactical options beyond positioning and focus fire, including more useful abilities and customisation
- Smoother onboarding and a fairer early game, with better warning of or counterplay to ambushes
- Quality-of-life fixes such as scrollable objective text, seeing details of unaffordable shop items, and fewer button presses in the shop on controller
- Stronger story and world-building payoff, plus more polished art and audio
- An update or sequel; recent positive and negative Steam reviews both ask for more support

#### Unique Things

- Real-time vehicle combat as a high-speed car chase, where you position units and use abilities, rather than FTL-style ship system management
- A central mobile command vehicle that cannot move from the middle of the screen and soaks up damage each fight
- Three competing factions that grow in strength over the run, forcing adaptation
- A procedurally generated planet with different objectives, events and consequences each playthrough
- Tactical pause plus radio-signal encounters that branch into combat, dialogue or chance-based role-play
- Steam Workshop support exists, though Steam reviewers note there are very few mods

#### Tedious Or Disliked

- Fights are long and similar, so many players report the game getting boring quickly
- Fuel drains most of the money early, and backtracking across the wasteland is punished
- Repeated early losses: players report dying in the first ambush or first side mission and quitting
- Repair costs and losing vehicles can snowball into a dead run, which players say does not respect their time
- Shop and menu handling on controller takes too many button presses

#### Divisive Points

- Difficulty: some love the challenge on harder modes, others call it janky and too hard from the start (Steam threads suggest starting on Easy and getting a third vehicle early)
- Depth vs repetition: some call it a satisfying roguelike and the only true FTL-like, others say it lacks tactics and depth compared with FTL
- Metacritic user score is much lower (5.3 from 34 ratings) than critics (72) and Steam (Mostly Positive)
- Pixel art and soundtrack are charming to some and bland or repetitive to others
