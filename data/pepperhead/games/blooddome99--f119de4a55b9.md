#### Entry 1

- **Part:** 3
- **Label:** Tier list Part 3 (2025)
- **Summary:** Blood Dome 99 is a horde-like shooter sent to the channel by a viewer; he played it in early access and it is now fully released with three levels and an endless mode. You shoot from the center of the arena with a dash, and the twist is a money system: kills give XP and gold, gold buys upgrades, and the goal is to reach a gold target as fast as possible. He admires its unique visuals and mechanical upgrades but wants more depth in the money mechanic and end-of-run stats. Verdict: a solid B tier that stands out without reinventing the genre.
- **Quote:** Blood Dome 99 is a solid B tier.
- **Rerank Note:**

##### Praise

- Minimalist old-TV visual style with one color per element (player, bullets, blood, enemies), keeping the screen readable and cutting visual chaos
- Accessibility options, including changing the colors
- Money mechanic: spending gold on upgrades versus finishing the run quickly creates a real timing decision, since enemies scale and will eventually overwhelm you
- Upgrades are mechanical changes rather than flat percentage boosts, with unique per-weapon upgrades (e.g. the bee gun)
- Weapons offered every few minutes as a choice of three you must pay for, forcing spend-or-save choices
- Meta unlocks do not feel bad because items do not depend on narrow synergies
- Dash and movement feel great; music and the announcer narrator add charm

##### Criticism

- Run stats are lacking: only clear speed matters, not gold earned, and he wants big numbers at the end
- No damage breakdown per weapon or item, which makes it hard to judge what is actually strong in a chaotic horde-like
- Money and XP are effectively the same since both come from kills, so the fast-versus-level-up tension feels muted
- The money concept was hard to grasp at the beginning
- Wants more things to find during runs, such as events and mini bosses

##### Unique Points

- Gold as both currency and win-condition progress makes you choose between speed and power, a mechanic he rarely sees in horde-likes
- Upgrades that change behavior per weapon instead of stat bumps
- A game does not need to be wildly original; standing out visually and mechanically is enough
- Suggests end-of-run damage stats should be a staple of horde-likes

##### Comparisons

- Balatro
- Other horde-likes (Vampire Survivors-style)

##### Design Criteria

- Visual clarity: one color per element avoids visual chaos and aids evasion
- Risk vs reward: spend gold on upgrades or rush the run before enemies overwhelm you
- Upgrade design: mechanical changes and per-weapon upgrades beat flat stat boosts
- Meta-progression: unlocks add variety without bad synergy traps
- Game feel: dash and movement are satisfying
- Run information: stats and damage breakdowns help players learn
- Audio and charm: music and narrator add personality
- Content variety: wants more events and mini bosses in runs

#### Best Things

- Retro pixel-art style and 80s dystopian/retrowave atmosphere, repeatedly called the best part
- Soundtrack described as a banger by reviewers
- Diverse arsenal of 40+ weapons (paper planes, flamethrowers, bee guns, snow blowers) and interesting perks enabling different builds
- Addictive, well-made core loop that feels like a Vampire Survivors / Smash TV hybrid
- Satirical premise where earning and spending money trades off against winning your freedom
- Good achievement chase and challenge for completionists (one reviewer compares it to a polished version of mobile horde games)
- Steam Deck Verified; one reviewer reports it runs fine on an M4 Mac mini
- Reviewers say it is nothing groundbreaking for the genre but very well made, with real love and effort behind it

#### Worst Things

- Shallow for some: lack of variety and little progression according to a 5/10 reviewer
- Key options like auto-aim/auto-fire are gated behind an unlock (the bouncer) instead of being a plain menu setting
- Character moves very slowly while auto-firing and some upgrades slow movement further
- Bosses have a huge health pool that some find infeasible early with few weapons
- Enemy projectiles can be hard to read amid bullet hell (for example slow carriage projectiles easily missed)
- Late-game EXP scaling is steep (asked to be easier)
- Pace is noticeably slower than other horde survivors
- Reported bug where the bouncer weapon can spawn dozens of looping bullets and cause lag

#### Improvements

- Make auto-aim/auto-fire plain menu options rather than progression unlocks
- Remove or reduce the movement-speed penalty when firing
- Improve projectile readability and color clarity in busy fights
- Let players ban unwanted perks from the rotation, or start with better default perks so unlocks do not dilute the pool
- Ease late-game EXP scaling and reduce boss health or give more early firepower
- Balance autofire versus click-spamming so autofire is not slower (fire rate clamp or ramp)

#### Unique Things

- Economy twist: money buys power but pushes you further from the freedom goal
- Satirical 'Running Man' style 1999 gameshow setting
- Twin-stick arena shooter feel with manual aiming rather than pure auto-attack survivors gameplay
- Meta-progression that unlocks weapons, perks, music, cosmetics and game modes
- Started as a Juice Jam II entry with a free early version on itch.io

#### Tedious Or Disliked

- Grind to pay for access to the next arena even after beating the first one
- Slow-paced progression and slow character movement compared with peers
- Unlocking basic quality-of-life (auto-aim) through the bouncer gate is considered annoying
- Long boss fights with few weapons
- Late-game XP curve requiring long grinding
- Unlockable perks that worsen your build diluting the rotation

#### Divisive Points

- Twin-stick manual shooter vs survivors-like auto-attack identity; some want it to pick one
- Pace: called a slow burn by some and sluggish by others
- Value: a 5/10 reviewer calls it exceptionally shallow for the price, while others call it a fantastic, challenging 100% chase
