#### Entry 1

- **Part:** 5

- **Summary:** Moonlighter 2 is the early-access sequel to Moonlighter, pairing a Hades-style room-by-room action run with a shopkeeping and inventory-fusion loop. He has played a lot of it and is really enjoying it, calling the amount of content and the out-and-back loop standout. Combat feels decent but clunky, and the boon system and run pacing need polish. He places it in a solid A tier for now and expects it to rise as the game is fleshed out.
- **Quote:** Moonliner 2 is going to be a uh solid A tier and can only go up from here.
- **Rerank Note:** Early-access evaluation; he has discussed it on the channel before and notes it can only go up as it develops.

##### Praise

- Run-to-shop loop makes you want both to finish a run and start a new one
- Hades-like daily selling boons (e.g. legendary sales give more gold but leave a stain to sweep) make selling interesting
- Item curses and fusing effects to raise item quality is a fun inventory mini-game with big payoffs
- Huge meta-progression breadth: weapons, shop, pets, guns, badges, potions, stickers, and more
- Vault money goal gives visible progress and permanent big upgrades
- Music, visuals and story are excellent; the hub is pleasant to just sit in
- Runs are quick with lots to do (chests, hidden events, secrets) and boss fights are impressive

##### Criticism

- Combat feels clunky: input buffering, slow animations amid fast surroundings, hard-to-read action
- Some weapons (e.g. spearheads needing positioning) feel weak; gun aiming can be slow
- Buffed basic attacks make specials pointless, leading to repetitive play
- Some boons are too situational, relying on environmental triggers absent in boss fights
- Enemies that run away while you are slow; up to five rooms can pass with no build change
- Only three biomes (day and night each), and bosses can drag; inventory management may feel tedious to some

##### Unique Points

- Combines two very different loops (dungeon and shop) and the sequel handles that hard problem better than the original
- Selling-day boons borrow the Hades boon structure for the shop side
- Cursed items are not purely negative; their effects are combined to raise item quality and price
- Trade-off between taking boons versus chests for loot to sell and progress meta

##### Comparisons

- Moonlighter (original)
- Cult of the Lamb
- Soulslike games

##### Design Criteria

- Two-loop integration: how well the dungeon and shop halves complement each other
- Combat feel: input buffers, animation speed and readability
- Boon/build system: situational boons and synergy usefulness in boss fights
- Meta-progression: breadth of shops and upgrades driving replay
- Run length and pacing: quick runs, but stretches with no build change
- Content quantity: only three biomes in early access
- Audio and visual charm: music and art praised heavily
- Risk vs reward: boons versus chests for loot
- Boss design: big impressive fights that can run long

#### Best Things

- Addictive 'one more run' loop: expeditions, shop pricing and progression all feed each other (The Big Lead called the two loops much deeper than in the first game).
- Backpack inventory puzzle is praised as improved. Players enjoy stockpiling item categories and building chain-reaction synergies that multiply item value.
- Combat is cleaner and more responsive than ML1, with four weapon classes and unlockable weapons that change playstyle noticeably.
- Cute, cohesive 3D art and cozy-yet-adventurous atmosphere; several reviewers defend the move away from pixel art.
- Risk/reward push-your-luck decisions inside runs (take one more level or risk losing part of the haul's value) plus plenty of meta progression.
- Relic/perk system with many relics and build variety, and traps/turrets that reward creative play.
- Developer responsiveness on performance: stuttering was reportedly fixed in Nov 2025 patches and some Steam Deck reviewers flipped to recommended.

#### Worst Things

- Not a true continuation of ML1 for many fans: dungeon crawling replaced by a node-based island map, reducing exploration and the layered crafting/loot gameplay.
- Loot comes mostly from chests rather than monster drops, and items do not stack, which makes gathering feel like a chore.
- Perk/boon interactions are shallow compared to Hades, so builds feel less varied and runs become samey.
- Boss and miniboss design criticized: long invulnerable phases on bosses, overly predictable miniboss patterns, and damage sponginess on high difficulty.
- Combat clarity issues: camera loses enemies behind objects, ranged enemies fire from offscreen without tells, charged shots cannot be interrupted, and input priority feels inconsistent.
- Stability and polish: crashes when continuing a run, soft-locks, infinite-fall animations, NPC dialogue getting stuck, audio distortion in tech areas.
- Weapon balance: many weapons (for example fists and gun variants) seen as weak, and upgraded weapons can carry negative traits, so players keep the starter gear.
- Some see the shop layer as simplified versus ML1 (armor, elemental weapon effects and crafting reduced) and the story as average.

#### Improvements

- Stagger or interrupt options for charging ranged enemies, telegraphs for projectiles, and better camera behavior.
- Rebalance weapons and the late-game economy so upgrade costs do not balloon and spending choices feel meaningful.
- More depth in perk synergies and more run variety, such as Hades-style optional difficulty modifiers.
- Shop customization: layout freedom and customer-flow mechanics, since the shop reads as static to some.
- Steam Deck/handheld optimization and shorter load times.
- Save system that lets players resume mid-day instead of having quitting skip ahead in the day/night cycle.

#### Unique Things

- Fuses a shopkeeper sim (price-setting, customer reactions) directly with an action-roguelite, in the tradition of Recettear but in 3D.
- Backpack-management puzzle whose item placement produces synergies and affects profit, blending Backpack Battles-style inventory play with roguelite relics.
- Push-your-luck extraction mechanic where the value of hauled loot is at stake when deciding whether to continue a run.
- Move from 2D pixel art to a stylized 3D look with Hades/Wind Waker-like influences, per Wikipedia's development section.

#### Tedious Or Disliked

- Late-game grind: upgrade and shop costs scale steeply, with one 150-hour reviewer describing repeated boss farming for upgrade resources.
- Non-stacking, chest-sourced loot makes collection feel like busywork.
- Dungeons and runs start feeling samey and repetitive over a long playtime.
- Too many competing money sinks (weapons, health, shop, town, relics) make progress feel slow.
- Bosses with long invulnerability windows make fights a waiting game.
- Quitting mid-run skips forward in the day cycle, punishing short play sessions.

#### Divisive Points

- Hades-like influence: some call it a watered-down or poorly done Hades hybrid, while others say it stands as its own game and that the Hades complaints are unfair.
- The 3D art style: one camp misses the pixel art and charm, while another finds the new style cute and cohesive.
- Shop management: some find it deeper and more rewarding, others find it repetitive or simplified compared with ML1.
- Difficulty and combat feel: fluid and fun for some, annoying or unfair on higher difficulties for others.
