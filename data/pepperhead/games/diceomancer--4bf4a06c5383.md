##### Praise

- Unique hand-drawn art with animation on most cards, not lazy hand-drawing
- Immersive first-person view where you see your hands using the cards
- Core mechanic of rolling a d6 to replace any number on screen, including enemy health, gives huge creative possibilities
- Enemies having multiple health bars keeps the number-changing mechanic from being cheesed
- Replayability from combining classes/decks at the start, plus many events and enemies
- Cards are unique and complex, rewarding players who take time to understand the possibilities
- Something he has not seen done before, both mechanically and visually

##### Criticism

- Fairly complex, not recommended as a first deck-building game
- Some balance issues are inherent to the design
- Once you understand the mechanic you can exploit it pretty well
- Looks simple visually but many cards get quite complicated

##### Unique Points

- Ability to turn any on-screen number (UI or otherwise) into a d6 roll
- Multiple enemy health bars as the balancing answer to that mechanic
- Combining classes/colored decks from the start for customization

### Community

- **One Liner:** A hand-drawn roguelike deckbuilder from Ultra Piggy Studio where you can replace almost any number on screen (enemy HP, energy, card values, gold, relic charges) with a dice roll, built around a five-colour mana system.
- **Consensus Summary:** DICEOMANCER is an Overwhelmingly Positive Steam hit (about 96% of roughly 5,600 reviews, released Oct 9-10 2024 at $14.99). It reportedly sold 50,000 copies in two weeks, and estimates put lifetime ownership at 100k-200k. Players love the change-any-number gimmick, the class variety and the storybook art, and often pitch it to Slay the Spire and Balatro fans. #### Best Things

- The change-any-number dice mechanic is seen as genuinely novel, and players enjoy gradually discovering how many numbers it can touch (enemy health, energy, gold, shop prices, event checks, relic charges)
- It lets you break the game in creative ways, for example a burn-everything deck or turning shop items into near-free purchases, which players say produces memorable stories
- Distinct classes with their own gimmicks and playstyles, and a large pool of cards (about 500) with unusual keywords like Load, Summon, Construct, Phantom and Flow
- The five-colour mana system, where you discard, exhaust and manipulate cards to pay for powerful ones, adds a Magic-like layer of decisions
- Hand-drawn storybook art with 8fps animation, a humorous tone (the goal is simply to go fishing) and a well-liked soundtrack
- Corruption/purple mist on the map pressures you to limit dice use while allowing backtracking, which is unusual for the genre
- Strong value for players who like Slay the Spire, with some reviewers saying it surpasses it in places; 20-30 hours of fun is a common estimate, and some players log 70-200+ hours

#### Worst Things

- Too easy: overtuned cards, easy energy and draw, and stockpilable dice charges let experienced players delete bosses, sometimes on turn 1
- Balance is uneven between classes (Builder and Berserker are named as strong; red classes can feel samey), and every class tends to have a run-breaking card or two
- Limited enemy variety; outside a handful of bosses, enemies barely matter and you can ignore their intents
- Little or no post-launch support; multiple reviews from 2025-2026 say the developers disappeared and did not expand a great concept
- Content can feel thin for the price; one reviewer beat it in about 6 hours, and another said to wait for a sale
- Complexity and keyword overload make the early hours confusing for some, with the demo cited as confusing
- Some players say the dice gimmick is over-hyped in trailers because in most cases you only use it for things like cutting huge boss HP to a small number
- A few reviewers find early enemies scaling too harshly or off-colour mana generation frustrating, which is the opposite complaint to the too-easy camp

#### Improvements

- Add new content (enemies, cards, events, upgrades); veteran players with 130+ hours specifically ask for new enemies
- Add harder late-game difficulty: a fourth act, harsher ascension scaling and more meaningful modifiers
- Share ascension progress across characters instead of making players climb 20 ascension levels separately for each of the six classes
- Give a reason to linger in corruption or explore the map, such as rewards or new cards for taking risks
- Put some limit or cost on stockpiling dice charges and on altering non-card numbers so the core mechanic stays tense
- Improve enemy variety and make enemy intents matter more, and tune class balance

#### Unique Things

- You can swap almost any on-screen number for a die roll, including meta-genre numbers like cards per turn, reward choices and exhaust, which reviewers describe as commentary on deckbuilder tropes
- Origin as the GMTK 2022 game jam entry Dice is the Way, which placed in the top 20 of more than 6,000 entries
- Five-colour mana with a purple dice/chaos colour and cards that double as resources
- Enemies with multiple health bars and a spreading purple fog that punishes dice use, balancing the power of the mechanic
- Backtracking on the map, which most roguelike deckbuilders do not allow
- Whimsical fishing-trip premise in a world with swords, magic and guns, plus a fully hand-drawn art style

#### Tedious Or Disliked

- The 20-level ascension ladder has to be repeated for each character, which reviewers call artificial padding when runs are all similarly easy
- Runs can feel samey for classes that want a tiny deck, because you skip most card rewards
- Enemies repeat often and feel largely irrelevant outside a few bosses, so runs play out as executing the combo you built
- Some players dislike the dice mechanic as too game-defining or feel it trivialises the game, and a minority complain that dice feel rigged or rolls look seeded after restarting (an anecdotal claim in one review)
- Keyword overload and complex cards make the early hours hard to parse (even the demo is cited as confusing)

#### Divisive Points

- Difficulty: a large group finds it far too easy, while newcomers and a few reviewers find it hard or scaling harshly
- Dice mechanic: hailed as the most creative deckbuilder idea in years by many, but a few call it a cheat button or a gimmick that wears off after a few runs
- Complexity: some find the mana and keyword system clever depth, others find it muddled and hard to understand
- Value: players with 20-200 hours call it a bargain, while a few say it is too dry on content for $15
