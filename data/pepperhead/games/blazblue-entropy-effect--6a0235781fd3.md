##### Praise

- Fighting-game-like action: each character has a drastically different move set with input-timed combos
- Roughly 20+ bosses, each designed uniquely with different mechanics; bosses are where the game shines
- Strong story focus with many cutscenes and NPCs, giving an RPG-like feel
- Stunning visuals, great VFX and sound effects, character voice lines
- Epic anime-style final boss
- Many characters, each with two to three viable builds, giving a good amount of runs

##### Criticism

- Repetitive once you have played each character a couple of times; not much new per run
- Elemental/proc abilities are always the same and not very interesting
- Flashy visuals make attacks hard to read, including the final boss, and he lost runs to it
- Inconsistent telegraph colors, e.g. the lone enemy firing yellow projectiles that always hits him
- Few unique events or levels, and rooms of fighting enemies take a while
- Voice lines have no cooldown so jump-spamming makes characters shout repeatedly

##### Unique Points

- Pushes roguelite action toward a fighting-game style with per-character move sets and combos
- Story progression gated behind talking to particular NPCs, RPG-style
- Design lesson: elaborate visuals can undermine readability compared to simple pixel art telegraphs

- Character/move set diversity: drastically different characters with combos drive variety

### Community

- **One Liner:** A 2D action roguelite by 91Act (published with Astrolabe Games) that turns BlazBlue fighting-game characters and moves into a fast combo-driven run-based brawler, with solo and co-op play.
- **Consensus Summary:** Players and critics agree the combat is the draw: fluid, combo-heavy, fighting-game-flavoured action with a large roster and many build paths. Steam sits at Very Positive (94.3% of about 36.7k reviews), Metacritic has 83 from 6 critics and OpenCritic 84 from 15 reviews. Weak spots are a thin, generic story and setting, repetitive rooms and enemies, a cluttered neon look, mediocre music and a BlazBlue brand that feels tacked on. The game passed through early access and has been updated often (new characters, a Dead Cells collab with The Prisoner, the larger 'X' story content that also shipped on consoles). Recent Steam reviews are noticeably worse than lifetime ones because of post-update stuttering, crashes and co-op desyncs, and the later release revamps split early-access fans.

Launch reviews were strong, with critics praising combat and docking story and BlazBlue integration. #### Best Things

- Combat feels fast, snappy and deep, with ground combos, launches, air follow-ups and cancels that reward fighting-game-style execution
- Large roster of characters with distinct playstyles, adapted from their BlazBlue fighting-game moves (including guest characters such as The Prisoner from Dead Cells)
- Strong build variety: tactics (elemental and status upgrades), legacies and unlockable move branches let a run change how a character plays mid-run
- Approachable for people who are bad at fighting games, yet with a high skill ceiling for mastering characters
- Scalable difficulty via the Entropy/Extreme settings, where harder modes also let you get stronger and bosses change behaviour
- Solo, local co-op and online co-op are all supported
- Developers keep supporting it with content and updates; many reviewers call it great value, and the standard roster is considered enough without paid characters
- Bosses and set-piece fights are often cited as a highlight

#### Worst Things

- Story and setting are generic sci-fi and hard to follow; many reviewers say it feels disconnected from the BlazBlue universe
- Stages and rooms repeat a lot, with the same bosses and stage layouts run after run, so variety wears thin after the early hours
- Enemy designs and animations are plain compared with the detailed playable characters; heavy neon glow and screen clutter hurt visual clarity
- Soundtrack is called only okay and repetitive, not matching BlazBlue's reputation for music
- Performance and netcode complaints: stuttering, crashes and multiplayer desyncs, worse after recent updates; one review also blames the dev-authoritative netcode for input delay with distant partners
- Quick boss kills or very high damage in co-op can cause desyncs or softlocks that wipe a run
- Mixed comments on the hub: slow to navigate and heavy on dialogue/quests for a fast game
- Systems and skill descriptions are dense and not well explained, so players turn to guides
- Some players find the combat and content too simple or limited: it is fun but wears thin after a few hours, with little reason to continue once the story is beaten

#### Improvements

- Fix performance, stuttering, crashes and co-op desync/softlock bugs introduced or exposed by recent patches
- Add more BlazBlue-specific content, story integration and lore so the license feels like more than a skin
- Open up the new X story content earlier on PC instead of locking it behind finishing the original story
- Improve enemy variety and level design, and reduce room and boss repetition across runs
- Better explain progression, evolutions and legacies in-game and improve visual clarity of effects and boss attacks
- Shorten or vary the start of each run and streamline the hub

#### Unique Things

- Fighting-game move lists translated into a roguelite, so characters unlock and expand their own movesets during a run rather than only stacking stat upgrades
- Combo-count and cancel-based play that feels closer to character-action or a 2D brawler than a typical dodge-and-shoot roguelike
- Entropy system that lets you raise difficulty in exchange for stronger buffs, with bosses changing behaviour at higher levels
- Characters from other franchises (for example The Prisoner from Dead Cells) implemented as fully playable roguelite characters
- Support for local and online co-op in a fighting-game-derived action roguelite
- Layered build system of elemental tactics, move-expanding potentials/legacies and evolutions, supporting dozens of distinct builds

#### Tedious Or Disliked

- Early progression leans on grinding and unlocking; some players dislike having characters stripped of their signature fighting-game moves until they get them by RNG within a run, with nothing carrying over
- Runs feel long and similar at the start, and some find them easy until a late boss spikes the difficulty
- Astral Finish (a boss instant-kill type move that bypasses revives unless perfectly dodged) is called unfun by at least one 300-hour player
- Dialogue, quest and hub overhead between runs can interrupt players who just want to keep running
- New story (X) content locked behind finishing the original story, which can take new players 20+ hours
- Waiting for online co-op partners in your region can take a long time, according to one heavy player

#### Divisive Points

- Difficulty: some find it too easy because recovery frames were added and enemies are not threatening, while others praise Extreme 100 with max mutations and complain about a few unbalanced late bosses
- The 1.0 and X revamps: early-access fans say removing or reworking systems (corruption, mind upgrades, arena, a story subplot) made combat feel slower and more RNG-driven, while newer players praise the clearer new story
- BlazBlue link: fans of the franchise split on whether it is a great action roguelite with BlazBlue paint or a missed chance at a real BlazBlue experience
- Complexity: some love the depth of systems and skill trees, others feel overwhelmed by the number of attacks and mechanics
