##### Praise

- Monster-taming gimmick is unique within roguelikes
- Large world that expands as you explore, with a campaign-like feel
- Thrill of discovering far-off locations and bosses
- Cute art style with its own identity
- Unique passives found during runs are cool

##### Criticism

- Gets repetitive very quickly
- Every monster has four abilities, many very similar, so it is overwhelming and hard to keep track of as you swap monsters
- Same passives show up fairly regularly
- New dungeons feel much the same after about 10 hours; travel between locations is repetitive with tile types
- Sidekick character has an annoying voice (but it does not affect rating)

##### Unique Points

- Taming monsters as a core roguelike mechanic, each with its own abilities
- Campaign-style world expansion layered on runs

##### Design Criteria

- Diversity/variety: monster abilities too similar, dungeons feel the same
- Uniqueness: taming gimmick and world exploration stand out
- Charm/visuals: cute, distinctive art style
- Complexity/readability: four abilities per monster overwhelms
- Pacing: fast-paced, repetitive traversal and tile types
- Progression/discovery: expanding world and bosses to discover
- Passives: cool but repeated too often

#### Best Things

- Lasso-and-ride mechanic: taming any monster and using it as a mount with its own attacks and traversal ability (leap, glide, surf, tunnel, websling) is the signature hook and widely loved
- Charming hand-drawn art, goofy monsters and pun-heavy writing; several players said the art put them off at first, then became part of the charm
- Very smooth, responsive twin-stick controls; one reviewer called them among the smoothest they have played
- Deep customisation: ammo fruits and charge shots, badges/perks, pets, mounts, biomes and a customisable home base give lots of build variety
- Metroidvania-style exploration with pins, shortcuts and gadgets gives persistent progression on top of the roguelite runs; players report a shift from 'fooling around' to strategising runs
- Local co-op is praised by one reviewer who 100%'d it with a partner as one of the best co-op games out there (local only; others wanted online co-op)
- Low-commitment 'one more run' loop with lots of content: reviewers describe getting sucked in for hours and a real sense of progression

#### Worst Things

- Repetition: several negative reviewers say they had seen nearly everything after roughly 5-7 hours; enemies and bosses behave similarly across the map
- The map is huge and fixed (not randomly generated), so crossing it repeatedly is slow; one reviewer estimated about 20 minutes just to reach a far goal
- Enemy damage ramps up over time, forcing restarts roughly an hour into a run and making players re-tread the same ground
- Enemies are very tanky so encounters drag, and some players feel rewards (treasure perks, unlocks) are underwhelming relative to the effort
- Fast travel only works on tiles visited in the current run
- Mounts and bullet types feel too similar to some players, and the gun itself never changes, only its effects
- Co-op is local only; a few players wanted online co-op and did not find a way to play remotely (Remote Play Together is the only listed workaround)
- A minority report technical problems: startup crashes, frame drops, sticky movement (Steam community thread titles and one negative review)

#### Improvements

- Shrink or restructure the map, or make shortcuts and fast travel much stronger so backtracking is less of a slog
- Add variety to enemy behaviour and boss patterns so monsters across regions feel different
- Rebalance enemy health and damage scaling so runs are not forced to reset and fights are quicker
- Let players bring more than 3 pets per run and carry spare pins/decorations between runs, to reduce completionist grind
- Add real online co-op (players asked for it, and local-only is repeatedly called out)
- Improve the late-game reward quality and story payoff; one player found the story fine but unsatisfying

#### Unique Things

- Monster-taming in a bullet hell: every enemy you can capture becomes a playable mount with its own moveset, rather than picking from a fixed character roster
- Fusion of genres: twin-stick shooter, Pokemon-style creature collecting, roguelite runs and metroidvania map progression in one package
- Ammo crafting by blending fruits/plants into different bullet effects, with charge shots per ammo type
- A large hand-made (not procedurally generated) labyrinth map with shortcuts that persist between runs
- A home-base island with pets, decorations and customisation, plus a post-game challenge mode on hardest difficulty

#### Tedious Or Disliked

- Long, interrupted walks across a very large map, especially after dying or after the damage scaling forces a restart
- Completionist chasing: missed pins and decorations are hard to track down, decorations require clearing 4 tiles each, and some do not spawn in a given run
- Only 3 pets can be brought back per run, which a reviewer computed as about 20 runs to collect them all
- Needing both sticks constantly; players on keyboard and mouse find it awkward and it has a learning curve
- Bosses and some enemies create very dense bullet patterns; a few reviewers describe being killed in 5-6 hits even with maxed stats

#### Divisive Points

- Difficulty: some call it surprisingly hard and brutal, others say the casual mode, bullet cancelling and assist options make it friendly to casuals as well as hardcore players
- Length and map size: seen as content-rich and engrossing by many, but as bloated and padded by the negative reviewers
- Repetitiveness: critics and players split on whether the creature variety carries the loop or whether 'once you learn one monster you know them all'
- Value: several positive reviewers bought it on deep sale ($3) and say it would be worth full price, while one negative reviewer felt it was not worth $15
