He calls it extremely punishing, possibly one of the hardest roguelikes ever, with a wand-building spell system that produces wild and often lethal interactions. ##### Praise

- Fully simulated particles make fire, water and other materials behave in distinctive, realistic ways
- Enormous number of secrets and things to discover, with a very committed community
- Wand crafting where spells interact in unexpected, creative ways
- Difficulty is deliberate and part of the design
- Permadeath feels less painful than in Darkest Dungeon since you never get attached to a character
- Fans love it intensely

##### Criticism

- Extremely punishing and brutal, which limits who it suits
- Experimenting with an untested spell combo can kill you instantly, even 30 minutes into a run
- Demands a big time commitment, so he won't recommend it to many people
- The simulation concept alone could not carry a whole game (implied need for other layers)

##### Unique Points

- Every particle in the world is simulated, enabling emergent physics interactions
- Around a hundred secrets intended to be nearly undiscoverable, taking years to understand
- Dying to your own spell experiments is an accepted, expected part of the loop

##### Comparisons

- Darkest Dungeon

##### Design Criteria

- Uniqueness of core gimmick: particle simulation is distinctive
- Depth and secrets: huge discoverability and community mystery
- Difficulty: brutal by design
- Synergies: spell and wand combinations interact in crazy ways
- Risk vs reward: experimentation can instantly kill you
- Accessibility/commitment: not for casual players, playtime ranges from 45 minutes to 2000 hours

#### Best Things

- Fully simulated pixel physics: every pixel of sand, water, lava, acid, gas and fire reacts, so the world can be burned, flooded, melted or blown apart
- Wand and spell crafting is hugely deep, with a very high skill ceiling; players describe building absurdly overpowered wands, black holes and infinite-heal setups
- Enormous depth of secrets, alchemy, lore, puzzles and hidden content that the community is still uncovering years later (one reviewer with 1,000+ hours says mysteries remain)
- Emergent, funny and spectacular deaths and moments; the chaos is often described as a large part of the fun
- Huge replay value and the sense that you yourself improve since there is no persistent character power progression
- Distinctive pixel art and particle effects that make the physics visually satisfying
- Strong modding scene and Steam Workshop support extending the game
- Rewarding 'breaking the game' fantasy: late runs let you become a near-unkillable god after struggling early

#### Worst Things

- Extremely punishing, often feels unfair: instant deaths from off-screen fast projectiles, single pixels of lava/acid/fire, polymorph, or accidentally hurting yourself with your own wand
- Very little is explained; much of the knowledge needed to play well is hidden or requires a wiki, leading many to say it needs 50-500 hours before you feel any agency
- High variance and randomness: several critics and reviewers say outcomes depend heavily on lucky early spells and wands rather than pure skill
- Long, slow early game that you must repeat after every death, with enormous levels to crawl through for gold
- Secrets and quests are cryptic (ARG/cryptography-style) and often require tedious trial and error or outside resources
- Inventory is small relative to the number of spell options, making optimal experimentation feel like a walking and swapping chore

#### Improvements

- Better onboarding and in-game explanation of wand building, materials and core systems so players are not forced to use a wiki
- A minimap or better navigation and possibly some fast-travel or shortcut to cut down on re-traversing huge levels
- Less punishing instant-death sources or clearer telegraphing of off-screen and one-pixel hazards
- Ways to skip or vary the early game and first-biome loop after repeated deaths
- More inventory or quality-of-life for swapping wands and spells while experimenting

#### Unique Things

- Every pixel is an individual physically simulated material, unlike tile-based roguelikes
- Wands are built from modular spells with modifiers, triggers and multicasts, closer to programming than picking items
- A hidden alchemy system and layered secrets, including multiverse and 'new game plus' style content beyond the obvious ending
- No persistent character upgrades; progression is entirely player knowledge plus what you find in a run
- Made by veteran indie devs (Petri Purho of Crayon Physics Deluxe, Olli Harjola of The Swapper, Arvi Teikari/Hempuli of Baba Is You), and praised as a technical showcase (GDC Best Technology nominee)
- Fully destructible world where digging, flooding and explosions are core strategies, not just scenery

#### Tedious Or Disliked

- Repeating the first mines area hundreds of times; several reviewers spent most of their time in it and found it dull after many runs
- Crawling through huge levels clearing enemies for gold, with hours of tedious re-exploration after a cheap death
- Secrets hunting and cryptic puzzles that feel like wasted time without a guide
- The first boss ('tutorial boss') taking players dozens or even hundreds of runs, which some find off-putting
- Walking back and forth to swap wand and spell loadouts because of the small inventory
- Feeling underpowered even while dealing large damage, due to fragile health and one-shot hazards

#### Divisive Points

- Difficulty and opacity: fans call it resilience-building and rewarding, detractors call it vindictive and time-wasting ('the community has Stockholm Syndrome' is a representative complaint)
- Luck versus skill: long-time players say skill and knowledge dominate and luck is minor, while critics and some players say early runs depend on random lucky drops
- Hidden knowledge as a feature: many see the wiki and community-solved mysteries as part of the magic, others see it as a design failure
- Critic reception: GameGrin gave 95 and Attack of the Fanboy 90, while GameCritics gave 45 and DarkZero 60, showing a wide critic split
