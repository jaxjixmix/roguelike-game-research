### Community

- **One Liner:** A gun-themed bullet-hell dungeon crawler from Dodge Roll (published by Devolver Digital, 2016) in which quirky Gungeoneers dodge-roll through procedurally generated chambers to reach the gun that can kill the past.
- **Consensus Summary:** Enter the Gungeon is widely regarded as a genre standard-bearer for action roguelikes. It holds an Overwhelmingly Positive Steam rating (about 95% of roughly 90k reviews) and Metacritic critic scores in the low-to-mid 80s (PC 84, PS4 82, Xbox One 85, Switch 87), with a more lukewarm Metacritic user score of 7.7. Players and critics praise the weapon variety, tight dodge-roll combat, pixel art, theming and humour, and the huge depth of secrets. Complaints centre on its punishing difficulty, heavy RNG, unlock-only (no permanent stat) progression and weak-gun clutter. It is not early access; the last major content patch (A Farewell to Arms, April 2019) ended development, although it reached mobile in 2025 and Steam reviews show players still active in 2026. The sequel Enter the Gungeon 2 has since been announced/released on Steam.
- **Reception Notes:** Launch week sold over 200,000 copies (about 75% on Steam), 800,000+ by January 2017 and 3+ million across platforms by January 2020 (Wikipedia). SteamSpy lists 2-5 million Steam owners, and steam-revenue-calculator estimates roughly $67.6M gross and $20M net on Steam (Boxleiter-method estimate, not audited). Free content updates followed: Supply Drop (Jan 2017), Advanced Gungeons &amp; Draguns (Jul 2018) and A Farewell to Arms (Apr 2019), after which development ended; some recent Steam reviewers note no updates since 2019. Critics at launch compared it favourably with The Binding of Isaac and Nuclear Throne, and PC Gamer called the bosses among the hardest things the reviewer had met. Nintendo Switch is the best-rated version on Metacritic (87); console reviewers noted controller aiming is less precise than mouse. A mobile release arrived in August 2025 and a 3D sequel, Enter the Gungeon 2, followed. Steam lists Windows and Linux, no native Mac. The 10th anniversary in April 2026 prompted renewed positive reviews.

#### Best Things

- Huge, whimsical roster of guns and items packed with pop-culture references, and a consistent gun-pun theme (Chambers, Gundead, Blanks, Ammonomicon)
- Tight, responsive dodge-roll and bullet-hell combat that rewards skill over stacking overpowered builds
- Polished pixel art, music and charm; reviewers say the love for gaming culture is obvious
- Enormous endgame of secrets: hidden floors, secret bosses, secret characters and quests that keep players returning for hundreds of hours
- Fair-feeling difficulty curve in the eyes of fans: deaths feel earned and improvement is tangible
- Local co-op, pause-and-save, free DLC updates and a very low price for the content
- Bosses are memorable and distinct, with no-hit bonuses that reward mastery

#### Worst Things

- Progression is almost entirely item unlocks; you always restart with the same stats, so meta-progression feels thin compared with Hades-style rogue-lites
- Runs are heavily RNG-dependent; a run can hinge on getting a decent boss-killing gun and a second room-clearing gun
- Many guns and items are considered weak or useless, and unlocked items still have to drop by chance
- Limited variety in room layouts and enemy encounters; players who master it say rooms become memorisable and content after beating the Lich is sparse
- Characters often feel similar, with some commonly described as near re-skins apart from special ones such as the Bullet and Robot
- Normal rooms feel like a long runback to the boss fights; any damage taken in a boss fight (no-hit bonus) can feel demoralizing, and some reviewers call normal fights spammy or filler
- Cryptic item descriptions and steep learning curve for newcomers
- Some players report the camera/screen motion causes dizziness, high GPU usage, and an occasional co-op soft-lock with no settings fix

#### Improvements

- Add a way to ban or filter weak guns/items from the loot pool
- More meaningful permanent progression or build-shaping choices at chests and shops
- Online co-op (only local co-op exists), ideally with separate screens
- More variety in room layouts, enemies and late-game challenge content
- Camera and accessibility options (one reviewer reports motion-sickness dizziness and no fix in settings) and GPU usage tuning
- More clarity on what unlocks and items do before selecting them

#### Unique Things

- Fully committed gun theme: floors, enemies, currency-like items and UI are all bullet or gun puns
- Dodge roll with invincibility frames in a bullet-hell context, which divides hardcore shmup fans
- Reward system for no-damage boss fights (extra heart containers/currency) that pushes skill play
- Unusually large secret ecosystem: Rat fight, secret floors, secret NPC quests, alternate chambers, extra characters
- Weapons with bizarre mechanics and references (for example a T-shirt cannon), with guns treated as characters
- No permanent stat upgrades; progression is sideways, via unlocks, so skill remains the main variable

#### Tedious Or Disliked

- Grinding for the last unlockable characters and 100% achievements, including NPC quest chains that some find irritating (e.g. Frifle's quests, finding the Lost Adventurer)
- Slow, sideways meta progression that never makes you stronger
- Thieving rat stealing saved ammo, plus key and chest shortages frustrating runs
- Runs ruined by ammo starvation or only having the starter pistol at the first boss
- Long repetitive room-clearing stretches to reach the short, high-stakes boss fights

#### Divisive Points

- Difficulty: fans see it as fair and skill-based; others find it too punishing and unrewarding for those who struggle
- RNG: some praise the huge synergy space, others say gear luck decides too much of each run
- Dodge roll: loved for accessibility of movement, criticised by some bullet-hell purists as undercutting the genre
- Content depth: fans cite hundreds of hours of secrets, while others say there is little post-game challenge and little run-to-run variety
