### Community

- **One Liner:** A satirical action roguelite from Aggro Crab (published by Team17) where an unpaid intern fights through dungeons built from failed tech startups, using office junk as breakable weapons.
- **Consensus Summary:** Players rate it Very Positive on Steam (about 90% of roughly 4,175 reviews) and critics were mostly favourable (Metacritic PC 78 from 11 reviews). Almost everyone agrees the art direction, writing and corporate-satire theming are the main draw. The action roguelite gameplay is the weak point: many reviews call the combat sluggish, shallow or janky, and the content thin. Commercially it underperformed: Wikipedia says Aggro Crab cancelled a planned extra dungeon because of poor base game sales. It is a complete, 2020-released game with a post-launch 'Working from Home' update, it is frequently discounted to around $2, and a recommend-on-sale verdict is common even among lukewarm reviewers.
- **Reception Notes:** Launched September 24, 2020 on PC, Switch, PS4 and Xbox One, with physical editions for Switch and PS4 from Limited Run Games. Critics were mostly favourable, with platform Metacritic scores ranging 73 to 81, praising the satire and weapon variety, while a minority such as TheGamer (2.5/5) found the combat repetitive versus Dead Cells or Hades. Post-launch the 'Working from Home' update added weapons and a hard mode, but a planned additional dungeon was cancelled because of weak base sales. #### Best Things

- Sharp, funny satire of startup and gig-economy culture; several reviewers say it has aged well and will trigger flashbacks for anyone who worked at a startup
- Distinctive art direction that turns corporate clip-art/Memphis-style design into something charming rather than soulless, plus a solid soundtrack
- Charming, archetypal cast and witty dialogue; talking to coworkers between runs is a highlight
- Everything-is-a-weapon system: you pick up desks, office supplies and more to swing, throw or shoot, with a varied weapon pool
- Generous accessibility and difficulty options: an Assist mode and difficulty changes at any time without disabling achievements
- Choose-your-starting-item mechanic avoids runs ruined by a bad first pickup
- Good length that does not overstay its welcome (roughly 10 hours for a casual clear) and runs well, including on Steam Deck
- Boss fights and enemy/dungeon theming (three themed dungeons) praised as creative

#### Worst Things

- Combat described as floaty, sluggish and clunky: commitment to attack animations, slow dodges, imprecise hits, and enemies able to stun-lock you
- Cluttered, cramped rooms and exploding traps can make damage feel unfair rather than hard, with off-screen enemies hard to spot
- Thin content: only three dungeons, and the second half replays them in a hard mode, so many feel they saw everything in the first section
- Weapon durability means weapons break every few fights, which some find tedious rather than fun, and shields on many enemies make this worse late game
- Humour leans on dated memes and references for some reviewers, and characters lack depth
- Final boss and late game spikes are called out as frustrating or unfair
- Keyboard support is poor: controls cannot be rebound, and the weapon-switch keys are awkward; it plays best on controller
- Progression and unlocks feel unimpactful; runs feel samey and skill descriptions are unclear
- Sluggish, poorly optimized menus; one top negative review also says known bugs were never patched

#### Improvements

- Add rebindable controls and better keyboard/mouse defaults, including an option to stop auto-pickup on dodge
- Speed up the post-run flow: multiple menus and loads before you can restart a run are a frequent complaint
- Tune combat: more responsive attacks and dodges, clearer enemy telegraphs, less unavoidable off-screen damage
- More content and variety: a fourth or fifth dungeon (the cancelled extra dungeon is the obvious gap) and less copy-paste in the hard-mode half
- Clearer perk and skill descriptions and more meaningful unlocks
- Camera improvements, or a locked isometric-style view, so furniture and walls do not hide threats

#### Unique Things

- Dungeons are the ruins of failed tech startups, each themed on a parody of a startup archetype
- Breakable office-supply and furniture weapons you can also throw, instead of a fixed weapon loadout
- Mentor and coworker bonding system that unlocks abilities, tied into the narrative
- Corporate clip-art aesthetic with a mix of 2D and 3D art and gibberish-style voice work
- Narrative-first roguelite where the satire drives the structure, and the ending is widely praised by fans
- Assist and difficulty toggles that can be changed mid-game without penalty

#### Tedious Or Disliked

- Long menu and loading sequence between death and the next run
- Constant weapon breakage and needing to spend scarce cash on food and healing in shops
- Repetition: hard mode repeats the same three dungeons, and runs feel similar
- Grinding for unlocks, with many skills described as pointless or unclear, which some players only finish by beating the game before unlocking everything
- Dodge and stun-lock frustration when crowds of enemies or explosions pile on in tight rooms
- Slow animations combined with imprecise weapons make fights feel like work

#### Divisive Points

- The humour and writing: many find it the best part, a minority find it dated, meme-heavy or unfunny
- Combat feel and difficulty: some call it light and accessible, others call it janky or unfair, with Assist mode the usual recommended fix
- Whether the game is a worthwhile roguelite or mostly a narrative/satire experience: several positive reviewers say it is for people who want the theme, not the mechanics
- Art style: praised as distinctive, but a few find it fuzzy or hard to read
