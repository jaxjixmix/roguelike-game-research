#### Entry 1

- **Part:** 3
- **Label:** Tier list Part 3 (2025)
- **Summary:** Darkest Dungeon 2 is the sequel to the gothic turn-based party roguelike, and it drops the hardcore persistent-roster mechanic in favor of a more traditional run-by-run structure with meta progression. He feels the game was unfairly judged against the first one, since the combat is still solid and the content is deep. The carriage-based travel is more minimalistic than dungeon crawling, and the UI and tutorial are clunky. He thinks that judged on its own it is a really good roguelike and gives it a very solid A tier.
- **Quote:** it's a really good game
- **Rerank Note:**

##### Praise

- Combat gameplay is essentially the same solid core as the first game, with new abilities and enemies
- Animations are some of the best he has seen in any roguelike, with 2D sprites attacking in 3D environments
- Visual style is distinct and immersive, improved over the first game, and the music is still fantastic
- Lots of unique events and content, and each run feels different depending on what you find
- More lore per character, with quests that unlock abilities via little puzzle minigames
- A relationship system between characters adds depth
- Large amount of meta progression to unlock

##### Criticism

- Removing the hardcore mechanic makes boss deaths feel less stressful and intense than in the first game
- Carriage travel is minimalistic and gives less sense of control than dungeon crawling, and can feel wonky
- More complicated than the first game, with a very long tutorial and a slow learning curve
- UI control is awkward, such as escape not closing windows and multiple stacked windows
- Single shared currency for unlocking new items and for power upgrades forces a choice between more variety and stronger characters
- Characters are harder to get attached to because they are not persistent

##### Unique Points

- Carriage on a road replaces dungeon crawling, letting you pick direction and encounters
- Character relationship system that changes based on what happens, which he says could have suited the first game
- Character-specific story quests with small puzzle minigames that permanently unlock abilities
- Design lesson: ditching a beloved signature mechanic can hurt reception even when the game is good

- Darkest Dungeon

##### Design Criteria

- Risk vs reward and stakes: loss of the hardcore permadeath mechanic lowers tension
- Meta progression: lots to unlock, but a single shared currency creates tension with power upgrades
- Animation and visuals: standout smooth animation and distinct art style
- Audio: music remains fantastic
- Content and variety: many events, characters and differing runs
- Complexity and UI/tutorial: heavy mechanics, long tutorial, awkward UI controls
- Exploration design: carriage travel offers less control than dungeon crawling
- Characters and lore: more story per character but less attachment

### Community

- **One Liner:** A turn-based gothic-horror roguelike from Red Hook Studios in which a party of flawed heroes rides a stagecoach across a ruined land in long, permadeath-driven expeditions to stop a cosmic apocalypse.
- **Consensus Summary:** Critics received it well (Metacritic 81, OpenCritic 81, 'Strong' tier), praising the combat puzzle, art and atmosphere, while players are more split (Steam 'Mostly Positive', about 75% of roughly 27k reviews; Metacritic user score 6.3/10). Much of the player backlash comes from the shift from the first game's hamlet-and-roster management to a roguelike with long expeditions and meta-progression, which many series fans did not want. It launched in Early Access on Epic in October 2021, hit 1.0 on Steam in May 2023, and has since received major updates, including the free Kingdoms mode and the Inhuman Bondage DLC in January 2025, plus console releases. Some recent Steam reviews (Dec 2025) still argue it feels unpolished, with classes and systems still being reworked.
- **Reception Notes:** Early Access began in October 2021 on the Epic Games Store; 1.0 launched on Steam on May 8, 2023 with strong critic scores but a notably mixed player response driven by comparisons to the first game and accusations of review bombing versus real design disappointment. Since then: the Binding Blade DLC, then in January 2025 the free Kingdoms mode (with the Hunger of the Beast Clan module) and the Inhuman Bondage DLC with The Abomination hero, which reached PS5 and other consoles later in 2025. Available on PC, Mac, Switch, PS4/PS5 and Xbox. Third-party estimates (steam-revenue-calculator, Boxleiter method, not audited) put Steam gross revenue near $41M, with SteamSpy owners at 1-2 million. #### Best Things

- Combat plays like a tactical puzzle: party composition, positioning and the token system reward planning and cut some of the first game's pure RNG
- Gothic/Lovecraftian art direction, improved 3D-style animation and atmosphere are praised as top tier
- Narrator (Wayne June) and atmosphere carry the dread and tone, though some players find the soundtrack and narration less memorable than the first game
- Hero backstories and the affinity/relationship system between party members add personality and strategic complexity
- Varied regions and hazards make each expedition feel distinct and tense
- Runs are more compartmentalised and experimental than the first game's endless roster grind, so some find it less of a slog
- High-stakes, white-knuckle difficulty feels cathartic and rewarding once it clicks
- Post-launch support has been substantial: new heroes, Kingdoms mode, balance reworks and console ports
- Fresh take on the DD formula: some players prefer it to a near-copy of DD1 and like its more synergy-focused party building and avoidance of DD1's snowballing party-wipe problem

#### Worst Things

- Too different from Darkest Dungeon 1 for many fans: no hamlet, no persistent loot, and characters reset after each run
- Poor onboarding: mechanics are opaque, tutorials thin, and success can depend on wiki knowledge
- Early runs lean heavily on RNG, with long 2-3 hour Confessions expeditions that can be lost to bad luck or a single mistake
- Meta-progression unlocks (stagecoach upgrades, candles for items, trinkets and inn items) are slow and feel like a prerequisite grind
- Some boss gimmicks feel like instant-fail encounters without prior knowledge, for example the Confession 5 mirror mechanic when the copy is stuck in an unreachable position
- Stuns need two hits, healing is conditional and on cooldown, and reliable damage/stress mitigation was removed, so incoming damage feels overwhelming and difficulty feels 'fake' or RNG-driven
- Random quirk replacement and the lack of a separate fixed character for re-specs annoy players
- Combat math and buffs feel inconsistent (e.g. a 50% damage-reduction buff cutting far less than half), undermining trust in planning

#### Improvements

- Better tutorials and in-game explanation of systems so the wiki is not required
- More shortcuts, checkpoints or ways to soften the loss of a failed 2-3 hour run
- Faster, less grindy meta-progression and unlock pacing
- Replace quirks in a predictable order (oldest first) rather than randomly
- Fix frustrating boss gimmicks, especially Confession 5 mirroring and unreachable copies
- Further polish and balance of classes and mechanics that are still being reworked

#### Unique Things

- Sequel that turns a dungeon-crawl roster game into a roguelike road trip with a stagecoach as a mobile base
- Mental-health stress/meltdown system where heroes can crack and turn on the party
- Affinity and relationship system between heroes that affects combat and story
- Two modes: long Confessions expeditions and the free Kingdoms campaign-style mode
- Token-based combat and positioning puzzle layered over the original's turn-based formula
- Lovecraftian tone with a narrator voice as a signature feature

#### Tedious Or Disliked

- Unlock grind: reviewers report roughly 20 hours to gather stagecoach upgrades, then many more hours for other unlocks, before later bosses feel viable
- Long runs of 2-3 hours with no checkpoints make failures costly
- Grinding candles and stagecoach upgrades to reach a viable power level for later bosses
- Slow stagecoach travel and repetitive battles, though the developers reduced battle counts from Early Access
- Constant reliance on outside guides because of unclear mechanics

#### Divisive Points

- Whether it should be judged as a sequel to DD1 or as its own roguelike; reviewers who do the latter tend to rate it higher
- Paid DLC heroes are seen as greedy by some and as fair value by others
- Difficulty and RNG: hardcore roguelike fans find it rewarding, while DD1 loyalists find it unfun and punishing
- Roguelike structure itself: some see it as hiding a lack of content behind a linear grind, others prefer it to DD1's roster grind
