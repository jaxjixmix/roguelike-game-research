- **Rerank Note:**

##### Praise

- Huge roster of unlockable minions that each play drastically differently, giving strong replayability
- Every run feels different because of unit variety
- Big-band jazz music is a standout, and characters' idle animations feel like dancing to it
- 2D sprites in a 3D environment look unique and fitting
- Higher difficulty on replayed worlds introduces new mechanics rather than just bigger numbers
- Fast team-building mode with limited moves or time is a fun, low-commitment extra that still unlocks things
- Elemental traits and stat upgrades on units meaningfully change how they play

##### Criticism

- Strictly one-action-at-a-time turns make the game very slow, especially on big boards
- Endgame situations can turn into back-and-forth shuffling with a single enemy
- Boss fights can be trivial with the right team, or stall for several turns with nothing to do
- Cannot choose which units from the roster are present in a fight or where they spawn; it seems random to him
- Roguelike elements and run events (shops, events, side quests) are not very deep

##### Unique Points

- Chess-like movement and attack patterns turned into many distinct minion designs, with the player acting as a king-like piece
- Higher-difficulty replays add new mechanics rather than stat inflation
- Timed/move-limited team-building mode as an alternative to full runs

- Pacing/turn structure: one action per turn feels slow on large boards
- Player control: cannot choose fielded units or spawn positions
- Roguelike depth: elemental traits and stat upgrades exist but stay shallow

#### Best Things

- Charming, silly tone and writing, with a fever-dream wizard theme
- Unexpectedly jazzy, lighthearted soundtrack that reviewers singled out
- Large roster of 50+ distinct units with their own stats and abilities, so encounters feel varied
- Distinct, readable pixel units and an easy-to-control camera
- Procedurally generated rooms, enemies and rewards vary each run; runs are fairly short (~30 min sessions per the dev's itch.io page)
- Recruit/upgrade/sacrifice/trade loop lets players experiment with odd combinations
- Steam Deck and full controller support, Steam Cloud and achievements

#### Worst Things

- Much information is hidden: many piece rules and permanent buffs can't be seen until accepted, and temporary buffs/debuffs are not clearly shown
- Balance: stacking every buff on one or two mobile units creates an unstoppable mega-unit, making strategy shallow
- Runs can start in apparent no-win situations because you don't choose which units enter a round and can be handed weak units (e.g. 0-attack chickens)
- Reports of frequent bugs and crashes, including one just before a first final-boss win
- UI is clunky; the in-game selector lags behind the mouse cursor, so clicks can land on the wrong square with no misclick protection
- Content feels thin to some (one reviewer noted only 3 bosses)
- Rewards cannot be rejected, so weaker forced pieces enter the army
- Reviewers say it is not meaningfully chess, so the title feels misleading

#### Improvements

- Let players reject or choose rewards (a Steam thread asks this directly)
- Show all rules, buffs and temporary effects in tooltips, and keep a visible log of permanent buffs
- Cap or rebalance stacking onto a single unit
- Add mid-run save and exit (requested in reviews and a Steam thread)
- Add misclick protection and fix cursor/selector lag; fix bugs and crashes
- Improve the tutorial and make it skippable; clarify vague achievement descriptions; add more bosses; Linux support was also requested

#### Unique Things

- Chess-flavored grid tactics (moving orthogonally/diagonally) but with bizarre wizard units rather than standard pieces
- Cards/units are bought and recruited like a deckbuilder, yet play is pure tactics, not a card game
- Unit sacrifice and trading as a core progression mechanic
- Three stats (ATK, DEF, SKL) with 50+ unit classes and elemental interactions
- Whimsical shamanic-vision framing and a light jazzy score, unlike most grim roguelikes
- Arcade (quick run) and Story (exploration) modes

#### Tedious Or Disliked

- Writing buffs down externally because the game doesn't track them
- Required tutorial parts that players say many never finish (one reviewer cited low tutorial completion stats)
- Early encounters some feel unwinnable, making runs feel unfair and swingy
- Being unable to save and exit mid-run, and the diagnostics prompt reappearing on every return to the title screen
- Too many units in Arcade mode was raised as a forum complaint

#### Divisive Points

- Whether the name and chess framing is misleading clickbait or just loose inspiration for a fun tactics game
- Whether the chaos and randomness is a feature (experimentation, unpredictability) or simply bad balance and hidden info
- Difficulty: some find it frustrating and unfair, others rate it around 4/5 and 'feels good' even without liking strategy games
