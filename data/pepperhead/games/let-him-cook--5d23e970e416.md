### Commentary


#### Entry 1

- **Part:** 3
- **Label:** Tier list Part 3 (2025)
- **Summary:** Let Him Cook is a food-themed horde-survival game in early access that uses waves and a between-wave shop rather than level-up upgrades. You carry two weapons and hire assistants who hold weapons and bring their own mechanics. He loves the jazzy music, comedic enemy designs, charm and exceptionally clear UI. He is bothered by balance problems, boss rules that nullify some builds, and a food-family mechanic that does not really change how he builds. He slots it currently in lower B with room to climb as it develops.
- **Quote:** a ridiculous HordeLike with great music, a great theme, and solid gameplay
- **Rerank Note:** He played it before and notes it has improved since, especially accessibility options; he places it currently and says it could climb in tier as early access progresses.

##### Praise

- Wave-and-shop structure with two weapon slots plus hired assistants that carry weapons and create synergies
- Music is super jazzy, and parts of gameplay sync to the beat, such as the game slowing just before the beat drops
- Strong charm and vibe: hilarious food-themed enemy designs and comedic theme
- Food-type mechanic where weapons and assistants counter or synergise with specific food families
- Fantastic UI: fusions are automatic and it clearly tells you about owned weapons, synergies and what to buy
- Lots of content for a small early-access indie and a good amount of accessibility options, such as adjustable outline strokes on enemies, bullets and player
- Bosses are really cool

##### Criticism

- Game feels somewhat unbalanced, with some mechanics broken and others too weak
- Bosses differ so much from normal waves (no life steal or health regen) that some playstyles are nullified, making those stats pointless to pursue
- Food-family counter mechanic is a nice concept but he will not rebuild around the next wave's food type, so it has little effect on decisions
- Visuals can be overwhelming and hard to read with the semi-top-down camera and bullets, though accessibility options help

##### Unique Points

- Hiring assistants that carry weapons and have their own mechanics adds a distinct synergy layer to a horde game
- Gameplay tied to the music, with the game slowing on the beat drop that players can exploit
- Food-themed damage types (vegetable, dairy, meat) with weapon/assistant synergies, though the boss preview pushes you toward one family

##### Comparisons

- Other horde-like games (e.g. Arcane-type horde games from earlier in the video)

##### Design Criteria

- Core mechanics: simple move, auto-shoot and dash, judged as accessible and not overly complex
- Progression structure: wave and shop system instead of in-run levelling
- Synergies: weapon, assistant and food-family bonuses
- Boss design: bosses diverge from normal runs, invalidating some builds
- Balance: some options broken, others weak
- Charm, theme and visuals: funny food enemies and comedic vibe, but visual clutter
- Audio: jazzy music integrated into gameplay
- UI and information clarity: very clear explanations of synergies and fusions
- Accessibility: outline and stroke options
- Content and polish for an early-access indie: plenty of content, potential to grow

#### Best Things

- Enemy variety: one reviewer says enemies differ enough to keep each run fresh, unlike many survivor-likes
- Weapons feel distinct, with many combinations possible (store page lists around 25 weapons)
- Bosses feel like real bosses and are described as challenging
- Music is a core mechanic (music-synced maps, per the store page and studio site)
- Food-industry theme with live food puns and cartoony hand-drawn 2.5D art gives it personality
- Class variety (store page says 10+ playable characters; the studio site lists Executive Chef, Sous Chef, Bartender, Butcher)
- Cheap price ($6.99 USD, roughly $5 in regional pricing) and a free demo
- Described by one player as a mix of Vampire Survivors and Brotato, with an endless mode and an achievement encyclopedia

#### Worst Things

- Seemingly stalled development: a negative Steam review cites no updates in over a year and calls it abandoned
- A Steam forum thread titled 'Abandoned Game?' (March 2025) shows the same concern
- Bug reports on the Steam forum: character unlock problems, a 'Chicken Fingers' character bug, controller menu navigation, trouble starting a run
- Windows only (no Mac or Linux)
- Very small player base, so little community content, guides or discussion

#### Improvements

- Ship the promised full release with the planned ~4x content (more bosses, characters, enemies, weapons)
- Communicate a clear roadmap and update cadence (the studio site only points to Discord for news)
- Fix unlock, character and controller menu bugs reported on the forum
- Improve language and localization options
- Address early-game balance and luck dependence raised in a forum thread ('Early Game Woes and Luck')

#### Unique Things

- Kitchen and restaurant theme: hero classes like Executive Chef, Sous Chef, Bartender and Butcher, with weapons such as chef's knife, peeler, salt rifle and cleaver
- Hiring assistants as part of the run
- Music-synced maps where the soundtrack drives the gameplay
- Food-pun enemies and a humorous tone
- Hand-drawn cartoony 2.5D presentation for a survivor-like

#### Tedious Or Disliked

- Waiting on updates: the stalled Early Access status is the main complaint
- Early-game difficulty and dependence on luck, per a forum thread title (details not retrievable)
- Unlocking characters is reportedly buggy, which makes progression frustrating for some

#### Divisive Points

- Whether the game is abandoned (forum and negative review) or still in active development (store page plans a late 2026 to early 2027 full release)
- Whether it is 'better than most Vampire Survivors-likes' (one positive reviewer) or simply another entry in a crowded genre (no critic coverage to confirm either view)
