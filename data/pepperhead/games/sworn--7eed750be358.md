### Commentary


#### Entry 1

- **Part:** 4
- **Label:** Tier list Part 4 (2025)
- **Summary:** Sworn is a multiplayer-focused Hades-like that openly mimics Hades in music, animation style, gods and boss structure. He finds it a well-made near-spin-off, with heavy roguelite progression and lots of pre-run customization. He was a bit disappointed that the full release added little over early access, and he notes lingering bugs. Despite being not very unique, he respects the effort required to make a Hades-like and places it in a solid A tier.
- **Quote:** It's just Hades. It doesn't even try to hide it.
- **Rerank Note:** Was covered in early access previously; after full release his thoughts have not changed much and he places it in A tier.

##### Praise

- Huge pre-run customization: four characters, four weapons each, four spells each, with flippable variations
- Distinct meta-progression with multiple skill trees (support, god, defense, offense), some flippable into two versions
- Weapons, spells and boons feel good and support many different build paths
- Final boss and true final boss are really well designed
- Art style is fairly different from Hades and the music is good
- Executes the Hades formula very well, feeling like a spin-off; added voice acting at full release

##### Criticism

- Full release added little over early access, leaving him a bit disappointed
- Bosses are always the same; he hoped for variations
- Heavy meta-progression: weapons can be terrible without upgrade-branch investment, which he wished was toned down
- Still has bugs, including boons whose numbers do not change and a stuck on-screen text visual bug
- Story and characters engage him less than Hades; not very unique overall

##### Unique Points

- Multiplayer-focused Hades-like, which Hades itself lacks (he only played solo)
- Loadout diversity is intended so players in multiplayer do not all share the same setup
- Doing a good Hades-like is much harder than a survivor-like, which earns it respect despite heavy copying

##### Comparisons

- Hades
- Survivor-likes

##### Design Criteria

- Customization/variety: character, weapon and spell combinations before a run
- Meta-progression: multiple skill trees, but weapon-upgrade gating is too heavy
- Boss design: strong final bosses but repetitive regular bosses
- Game feel: weapons, spells and boons feel good
- Build diversity: many paths to different builds
- Originality: heavy derivation from Hades limits uniqueness
- Polish: remaining bugs and non-working boons
- Art and audio: distinct art style, good music
- Story: added voice acting but less engaging than Hades

### Community

- **One Liner:** A 1-4 player co-op, Hades-style isometric action roguelite by Windwalk Games (published by Team17) in which you play a Soulforged champion retaking a corrupted Camelot from Arthur and the Knights of the Round Table.
- **Consensus Summary:** SWORN is received as a competent, fun but heavily derivative 'Hades with co-op': Steam sits at Very Positive (about 80% of roughly 5,200 reviews) while Metacritic's three critic reviews land between 73 and 85 and its user score is a Mixed 7.2. Players most often say it is worth buying on sale, mainly with friends, and that solo it feels like a slower, less polished, less charming clone. The biggest recurring sore spot is multiplayer stability: disconnects are common and there is no way to rejoin or save a run, which drives most of the negative Steam reviews. It left Early Access (started Feb 2025) with a 1.0 launch on Sep 23, 2025; recent Steam reviews were noticeably worse than all-time at one point (Mixed, about 64%), with some players fearing slowed support and missing content, though later reviews mention new content updates.

#### Best Things

- Four-player co-op is the main selling point; many reviewers say it is the best part and the reason to pick this over Hades itself
- Build variety from four characters, each with four weapons and four spells, plus 200+ blessings from eight fae lords, yielding very different builds
- Solid, responsive moment-to-moment combat with both controller and keyboard and mouse
- Boss fights are frequently singled out as a highlight (King Arthur especially), with alternative bosses per act and a boss rush mode
- Strong Mignola-inspired hand-drawn art style and Arthurian setting
- Good progression feel: every run earns resources and there are many things to unlock, giving a lot of hours of play (many reviewers at 40-100+ hours)
- Challenge events and optional modifiers break up the room-clearing loop, and higher difficulties offer more variety

#### Worst Things

- Multiplayer disconnects are frequent and a dropped player (or host) cannot rejoin, so the whole run is lost; this is the single most common complaint in negative Steam reviews
- No mid-run save and quit, so runs of 15-45+ minutes cannot be paused, and a crash loses everything
- Seen as a near 1:1 Hades copy (room layouts, boon structure, duo boons, pets, hub) that lacks Hades' writing, voice acting, music and personality
- Regular enemies and levels feel repetitive and copy-pasted, with few biomes and limited enemy variety
- Slower, slightly clunky combat pacing (dodge cooldown, attack recovery, floaty movement) that stops players reaching a flow state; hits can feel cheap
- Balance problems: some blessings and weapons are far stronger than others (Gogmagog repeatedly cited), while melee versus ranged balance and some weapons feel underwhelming
- Story is thin and social/narrative content is sparse
- Rough UX details: awkward controller menu navigation, small interaction ranges at the blacksmith, player collision in the hub, barebones options menu, hard-to-see cursor
- Crashes and forced restarts during co-op sessions (some players report the game taking down their PC), adding to the lost-run frustration

#### Improvements

- Add rejoin-after-disconnect and more stable netcode/servers for co-op, and possibly host migration
- Add save-and-quit mid-run for solo play
- Unlock boss rush from the start and add a skip option for boss intro sequences
- More content: additional biomes, bosses, routes, weapons, abilities and regular enemy types, plus clearer roadmap communication
- Rebalance blessings, weapons and higher-difficulty enemy health so builds beyond the top few are viable and fights are not damage-sponge slogs
- More impactful upgrades and meta-progression (many are flat percentage bonuses) and better late-game rewards once currencies are capped
- Crossplay, better in-game communication in co-op (only emotes and a few phrases), and more UI and controller polish

#### Unique Things

- Four-player online co-op in a Hades-like structure, which the original Hades lacks
- Arthurian legend setting with a corrupted Camelot, Merlin's Soulforged champions, and fae lords as the blessing-givers
- Class system: four distinct characters each with their own weapons and spells, rather than one protagonist with a weapon roster
- Hand-drawn isometric art inspired by Mike Mignola
- Boss rush mode and alternative bosses per act, plus a secret late area built around a curse mechanic

#### Tedious Or Disliked

- Higher difficulties: enemies become damage sponges and you must grind meta-progression before you can realistically progress
- Early-game upgrades feel negligible until stacked, and weapon upgrade materials such as Holy Grail Water are scarce
- Meta upgrades are described as boring flat bonuses and some players run out of meaningful currency sinks around the 15 hour mark
- Boss rush is buried behind much of the game (roughly 15-30 minutes of a run to reach) and boss intro cutscenes cannot be skipped
- The secret final area and the penultimate chapter were called unfun or pointless by some players because of the curse mechanic and wave-style combat
- Losing long co-op runs to disconnects makes hour-long hard-difficulty runs feel risky

#### Divisive Points

- Hades comparison: some love having more Hades-style gameplay with co-op and call it a great clone, others dislike the derivative feel and lack of its own identity
- Combat feel: some call it responsive, snappy and deep, others find it slower and clunky than Hades
- Solo worth: several reviewers say it is only good with friends, while others with 50-100+ hours played it mostly solo and recommend it
- Multiplayer reliability: some report 20+ hours of co-op with almost no issues and call the netcode robust after Early Access fixes, while many others report disconnects in most runs
