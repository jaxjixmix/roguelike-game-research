#### Entry 1

- **Summary:** Ember Knights is a basic, traditional top-down action roguelike dungeon crawler that does not try any unique mechanic but executes the fundamentals very well. He loves its visuals, distinct worlds, bosses with difficulty-scaled forms, and long meta-progression. His main gripes are disconnected spells and overpowered crit builds at high difficulty. He sees it as a very solid Hades-like and places it in A tier.
- **Quote:** if you liked Hades odds are you will like Ember Knights
- **Rerank Note:**

##### Praise

- Great visuals and art style, with each world looking completely different, giving a strong sense of adventure
- Cool bosses; higher difficulties give bosses entirely new move sets and forms (e.g. the first-world spider has three forms)
- Meta-progression is substantial, requiring many roguelite elements to reach the first boss and ten more levels of difficulty to reach the true final boss
- Weapons have unlockable forms and play fairly differently from each other
- Satisfying, fun fights and gameplay without needing gimmicks
- Probably fun in multiplayer

##### Criticism

- Spells feel disconnected from the rest of the game and are not impactful
- Crit builds are overpowered and practically required at the highest difficulties, a balance issue
- He could be nearly invincible against the final boss on top difficulty because the game is easy to break
- Can get chaotic on higher difficulties with fast enemy move sets bundled together

##### Unique Points

- Deliberately does nothing unique mechanically; shows action roguelikes can succeed on satisfying fights alone
- Boss difficulty scaling swaps in different move sets and forms rather than just raising stats
- Each world has a very distinct visual identity, which he says many top games struggle with

##### Comparisons

- Hades

##### Design Criteria

- Visual identity: distinct worlds and art style aid immersion
- Boss design: difficulty-scaled move sets and forms
- Meta-progression: long gated path to true final boss
- Weapon diversity: multiple forms with differing playstyles
- Build balance: spells weak versus overpowered crit builds
- Combat feel: satisfying fights over unique mechanics
- Difficulty: hard, with chaos at higher levels

### Community

- **One Liner:** A Hades-inspired top-down action roguelite by Doom Turtle (published by Twin Sails Interactive) built around 1-4 player local/online co-op, relics, weapon mods and skills, fighting the sorcerer Praxis across planets.
- **Consensus Summary:** Ember Knights is well liked by players: Steam shows Very Positive overall (about 94% of roughly 7,000 reviews positive in the local snapshot), and is most often recommended as a polished, snappy co-op roguelite with lots of build variety and an endless supply of relic synergies. Critical coverage is thin: Metacritic has only one critic review (God is a Geek, 85) so no Metascore exists yet, and the user score is 7.5 from a handful of ratings. The recurring caveat is value and depth: several reviewers call it solid but not standout, more a discount or co-op pick than a Hades replacement, and some say it gets repetitive or grindy. It left Early Access (April 2022) in July 2023, later got free modes (Daily Dungeon, Boss Rush, Chaos Run, pets) and a paid DLC, Wrath of the Architect. #### Best Things

- Co-op is the headline: 1-4 players, local split-screen and online, described as easy to access and not drastically raising difficulty
- Fluid, responsive controls and snappy combat; a large build space (7 weapons, 9 mods per weapon, dozens of skills, 100+ relics per the store page) with synergies that can get gloriously broken
- Cohesive, cute pixel-art visuals and charming touches (pet companions, dialogue, secrets, weapon skins from trials)
- Good performance and polish for most players; described as optimized with no crashes or slowdowns
- Plenty of post-clear content: Endless mode, Boss Rush, Daily Dungeon, Chaos Run, trials and the DLC, which adds biomes, weapons and relics
- Reads as Hades-like without being a clone; often recommended to Hades fans and for playing with kids or family as a gentle intro to build-crafting
- Meaningful two-currency meta-progression (ability/weapon upgrades plus permanent health and damage buffs)

#### Worst Things

- Multiplayer loot flow: rewards are picked one player at a time and stat gems drop in a shared pile, which some find a slow, excruciating time waster and poor design
- Unskippable cutscenes and boss intros/outros, plus invulnerable boss phases that force waiting, are repeatedly called out as killing the pace
- Enemy design: many enemies have ranged or dash attacks from across the screen, which makes ranged weapons much stronger than melee and makes charge attacks impractical
- Balance and depth complaints: weapons unbalanced, build diversity felt lacking by some, and the DLC final boss described as easy or a joke
- Technical problems for some: freezing or stuttering, high CPU use in menus, launch hangs, and it fails to run under Proton for at least one reviewer
- Online lobby friction: hard to rejoin after a crash, no Steam direct join, no visible lobby ID, plus multiplayer lag and lobby-start failures discussed in the Steam forum
- Value: multiple reviewers say it should cost about $10 rather than $19.99, and that the DLC feels like a reskin of the main campaign

#### Improvements

- Let co-op players pick rewards simultaneously and offer personal loot or a shared-out option instead of a shared pile
- Add quality-of-life options: skippable or shorter cutscenes, pause or stop the speedrun timer out of combat, and show the time goal for the time-limit capsule
- Add more skills (one reviewer suggests about 45 versus the roughly 32 they counted) and more build variety or balance between weapons
- Improve online connectivity tools: direct Steam join, visible lobby ID, reliable rejoin after a crash
- Consider checkpoints or shorter runs, since replaying the same worlds is boring for some
- Make unlocks require using items or skills in runs (Binding of Isaac style) rather than unlocking nearly everything in about 10 hours; clearer indicators for attack-recharged abilities near the character

#### Unique Things

- First-class 1-4 player co-op, including couch split-screen, in a genre that is mostly solo
- Abilities recharge by landing attacks rather than on pure cooldowns
- Weapon modification system (9 mods per weapon) layered on top of relics and skills
- Hades-style structure combined with a planet-hopping sci-fantasy setting and cute pixel look
- Game modes beyond the main run: Daily Dungeon, Boss Rush, Endless and Chaos Run, plus pet companions you can pet
- Time-limit challenges per planet and tiered trials that unlock cosmetic weapon skins

#### Tedious Or Disliked

- Long, unskippable cutscenes and boss invulnerability phases in every run
- Solo players report a grind-heavy progression; the Daily Dungeon pays far more resources per room than regular runs, pushing players to repeat it
- Repeating the same worlds with no checkpoints makes runs feel samey after a while; several reviewers say it wears out its welcome or gets boring quickly
- Taking turns at the end-of-stage loot pick in co-op, and loot piling up on top of each other or under the HUD
- Screen clutter from enemies and effects makes combat hard to read; you can usually only land a light attack or two before needing to dodge

#### Divisive Points

- Multiplayer: praised by most as the best reason to play, yet a vocal minority calls it one of the worst co-op roguelites because of loot handling
- Solo play: some say it is amazing alone, others say it is not built for single player and is grindy
- Hades comparison: a strength for fans who want more, a turn-off for others who say just buy Hades
- Price and value: many call it a great deal or worth it with DLC, others say it is only worth it on sale
