#### Best Things

- Every turn plays like a compact tactical puzzle because you choose which dice to reroll and how to assign results to heroes, so luck feels manageable rather than arbitrary
- Class identity is built directly into each hero's die faces, which makes the 100+ heroes feel mechanically distinct
- Enormous build and replay variety: hundreds of items, many monsters and modes, and randomly generated heroes, items and spells still producing new combos after hundreds of hours
- Surprisingly strong balance and non-obvious decisions (junk tier-0 items having synergies, skipping level-ups, committing to one combo unit)
- Undo/take-back lets you experiment with lines and treat fights as puzzles
- Short runs suit both desktop and phone; many describe it as the ultimate time-killer on a bus or break
- Modding via the built-in TextMod system and level editor is praised by some as the easiest custom-content sharing they have seen, extending replayability indefinitely
- Distinct minimalist pixel art and music; good value at about $9 and a no-AI-assets, no-sales-or-bundles stance from the dev

#### Worst Things

- High RNG variance: a run lost to bad rolls, especially at the final boss, can feel demoralizing and some reviewers dismiss it as RNG slop
- Unknown bosses and the final boss create a risk-management problem, since you cannot prepare for something you have not seen
- Item and ability descriptions are described as poorly worded and obtuse, and a few items are reported as non-functional
- Balance is uneven: some classes or heroes are called useless or a burden while others dominate
- Permadeath after a single lost fight means a long run can end abruptly
- Presentation is deliberately small, so players wanting big visuals, story or narrative adventure will find it thin
- Some find the core loop simply not fun or hard to get into if they dislike adapting to imperfect rolls

#### Improvements

- Rewrite item and spell descriptions so effects are clear and accurate
- Give players some way to know or preview the possible final boss and unknown bosses so they can plan
- Rebalance weak classes/heroes and fix non-functional items
- Make mod installation less convoluted; a negative reviewer objected to mods being gated behind progression and imported as pasted text
- Offer a built-in unlock-everything option for players who do not want to grind unlocks (requested in a review)

#### Unique Things

- Heroes are literally dice: each class's kit is encoded in the faces of its die, with 3D dice physics and selective rerolls
- Lose one fight and the run ends, yet every fight is solvable-feeling because of undo and careful dice assignment
- Ultra-minimalist design (no XP system, small numbers) that reviewers compare to chance-operations art and to Balatro and Vampire Survivors in spirit
- Native TextMod API and level editor let the community build total conversions, including a Slay the Spire-style mod
- Huge content library from a single developer working about seven years: 128 hero classes, 73 monsters and 473 items per the Steam page, plus difficulty modifiers and Nightmare mode
- Same game across Steam, itch.io, Android and iOS, with cross-platform fans buying it multiple times

#### Tedious Or Disliked

- Losing at the final boss after a long run, especially with no foreknowledge of what it will be
- Unlock progression: players who want to try mods or all content have to progress through the game first
- Decoding cryptic item text and learning which weird items actually work
- Modding workflow based on copying and pasting text files, which one refunder called the worst modding experience they had seen
- Dice-builders in general can feel unrewarding: a low-playtime reviewer simply could not see how the loop is meant to be fun

#### Divisive Points

- Randomness: fans see tense, skill-expressive dice management, while detractors see an RNG fest where risk management cannot save a run
- Text-based modding: praised as intuitive and powerful by modders, but criticised as clunky and gated by a newcomer
- Permadeath on a single loss: adds tension for some and demoralises others, especially at the end of a run
- Minimal presentation: charming and focused to most, too small for players who want spectacle or story
