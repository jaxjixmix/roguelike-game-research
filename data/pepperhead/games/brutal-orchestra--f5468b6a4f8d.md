##### Praise

- Extremely distinct art style, music and disturbing overall vibe
- Very unique gameplay built on colored pigment mana and overflow damage
- Strong emphasis on positioning for both attacks and evading, with depth despite simple abilities
- Great character variety, with characters that play drastically differently
- New enemies, biomes and bosses with fresh mechanics keep appearing and are a blast
- Dark, insulting story and plenty of content

##### Criticism

- Roguelike elements are quite minor, so runs get repetitive
- Character abilities are fixed per run and can be upgraded but not changed; items are limited (about one per character) and often uninteresting
- Protagonist must always be on the team, limiting team freedom
- Unlocks of new characters and side quests come too late; meta-progression pacing is slow
- Random enemy attack directions feel out of place in a planning-heavy game
- Few rewards beyond limited money; full party conflicts with keeping open space to evade

##### Unique Points

- Pigment/mana system where overflow causes damage, with very few comparable mechanics elsewhere
- Tension between a full team and open tiles for dodging forces a meaningful choice
- Enemy attacks telegraph direction so encounters can be planned like a puzzle

##### Comparisons

- Pikmin (loosely, re the colored pigments)

##### Design Criteria

- Gameplay uniqueness: novel pigment and positioning mechanics weighed heavily
- Visuals and audio/vibe: distinct art, music and atmosphere are a major plus
- Roguelike depth: minimal run-to-run variation in abilities and items counts against it
- Character variety: very different characters, but the team is largely fixed once chosen
- Meta-progression pacing: slow unlock of characters and content is a flaw
- RNG: random enemy moves undermine the strategic planning
- Content amount: boss, biome and enemy variety supports its rating
- Risk vs reward in team composition: full team versus evasion space

#### Best Things

- Striking, grotesque art direction drawn from Hieronymus Bosch, with unusual enemy and companion designs
- Music and atmosphere: reviewers repeatedly call the soundtrack and per-enemy battle themes a highlight
- Pigment system: damaging enemies yields pigment matching their health-bar colour, which you spend on abilities, creating a dynamic puzzle of who to hit and in what order
- Positioning and enemy mechanics give turn-based fights a tactical, puzzle-like feel; many say each enemy and boss has its own mechanics
- Dark, bittersweet humour and a personal-feeling story; several reviewers call the narrative moving
- The full game opens up after the first winning run, with extra enemies, bosses, companions, difficulty and story that pull players to keep playing
- Large unlock pool (items, fools, achievements) that encourages replay without feeling like a chore, according to some reviewers
- Active Steam Workshop modding scene (40+ mods catalogued on the community wiki, including character, enemy and item packs) extends playtime for veterans

#### Worst Things

- Heavy RNG: random starting party members, coin-flip movement shifts on hit or attack, a roughly 40% chance for extra pigment, and random instant-kill moves on some enemies
- Pigment lockouts: if enemies do not supply the colour your party needs, you can be stuck with bad or forced abilities, or overflow pigment and take damage
- Characters have very few moves (two or three, sometimes randomly assigned) and one item slot, so builds feel limited and upgrades feel small
- Many items, especially before unlocks, are described as weak or useless, and some party members as nearly dead weight
- Only three zones and a limited enemy pool, so the same content repeats quickly (a Metacritic user notes seeing every enemy and boss by about the fifth run)
- Many enemy abilities and status effects are hard to keep track of; some players resort to guides or other players to learn the rules
- Some find the UX poor, and one Steam reviewer notes the tutorial could not be saved mid-way and lacks Steam Cloud saves

#### Improvements

- Reduce or give players more control over RNG (party selection, random skill assignment, coin-flip movement)
- Give characters more moves and more meaningful equipment or build customization so pigment restrictions feel less punishing
- More zones, routes and variety, with some fans explicitly asking for a larger sequel with more than three routes
- Add Steam Cloud saves (the store page lists none) and let the tutorial be saved or resumed mid-way
- Make normal fights less trivial on hard mode and strengthen meta-progression, since some players feel their team does not grow across runs
- Clearer onboarding and in-game explanation of statuses and enemy abilities, since many reviewers say it is confusing until it clicks

#### Unique Things

- Pigment economy where hits generate colour you then spend on abilities, tying positioning, targeting and ability choice together
- Bosch-inspired Purgatory setting with grotesque, humorous characters and a story that unlocks more fully after the first winning run
- Party of oddball companions ('fools') with very few but distinctive abilities, instead of a large skill tree
- Boss and enemy fights with bespoke mechanics and theme songs, described as puzzle-like
- Strong mod support through Steam Workshop and a dedicated modding API and wiki

#### Tedious Or Disliked

- Combat can be slow and methodical because losing HP is costly, so players feel they must be careful in every encounter
- Being 'pigment-screwed' (missing or overflowing colours) is compared by reviewers to mana screw in Magic, which makes some fights feel low-agency
- Hard-mode bosses are criticized as unforgiving, with healing transformations, repeated spawns and one-shot moves that can drag fights out (one review cites about 25 minutes on a single boss)
- Needing to read and memorise many enemy abilities and passives before each fight overwhelms impatient players
- Repeated runs through the same three zones feel repetitive to some, with less novelty after the first clear

#### Divisive Points

- RNG: critics call it excessive and unfair while other reviewers argue the complaint is overstated and that knowledge and planning dominate outcomes
- Pigment mechanic: loved by many as the heart of the game, called annoying or agency-removing by others
- Whether it is truly a roguelike: one reviewer argues it is not a roguelike at all, and another says it should not have been one, while many others name it a favourite of the genre
- Story after the first win: praised by some, but one reviewer felt it fades after the first successful run on each difficulty
