### Community

- **One Liner:** A gothic, Lovecraft-flavoured turn-based party RPG by Red Hook Studios where you run a roster of flawed heroes through procedurally assembled dungeons, managing both health and mental stress, with permanent death and a hamlet hub that carries progress between expeditions.
- **Consensus Summary:** Darkest Dungeon is widely regarded as a modern classic of the genre: about 91.7% of roughly 163k Steam reviews are positive (Very Positive), Metacritic sits at 84 and OpenCritic at 84 with about 93% of critics recommending it. Critics and players praise the art, narrator, stress system and tense, consequential party tactics. The recurring complaint is that the late game turns into a grind, compounded by swingy RNG (back-to-back crits, surprise ambushes) that can wipe out heroes you spent hours levelling. It was in Steam Early Access from Jan 2015 and launched fully on 19 Jan 2016, and it is still being updated: a new DLC, The Fire's Edge, appears in current Steam data. Sequel Darkest Dungeon II (Steam appid 1940340) is a different, more run-based roguelite structure.
- **Reception Notes:** Early Access launched Jan 2015 after a Kickstarter that raised over $313k; the early period drew complaints that it was too difficult, too random, and unfair, and streamer feedback shaped balance before the Jan 19, 2016 release. Sales reportedly hit 650k in the first week, 1M by Nov 2016 and 2M by Dec 2017 (Wikipedia); estimators put Steam owners at 2 to 5 million and gross revenue around $23.9M (Steam-revenue-calculator estimate, not audited). Ports followed for PS4/Vita, Switch, Xbox One, iOS and Linux; Metacritic platform scores range 80 to 85. DLC: The Crimson Court (2017), The Color of Madness (2018), The Butcher's Circus (2020), and The Fire's Edge (newly listed in Steam data). Some critics felt the ending stumbled by lengthening the game with tougher endgame limits, and the Radiant difficulty and mods are common recommendations to ease the grind.

#### Best Things

- Distinctive gothic storybook art direction and Lovecraftian atmosphere that critics and players call hauntingly beautiful
- Wayne June's narrator performance and the audio/music, repeatedly singled out as elevating every expedition
- The stress/affliction system: mental health as a second resource that creates emergent story moments and meaningful risk
- Tactical turn-based combat built around rank positioning, with deep hero class variety, quirks, and team synergies
- High stakes and permadeath make every decision, victory, and loss feel weighty; 'easy to learn, hard to master' as one long-time player puts it
- Hamlet hub progression gives persistence even when heroes die, so failure still moves the meta forward
- Rewarding loot and trinket hunting, lots of bosses and dungeon variety, plus a large modding scene
- Strong content additions over time (The Crimson Court, The Color of Madness, The Butcher's Circus PvP arena)

#### Worst Things

- Late-game grind: players feel they must repeatedly farm low-level dungeons to replace fallen heroes before attempting the Darkest Dungeon itself
- RNG swinginess: chains of enemy crits, missed high-accuracy attacks, and turn-order variance can undo 20 hours of progress
- Hero level restrictions that bar a stronger hero from easier quests, which forces awkward roster juggling and farming
- Quests are slow (20 to 60 minutes) with slow animations and back-and-forth map movement, so the game can feel like it does not respect player time
- Knowledge-check design: you often learn a boss or curio mechanic only by dying to it, so many players lean on wikis and guides
- Heavy economy drain from stress relief, disease cures, quirk removal, provisions and expensive final gear upgrades (for example thousands of gold per top-tier weapon, 100+ deeds mentioned by one forum poster)
- Some reviewers found the stress acting-out and Crimson Curse systems annoying, and a critic cited rough mouse controls and a thin tutorial
- Game does not respect player time: reviewers note most players never field a full roster of max-level heroes, and losing a few heroes means regrinding many low-level dungeons

#### Improvements

- Give end-game experience a better path, such as resolve-level XP or easier ways to level heroes, so replacing losses is less of a grind
- Relax or remove the hero-too-strong-for-quest restriction so players can farm efficiently
- Tune the three-tier structure to feel less padded (one forum suggestion: two tiers plus a max-level run at the Darkest Dungeon)
- Reduce the randomness of ambushes, camping attacks, and crit streaks, or offer more counterplay and information beforehand
- Better onboarding and in-game information so boss and curio mechanics are not pure trial-and-error; smoother mouse controls
- Speed options and less tedious quest pacing (animation speed, map traversal) for respect of player time

#### Unique Things

- Psychological stress as a core mechanic alongside hit points, with heroes developing afflictions or virtues that can help or sabotage the party
- Rank-based positioning combat in which skills work only from certain slots and move enemies and allies around
- Permanent death in a persistent hamlet hub: a roguelite meta-loop of rosters and town upgrades rather than fresh runs
- Torchlight and provisions management that raises tension and alters loot and enemy strength
- Narrator-driven tone that comments on your successes and failures with a dark sense of humour
- Hero quirks and diseases that make each recruit feel like a character rather than a disposable unit

#### Tedious Or Disliked

- Repeating near-identical low-level missions to level replacement heroes before high-tier content
- Expensive upkeep for stress relief, curing diseases, and removing bad traits that drains gold after every trip
- Slow animations and traversal that stretch each quest to 20 minutes or more
- Ambushes while camping and unavoidable hallway encounters that can kill high-level heroes
- Emotional toll of losing favourite heroes; some players say sessions leave them stressed rather than entertained, and the Butcher's Circus arena was not liked by at least one long-time player

#### Divisive Points

- Whether the difficulty is fair: many fans say knowledge and preparation remove most RNG, while detractors say it is about 50% luck
- Whether the grind is a satisfying meta-loop or a waste of time; fans defend the economy as part of the tension, critics call it padding
- Stress system: loved as innovative storytelling by most, but some find the random acting-out and afflictions infuriating
- Loss tolerance: fans see permadeath as the point of the game, others bounce off after losing a high-level hero
