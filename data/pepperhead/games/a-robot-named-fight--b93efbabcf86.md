### Commentary


#### Entry 1

- **Part:** 3
- **Label:** Tier list Part 3 (2025)
- **Summary:** A Robot Named Fight is a Metroidvania roguelike that openly imitates classic Metroid, with randomized levels, enemies and bosses each run. He considers it the best attempt at fusing the two genres, since they conflict by nature. He enjoyed it and finds it unique, but it is slow to get going each run and works better as a Metroidvania than a roguelike. He places it as a solid B tier.
- **Quote:** I can't think of a better game out there that does it.
- **Rerank Note:**

##### Praise

- Unusual, faithful blend of Metroidvania and roguelike; best of its kind in his view
- Randomized levels, enemies and bosses vary by run
- Great enemy variety and plenty of items and bosses to find
- Pixel art and music fit well and evoke nostalgia
- First-time exploration feels strong, like a real Metroid game
- Simple, straightforward lore and strong vibe

##### Criticism

- Genres conflict: replayability suffers and each run restarts from a weak, minimalist start
- Gameplay is simple and awkward, with old Metroid controls (hold button to aim diagonally)
- Early biomes have limited enemy pools, so runs start feeling the same
- Levels can be long; backtracking and enemies respawning every room feel tedious
- Unexplained items and shrines make risky purchases punishing since death is permanent
- Wishes he could skip to the midgame more often

##### Unique Points

- One of very few true Metroidvania roguelikes; he does not count Dead Cells as one
- Bosses differ by seed, giving new encounters each run
- Metroid-style opacity clashes with roguelike permadeath, making unknown gambles feel harsher

##### Comparisons

- Metroid
- Dead Cells

##### Design Criteria

- Genre fusion: how well Metroidvania exploration meshes with roguelike runs
- Replayability: runs restart weak and slow to reach the interesting midgame
- Level design: long levels with tedious backtracking and respawns
- Enemy design: good variety but limited pools in early biomes
- Controls: intentionally awkward old-school Metroid aiming
- Risk vs reward: unknown shrines/items punish gambles under permadeath
- Charm/visuals/audio: pixel art and music fit and feel nostalgic
- Randomization: levels, enemies and bosses generated per run

#### Best Things

- Movement and shooting feel very close to Super Metroid, which Metroid fans treat as the main draw
- Huge pool of randomized weapons, items and synergies makes each run and build feel different
- Strong 'one more run' loop once the slow opening minutes of a run are past; some players report hundreds of hours
- Procedural maps are still gated by ability checks, so it keeps a sense of metroidvania exploration and secret hunting
- Lots of secrets, multiple endings and final bosses, plus unlocks that keep replays fresh
- Local co-op and full controller support
- Chunky, gross enemy and boss designs are called out as a highlight even by lukewarm critics

#### Worst Things

- Presentation is seen as generic or bland, lacking the moody atmosphere of the game it imitates
- Procedural level design is inconsistent; generator can occasionally produce awkward or dead-end layouts
- Some runs do not give enough upgrades to handle later bosses, making RNG swingy
- Breakable secret walls are unmarked, so players feel they must shoot every surface; walls and enemies respawn and undo opened passages
- Difficulty is uneven: early bosses are considered too easy for a roguelite while later ones require long runs to reach
- Reviewers flagged very long item-pickup fanfare and some platforming precision issues on tight gaps
- Story is minimal and voice acting is heavily processed and hard to understand
- A few bugs and annoyances remain and the game is no longer actively updated
- Some later enemy groups with lock-on projectiles can cause cheap, instant-feeling deaths (Metacritic user complaints)

#### Improvements

- Allow rebinding movement abilities such as slide, which is tied to a two-key combo on keyboard and easy to misfire
- Give some form of persistence or run-saving so death does not always mean restarting from the very beginning
- Make breakable walls telegraphed and stop them from respawning instantly
- More consistent boss counts per region, and fewer reused bosses; give the final region an alternate variant and treat the hard mode as a proper mode with achievements
- Larger, more intricate environments and more enemy, boss and biome variety (a sequel is frequently requested)
- Explain the cryptic shrine and offering system in-game instead of requiring outside guides

#### Unique Things

- Grafts a roguelike run structure directly onto a Super Metroid skeleton, rather than following the Dead Cells or Rogue Legacy action model
- Slower, more old-school pace than typical action roguelites, emphasising verticality and wall-jumping
- Procedurally generated maps still use gated upgrades, so runs feel like miniature metroidvanias
- Machine-god shrines with number-based offerings add a cryptic meta layer
- Multiple endings, final bosses and a lore-heavy hard mode behind repeated completions
- Couch co-op on a Metroid-style game, which is rare in the subgenre

#### Tedious Or Disliked

- Early minutes of every run feel slow while you only have weak starting weapons and limited movement
- Restarting after every death becomes repetitive, especially for players who want metroidvania-style persistent progress
- Constantly shooting walls to find secret passages, then re-breaking walls that respawn
- Reaching the harder late bosses can take an hour or more, so failed attempts are costly
- Learning what items do requires many runs and some outside research
- Co-op camera system is a turn-off for some players

#### Divisive Points

- Whether roguelike structure and procedural generation belong in a metroidvania at all; 2018 commenters argued permadeath and randomness are opposite to hand-crafted exploration, while fans say it works surprisingly well
- Difficulty: some find it too lenient and easy to break, others find bad RNG runs and late bosses punishing
- Originality: praised as a loving homage to Super Metroid by some critics and dismissed as close to a fangame by others
- Player skill fit: fans of Super Metroid find it comfortable, while players unfamiliar with that movement hit an extra learning hump
