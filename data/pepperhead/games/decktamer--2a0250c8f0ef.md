- **Summary:** Deck Tamer is a board-centric deck-building roguelike where you fight beasts, capture them into your team, and extract their actives and passives to move onto other monsters. Combat is simple: place beasts, pick an ability each, and they attack automatically left to right. He finds it a deeply strategic, under-the-radar game that more people should know about. He beat it, calls it challenging, and places it in a really solid A tier, describing it as one of the best deck builders of recent years.
- **Quote:** one of the best deck building games I've played in the last few years
- **Rerank Note:**

##### Praise

- Very simple core gameplay (place beast, choose ability) that produces extensive, domino-effect strategy
- Fair encounters: you always fight things you could field yourself, so nothing feels unfair
- Capture-and-extract system (syringes, even mid-combat) creates unique self-made synergies and varied runs
- Pokemon-like tension of weakening enemies to capture them rather than kill them
- Every monster stays relevant, even weak starters, since they can absorb blows; constant deck churn
- Undo/redo mechanic that scales down with difficulty, good for teaching then committing
- Strong audio-visuals with attack art popping out; not AI generated; single final boss makes no-capture feel justified

##### Criticism

- Animation/zoom is a bit claustrophobic; he wishes it could zoom out to appreciate visuals
- Only two path choices before each battle; he wants a third
- Capture chance RNG (e.g. 80%) can abruptly end a deep run, which feels bad at high levels
- Losing monsters permanently feels rough at first and takes getting comfortable with
- Challenging; requires wrapping your head around many possibilities

##### Unique Points

- Monster-collecting plus extracting abilities/passives between monsters for self-made synergies
- Forced use and disposal of monsters and consumables teaches you not to hoard
- Limited redos as a core, difficulty-scaled mechanic
- Starter monsters always retain value

##### Design Criteria

- Simplicity vs depth: simple inputs yielding extensive strategy
- Synergy/build variety: ability extraction and capture create diverse runs
- Risk vs reward: capture chances and sacrificing monsters
- Fairness: enemies are always fieldable by the player
- Difficulty/teaching: redos scale down with difficulty
- Art and audio: strong visuals, immersive attack art, biome feel
- Meta-choices/pathing: wants more than two path options
- RNG: capture failure can ruin runs

#### Best Things

- Striking, surreal, Lovecraftian hand-drawn creature and card art (reportedly by essentially one artist/dev), cited in nearly every positive review
- Taming mechanic adds real tension: monsters you tame are permanent-until-dead, so you weigh healing vs taming a stronger unit vs sacrificing
- Mutation/syringe system lets you move abilities between creatures, enabling inventive and sometimes broken synergies (e.g. speed-buff stacking snowballs)
- Deep tactical, puzzle-like combat; reviewers describe it as highly deterministic and min-max friendly
- Moody soundtrack and atmosphere, plus well-written bestiary and explorer diary lore entries that reward collecting new creatures
- Good value at $15.99 with many modes, bosses, secret bosses and high replayability for players who click with it
- Responsive developer who engages the community, runs creature-submission events and published a roadmap to address complaints

#### Worst Things

- Difficulty is widely called overtuned, even on the lowest settings; one reviewer cited under 41% of owners beating the medium difficulty
- Attrition problem: your creatures do not heal between fights while enemies do, so regen/lifesteal tanks dominate and glass-cannon or gimmick builds become liabilities
- Healing items and taming items are the same resource, forcing painful tradeoffs that feel punishing rather than fun to some
- RNG dependence at higher difficulties: missed tames, weak event rolls or no synergistic creatures late in a run can make it unwinnable
- Performance and optimization complaints: high GPU use, card shine effects with many syringes, older PCs struggling
- Some UI clunkiness and unclear unlocks/rewards (scrolling through abilities, battle reward clarity)
- Windows only on Steam (no Mac/Linux) and only partial controller support
- Limited long-term depth: some reviewers feel they have seen most of what the game offers after a few runs, citing too few ascension-style modifiers

#### Improvements

- Retune the difficulty curve and give better guidance for learning higher difficulties, where reviewers say there is no reference for what works
- Add more between-battle activity and events, such as a Slay-the-Spire-style run map; events are few and their order is random
- Offer a toggle to turn off or speed up effect explanations and animations, especially for Endless mode where battles drag on
- Make the undo/redo on easier modes more useful; it only returns to before combat and does not refund items or placements
- Add more content and meta-progression: more ascension-style modifiers, since extra modes are seen as only added harshness, plus more bosses and events
- Broaden viable builds so that not only high-HP regen units work, and add optimization options
- Additional language support (Russian requested repeatedly) and platform support

#### Unique Things

- Creature-catching as the deck-building engine: enemy cards are recruited into your deck rather than picked from reward pools
- Nuzlocke-like permanence: units that die are gone, and low-tier tames are treated as disposable fodder to trade up
- Ability extraction and mutation system (syringes) that moves abilities between monsters, which affects how creatures evolve
- Enemies have non-static moves and abilities, so the same monster species can appear stronger or weaker in later stages
- Lore delivered through a bestiary and explorer diary tied to the monsters you tame
- Turn-based battles closer to tactical puzzles or XCOM-style positioning than typical card-draw combat

#### Tedious Or Disliked

- Runs feel draining: roughly 8 encounters per depth, each needing heavy thinking with little breathing room between them
- Very long turns and runs (turns of several minutes; Very Hard runs around 1.5-2 hours; Endless waves taking about 15 min each due to effect text)
- No sense of attachment or build identity: every creature eventually gets scrapped, so some players feel nothing builds on itself like in Slay the Spire
- Counterpoint complaint that levels are too short to assemble a deck, leaving finished levels feeling unsatisfying
- Losing runs teaches little, because the next run's creatures and events differ so much

#### Divisive Points

- Difficulty: some say it is fair and rewarding once you adopt the sacrifice mindset, others say it is brutal or unbalanced even on easy
- RNG vs determinism: some praise it as deterministic and min-maxable, others find late-game RNG and unreliable tames decisive
- Run length and pacing: slow deliberate turns are a feature for some and draining for others
- Disposable units: seen as fresh tension by some and as a lack of long-term attachment by others
