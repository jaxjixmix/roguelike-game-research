### Community

- **One Liner:** A pixel-art, top-down action roguelite from Crackshell (published by Team17) where you push through procedurally generated dungeon floors solo or in 4-player online co-op, sending gold and ore back to a persistent town to upgrade seven (nine with DLC) heroes.
- **Consensus Summary:** Players rate it Very Positive on Steam (about 87-88% positive, roughly 9.5k reviews in the local data; the store page shows about 4.7k English reviews, with the last 30 days sitting at Mostly Positive, 75% of 20 reviews). Metacritic has no critic reviews, and its 14 user ratings average 7.2, which is less enthusiastic than Steam. The core combat, class variety and co-op are widely liked, but the most common complaint is that the meta-progression is slow and padded, which Steam reviewers describe as grindy well beyond the norm for a roguelite. It is a finished, content-rich game with three DLC expansions (Witch Hunter, Pyramid of Prophecy, Moon Temple) and a Workshop/level editor. Reviewers still report old bugs as of late 2025, including a character-overwrite save bug and black-screen issues, so polish is uneven. Its successor Heroes of Hammerwatch II now exists, so this entry is in maintenance territory. I could not retrieve critic reviews from major outlets, so critic sentiment is essentially absent.
- **Reception Notes:** Released March 1, 2018 (Steam, Windows/Linux, no Mac) at $14.99, developed by Crackshell and published by Team17. #### Best Things

- Moment-to-moment top-down hack-and-slash/shoot combat is praised as good fun, even by reviewers who quit over grind
- Persistent town with shopkeepers and upgrades gives a satisfying early progression loop; town unlocks carry across all heroes
- Strong co-op: up to 4 players online, and several reviewers say it is far better with friends than solo
- Seven distinct classes (nine with DLC) with different skill sets, plus item sets that scale builds as you collect pieces
- Simple item model: no equipment management, pickups apply instantly, which keeps runs quick and easy to jump into
- Risk/reward gold banking: you must send gold to town (with a random tax rate) or lose it if you die, which creates tension
- Huge content volume: lots of floors, secrets, traps, bosses, New Game+ tiers and three expansions
- Steam Workshop and a built-in level editor extend the game; mods are popular for speeding up or unlocking content

#### Worst Things

- Grind is the dominant complaint: maxing one character reportedly takes hundreds of thousands of gold while a run earns about 10-20k before tax
- The gold tax mechanic (more gold held means a worse rate) is called pointless padding that slows progression
- Upgrades and town purchases are per-character in many cases, so each new hero means re-buying expensive upgrades
- Most traps and some enemy attacks have no invincibility frames, so players can be chain-hit and killed instantly
- Chest/key system feels random: players report holding keys with no chests, or finding locked chests with no keys, and needing 40+ of a material that does not drop
- Build diversity is shallow; many items are flat damage or speed boosts, and late runs feel like stacking bigger numbers
- Reused assets and enemies from the first Hammerwatch make it feel less fresh to returning fans
- Long-standing bugs: a reported save bug that deletes a character and replaces it with a copy of the last played one, random crashes, black screens on exit or at the final boss, and lobby disconnects in co-op

#### Improvements

- Share more upgrades (potions, blessings, stat upgrades) across all heroes instead of per character
- Reduce or remove the gold tax, and lower prices of items like pets (one reviewer says 25k for a pet is too much)
- Fix the character-deletion save bug and black-screen/crash issues that players say have persisted for years
- Give more build variety and less flat-number item design so New Game+ runs feel different
- Raise or remove shopkeeper purchase limits in dungeons and make key/chest and material drops less random
- Add crossplay and improve multiplayer stability and desync handling; also better controller and Steam Deck support

#### Unique Things

- Town-building meta progression shared across a roster of heroes, rather than a single run-based unlock tree
- Gold banking with random tax: send money home to keep it, or carry it at risk of losing everything on death
- Item-set system with instant-apply pickups and no inventory or gear slots
- Shortcut/skip mechanics and alternate paths through tiers, plus a huge New Game+ ladder (achievement hunters cite NG+7/NG+8 requirements)
- Built-in level editor and Steam Workshop support, with popular mods adding classes and unlocks
- Roguelite evolution of the original Hammerwatch's lose-it-all roguelike formula, set in the same universe

#### Tedious Or Disliked

- Repeating the same floors to farm the same two materials (gold and ore), with progress that feels slower the further you get
- Needing to beat every boss with every character because those clears give permanent bonuses to all heroes, even classes you dislike
- Progression slows after roughly NG+2 as runs become numbers-only scaling, per a 112-hour solo player who stopped at NG+4
- Small corridors and heavy ranged enemy spam make individual fights annoying for some, especially solo
- Death resets feel punishing because runs that die before banking resources yield nothing, which one Metacritic user called a hamster-wheel design

#### Divisive Points

- Co-op vs solo: reviewers repeatedly say it shines with friends but the grind is hard to justify alone
- Grind tolerance: some players love the long town-and-character progression while others call it worse than mobile gacha games
- Difficulty and build freedom: some find it easy and relaxed, others feel underpowered without gold spent on upgrades and find traps unfair
- Workshop mods: several players say the game is only fun with progression-speed mods, which others see as a fix for design flaws
