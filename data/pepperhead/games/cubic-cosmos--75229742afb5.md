##### Praise

- Permanent buffs from nearly every battle make progression feel big and unusual for the genre
- Building broken combos is super satisfying, especially the first few times
- Lots to do between battles, plus triple and fuse mechanics to duplicate effects
- VFX are satisfying: explosions and flying tokens
- Rewarding to learn, since system knowledge directly produces crazy effects
- Different champion classes change things somewhat
- Developers were not afraid to use huge effects

##### Criticism

- Game is very easy to break, so it often ends up as one card then waiting for animations (his Steam review: the deck played itself)
- Repetitive and redundant after a few runs
- Art style feels uninspired, almost AI-generated in vibe; music and sound design are weak
- Too much on screen, enemy effects are hard to track, and player info is meh since you usually just kill everything
- Card effects and complicated keywords are poorly explained and hard to visualize

##### Unique Points

- Permanent run-long buffs on most units and cards are the core hook, which also makes balance very fragile
- Triple-and-fuse of units duplicates permanent effects, allowing runaway builds
- Pro and con: extreme breakability is both the fun and the weakness

#### Best Things

- Anything-goes synergy building: players love assembling absurd, over-the-top combos and scaling to huge numbers, and like that the game does not try to cap power the way Slay the Spire-style games do
- Stat boosts from spells, monsters and events last for the whole run, so cards genuinely grow and you plan around growing specific units
- Selling cards in shops (about 50% value) instead of paying to remove them eases RNG pressure and encourages pivoting builds
- Lots of content and variety: 12 heroes across 3 classes with distinct synergies, 240+ cards, 50+ relics, multiple modes and 10+ difficulty levels
- Strong 'one more run' pull; several reviewers report 25-60 hours and one 260+ hours
- Satisfying combo chains with lots of animation and effects going off; the game feels powerful from the very first run
- Doll Code system lets you export cards and share/receive others' pawns and replays, adding a light meta layer
- Low price ($12.99) relative to the amount of content, frequently cited as great value

#### Worst Things

- Undisclosed generative-AI card art is the top complaint: the two most-upvoted negative Steam reviews (93 and 125 helpful votes) cite odd fingers, repeated poses and 'obviously AI' cards, and people say they feel misled
- Game is easy and repetitive for some: one reviewer beat the top difficulty with every character without a loss, with decks effectively locked in by the first boss
- Heroes are said to play too similarly, with hero abilities easy to ignore (only a couple felt truly distinct to that reviewer)
- Stat stacking has little downside, so builds converge on a few stat enablers plus a few hard hitters
- UI/readability gaps: card types (artifact vs sorcery etc.) are unclear, keyword phrasing is odd, and many things are left unexplained
- Chain reactions can be too fast or opaque to follow, leaving some players confused about why their board was wiped
- Technical rough edges: game freeze when starting a new run (pinned Steam workaround), buggy controller support, poor text on Steam Deck, a Linux launch failure (no native Linux build), a reported crash
- Repetitive, looping music mentioned by a reviewer

#### Improvements

- Disclose or replace the generative-AI art and be transparent about the art pipeline
- Better in-game explanation of keywords, card types and monster effects, plus general QoL and UI clarity work
- Add downsides or tension to stat stacking and make higher difficulties and late-game builds more varied (late-game builds called limited)
- Differentiate heroes more so their special abilities meaningfully change play
- Improve controller and Steam Deck support and fix the new-run freeze; community also asks for ultrawide support and more languages (Russian, German)
- More Steam achievements, and clearer purpose for the dolls/meta systems (one reviewer collected dolls without knowing what they do)

#### Unique Things

- Monsters attack from back to front in a lane/tower-defense style layout, so reaching enemy backlines is a real strategic lever
- Most attribute boosts persist for the entire run rather than one fight, pushing a 'grow your cards' play pattern
- Tension between prolonging fights to buff cards and finishing fast to earn more lucre (currency)
- Selling cards for lucre instead of paying to discard
- Doll Code system for exporting cards, sharing pawns and battle replays with other players
- Hearthstone/Battlegrounds-like creature-and-spell feel mixed with roguelike path choices; reviewers compare the card-modifier depth to Inscryption

#### Tedious Or Disliked

- Undisclosed AI art (some buyers refunded or wrote negative reviews purely over this)
- Feeling the run is decided early, then just watching the deck play out
- Long chains of auto-triggering effects that some find hard to follow
- Having to learn unusual keyword terms (mobility, auto, death, lasting, charge) before the game clicks
- Steam-page tags/marketing: players who expected a roguelite with persistent power-up meta-progression felt misled, since the dev reportedly said it is not meant to have that (though the game does have XP-based unlocks of cards/characters)

#### Divisive Points

- Is it a roguelite or a roguelike? Some say the tag is misleading because there is no permanent power meta-progression, while others note unlocks/XP and Doll Code carry-over count as meta progression
- Difficulty and balance: fans love that it is wildly breakable, while critics find it too easy and shallow once you learn the stacking loop
- AI art: some players call it a dealbreaker that warrants a negative review, while others say they enjoy the game regardless and dismiss the complaint
- Hero diversity: one reviewer with hundreds of hours found some heroes 'world enders' with very different styles, while another felt they mostly play the same
