- **Label:** Tier list Part 2 (2024)
- **Summary:** Spiritfall is a 2D action roguelike with a Hades-style boon structure that plays like a platform fighter. He played it in Early Access and was excited for the full release. As a former competitive Smash player, he found the combat a natural fit and considers the gameplay outstanding. He calls it an easy S tier but says he would still put Hades above it, and admits some Smash bias.
- **Quote:** this is going to actually be a pretty easy s tier for me
- **Rerank Note:**

##### Praise

- Combat plays like a platform fighter: aerials, smash attacks, down attacks and even spiking enemies
- Every weapon feels like a different character with its own moveset
- Hades-style boons from gods, with fusions and combo boons, change how each weapon plays
- Strong roguelike layer on top of excellent core gameplay, which is rare for combat-focused games
- High replayability from different weapons, blessings and approaches each run
- Good sense of progression, with visually distinct worlds and awesome bosses
- Plenty to unlock and secrets, including a very hard secret true boss

##### Criticism

- Wants even more content and secrets
- Bosses repeat in every world; he wants variations of each boss (like a Pact of Punishment style upgrade)
- Synergy blessings are not very impactful and feel underwhelming compared to Hades
- Boon choices are sometimes situational, and sometimes all three offers are bad

##### Unique Points

- A genuinely combat-focused, Smash-like roguelike that succeeds at both combat and roguelike elements
- Copies Hades' boon framework but applies it to a very different kind of combat
- Each god's blessings drastically change how weapons are played

##### Comparisons

- Smash
- Hades
- Blazblue Entropy Effect

##### Design Criteria

- Combat depth and feel: weapon movesets and platform-fighter mechanics are the core strength
- Replayability: weapon and boon combinations create different runs
- Synergies and boons: fusion/combo boons exist but are less impactful than Hades
- Meta-progression and content: lots to unlock and secrets, but wants more
- Boss design: good and varied between worlds but repetitive within a world
- Level/world variety: each world looks and feels different

#### Best Things

- Combat feels like a platform fighter: launching, wall-splatting and comboing enemies is described as satisfying, with very polished animation and hit feedback
- Weapons are distinct from each other and have alternate forms (e.g. Sisterhood Gauntlets, scythe) that change playstyle
- 150+ blessings, elemental upgrades and assist/bolt abilities that synergise; reviewers say few feel unimpactful
- Adjustable difficulty and accessibility options are praised, letting weaker players finish the story
- Hand-drawn, Miyazaki-flavoured art and a strong soundtrack (though one critic found the music forgettable)
- Permanent upgrades between runs feel meaningful without being mandatory; hidden rifts and secrets reward exploration
- Scope is reasonable and what is there is polished; $19.99 price viewed as fair, especially on sale

#### Worst Things

- Low content: the same bosses on the same levels every run and every clear, so it feels repetitive after roughly 6-10 hours
- Finishing requires about 5 full clears, and the final payoff is reported as just one extra boss phase, which many call anticlimactic
- Only a limited number of blessings/relics and repeated active abilities and status effects; some feel they have solved the game after a couple of runs
- Much of the combat is 'ragdolling' enemies in hitstun; true platform-fighter play (knockoffs, edge-guarding) mostly appears only in rifts or the last area
- Crowds of overlapping 2D enemies are hard to read, leading to cheap hits and spam-the-dash/projectile play
- Enemy telegraphs reported as mismatched with real attack timing; some mobs ignore hitstun to add artificial difficulty
- Weak plot and sparse lore; story is minimal and sprinkled in
- Reported bugs: credits freeze/loop on completion, brief freezes, enemies attacking before level entry, a boss path glitch, new saves granting achievements; Steam Deck black-screen at launch per ImpulseGamer
- Weapon quality is uneven per several negative reviews: outside the fists many weapons are called slow and clunky, with combos that do not mesh with most blessings; the top negative review says knockoff play only appears in the final area or bonus fights
- Some players report launch/graphics problems (black screen on Steam Deck, graphics glitching on launch, one refund over it), so performance is not universally smooth

#### Improvements

- More bosses, more rooms/biome variety and procedural variation so runs differ more
- More blessings, weapon movesets and build synergies to avoid the feeling of solving the game
- Make movement/tech (e.g. wavedash and other advanced platform-fighter mechanics) available earlier instead of behind skill unlocks
- Better enemy readability and clearer, consistent telegraphs in crowded fights
- Smarter difficulty curve: late-tier modifiers are said to spike damage; first two bosses can be harder than the last
- A more rewarding ending and stronger narrative for the multi-clear structure; ability to reset permanent upgrades; clearer info on secrets

#### Unique Things

- Combat built on platform-fighter ideas (knockback, launching, wall-splat, sweet spots) rather than standard hack-and-slash
- Hades-like structure with Slay-the-Spire-style node map for choosing rooms and shared HP across a run
- Weapons with alternate forms that change inputs and moveset
- Hidden rift rooms that turn into one-on-one knockback duels for extra loot
- Hard versions of bosses and Pact-like challenge modifiers after the first clear, plus a boss-rush room
- Runs on Mac (no Linux build) and offers granular difficulty adjustment

#### Tedious Or Disliked

- Repeating the same boss lineup five times to reach the ending
- Grinding skill points and permanent upgrades after the first clear; unlock gating of core moves
- Long stretches of low-threat filler rooms before a boss, since shared HP makes small rooms unchallenging
- Shielded bosses that do not flinch, which negates the combo-heavy combat exactly where challenge lives
- High-tier modifier runs needing a precise build, making 10-skull clears feel unreachable for some
- Visual clutter and cheap damage when many enemies and effects are on screen

#### Divisive Points

- Whether it truly delivers platform-fighter depth: fans of Smash/Brawlhalla call it a great fit, while competitive fighter players call it bare-bones with no real shield/dodge/grab
- Responsiveness and movement: praised as fluid by many, described as sluggish or non-responsive by others
- Derivative vs fresh: labelled 'Hades clone' by some critics/users, called one of the most innovative roguelites by others
- Value: fine at $19.99 for some, only worth it on discount for others given the content volume
- Weapon feel: praised as distinct and build-changing by many, called clunky and poorly synergised with blessings by others
