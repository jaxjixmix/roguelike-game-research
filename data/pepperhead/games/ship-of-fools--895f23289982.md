- **Summary:** Ship of Fools is an action roguelike where you defend a boat by manning cannons, refilling ammo and using a paddle to reflect projectiles, with a destroyed island as the hub. It is clearly designed around co-op, and he played both co-op with his brother and solo, finding co-op more fun. He loves the visuals, music, animation and early-game discovery, but finds the moment-to-moment gameplay thin and the item system too narrow and build-dependent. Post-clear rewards are weak, so overall he lands on B tier, admitting co-op may have lifted his opinion.
- **Quote:** I'm still going to give Ship of Fools a B tier.
- **Rerank Note:**

##### Praise

- Art style, environments, animation and music are top-notch and were what first drew him in
- Hub island exploration, NPC discovery and world immersion make the first few hours very enjoyable
- Trinkets/amulets give cool, fun benefits and were his favorite roguelike element
- Each boss ends with a different reward (recycle an item, duplicate an item, big sell shop)
- Areas look distinct and enemies feel varied; bosses look fantastic
- Co-op is more fun than solo
- Paddle reflect mechanic feels good when it lands

##### Criticism

- Gameplay on the boat is shallow and repetitive: refill cannon, shoot, occasionally paddle
- Paddle charges slowly and is visually hard to read
- Only about four ship slots, filled before the first world boss, so most new items are ignored
- Items are highly build-dependent (harpoon, paddle, egg, dynamite), so off-build picks feel useless
- Higher difficulties cost currency to unlock and give minimal rewards, so post-clear motivation is low
- Bosses repeat with too little variation; auto-turret seems too strong not to use

##### Unique Points

- Ship-defense cannon/paddle loop feels like Overcooked-style co-op in a roguelike
- Boss-end rewards differ each time rather than being a generic upgrade
- Design lesson: build-specific items plus very limited slots leads to ignoring most of the content

- **One Liner:** A two-player (or solo) cooperative seafaring roguelite by Fika Productions (published by Team17) where you run a ship together, juggling cannons, repairs and steering against sea monsters to stop the Aquapocalypse.
- **Consensus Summary:** Ship of Fools is well liked, especially as a couch or online co-op game for exactly two people: Steam sits at Very Positive (about 82% of roughly 6,300 reviews) and Metacritic critics give it a 77 (PS5, 10 reviews) while Metacritic users are lukewarm at 7.0 from only 30 ratings. #### Best Things

- Frantic, Overcooked-style co-op on a shared ship (steering, loading and firing cannons, patching leaks) that generates funny teamwork and arguments
- Charming, cohesive art direction with distinct characters, enemies and bosses, plus a well-regarded soundtrack
- Good couples/friend pairing game: challenging but not overwhelming, and partners can cover each other's weaknesses
- Lots of item and talisman combinations, including memorably game-breaking synergies, without any single item being mandatory
- Replayability through unlockable ships, characters, brightness (difficulty) levels and achievements; players report 30-130 hours
- Excellent controller support and local couch co-op feel; also runs well on Linux for some
- Low entry price ($9.99 on Steam) and frequent sales make it an easy buy for two players

#### Worst Things

- Hard 2-player cap: the single most upvoted review theme; players want 3-4 player support and say the game is a poor fit for groups of friends
- Local co-op bug where the second controller is not recognised, reported since 2023 and still cited in recent reviews
- Thin post-launch feel: several top reviews describe the game as abandoned and the developer advertising its other game and Discord inside the menu
- Multiplayer progression is per-player and does not sync: one partner can reach 100% while the other is stuck, with developer support reportedly unable to fix it
- Save/progress loss reported by several players, including Steam Cloud not recovering it and runs randomly kicking to the main menu
- Post-update instability on Steam Deck (audio loss, lag) after the Unity engine update, and small/blurry text on TV for couch play
- Little in-game information: no bestiary or codex, stats of guns and talismans not shown clearly, so players look things up externally
- Thin content: only four bosses always in the same places, limited enemy variety, characters differ mostly by one passive
- Network reliability in online co-op: trouble joining, staying on the ship or resuming runs even on good connections
- Missing options: only resolution is adjustable in graphics, gamepad controls cannot be remapped, hardcoded menu binds

#### Improvements

- Support for more than two players (a commonly requested feature since the game is hard-capped at 2)
- More bosses per area and more levels/enemy types so runs feel less identical
- A bestiary/codex and clearer UI showing stats for guns, talismans and upgrades
- Fix multiplayer progression syncing and multiple save slots; better solo mode and difficulty balance
- Fuller settings: graphics options, gamepad rebinding, per-action binds, correct PS5 glyphs, readable text for TV play
- Fix the local second-controller bug and engine-update regressions (Steam Deck, optimization)

#### Unique Things

- Co-op roguelite built around physically running around a ship and moving gear (guns, ammo, repair tools) rather than controlling one character
- Tower-defense-meets-roguelite structure: you defend a moving ship in naval battles, compared by critics to a deck-defence game with Overcooked energy
- Paddle-based ship steering with friendly fire, which blends party-game chaos with hardcore roguelite difficulty
- Difficulty driven by unlocking successive brightness levels rather than purely random runs; Binding of Isaac-style item collecting but less punishing
- Deliberately couch-first design for two players, with solo mode as a secondary option

#### Tedious Or Disliked

- Unlocking each brightness level means replaying the same few levels repeatedly; restarting runs can feel like a chore
- Once you beat the four bosses, unlock-chasing and achievement grinding are what remains
- Reloading guns and shuffling them around the ship becomes repetitive, with hectic moments that some find more stressful than fun
- Sudden difficulty spikes (final boss, high brightness levels) and later-game balance that some call unfun, especially solo
- Ads for the developer's other game and Discord in the menu
- Short runs of content: several reviewers say they run out of new things to do after about 5 hours and are left grinding to 100%

#### Divisive Points

- Price: seen as a bargain at $9.99 by many, but a reviewer calls 22 EUR pricey for the amount of content
- Difficulty: described as well measured by some co-op pairs, but too casual early and unbalanced late by others
- Friendly fire and chaotic design: adds hilarious arguments for some couples and feels like a clash of tones for others
- Solo play: some say it is very manageable, critics and other players say it is frustrating and clearly geared to co-op
