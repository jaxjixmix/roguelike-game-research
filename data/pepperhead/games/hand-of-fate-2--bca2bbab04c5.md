### Community

- **One Liner:** A third-person action RPG wrapped in a roguelite deck-builder, where a mysterious Dealer lays out a card-built board-game dungeon and you fight real-time combat whenever the cards demand it.
- **Consensus Summary:** Critics and players largely agree it is a clear, more ambitious step up from the 2015 original, with a Metacritic score of 80 and Steam 'Very Positive' (about 84% of roughly 5.8k reviews in the local data). The atmosphere, writing, narrator and the unusual card-as-dungeon loop are the main draws. The persistent complaints are combat that is serviceable but shallow and repetitive, randomness that can ruin a run, and bloat or grind in longer play. The developer Defiant stopped making new games in 2019 and only maintains the title, so the game is effectively in maintenance mode (not early access). The Steam store page showed recent reviews as only Mixed (66% of 15) against Very Positive overall, which suggests a small, aging player base with some lingering technical complaints.
- **Reception Notes:** Launched November 2017 on PC, PS4 and Xbox One to generally favourable reviews (Metacritic 80 from 27 critics; user score 7.0 from about 99 ratings). It won an AGDA Game of the Year award and was listed among Kotaku's top 10 games of 2017 per the Steam page. Defiant reported Hand of Fate 2 earned about 40% more revenue than the original in its first six months. Three story DLC packs followed (Outlands and Outsiders, The Servant and the Beast, A Cold Hearth). Defiant stopped making new games in July 2019 and moved to a caretaker mode, so the game gets no active development; in 2025 it only began publishing the spin-off Hordes of Fate. Steam's recent reviews were Mixed on the store page, driven by technical complaints like load times and bugs. Third-party estimates (Steam revenue calculator) put gross revenue around $1.67M and SteamSpy owners at 200k to 500k, but these are Boxleiter-style estimates and exclude console sales. #### Best Things

- Atmosphere and presentation: art, music, sound design and the Dealer narrator's personality are repeatedly called out as the standout feature
- A genuinely unusual gameplay loop: you build a hand of encounter and equipment cards, then navigate a board-game map that unfolds like a choose-your-own-adventure
- Strong writing and story-driven scenarios (20+ campaign quests plus endless mode) with varied outcomes built from the cards you unlock
- New mini-game-style challenges (dice, pendulum, wheel) add skill-based variety beyond the original's card-shuffle challenge
- Companions with their own questlines and abilities improve both exploration and combat compared with the first game
- Unlockable gear that levels up and token challenges give a steady reason to keep unlocking cards and equipment
- Combat grows more satisfying in later acts once riposte and block-breaking mechanics open up (Arkham-style comparison appears in several Steam reviews)
- Character customization and more diverse cast were noted as improvements

#### Worst Things

- Combat is the weakest pillar: Arkham-lite, one-enemy-at-a-time feel, repetitive and often just spam-attack once you out-gear encounters (echoed by PC Gamer and Game Informer per Wikipedia summary)
- Randomness and luck-based challenges can be run-killing or feel unfair in later campaigns; GameSpot noted some quests lean too heavily on luck
- Late-game bloat: decks of 20+ cards with no search or filter make pre-run deck building a chore
- Technical rough edges in some Steam reviews: long loading screens before each fight, enemy AI breaking (reported fix: turn vsync on), bugged dice rerolls, an unlocked sword not appearing in equipment
- Sluggish-feeling hero evade and chaotic, hard-to-read fights in crowded encounters according to negative reviewers
- Progression and grind: Wccftech scored it 60 citing unsatisfying progression, punishing randomness and clunky combat; some Steam reviewers call it overpriced at $30 and repetitive
- Later campaigns spike in difficulty and feel unfair to some players, leading to frustration (and grinding for gold-token gear) before progress is possible

#### Improvements

- Deeper, more varied combat: more enemy variety and more tactical options beyond attack, counter and block
- Deck-management quality of life: a search or filter for the growing card collection and a faster way to build hands
- Shorter or skippable loading screens and intro animations when restarting encounters
- Better balance for Endless mode, where item degradation makes daggers nearly useless
- Less punishing RNG or more mitigation tools so one bad draw does not sink a long run
- Bug fixes, since the studio is in maintenance mode and some bugs reported in 2025 remain

#### Unique Things

- The dungeon is literally a tabletop built from cards that the Dealer lays out, and the cards you choose determine encounters, loot and events
- A sitting-in-the-Dealer's-wagon framing with a charismatic narrator who comments on your successes and failures
- Hybrid of deckbuilding and real-time third-person action combat, often compared to Arkham-style fighting with a card-game shell
- Tarot-flavoured theming and choose-your-own-adventure storytelling rather than a pure arena roguelike
- Hand-built campaign scenarios with persistent unlocks, rather than fully random runs, plus a separate Endless mode
- Steam tags label it a roguelike deckbuilder, but most players describe it as closer to a narrative adventure with roguelite unlock progression

#### Tedious Or Disliked

- Repeating the same lock-on combat over many encounters; some players mash attack to get through it
- Scrolling a large card collection to build a deck before each run
- Long loading and intro sequences each time you retry a level
- Luck-heavy mini-games that must be passed for story progress and can be frustrating
- Grinding the campaign to unlock the cards and gear needed for later, harder scenarios
- Endless mode's item degradation making some weapon types feel pointless

#### Divisive Points

- Combat: some find it fun and increasingly rewarding, others find it the weakest, clunkiest part of the game
- Difficulty and RNG: some enjoy the tension and sense of accomplishment, others see late campaigns as unfair and want to uninstall
- Value: recommended by many at full price as a unique experience, but some say wait for a sale at $15 or less
- Roguelike label: players split on whether it satisfies roguelike cravings or plays more like a campaign-based adventure
