### Community

- **One Liner:** A turn-based, grid-based traditional roguelike from Adventurepro Games in which each dead hero feeds a persistent Dungeonmans Academy hub that strengthens future characters, wrapped in a light, joke-heavy fantasy tone.
- **Consensus Summary:** Players rate it Very Positive on Steam (about 91% of roughly 1,230 reviews), and the recurring verdict is that it is an unusually welcoming traditional roguelike with real depth and a clever meta-progression loop. Critical coverage is thin: only two critic reviews sit on Metacritic (RPG Fan 85, Twinfinite 40), so no Metascore exists, and the six-rating user score of 6.5 is too small to mean much. It is not abandoned: it had a 2013 Kickstarter, Early Access, a full release in Dec 2014, and still draws praise for updates more than a decade later, including new classes and a daily challenge mentioned in 2026 reviews. The player base is now small (a few concurrent users per Steam data, and one reviewer jokes that only a handful of players remain) and the game is Windows-only. Criticism centers on tiny UI text, clunky inventory and controls, divisive humor, and difficulty spikes around the early-to-mid game.
- **Reception Notes:** Launched after a roughly $43,000 Kickstarter in 2013 and Early Access on Steam, with full release on Dec 9, 2014 at $14.99. Early press was warm: RPG Fan named it among its top games of 2014, and Kotaku and Indie Gems praised its welcoming design per the Steam store page. It has stayed Very Positive on Steam and keeps receiving new reviews in 2026, many from long-term players noting continued updates. Metacritic has only two critic reviews (no Metascore) and six user ratings at 6.5, so those numbers are not representative. Steam estimates (SteamSpy 100k-200k owners; steam-revenue-calculator gross about $553k, net about $163k) are rough. The developer also released a Pay2Lose DLC/companion listing. Steam community has roughly 270 discussion topics and a 1,800-comment bug thread. #### Best Things

- Academy meta-progression: fallen heroes leave gear, experience and deeds that permanently upgrade the hub, so every death still feels like progress
- Unusually accessible for a traditional roguelike while keeping permadeath and tactical depth, praised by both newcomers and genre veterans
- Flexible mastery/skill system that lets you mix skills across classes, with special Master's Programs for effective combinations and lots of fun build experiments
- Humor and personality: silly names, corny jokes, enemy sound barks, battle poultry; many reviewers say it never takes itself too seriously
- Large open, persistent world with many monsters, dungeon and battlefield types and hidden discoveries (Steam lists 75+ abilities, 50+ enemy types, 12 dungeon types)
- Soundtrack and charming sprite art, with RPG Fan singling out the PS1-era Final Fantasy-style music
- Long-running developer support: multiple reviewers credit patches, new content and responsive bug fixing over 12+ years
- Strong long-term hook: reviewers with 100-270 hours describe a 'one more dungeon' pull and a game that keeps getting better with updates

#### Worst Things

- Tiny, unscalable UI text at 1080p and above; the most upvoted negative Steam review is about unreadable fonts
- Clunky controls and inventory: delayed ability firing in a turn-based game, hard-to-find potions, a small scroll button on the quick bar with a hotkey clashing with a system key
- Bugs noted in reviews: targeting cursor problems, map generation issues, inaccessible graves (RPG Fan), and misbehavior when alt-tabbing (Steam review)
- Twinfinite scored it 40, calling gameplay stiff with unintuitive controls
- Difficulty spikes: bosses in the second dungeon and around character level 10 can one-shot players, and triple-stacked tigers became a well-known killer
- Windows only (no Mac or Linux) per the Steam store data
- Name and naming quirks, such as no duplicate character names and no random name option
- Some reviewers say builds are unbalanced toward ranged combat and that replayability trails other roguelikes

#### Improvements

- Scalable UI and larger fonts
- Better inventory handling and quick-access bar, with more forgiving controls so abilities cannot misfire a turn later
- More build variety, more passive skills and deeper skill trees; some builds are clearly stronger than others
- Clearer feedback on what upgrading each Academy room actually gives you, since some rooms feel cosmetic or one-time
- More crafting, alchemy and enchanting options beyond NPC services
- More end-game content, requested in Steam community threads; also Steam Deck camera pan/zoom and control improvements

#### Unique Things

- The Academy hub is a persistent, growing base that turns every death into long-term progression instead of a pure reset
- Dead heroes leave graves, loot and deeds for successors, and legendary heroes can eventually train later characters
- Lighthearted, tabletop-RPG tone that deliberately lowers the intimidation of the traditional roguelike genre
- Multi-mastery characters rather than fixed classes, so you can build combinations like a necro-archer
- Whimsical extras such as battle poultry and a daily challenge that doesn't add to your graveyard
- Open overworld with wilderness zones, ruined strongholds and places of power alongside dungeons

#### Tedious Or Disliked

- Pre-mid-game stretches can feel repetitive, with one reviewer finding early game slow before it opens up
- Academy progress is tied to dying, which some players find forced and others find it makes losses feel like a chore
- Over-the-top humor and forced jokes grate on some players to the point of killing motivation
- Being one-shotted by bosses or special enemies after long runs feels unfair to some players
- Finding the right item in a clumsy inventory takes time mid-fight
- A gold-statue gambling mechanic was heavily nerfed, which annoyed players who enjoyed the risk and whose characters were wiped by its punishment

#### Divisive Points

- Humor: the top selling point for many reviewers, but a 'cringe' dealbreaker for others
- Difficulty and accessibility: newcomers call it welcoming while others hit walls around level 10 or at early bosses even on easy
- Academy meta-progression: loved as a clever incremental system, but criticized as shallow or confusingly communicated by others
- Replayability: some say it lacks replay value versus other roguelikes, others put in hundreds of hours
