### Community

#### Best Things

- The body-part assembly system: mixing limbs, cores and weapons into odd creatures (skeleton chickens with lasers, vampires, robots, dragons) is called addictive and funny
- Recipe discovery: valid combos are saved to a book, get cheaper in mana and gain bonus stats, and can be summoned with one click
- Sense of progression and learning across runs; the game speeds up and gets fun after some early upgrades
- Pixel-art style, dark humor and personality; compared by reviewers to Vampire Survivors and old Flash games
- Very cheap; reviewers say it is worth a few dollars, with roughly 10-30+ hours of play reported
- Large variety of unit types to build, which reviewers applaud
- Good for casual, low-stress 'throw parts together and see what happens' sessions

#### Worst Things

- Minion AI is poor: units wander out of the base area, into dangerous biomes, and fail to defend the tower
- Direct control is limited: only one creature at a time via WASD, and the control spell reportedly lasts only a few seconds before the camera snaps back with very sensitive scrolling
- Late-game balance: enemy lairs spawn swarms far stronger than your units, and the 15-minion cap makes that hard to overcome
- Boss/timer pressure combined with meta progression that is weak early and then near-mandatory (e.g. daily starting parts upgrade), so runs can feel walled
- Permanent upgrades can make completing the game harder according to one reviewer, and one says it takes at least 3 runs for a single upgrade
- Limited replayability and depth: short, no story, strategy reduces to picking which units to keep and casting spells
- Some players report launch crashes or black screens on old installs, and the map is not very interactive

#### Improvements

- Better AI and stronger tactical commands (defend area, ranged units keep distance, hold position without being defenseless)
- Make the 'control minion' effect last until cancelled, or allow finer control of more than one unit
- Auto-craft specific units when parts are available and auto-sell surplus parts for mana
- Rebalance late-game enemy lair spawns and the 15-unit cap, and smooth the jump in meta-upgrade value
- More interactivity and variety in the map and more content/replay value
- Clearer guidance on recipe discovery (a Steam thread asks how to intuit recipes)

#### Unique Things

- Necromancer workshop fantasy: you build the army from harvested body parts rather than picking premade units
- Hybrid of tower defense, action roguelite, light strategy and factory/automation management
- Tower upgrades (assembly line, cat house, blueprints) are gathered by destroying enemy lairs, with the goal of building an escape from the world
- Recipe book that unlocks cheaper, stronger versions of discovered combos

#### Tedious Or Disliked

- Slow, grindy start before upgrades make the game 'even out'
- Micromanaging minions or spamming the control spell because the AI cannot be trusted
- Heavy RNG on parts and repetitive late-game loops
- Needing several runs per meta upgrade
- Some players say they do not understand how to win, and a few threads ask whether micromanagement is required

#### Divisive Points

- Short length and low replayability: some call it fine value for $3, others call it too thin
- Meta progression: some find it a satisfying ramp, others say it swings from tangible to mandatory and can wreck a save
- Unit AI and autonomy: some enjoy the hands-off chaos, many consider it the main flaw
- Versus Necrosmith 2: several reviewers say the sequel is strictly better, while a few say the first is more forgiving than later entries
