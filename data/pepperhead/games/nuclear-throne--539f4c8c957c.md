### Community

- **One Liner:** Vlambeer's fast, brutal top-down roguelike shooter where a cast of mutants fights through irradiated wastelands toward the Nuclear Throne.
- **Consensus Summary:** Nuclear Throne is widely treated as a genre landmark of arcade-style action roguelikes: Metacritic 89 from 15 critics, Steam Overwhelmingly Positive (about 96% of roughly 19.8k reviews), and the last-30-day snapshot on the store page still Very Positive (92%). Players and critics praise its gunplay, game feel, sound and soundtrack, and run-to-run variety, and many reviewers describe it as addictive. The recurring complaint is difficulty that feels cheap to some players: near-instant deaths, no invincibility frames after a hit, and luck-heavy spawns. It spent over two years in Early Access (launched fully Dec 2015), went quiet for years, then got a surprise 10th-anniversary Update 100 in Dec 2025 (13th character, four-player co-op, widescreen, higher frame rate), which brought a wave of warm Steam reviews. The Metacritic user score (7.7 from 294 ratings) is noticeably lower than the critic score.
- **Reception Notes:** Launched fully Dec 5, 2015 after 2+ years in Early Access (over 100k units and $1M+ in Early Access). Critics were strongly positive (Metacritic 89); Eurogamer rated it Essential per Wikipedia. Later released on Switch (2019) and Xbox One (2021). After roughly eight years without updates and a 2020 studio disbanding, Update 100 arrived for the 10th anniversary in Dec 2025 with a 13th character, four-player online co-op (Nuclear Throne Together), widescreen support and 60+ fps; Steam reviews from that period are heavily positive and appreciative, though post-update issues like Remote Play Together problems and Steam Deck launch trouble show up in negative reviews. Revenue estimate from steam-revenue-calculator is about $8.1M gross and $2.4M net on Steam (Boxleiter-style estimate, order of magnitude only); SteamSpy lists 500k-1M owners. #### Best Things

- Gunplay and game feel: weapons feel punchy, with screen shake, audio feedback and fast movement (Steam reviews repeatedly mention 'juice' and crisp sound effects)
- Jukio Kallio's soundtrack and sound design, frequently singled out as a major reason the game resonates (an IGF audio honorable mention in 2015)
- Distinct cast of mutant characters, each with a special ability that changes playstyle, giving strong replay value
- Short, pick-up-and-play runs with an arcade score-chasing feel, plus daily challenges and loops past the final area for long-term play
- Clear readable action, bright pixel art and strong presentation; reviewers call it stylish and chaotic in a good way
- Weapon variety and mutation choices that add variety rather than making success mandatory, per several positive reviewers
- Modding community (e.g. Nuclear Throne Ultra mods mentioned by players) extends life of the game
- Developer goodwill: players highlight the studio reuniting years after disbanding to ship the anniversary update

#### Worst Things

- Perceived cheap deaths: many enemies and explosions can kill very fast, and the lack of post-hit invincibility frames is the most repeated Steam complaint
- Heavy reliance on RNG: spawn positions next to enemies or explosive objects, bad starting weapons, and chest-gated secrets can doom a run outside the player's control
- Final area difficulty, including a final boss that some players say can kill them before the fight properly starts
- Difficulty spike between mid areas (3-1 to 4-1 cited) and a progression where upgrades feel underwhelming compared to broken-build roguelikes
- Input feel issues: one reviewer says it cannot decide between controller and keyboard/mouse, each favoring shooting or dodging
- Technical roughness: complaints of clunky legacy engine quirks (melee through walls, fire passing diagonal tiles), Steam Deck/Proton startup trouble, and Remote Play Together co-op issues
- Limited depth compared to knowledge-heavy roguelikes; one fan review concedes it lacks the depth of other roguelikes and leans arcade

#### Improvements

- Add post-hit invincibility or a more forgiving damage model, or at least clearer telegraphing for enemy attacks and explosive hazards
- Reduce RNG cheapness: safer level-entry spawns, and less luck-gated secret and route access
- Better information presentation and level transitions, and more meaningful or powerful perks and passives
- Quality-of-life fixes and bug cleanup (Steam community has a huge bug report subforum); Steam Deck/Linux stability
- Steam Workshop support for mods (repeatedly requested in Steam discussions)
- More content updates; fans treated Update 100 as a rare positive exception and want more

#### Unique Things

- Extremely lean, bullet-hose arcade design where aggression and reading chaos matter more than build theory or item synergy
- Each character has a signature mechanic (e.g. Melting, Robot eating weapons, Fish rolling), making character choice a core part of identity
- Mutation pick per level-up as the primary build system, with weapons found in the run rather than meta-progression power
- Loop mode after beating the game, escalating enemies into harder cycles, offers a second layer of endgame
- Created from a 2013 48-hour charity game jam and developed with live 'performative development' on Twitch, which helped build its audience
- Stylized post-apocalyptic mood paired with a western-tinged guitar soundtrack

#### Tedious Or Disliked

- Death feels sudden and often uninformative, so players say they must die repeatedly just to learn what kills them
- Restarting to chase specific secret areas or required items when a chest roll misses
- Hazards placed next to objectives (e.g. explosive car beside a portal) that punish piercing weapons or allies
- Keyboard versus controller trade-offs and some clunky legacy behaviors
- Reflex-driven gameplay with limited learnable strategy frustrates players who prefer planning (comparisons to Noita and Enter the Gungeon appear in negative reviews)
- Too much damage too quickly: players say 6-10 'HP' is effectively 1-2 hits, so levels feel unfair for casual players

#### Divisive Points

- Whether the difficulty is fair and skill-based or cheap and RNG-driven; the same no-i-frames design is praised by fans as arcade purity and condemned by critics of the game
- Whether the lack of build depth is a feature (lean arcade) or a weakness compared to modern roguelikes
- Whether the game has aged: some call it still unmatched a decade later, others say later roguelikes took its best ideas and it would need an overhaul
