- **Summary:** R.E.P.O. is a first-person co-op horror game from the makers of Void Go where players scavenge loot, haul it to a truck, and use the earnings to buy items and permanent upgrades across increasingly dangerous levels. He admits it is only a roguelike to an extent and was included partly for its popularity. - **Rerank Note:**

##### Praise

- Proximity chat is hilarious, especially the death mechanic with a brief window to speak in a high-pitched voice
- Clunky physics and buggy moments are part of the charm and create funny accidents
- Monster encounters produce great panic and social moments with friends
- Lots of content and steady developer support adding monsters and things to discover
- Variety in items, guns, and player roles (carrier, runner)
- Two layers of enjoyment: discovering monsters, then mastering how to handle each

##### Criticism

- Single player is poorly balanced and has no real point; needs fewer big items, less aggressive enemies, or unique solo twists
- Dying early means a long wait to be revived, and you come back on low health, punishing less comfortable players
- Loop is repetitive (hide, come out, drag loot, hide) and depends heavily on having good friends
- Some enemies feel similar to each other
- Less confident players can end up being carried, which can be annoying

##### Unique Points

- Social proximity chat with a death-voice cooldown mechanic is the standout hook
- Clunkiness is deliberately beneficial to the comedy and charm
- Game design is basic but works precisely because it is basic

##### Comparisons

- Void Go (same creators)

##### Design Criteria

- Roguelike-ness: runs with randomized items and permanent stats, only a roguelike to an extent
- Multiplayer/social design: proximity chat and co-op chaos are the core appeal
- Single-player balance: weak, lacks tuning for solo
- Enemy design: fun spooky variety though some feel alike
- Death and revival penalty: long downtime and low health frustrating
- Repetition/depth: simple loop relies on friends
- Content and replayability: lots of items, monsters, ongoing updates
- Charm/feel: clunky physics add humor

#### Best Things

- Physics-driven looting is the core joke and the core challenge: every object has weight and can break, so carrying a piano or sofa through a doorway becomes a team puzzle
- Emergent, story-generating chaos with friends (accidental deaths, friendly fire, using teammates as projectiles, cart mishaps) is the most repeated reason for positive reviews
- Proximity voice chat makes monsters that react to noise and speech part of the comedy and tension
- Very low price ($9.99) for the amount of fun; reviewers call it great value
- Easy to learn: mechanics are simple enough that friends pick it up within a run
- Charming, slightly cute-creepy art and tone that mixes horror with slapstick
- Shop/upgrade loop between levels plus varied maps and many enemy types keeps runs from feeling linear
- Reviewers see strong Early Access potential
- No microtransactions promised, regular quality updates and no bad update since launch per long-time reviewers

#### Worst Things

- Balance and fairness: players say many deaths feel like bad luck or instant one-shots rather than their own mistakes, especially with tougher tier-3 monsters appearing very early
- Huntsman AI is called inconsistent, sometimes shooting the nearest player instead of the noise source, with complaints it is worse when hosting
- Solo play and small groups are described as punishing; one bad situation can force a full restart, and recent changes are said to hurt single-player
- Recent updates are accused of adding many new monsters of uneven quality (some trivial, some frustrating, e.g. a fast ghost and a bug that locks out defending) and spawning multiple enemies near the player at once
- Server timeout issues where matches close on their own, plus regional connection and visual bugs reported in the Steam community
- Motion sickness complaints: heavy head bob/sway and no FOV option
- Some long-term players say the game has declined since the October 2025 update, with new monsters raising difficulty more than adding interesting challenge

#### Improvements

- Add a difficulty slider or host options for monster spawns/tiers so mixed-skill groups and solo players can tune the experience
- Rebalance early levels so tier-3 monsters do not appear before players have tools and upgrades
- Rework Huntsman detection (clear audio warning before firing, always target the sound source) similar to Lethal Company's eyeless dogs
- Add accessibility options: FOV slider and reduced head bob/camera sway
- Improve server stability and fix match timeouts and regional connection issues
- Reduce RNG punishment in shop spawns and make solo-friendly tools (revive item, vehicles) more accessible; give solo players smaller, sturdier cargo and solo-tuned monsters

#### Unique Things

- Fully physics-based valuables that degrade in value when knocked around, so handling items carefully is the scoring mechanic, not just grabbing loot
- Horror-comedy co-op with up to 6 players, larger than most Lethal Company-like games
- Dead players can be carried back to extraction and revived, leading to a lot of dark teammate-handling humor
- Robot-themed setting with permanent upgrades bought at a Service Station using in-game currency, and monsters that also obey physics
- Roguelike run structure with randomized room layouts, item placement and enemy distribution, now with cosmetics and a racing mode

#### Tedious Or Disliked

- Restarting at level 1 repeatedly after unlucky runs; one reviewer described replaying level 1 ten times before reaching level 2
- Reliance on luck for shop stock and item spawns, which can gate progression
- Solo runs feeling too dangerous and slow, with vehicles awkward to loot with
- A Steam community thread complains the game becomes time-consuming too quickly
- Weapon-less early rounds: hiding spots reportedly fail and monsters find you, so early levels feel like hide-and-hope until weapons randomly appear in the shop

#### Divisive Points

- Difficulty: many love the brutal, funny fail-state while others say recent updates made it unfun and unfair, especially solo
- Direction of updates: cosmetics, racing mode and many new monsters are welcomed by some and seen by others as distracting from core balance
- Huntsman and similar enemies: some think they are fine, others consider them broken or unfair
- Accessibility debate: motion-sickness complaints drew a hostile reaction from some fans in review comments
