##### Praise

- Claw-machine premise is fun, with a clever pun title and a stop-motion trailer
- Characters have unique abilities and suggest different item builds for the machine
- Items added to the machine synergize with each other
- Unique claws are very satisfying, if slightly overpowered
- Sound design, especially the claw machine movement, is ASMR-like and satisfying
- Music is good and relics/upgrades are pretty good
- Works well on Steam Deck

##### Criticism

- A good grab feels great but a miss feels terrible and wastes a lot of time
- Limited strategy in how to grab; items are loose and easily dropped
- The normal claw is always identical with no way to upgrade it
- Final boss is always the same with a specific mechanic, so it can be too punishing or too easy depending on build
- Core gameplay could be more sophisticated

##### Unique Points

- A claw machine turned into a turn-based roguelike, with two claws per turn grabbing items to drop on enemies
- Build around adding item types (e.g. metal) to the machine itself, varying by character

##### Comparisons

- Poker roguelike
- Peglin

- Core gameplay feel: satisfaction of grabbing with the claw, but misses are punishing on time
- Synergies: machine items combine and differ per character
- Character diversity: unique abilities encourage distinct builds
- Meta-progression: unlocking new characters drove his enjoyment
- Boss design: fixed final boss feels too specific and not broad
- Audio: claw sound design and music are standout
- Upgrade depth: base claw lacks upgrades while unique claws are strong
- Charm/theme: novel claw machine concept and stop-motion trailer
- Content: early access, more content expected

### Community

- **One Liner:** A roguelike deckbuilder where your 'deck' is a pile of weapons and items in a physics-based claw machine, and you grab them turn by turn to fight through a 20-floor dungeon.
- **Consensus Summary:** Players rate it Very Positive on Steam (about 89-91% positive across roughly 2.4k-4k reviews, depending on the snapshot), and the core idea of clawing your attack turn is widely called satisfying and surprisingly addictive. The 1.0 release came on April 30, 2026 after Early Access that began in November 2024, and the developer kept shipping new characters and items through and after launch. The long-running complaints are heavy RNG (where items land, which combos you draw), weak balance across a bloated item pool, a single repeating final boss, and runs that take 30-60 minutes. #### Best Things

- The claw itself is fun and tactile: grabbing a cluster of items for a huge damage turn, or fumbling a grab, produces a constant dopamine-and-frustration loop
- Strong 'one more run' pull, with some long-time roguelite fans comparing playtime to Balatro, Slay the Spire and Monster Train
- Large cast of characters with distinct playstyles and many viable build archetypes (poison, frost, fire, melee and strength, gold/money builds)
- Cute, charming art and animations (bunny protagonist, strong intro and ending animations) that give it a distinct identity
- Easy to pick up; cheap price and a lot of hours for the money, with a cheap Steam price noted by several reviewers
- Token system that lets you nudge the odds of getting certain item types
- Frequent free updates adding new characters, items, pachinko/slot style minigames and difficulty changes during Early Access and after 1.0
- Relaxing enough to play casually or while watching something; full controller support

#### Worst Things

- Heavy RNG in both item drops and physics: key items land in corners, get stuck out of reach, or get flung out of the machine, and a run can be lost before your build comes together
- Balance is poor: many items are niche or never used, some characters are near-automatic wins while others need very specific pieces, and several builds (fire vs poison, water items with little water support) are seen as redundant or weak
- Item pool bloat from each update's new characters makes builds more RNG-dependent; some players say it now feels like a slot machine rather than a claw machine
- Only one final boss regardless of character or difficulty, and limited enemy and boss variety, so runs feel repetitive after the first few wins
- Difficulty spikes: enemies that are trivial and then one-shot you, and a last boss that floods the machine with debuffs and counters some builds outright
- Long runs (about 30-60 minutes even on the fastest speed) with mundane early fights; some say time-to-fun is poor
- Shallow meta-progression: unlocks are mostly tied to winning runs with each character, and higher 'Debt' difficulties mostly raise enemy damage
- Basic, unattractive map navigation screen
- Losses often feel unearned: enemies that are easy until they suddenly one-shot you, so a run can end on a bad grab or bad item spawn rather than a decision (top-voted negative Steam review)

#### Improvements

- Add more bosses, enemies and act variety, especially alternate final bosses
- Let unlocked claws/paws be used by other characters immediately instead of requiring a winning run with each character first
- Add claw-machine-level modifiers or tools (extra grabs, bigger claws, a shake/jostle to reset a stuck board, swappable claws) to reduce dead runs from bad item placement
- Rebalance the item pool and trim dead items; give materials/alchemy/transmutation and water-themed items real payoff
- Stronger meta-progression and more customization, plus more music and layout variety
- Respect player time with a faster-than-fastest speed setting, shorter run option, or skippable long minigames (slot machine minigame repeatedly called too slow); also requested: modding support, a short opt-in tutorial, and mobile cross-platform progression

#### Unique Things

- Combines a real physics claw machine with deckbuilder and roguelike structure, so your 'hand' is whatever the claw manages to hold
- Items can be given different materials (for example floating or buffed ones) that change how they behave in the machine
- Pachinko, slot and other gamble-style minigames and mystery locations mix into the dungeon route
- Claw-specific 'paws' per character and escalating difficulty levels (reviewers mention 'anger' levels; the store/dev term may be 'Debt') that add handicaps
- Rare genre mix: very few games make a claw machine the central mechanic, and it also exists on mobile/Switch/consoles

#### Tedious Or Disliked

- Stalemates where a scaling item is stuck in an unreachable corner, the enemy cannot kill you, and the battle drags on
- Having to win a run with every character before unlocking their paws/items for others, which slows experimentation
- Relearning the game after updates that add characters or rework the difficulty system, particularly for completionists chasing achievements
- Slot machine minigame and long fights in early floors that get skipped or endured
- Repetitive runs after the first win: once a combo is set around floors 7-9, the remaining floors play out identically

#### Divisive Points

- Luck versus skill: some reviewers say the later updates added real strategy and it is not luck based, while others say it is now pure lottery
- Difficulty: some call it one of the hardest roguelites they have played, while others say experienced roguelite players can 'moonwalk' to victory or win on the first run
- Content and value: fans with 100-200 hours call it a must-buy at its price, while others say the content runs out after 10 hours and is not worth $14.99
- Frequent content updates are praised by most but seen by some as bloating the item pool and breaking balance
