##### Praise

- Shooting and hit feedback feel great, with clear visual damage on enemies
- Deep gun customization (e.g. shotgun into flamethrower or sniper) with many enchantment combinations
- Discovery-based cooking system where you combine random items to unlock recipes
- Lots of loot and content, even while still in early access
- Persistent gore, blood and barrel debris make dungeons feel immersive
- 3D world with 2D sprites is a cool, well-working art style
- High-stakes permadeath of equipment creates pressure and a Darkest Dungeon-like loot-and-return loop

##### Criticism

- Very slow to get going; the first cave takes long with bad gear and no understanding
- Inventory management is a mess with no rearrange option
- Gameplay is repetitive, mostly backing into corridors and shooting enemies
- No stealth option to get a first shot on important enemies
- Dungeons and biomes are samey: corridor, room, corridor, room; few bosses and few unique events
- Some bosses, like the cathedral one, are badly designed and ridiculous; some equipment is just stats

##### Unique Points

- Extraction-shooter structure within a roguelike, losing a gun is like losing a character in Darkest Dungeon
- Cooking and recipe discovery by experimentation
- Rare special moments like the sewer swim to a meta-progression reward are great and he wants many more
- Some items with unique effects such as double jump show the potential for more interesting gear

##### Comparisons

- Darkest Dungeon

##### Design Criteria

- Gameplay feel: shooting and hit feedback must feel good
- Risk vs reward: permanent loss of equipment on death raises pressure
- Customization and build depth: gun mods and enchantments
- Content volume and discovery: loot, recipes, cooking
- Level design variety: corridor-room repetition across similar biomes
- Enemy and boss design: few bosses, some poorly designed
- Pacing: slow, grindy early game
- Quality of life: inventory management
- Visuals: 3D with 2D sprites, persistent gore
- Item interest: unique effects versus plain stat items

### Community

- **One Liner:** A single-player first-person shooter from Swedish studio Perfect Random that blends a looter-shooter with a PvE extraction-style roguelite: you dive through biomes, loot, cook, craft and enchant guns, and lose everything you carry if you die before getting home.
- **Consensus Summary:** SULFUR is received as a strong, distinctive Early Access hit: Steam shows Very Positive at roughly 87.6% of about 10,500 reviews, with the launch-period score around 90%. Players praise snappy, satisfying gunplay, the deep weapon modification and crafting systems, the lo-fi 3D-plus-2D-sprite art and soundtrack, and the tense lose-it-all risk/reward loop. The most vocal criticism in recent reviews is about balance philosophy: several big patches (oil and scroll rebalance, ammo caps, durability costs, removal of picture-in-picture scopes) nerfed popular builds and pushed the game toward a slower, more hardcore economy, which many see as a betrayal of the power fantasy. Critic coverage is very thin (Metacritic lists only two early-access critic reviews, so no Metascore), so reception is driven almost entirely by players. The game is still tagged Early Access on Steam with ongoing updates (Dungeons &amp; Magic in Jan 2025, later Qiosk's Plenty and Scholar's updates, ghosts, endless mode) and console versions are advertised on the official site.
- **Reception Notes:** Launched into Steam Early Access on Oct 28, 2024 at $24.99 (the page warned the price would rise at 1.0) and was well received from the start, with a free demo helping discovery and frequent discount sales. Post-launch patches were frequent (for example 0.19.x patches and the Dungeons &amp; Magic update in Jan 2025, which added a dungeon overhaul, melee knockback, 16 new oils and quick-sort). Later in 2025 and 2026, reviews show mood splitting over big balance updates (Qiosk's Plenty, which replaced picture-in-picture scopes, and the Scholar's Update that rebalanced 100+ oils and capped ammo), alongside a later ghost mechanic and an endless mode. Despite the negative patch-day reviews, overall Steam score stayed Very Positive and long-time players report very high playtime, and several 2026 reviews call it a possible GOTY. #### Best Things

- Gunplay feels snappy and punchy, with distinct weapon handling, strong sound design and satisfying gore and enemy hit reactions
- Deep weapon-building: oils, scrolls, enchantments and attachments let you make oddball guns (homing magnum, near-instant LMG reloads, infinite-ammo style builds)
- High-stakes risk/reward: dying loses your whole loadout, and deciding when to use the recharge-limited teleport amulet creates constant push-your-luck tension
- Distinctive art style mixing 3D environments with 2D vector-style sprite enemies that have layered damage and vital areas
- Strong soundtrack, dry humour and a quirky lore without a heavy story burden
- Stash, storage and area unlocks give a sense of steady progression, and the hoarding/cooking/crafting side loops are addictive for many
- Flexible pacing: can be played as a slow, careful explorer or a fast bullet frenzy; an easy mode removes the death penalties
- Runs well, is good value around $25, and ships frequent updates (though some reviewers question the developers' balance direction)

#### Worst Things

- Major balance patches nerfed the most-used oils, scrolls and attachments (including rebalancing 100+ oils and adding an ammo cap), making many builds unviable, and changes apply retroactively to already-built guns
- Weapon durability drain per shot makes multi-projectile and heavily modded guns break quickly, forcing repair kits and discouraging experimentation
- Many scrolls and attachments are seen as strictly worse than an empty slot or are outclassed by a few best-in-slot picks, so build variety is narrower than it looks
- Repetitive level and enemy structure: several levels per biome feel alike and many enemies are faction reskins, with large rooms that trigger everything at once
- Enemy AI is considered easy to exploit and player power can outscale threats, while a handful of fast or cheesy enemies and bosses spike difficulty
- Endless mode scaling is criticised as unbalanced (enemy HP, size and damage scale while the player mostly scales HP), and reaching stage 20 takes hours for an achievement
- Some players feel it lacks a clear identity, doing neither roguelite meta-progression (stamps feel like minor QoL) nor extraction shooting as deeply as dedicated games
- Bugs and corruption worries have been reported, including save file issues, controller and Steam Deck quirks, and dialogue pacing bugs

#### Improvements

- Buff weak oils, scrolls and attachments instead of nerfing strong ones, so more builds are viable
- Add inventory sorting and the ability to suspend or save a run mid-dungeon (quick-sort was later added in the Dungeons &amp; Magic update, suspend remained a request in reviews)
- Add character progression or more meta-progression beyond gear, since some players say only storage and items progress
- More biomes and late-game content, since players who beat the desert area reported running out of levels
- Tune endless mode scaling and remove the multi-hour achievement requirement
- Improve enemy AI and variety, and reduce the grind needed to re-gear after dying in later areas
- Restore picture-in-picture scopes or fix the underlying optimisation so scopes feel good again

#### Unique Things

- Combines a looter-shooter and a PvE extraction loop in a single-player roguelite, with no classic meta-progression beyond what you stash and what you learn
- Hand-held teleport amulet you must charge at mid-run or pay for, and which you trigger manually, so you choose when to bail out
- Cooking, enchanting and oil-based gun modding form a crafting-heavy economy that players describe as an extraction cooking game with guns
- Weapon level-up by using the gun, with upgrade slots and powers that cost durability
- Insurance attachment that protects a single item, a rare and expensive hedge against the lose-everything rule
- Visual identity of flat, stylised 3D worlds with 2D sprite enemies, plus gory limb and organ looting

#### Tedious Or Disliked

- Grinding weapon ranks before you can fully mod a gun, made more costly by patches that stopped melee kills from awarding gun XP unless the gun hit the enemy first
- Re-running earlier areas to rebuild gear after dying in a late area, because all loot is lost on death
- Inventory hoarding and Tetris-style stash management, and the lack of an easy sort in earlier versions
- Ghosts added in a later update that can drain health, ammo and healing once they catch you, which some say wrecked atmosphere and difficulty curve
- Repeated same-feeling levels and the same few weapons used through an entire act, which drags out runs
- Annoying named threats called out repeatedly, such as forest birds, the castle boss that blinds and ignites you, and ambush dog packs
- Endless mode's stage-20 achievement takes roughly 4-8 hours in one session, which reviewers call a pointless time sink

#### Divisive Points

- Hardcore direction vs power fantasy: some welcome tradeoffs and scarcity, others feel developers are repeating the Helldivers-style pattern of nerfing what players enjoyed
- Death penalty: loses-everything is the loved tension for many, but others find it punishing enough to cause rage-quitting (an easy mode softens it)
- Difficulty and learning curve: first hours are confusing and hard, but once it clicks many say the game becomes easy and trivial versus the AI
- Whether it is a real roguelite or an extraction shooter in disguise, and whether that mix is a strength or a lack of identity
