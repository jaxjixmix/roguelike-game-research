### Commentary


#### Entry 1

- **Label:** Tier list Part 1 (2023)
- **Summary:** Voidigo is a chaotic, visually striking top-down, 3D-ish action shooter roguelike. He already covered it in a chapter of his roguelike design analysis video and calls it great. The standout is that nearly every item changes gameplay or visuals rather than tweaking a number. It can get overwhelming and is easy to break, but he enjoys the power fantasy. He places it in A tier, near the top.
- **Quote:** games that allow the player to become insanely powerful every now and then is very rewarding
- **Rerank Note:**

##### Praise

- Upgrades and items are each unique and change gameplay or visuals, not just stat numbers
- Very chaotic and visually pleasing; what you see is what you get
- Lets the player become absurdly powerful, which feels rewarding
- Enjoys that the game can be broken occasionally; satisfying
- Feels powerful and rewarding more often than many other games

##### Criticism

- Can be overwhelming to look at, especially with repeat loops
- Screen fills with enemies and explosions in later loops
- Easy to break; he hit very low frame rate
- Borders on being a little too much

##### Unique Points

- Nearly every item fundamentally changes something rather than adjusting a stat
- Deliberate insane chaos as the core design goal
- Design lesson: allowing the player to become overpowered now and then is rewarding, even with balance valued highly

##### Comparisons

- The Binding of Isaac

##### Design Criteria

- Item/upgrade uniqueness: items change gameplay rather than numbers
- Power fantasy: ability to get overpowered feels rewarding
- Balance: valued highly, but breakability is forgiven
- Visual clarity: chaos can become overwhelming
- Performance: frame rate drops in late loops
- Replayability via loops: repeat loops escalate enemies and explosions

### Community

- **Consensus Summary:** Players love it: Steam shows Overwhelmingly Positive (about 96.5% of roughly 4,900 reviews), with many calling it an underrated hidden gem comparable to Nuclear Throne, Enter the Gungeon and The Binding of Isaac. #### Best Things

- Huge pool of weapons and power-ups (reviewers cite hundreds) that stays reasonably balanced and gives strong replayability
- Fluid, snappy pixel animation, bouncy combat feel and satisfying gameplay loop
- Backflip/jump plus stomp combos give movement a distinct identity versus a standard dash or roll
- Level design built around finding and cleansing corrupted beacons makes most enemy encounters optional rather than lock-in arena rooms
- Strong music, sound effects, character design and humor; collected items are visibly represented on the character
- Multiple difficulty levels and a quick, useful tutorial; early runs feel fair rather than impossible
- Extremely cheap at $4.99 with local co-op and Steam Deck / full controller support
- Boss-focused structure with varied enemies and bosses that flee mid-fight
- Widely called a criminally underrated hidden gem and outstanding value, the most repeated theme in top reviews

#### Worst Things

- Screen clutter and visual chaos, especially late-game with lots of particles and projectiles
- Some weapons and starting items feel weak or useless, with melee weapons most often called out
- Final boss and ending disappoint some players: the last boss resembles a regular run boss and post-credits reward is mostly a harder difficulty
- Technical complaints: 60 FPS cap, stuttering reported by some, limited graphics settings and slowdown on laptops
- Art style and pixel look does not land for everyone; a couple of reviewers mention motion sickness or find it too busy
- Feels derivative to some, described as a mix of existing twin-stick roguelikes without much that is new
- Content can feel thin after a number of runs

#### Improvements

- Online multiplayer (co-op is local/split-screen only) is the most repeated request
- More graphics and performance options plus an uncapped frame rate
- Deeper item synergies and character-specific mechanics to make builds feel more distinct
- Rebalance weak starter items, perks and melee weapons
- More post-game content or a stronger ending, plus more content overall
- A sequel or new content from semiwork, asked for frequently since R.E.P.O.'s success

#### Unique Things

- Jump/backflip mover with stomp attacks used in combos, unlike dash-based twin-stick shooters
- Beacon-cleansing objective structure that makes enemy rooms optional and lets players rush the boss
- Wacky improvised weapons (for example wielding Mjolnir or martial-arts staff builds) and items that visibly show on the character
- Bosses that flee mid-battle, forcing chase and positioning decisions
- Split-screen local co-op and Remote Play Together in a genre often single-player only

#### Tedious Or Disliked

- Stomp mechanic is seen as forced by some, with parts of the game depending on it
- Late-game chaos can reduce play to spamming dodges rather than deliberate movement
- Hunting for weapons or perks that feel good can be unrewarding early on
- Unlocking everything (for example the last character essence) is confusingly unclear for completionists
- Repetition after several runs reported by lower-hour reviewers

#### Divisive Points

- Art style and visual busyness: loved as stylish and full of personality by many, called cluttered and off-putting by others
- Stomp and movement system: praised as distinctive, criticised as shoved onto the player
- Ending and final boss: some call the game a near-perfect S-tier but find the finale a letdown
- Originality: some see a refreshing take on the sub-genre, others see just another pixel-art twin-stick roguelike
