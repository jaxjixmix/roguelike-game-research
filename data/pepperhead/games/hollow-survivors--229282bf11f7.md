### Commentary


#### Entry 1

- **Part:** 4
- **Label:** Tier list Part 4 (2025)
- **Summary:** Hollow Survivors is a fairly traditional action roguelike where you climb a tower out of hell, with a Hades-style hub between runs and survivorlike hordes of enemies in the rooms. He was drawn in by its vibe and art style, and he enjoyed the journey to the ending thanks to meaningful meta progression. After completing it, though, he finds content and replayability thin: boons are the only thing that changes a run, and there are no shops, events or weapon-specific upgrades. He places it in the low-to-middle B tier for now and hopes for more content updates.

- **Rerank Note:**

##### Praise

- Great vibe and art style, with visually distinct biomes
- Hub world with characters and permanent upgrades between runs, Hades-inspired
- Fast-paced, hectic core gameplay, especially after unlocking the double dash
- Infinite wall-less arenas feel immersive
- Starting weapons each have their own move set
- Meta progression makes the path to the ending a satisfying adventure
- Challenging bosses and difficulty before meta upgrades are unlocked

##### Criticism

- Little content: few unique events, NPC interactions or unique floors, and mostly the same hordes
- Room rewards are weak; boons are rare and the only thing that changes a run
- No shop or mid-run currency, so little player choice during a run
- Many boons are not impactful, and weapons lack unique upgrades so runs feel alike
- Replayability is weak after the ending; hard mode gives no real reward once everything is unlocked
- Could lean harder into the survivorlike aspect with far more enemies

##### Unique Points

- Blends Hades-like hub and structure with survivorlike horde rooms
- Endless wall-less floors where you never run out of things to fight
- Design lesson: mid-run currency and a shop would add player agency and reward choice

##### Comparisons

- Hades
- Survivorlikes

##### Design Criteria

- Meta-progression: important and satisfying, but runs out once everything is unlocked
- Replayability: weak because runs feel similar and weapons lack unique upgrades
- Run variety and events: too few unique events, floors or NPC interactions
- Player choice and rewards: no shop or mid-run currency, rare boons
- Boon impact: some exciting, many not very impactful
- Visuals and vibe: strong art style and distinct biomes
- Combat feel and pace: fast, hectic, especially with double dash
- Difficulty: hard bosses early, hard mode lacks incentive

#### Best Things

- Fast, fluid, snappy combat when kiting hordes; several reviewers enjoy the roguelite-deckbuilder plus bullet-hell mix
- Pixel art and grim atmosphere praised even by players who dislike the gameplay
- Progression feels smooth and quick, with a steady ladder of unlocks so you rarely feel stuck
- Bosses, weapons (scythe, great axe and others) and spells give variety; spell-based upgrades are the ones with real synergies
- Story has a grim opening and a Loop Hero-like tone that one reviewer found enjoyable
- All achievements obtainable through normal play with no guides needed
- Difficulty described as fair, not too easy or too hard, for a cheap indie; controller support and Steam Cloud included

#### Worst Things

- Clunky combat: touching enemies deals damage, attacks lunge you forward into enemies, and dashes lack full invincibility so you often dash into damage
- Shallow content: only a handful of unlockable weapons and subweapons, and the same power-ups recur
- Most upgrade picks are generic stat buffs or simple triggers; few meaningful synergies outside spells
- Confusing boss hitboxes and inconsistent drops (a run with no health orbs, then four or five in one stage)
- No in-game encyclopedia or explanation of upgrade-card mechanics; weapons can only be judged from panel stats
- Price feels steep for the amount of content (about 10 USD) according to several reviewers
- Higher difficulties scale weakly, so post-game replay value is limited

#### Improvements

- Add an in-game codex and clearer explanations for upgrade cards and curse mechanics
- Fix contact-damage and forward-lunge combat, and give dashes real invincibility frames
- Add more content: more weapons, subweapons, enemies, stories or quests per character, and stronger endgame difficulty scaling
- Rework upgrade pools so there are fewer dead cards (curse cards need their enabler) and more build-defining synergies
- Make unlock and material acquisition less grindy, since rare materials come only from late bosses and boss rewards are random
- Post-launch support: a player notes no updates since Aug 2025

#### Unique Things

- Survivors-style auto-fight fused with a per-stage reward pick (alternating essence currency and upgrades) instead of XP-gem levelling
- Hades-like camp where you invest currency in permanent stat upgrades (one reviewer calls this artificial friction)
- Fixed-length tower (about 20 to 24+ floors) with a handful of set bosses rather than endless survival

#### Tedious Or Disliked

- Grind for meta upgrades: one reviewer with 4 hours and thousands of coins said the game still felt like its first five minutes
- Alternative weapons and spells are a grind to unlock and some are no better than the starter weapon
- Rare materials and keys come only from the latest bosses, punishing players who usually die around floor 17
- The per-floor reward alternation produces a linear, boring power climb
- Repetitive enemies and same-feeling moves become monotonous after about an hour for some

#### Divisive Points

- Whether combat feels fast and snappy or jerky and clunky, especially the forward lunge and contact damage
- Whether it is a worthwhile 10 USD buy or only a discount pick for its amount of content
