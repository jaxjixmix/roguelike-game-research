- Satisfying power fantasy of becoming completely overpowered and broken
- Chaotic, fun, explosion-filled gameplay that does not take itself seriously
- Level-up stat wheel gives wildly random boosts (5% vs 50% crit damage)
- Different game modes and several characters that play a little differently
- Hub world meta-progression points that let you max stats once you learn to break it

- Deliberately unbalanced, with no real care for balance
- Abilities are fun but somewhat forgettable

- Visual mess, such as the screen filling with a single colour from massive explosions

##### Unique Points

- Random stat wheel with huge variance between outcomes
- Breaking the game quickly unlocks full meta-upgrade maxing in about three or four runs

### Community

- **One Liner:** A cheap 3D-rendered, top-down roguelike auto-shooter (bullet-heaven/survivor-like) where you kite thousands of goofy blobs across islands, beat bosses and stack item and weapon synergies, from solo Swedish studio Hoganas Spel.
- **Consensus Summary:** Goobies is Very Positive on Steam (about 91% of roughly 1,960 reviews, 1,868 counted on the store page) and sells for about $6, so most players treat it as a charming, cheap, easy-to-pick-up survivor-like. The praise centres on the bubbly art style, build combos and quick runs. The criticism is slow progression, repetitive enemies and maps, balance that breaks once everything is unlocked, severe late-game lag and clutter, and cloud/save problems. It has no Metacritic critic coverage and no confirmed OpenCritic coverage, so reception is almost entirely player-driven. The developer shipped patches from 1.0.6 (Aug 2023) to 1.1.3 (Jan 2024), plus a small artifact tweak in Oct 2025, then announced development was on hold to focus on the sequel. Recent negative reviews complain of no content updates, and Goobies 2 (third-person co-op, demo out, 2026 release) is the successor.

#### Best Things

- Cute, goofy, bubbly blob visuals and humorous character names give it a distinctive charm; even negative reviewers call the look its strongest part
- Simple to start and easy to begin or abandon a run, which suits short sessions and casual players
- Item and weapon synergies, including 'lag-inducing' combos, and weapon upgrades that evolve into stronger versions with added traits rather than plain stat boosts
- Satisfying power fantasy of mowing through swarms of thousands of blobs; described as addictive and mindless in a good way
- Five champions with different passives, 30+ items and 20 artifacts give a fair amount of build variety for the price
- Developer was praised in Steam reviews for quickly implementing player suggestions (leaderboards, rerolls, boss mutations)
- Low price (about $6) and low barrier; one reviewer suggests it is a gentle, non-violent way to introduce kids to the genre

#### Worst Things

- Severe late-game performance problems: FPS drops, lag that can crash the engine, and lag from large numbers of XP pickups
- Visual clutter in mid and late game makes threats hard to read ('I can't see anything')
- Repetitive content: basic maps, low enemy and boss variety, and runs feel same-y after a few hours
- Balance issues: with everything unlocked from the start and no real stacking limits, builds converge and runs feel too easy or dull; some also call RNG harsh
- Cloud save handling is a recurring complaint; some players report save resets or wiped progress after touching save settings
- Bugs: Queen units spawning inside terrain and becoming invincible, a controller menu selection bug, and swarm spawns landing on the player
- Development has been paused, with no major content updates in a long time

#### Improvements

- Fix late-game optimization and lag from pickups and enemy counts
- Add more content: enemy and boss variety, maps, and a real objective or ending for runs, since runs are endless
- Rework balance and add limits or unlock gating so builds stay varied and challenge holds up
- Improve readability: transparent projectiles, fewer cluttering effects, and options to reduce screen shake or protagonist motion
- Make cloud saves work reliably and expose clear save management
- Quality-of-life and feature requests from reviews: multiplayer, better mouse and dual-monitor handling, and better keyboard control for some characters

#### Unique Things

- Soft, inky, non-violent blob aesthetic in 3D with a bouncy momentum-based player character, unlike gritty Vampire Survivors clones
- Island-to-island structure with discrete levels and a boss at each island, instead of one continuous timer
- Weapons evolve into stronger variants with added traits rather than just stat upgrades
- Boss mutations that accumulate over time, a daily leaderboard and a 'Mega Crit' mechanic (added in patches)
- Item and roulette rerolls (one per island) plus artifacts that change core mechanics
- Sequel Goobies 2 adds third-person camera, co-op, genetic mutations and Omens, showing the series moving beyond top-down

#### Tedious Or Disliked

- Slow levelling: around level 40 players report roughly one level-up per world or boss
- Steep meta-progression grind that feels unrewarding for the hours invested
- Kiting blobs in circles and dodging AOEs against a limited roster of enemies feels repetitive
- Endless runs with no satisfying conclusion; the only real goal is Steam achievements
- Late-game lag and screen clutter turn the endgame into a chore
- Motion sickness from the bouncing or wobbling protagonist movement around screen centre, reported by at least one reviewer

#### Divisive Points

- Simplicity: praised as approachable and kid-friendly, but others find the strategy too shallow and boring
- Difficulty and balance: some love the broken combos, others say everything unlocked from the start makes runs trivial and samey
- Momentum-based player movement feels charming to some and awkward or nauseating to others
- Value: at about $6 many call it a bargain, while some say it does not respect your time and recommend other genre entries
