### Community

- **One Liner:** An open-world, single-player roguelite deckbuilder by Supercombo in which you lead a party of three anthropomorphic-animal heroes across a semi-open procedural map to stop Akhan the Calamity.
- **Consensus Summary:** Players rate it Very Positive on Steam (about 82% of roughly 817 reviews), with praise for its hero variety, the three-character party and the editable deck, and a polished cartoon-animal look. Critics covered it only lightly: the two OpenCritic reviews (6.5 and 6 out of 10) call it enjoyable but complex, difficult and sometimes restrictive. Recurring complaints are a long grind to unlock everything, a slow UI and run pace, uneven hero balance, weak tutorialisation, and cards that turn out to be samey. It left early access on 22 September 2022 with three new heroes and an Arena mode, and later came to Epic, iOS and Netflix Games. It is a niche, low-volume title today, with only a handful of concurrent players on Steam.

#### Best Things

- Party of three heroes with a hand and deck for each, so every turn is a puzzle of splitting action points between characters
- Lots of heroes to play with and many party and difficulty combinations, which keeps things feeling fresh
- Deck is fully editable outside and between fights, with no forced bloat as in Slay the Spire style games
- Semi-open map you can roam freely, choosing which regions to hit for rewards or XP instead of following a fixed node tree
- Appealing cartoon-animal art style that draws people in
- Lots of unlockables (cards, artifacts, ability customization) and multiple difficulty levels give long-term replay; reviewers with 80 to 110+ hours report it
- Fun, approachable casual deckbuilder that is easy to pick up for relaxed sessions
- Items and artifacts can be reused after battle, which players found a pleasant surprise

#### Worst Things

- Heavy unlock and achievement grind; meta-progression needs a huge number of runs
- Slow UI and very large maps make a single run take a long time, so it is hard to fit in a session
- Hero balance is uneven, with some characters strong and others near useless
- Explanations are poor: artifacts and effects like Burn are unclear or lack audio feedback, and the game is hard to learn
- Card pool per hero is narrow, so builds get samey and depth can be false
- UI pain points: artifacts are hard to look up, and cards must be dragged rather than click-and-target
- Some boss mechanics (removing action points, damage immunity after a threshold) feel like turn denial instead of interactive challenge
- Bugs reported: quest rewards not granted, map nodes becoming undiscovered on reload

#### Improvements

- Add click-to-target card play and controller support (currently drag and drop only; no controller)
- Add an undo option for mistaken moves
- Improve tutorials and in-game tooltips or an artifact compendium, and add audio/visual feedback for status effects
- Speed up the UI and offer smaller or faster run formats; rebalance the unlock grind
- Expand card pools and rebalance weak heroes so builds are more varied
- Let players change their ultimate ability mid-run, possibly at a cost, to adapt to found artifacts and cards

#### Unique Things

- Three-hero party with three separate decks and hands, instead of the usual single-character deckbuilder
- Open-world semi-open procedural 3D map where you can travel anywhere and start from any point, and enemies get harder the longer you linger in a province
- Completely editable deck with a collection you swap cards in and out of, inverting the no-removal convention of the genre
- Hero leveling and ability card unlocks persist between runs, giving it an RPG progression flavour
- Arena mode focused purely on combat against corrupted versions of the world's heroes
- Distinct anthropomorphic animal setting mixing magic and technology

#### Tedious Or Disliked

- Unlock grind for heroes, cards and achievements described as abysmal by some players
- Runs are long and the UI is slow, so even one run per sitting is hard
- Dragging cards on every play is fiddly
- Having to manage three hands and many factors per turn is demanding and at times confusing because of poor explanation
- Late bosses can shut down your plan through AP loss or damage immunity, which feels unfair to some
- Lack of controller support and no undo irritate some players

#### Divisive Points

- Depth: some find unending combinations and a lot to learn, others call it false depth with few viable builds per hero
- Difficulty and complexity: praised as a challenge by some and cited by critics as limiting its audience to hardcore players
- Editable deck: seen as freeing by some and as undermining deckbuilding tension by others
