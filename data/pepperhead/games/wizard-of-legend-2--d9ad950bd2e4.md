#### Best Things

- Fast, responsive combat and character control; several reviewers say it feels smooth and natural, with i-frame dashing
- Large mix-and-match Arcana system across six elements (fire, water, earth, lightning, air, chaos) that supports experimental builds
- 4-player online co-op plus local split screen; friends describe it as chaotic fun and the best way to play it
- Good-looking stylised 3D visuals, enemy variety and biomes that do not overstay their welcome
- Bosses are fun and have combo-style attack sets that become manageable once learned

#### Worst Things

- Content cut versus the original: one detailed reviewer cites about 71 spells vs about 204 and about 145 relics vs about 315, with only 10 relic slots; many others note fewer Arcana and relics generally
- Online co-op bugs and netcode: desyncs, softlocks, crashes that scale with player count, and no ability to reconnect to a run
- Campaign is short (reports of about 4-9 hours to beat, one claiming a very fast clear) and only a handful of biomes, so replay feels repetitive
- Dialogue and voice acting criticised as excessive, badly directed and miscast, with the player character lacking personality
- Boss and enemy telegraphs often judged too short or unclear (few audio cues), plus knockback and fall damage frustrations
- Feels soulless and less snappy than the original: several top reviews (including a 1000-hour original player) call it sluggish, derivative of Hades, and stripped of the 2D style's charm
- Balance problems: lightning and some builds are far stronger than others, while many upgraded Arcana or trinkets feel weak or useless
- $24.99 price judged too high for the content and stability by many
- Abandoned after 1.0: the most-upvoted recent negative reviews say updates and developer communication stopped, leaving bugs and missing content unresolved

#### Improvements

- Restore cut content: more Arcana, relics, cloaks and possibly more relic slots, plus a gems-to-gold style conversion some miss from the original
- Fix multiplayer netcode, add reconnect/rejoin and pause options, and resolve softlocks and crashes
- Add more biomes, enemy variety and run variety to lengthen the campaign and reduce repetition
- Better tutorials and in-game explanations of spells and mechanics (flagged by reviewers as unclear)
- Co-op quality-of-life: option to reduce ally skill transparency/clutter, shared loot from events, and a possible return of a PVP mode
- Clearer telegraphs and audio cues for attacks, and ability to bank the ultimate; fewer forced dialogue interruptions
- Communicate a roadmap and ship the promised updates, since silence from the developer is itself a top complaint

#### Unique Things

- Full 3D take on the Wizard of Legend formula with 4-player online co-op (the original was 2-player)
- Arcana system where each spell has variants/enhancements and elemental mixing drives build identity
- Dash gives invincibility and enemy contact does not hurt you, changing the feel from the original's harsher rules
- Hub with a large cast of talkative NPCs and a Hades-like story framing around the Floating Lands
- Spell-combo focus combined with local split-screen and Remote Play Together options, uncommon among action roguelites

#### Tedious Or Disliked

- Unlock grind: basic starting equipment and badges are locked behind repeated runs, which some find annoying compared with the first game
- Progression feels unrewarding and weakly meaningful; starting strength barely grows between runs
- Runs repeat the same enemy order and biome sequence, so variety fades quickly
- Constant NPC chatter and lengthy, poorly voiced dialogue during hub and runs
- Multiplayer clutter making it hard to read attacks, plus one-person-only event rewards that force loot negotiation

#### Divisive Points

- Difficulty: some say it is easier than the original (easy to clear, dash immunity), others find bosses overtuned and punishing
- Whether it should be judged as a standalone game or as a sequel; newcomers often rate it fairly while original fans call it a step backward
- Solo vs co-op value: solo is called fine by some and not recommended by others, while co-op is the usual recommendation
- Art direction: seen by some as attractive and cartoonish, by others as generic and murky versus the original's clean 2D look
