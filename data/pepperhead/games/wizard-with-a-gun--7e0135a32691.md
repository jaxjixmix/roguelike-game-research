### Community

- **One Liner:** An isometric, Don't Starve-styled survival-roguelite shooter from Galvanic Games (published by Devolver Digital) in which you craft guns, elemental bullets and a wizard-tower base, then drop into procedurally generated worlds that collapse on a five-minute Chaos timer, solo or in 1-4 player co-op.
- **Consensus Summary:** Players rate it Very Positive on Steam (about 80 percent of roughly 4,000 reviews) and it carries a Mixed Metacritic critic score of 72 from 25 reviews, with Metacritic user score at 5.5 on a tiny sample. Nearly everyone loves the art, music and the gun-plus-bullet-plus-powder crafting system, and co-op is the best way to play. The recurring complaints are a core loop that runs out of surprises, grindy material gathering, clunky controls, weak solo balance and a thin or anticlimactic endgame. It is effectively finished: the Better Together update (May 2024) added four-player co-op and custom difficulty, and Galvanic Games closed in June 2024, so the game is not getting the extra content it hinted at.

#### Best Things

- Art direction, Don't Starve-like look and soundtrack are praised by both positive and negative reviewers
- The gun, bullet and powder crafting system is the standout: many bullet types, elements and gun classes (rifle, shotgun, Uzi, minigun, mortar, bounty weapons) that combine in fun ways
- Co-op with friends (up to four players after the May 2024 update) is where most people say it shines
- The five-minute end-times timer, extendable by killing Chaos enemies, keeps hub-to-expedition pacing tense
- Base building and gradually unlocking crafting stations and benches gives a satisfying sense of progression
- Engaging lore and a clear cliffhanger-style setup, plus biome and boss variety with distinct mechanics
- Good value on sale; PC Gamer called it a lean roguelite that does not waste your time

#### Worst Things

- Core loop becomes repetitive after a handful of runs; several reviewers say they had seen everything in 7 to 15 hours
- Controls and aiming feel loose or clunky on both mouse and gamepad; the camera angle makes judging distance awkward
- Combat and difficulty feel balanced for co-op, leaving solo play weaker (no self-revive) and some enemy abilities stun-lock you into losing your whole inventory
- Enemies and bosses are considered bland and unthreatening; the final boss and ending are called anticlimactic (IGN)
- Limited inventory space and having to run between 20+ chests in the base make crafting and resource logistics tedious
- Sparse endgame content; gameplay feels like a mobile or tablet game to some, with Unity-related rough edges and occasional connection or performance problems
- Multiplayer progression is host-centric: some achievements and progress only count for the host, with no cross-progression for guests

#### Improvements

- Better solo balance and a revive or escape option for solo players
- Clearer teaching of the crafting system and of niche mechanics (the summon-object tree, force bullets, powder combinations) which many players never use
- A better way to organize and pull materials for crafting, such as shared storage, since players resort to mods to avoid chest-running
- More powder and synergy variety plus properly tuned harder difficulties
- Tighter aiming, camera and control feel, including weapon-slot switching when out of ammo
- More endgame content and a stronger final boss and ending; content the devs seemed to have planned (DLC, more zones) never arrived

#### Unique Things

- Reimagines a wizard fantasy as a gunsmith game: customizable guns fed with crafted elemental bullets and powders rather than spell lists
- Mixes Don't Starve-style crafting and base building with extraction-style loot risk: die and you lose what you carry, so you must run home
- Five-minute end-of-the-world timer that progressively destroys the map and can be extended by killing Chaos beings
- Bullet effects interact with the world, for example burning trees for charcoal or filling the ground to build traps
- Open procedural worlds that you enter through a time portal, tied to rebuilding a time machine and assembling a band of wizards

#### Tedious Or Disliked

- Heavy material grinding for building and upgrades, felt most strongly late in the game
- Inventory management and chest-hopping in the hub
- Early game time pressure feels rushed in a game that otherwise wants you to slow down and craft, especially on high difficulty
- Overwhelming crafting systems dumped on the player at the start
- Stun-lock enemy auras and lost-inventory deaths feel unfair, particularly solo

#### Divisive Points

- Difficulty: some call it easy and chill, others find the early timer and stun-lock deaths punishing
- Crafting depth: lauded as the best part by many, but others say many bullet and spell combos are gimmicky or go unused
- Solo versus co-op: praised as a fun co-op game, but criticized as poorly balanced for solo play
- Genre fit: some call it a survival game, others say it is really a roguelite or extraction shooter and the survival label misleads
