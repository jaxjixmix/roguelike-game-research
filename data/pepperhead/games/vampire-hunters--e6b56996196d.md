### Commentary


#### Entry 1

- **Part:** 3
- **Label:** Tier list Part 3 (2025)
- **Summary:** Vampire Hunters is a first-person horde-survivor game where you collect and upgrade many weapons while dodging and shooting through swarms in 3D levels. He finds it chaotic and unique for its first-person view, and the first few hours were fun. He felt it was unfinished, with poor balance, repetitive gameplay and a broken final boss. He places it in low B.
- **Quote:** It really does feel like the game was dropped in development before it was finished.
- **Rerank Note:**

##### Praise

- First-person perspective is rare for a horde-like and feels unique
- More mechanical variety than typical horde-likes: jumping, dashing, character-specific abilities, an ultimate
- Characters feel different from one another
- Meta progression is enjoyable
- Different environments and visually cool bosses
- The chaotic, messy weapon-collecting is fun in the first few hours

##### Criticism

- Serious balance problems: some weapons are roughly ten times stronger than others and some feel terrible
- Some items feel unimpactful and there are few character upgrade options
- Runs are repetitive: the same move-to-edge, kite a train of enemies loop regardless of character, weapon or map
- Unlocks require playing one particular character a lot, and he saw all weapons and items quickly
- Bosses stop mattering once meta progression makes you strong, and the final boss Dracula seems broken
- Feels dropped before finished; the party-like music does not fit the game

##### Unique Points

- A first-person horde-like is rare and a fresh take on the subgenre
- Uses 3D space with jumping and dashing instead of top-down 2D movement
- Weapon upgrades are fixed but you choose which weapons to prioritise

##### Comparisons

- Other horde-likes / Vampire Survivors-style games

##### Design Criteria

- Balance: weapon power gaps and weak items hurt the game
- Variety/repetition: runs, characters and maps play too similarly
- Meta progression: enjoyable but unlock gating is grindy and trivialises later play
- Boss design: visually cool but irrelevant and buggy at the end
- Audio/vibe: music mismatched with the game
- Mechanical depth: jump, dash and ultimate add more than typical horde-likes
- Polish/completeness: feels unfinished for a full release
- Uniqueness: first-person view sets it apart

### Community

- **One Liner:** A first-person roguelite 'survivors' shooter from Gamecraft Studios where you stack up to ~14 guns and abilities while mowing down endless undead hordes across 30-minute stages.
- **Consensus Summary:** Vampire Hunters is well liked by Steam players (about 90% positive across ~2,200 reviews, Very Positive, though recent reviews dip to roughly 75%). It is a cheap (about $4.49-8.99) Unity game that left Early Access on Oct 30, 2024 after an EA start in July 2023. Fans love the novelty of a survivors-like in first person with real aiming and a huge arsenal; detractors say it becomes a flat camp-in-a-corner loop with weak weapon synergy and repetitive music. #### Best Things

- First-person survivors-like concept: you actually aim, so it feels more engaging than top-down auto-fire bullet heavens (often compared to Vampire Survivors crossed with Doom, Serious Sam, Painkiller or Unreal Tournament)
- Huge weapon and ability pool with absurd stacking builds (many guns at once, guns attached to guns), letting players feel very powerful
- Big unlock and achievement list, multiple characters and lots of maps with different enemies and bosses
- Level design inspired by Quake/Unreal, with shortcuts and platforming routes; bosses with map-specific gimmicks
- Cheap price and strong value on sale; some players report 60-130+ hours
- Retro PS1-style look and chaotic, silly tone, plus a story/ending some players found charming
- Developer kept adding content in and after Early Access (one reviewer even complains about 'too much content')

#### Worst Things

- Gameplay collapses into camping in a dead end or single-direction chokepoint and holding fire for 30 minutes per stage
- Weapons feel unbalanced: many are weak, small magazines, and debuffs like bleed/freeze/poison are underwhelming; a couple of guns dominate
- Little synergy between weapons; ten machine guns mostly just add raw damage rather than creating interesting builds
- Enemy AI is simple (mostly melee walking straight at you) and enemy variety is limited, so aiming rarely matters
- Music is repeatedly called repetitive and grating
- Unity performance issues: stutters on good hardware, described by one player as poor optimization
- Some glitches such as enemies stuck in walls in Classic mode making them unkillable
- Despite the huge gun count, the power fantasy feels flat to some: upgrades do not feel impactful and the game gets easy, then boring (one of the most-upvoted negative reviews)
- Bare-bones feel: nothing is explained, systems feel arbitrary, and only a handful of maps exist

#### Improvements

- More map and mode variety, with unique mechanics or events per stage so each 30-minute run plays differently
- Real weapon synergies (per-debuff scaling, cross-effects) and rebalancing so more weapons are viable
- Characters and playstyles that interact with weapons differently (movement-based or stationary-based bonuses)
- Better tutorial or in-game explanations of keywords, systems and the endgame win condition
- Smarter and more varied enemy behavior (flankers, chargers, ranged) to discourage camping, plus cleaner spawn/pathing in Classic mode
- More content/updates and DLC; some players also ask for multiplayer/co-op

#### Unique Things

- First-person shooter take on the survivors-like formula (aim yourself, not auto-fire)
- Two modes: Survivor mode (endless waves, 30-minute stages) and Classic mode (more traditional FPS pacing with rebirth levels)
- Stackable loadout of up to ~14 guns plus artifacts with absurd, genre-mixing arsenal (miniguns, shoulder lightsaber launcher, magic frog)
- Constellation-style run modifiers where harder settings give more score/XP, with online score leaderboards
- Retro Unreal/Quake-flavoured arenas and a goofy steampunk and mythology bestiary (lizards, cat people, evil trains, insect bosses)

#### Tedious Or Disliked

- 30-minute stages with long stretches of just standing and shooting before a boss appears
- Repetitive loop and repetitive music after a few hours; players say they have 'seen it all' in the first 30 minutes to 10 hours
- Grinding unlocks and rebirth progression, including restarting character progression, and repeating the same locations
- Confusing win condition: after defeating bosses, a late objective to close enemy portals while enemies become very tanky was unforeshadowed
- Score/XP tied to difficulty modifiers in Survivor mode, making safe play feel punished and leaderboards farmable through camping

#### Divisive Points

- Survivor mode versus Classic mode: some find Classic more satisfying, others prefer the trailer-style Survivor mode
- Depth: fans say build variety and player power are a strength; critics say builds do not matter and only a few weapons work
- Update cadence: some praise frequent content, others say content stopped and only a handful of maps exist
- Value: 'hidden gem worth it even at full price' versus 'get it on sale, gets repetitive'
