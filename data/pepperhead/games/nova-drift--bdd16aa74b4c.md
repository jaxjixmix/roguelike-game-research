#### Best Things

- Enormous build variety: 200+ mods across weapons, shields, bodies and 'super' upgrades that chain into wild synergies (homing fragmenting missiles, shield thrown as a blade drone, bullet-ship builds)
- Satisfying audio and visual feedback: reviewers call out the SFX, soundtrack, clean neon look and screen-filling explosions
- Inertia-based drifting movement feels distinctive and rewarding once it clicks, reminiscent of classic Asteroids
- Plenty of replay hooks: endless mode, draft mode, boss rush, daily challenges and stackable mode modifiers that act as custom difficulty
- Runs are short and decision-dense (about 30-40 minutes, around 30 upgrades), good for 'one more run' sessions
- Meta-progression unlocks steadily add new mods and super mods, so the game keeps changing as you learn it
- Good value and accessibility: cheap for the content, runs well on old hardware, controller support praised by some despite a 'Mostly Playable' label
- Scales from chill score-chasing to punishing high difficulty, with hard achievements for veterans

#### Worst Things

- Movement/steering (rotate and thrust, no twin-stick aiming, no move-backwards) is the most common reason players bounce off or refund; several negative reviews cite controls alone
- Enemy and arena variety is seen as thin: same empty space, recycled wave formations, and few genuinely threatening enemies
- Early difficulty levels are too easy, so some players feel they have 'beaten' it within a couple of runs before mutators unlock the real challenge
- Balance is lopsided in places: a few reviewers say missiles (or a handful of builds) are the only viable route for very deep runs, and many perks are narrowly specialised
- Some deaths feel cheap, e.g. being one-shot by enemies with extreme modifiers
- Controller polish gaps: no sensitivity setting, limited control customisation
- Occasional technical complaints: performance troubles on some machines, launch issues with certain capture hardware, Linux support dependent on Proton

#### Improvements

- Add controller sensitivity options and broader control remapping, plus alternative control schemes (twin-stick or a reverse/brake input)
- More bosses and more enemy/arena variety so later runs feel less repetitive
- Better onboarding: the practice run is progression-locked and gives little reward, and the UI shows upgrades that are not yet unlocked without explaining why
- Tune difficulty so early tiers are more engaging and weaker weapon families are competitive in deep endless runs
- Smooth out the time-to-unlock for the strongest super mods, which one reviewer says takes a while
- Add a multi-point/bulk upgrade option and more customisation, which players request on the Steam forums

#### Unique Things

- Fuses ARPG-style build theorycrafting with Asteroids-style arcade physics, an unusual mix in the roguelite shooter space
- Ship is assembled from interchangeable weapon, shield and body parts with deep interaction rules rather than generic stat boosts
- Score-attack/leaderboard framing rather than a story-driven goal, with mode modifiers letting players author their own difficulty
- Momentum and collision physics matter: you can ram, use shields as weapons, or turn yourself into the projectile
- Small-studio project (Chimeric, published by Pixeljam) with a long Early Access road and active community feedback loop

#### Tedious Or Disliked

- Grinding through meta-unlocks before the best mods and harder mutators appear, which some call slow and others accept as pacing
- Runs can feel samey because arenas are empty space and wave types repeat, leading some players to drop off after 5-10 runs
- Endless mode after the final boss feels like the same bosses plus more enemies, with little new content
- Score-chasing focus does not suit players who want a final goal or narrative progression
- Steering learning curve: newcomers often describe the first runs as awkward until drifting becomes second nature
- Some find heavy projectile and effect spam visually noisy

#### Divisive Points

- Movement: many call the inertia-based controls the heart of the game; others find them unintuitive or worse than twin-stick and leave
- Difficulty: some say it is too easy even with modifiers enabled, while others call late tiers and endless very hard
- Post-1.0 direction: at least one negative reviewer blamed the 1.0 planning and the developer's Discord community for decisions they disliked, though this is a single player's view and most reviewers are positive
- Depth vs repetition: fans see near-limitless builds, sceptics see the same handful of strong builds repeated
