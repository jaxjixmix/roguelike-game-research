#### Best Things

- Huge weapon and accessory pool that synergises into many different builds; several reviewers cite 'god runs' from item combinations
- Fast, snappy dash-based movement and combat that feels good once a build comes together (one reviewer likens the movement approach to Rogue Legacy)
- Character customisation per run, with many valid playstyles and a difficulty curve that adds new content gradually
- Food/hunger system: buying food for buffs fills a hunger meter that drains, forcing you to budget stat gains against saving room for better food
- No contact damage from enemies, which one reviewer singled out as a relief
- Active Steam Workshop: custom weapons, characters and even items imported from Sephiria extend replay value (reviewers call the Workshop community 'legends')
- Strong value at about $10 and good long-tail engagement, with many players returning every few months
- Pixel-art style and movement praised by several reviewers (one calls it a personal favourite 2D roguelike)

#### Worst Things

- Controls draw the harshest complaints: aiming and dashing follow the mouse pointer so keyboard players dash the wrong way, and keybinding/settings menus were reported as broken by some
- Controller support is inconsistent: one PS5-controller user reports the game flipping between Xbox and 'Undefined' input mid-run; others say it plays best on a gamepad
- Item and loot rotation feels repetitive: the same items recur in shops and chests, so the large library feels smaller than it is, and there is low visual variety across areas
- Balance is swingy: lucky weapon/evasion/block combos make you near-invincible while a poor-loot run leaves you with weak items; some legendary items are reported weaker than commons and some characters feel underpowered
- Late bosses are bullet sponges that flood the screen with projectiles; one complaint notes a boss using red/orange/brown projectiles in a red/orange/brown hell stage where attacks blend into the background
- Enemy attack timings feel slow compared with the very fast player and spam-attack weapons, making dodging feel jarring (detailed in one long negative review)
- Bland, minimal story and lore according to several reviewers

#### Improvements

- Better input handling: a working rebinding menu and reliable controller detection (PS controllers especially)
- More permanent upgrades or meta-progression to help struggling players, since progression tops out and later success is skill plus RNG
- Better item rotation/pool management so shops and chests do not keep showing the same items
- More area and visual variety, and more enemy/boss diversity to reduce repetitiveness
- Rebalancing rarity and weak characters so legendary items and unorthodox builds are not punished; clearer guaranteed healing rather than mostly RNG healing
- Fix or clarify achievement bugs (one 100% player reports the final food-eating achievement not unlocking) and quest progress not carrying across a single run

#### Unique Things

- Food/hunger meter as a build-management resource: buffs are bought and limited by stomach capacity, which is unusual for the genre
- 2D mouse-aimed shooter/melee hybrid platformer with a dash-centred movement kit and no contact damage
- Town-rebuilding and training meta layer: permanent stat training plus unlockable vendors and NPCs rescued from the dungeon
- Active Steam Workshop scene adding custom characters and weapons to a game with a simple pixel-art pipeline
- Available on PC, Switch, PS4 and iOS

#### Tedious Or Disliked

- Achievement hunting and the optional true final boss: reviewers describe completion as long, repetitive and in places annoying (one says the true boss looked more annoying than satisfying)
- Repeated runs through the same few areas, with a lack of area variety, leads to fatigue after dozens of runs
- Spending runs with only low-rarity gear and no healing is frustrating once meta-progression is maxed out
- Bullet-hell late stages and projectile-spamming bosses become a slog rather than a skill test for some
- Keyboard controls and cursor-relative dashing cause cheap hits, making the keyboard experience a chore

#### Divisive Points

- Difficulty and balance: some call it 'pretty easy' and relaxing, others call it punishing and skill-only with poorly balanced RNG
- Depth and longevity: fans put in 100-250 hours and call it a gold-standard roguelite, while others see a generic, shallow, cheap-feeling roguelite that falls flat after 15-20 runs
- Controls and game feel: praised as crisp and snappy (especially on controller) by many, described as awkward or unintuitive by others, partly due to the 2.5D perspective and mouse-relative aiming
- Item pool: 'massive loot library' versus 'same items keep showing up'
