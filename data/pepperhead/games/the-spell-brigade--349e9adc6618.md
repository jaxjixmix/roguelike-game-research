##### Praise

- Movement-only gameplay is a confident choice and the movement feel is excellent, possibly better than Brotato's
- Elemental combos on spells (fire, lightning, frost, acid, dark) create chaotic, varied builds with unique visuals per combination
- Randomized upgrade numbers based on luck
- Exploding enemies chain reactions act as a catch-up mechanic for players behind
- Revive system that works even in single player
- Mini quests during runs add variety
- Multiplayer is a blast and clearly the design focus; straightforward meta progression and many unlockable characters keep him playing

##### Criticism

- Only four maps and they feel repetitive, with not enough to do on them
- Characters feel too similar; unique stats are minimal
- Relic chests are oddly designed, small and specific, and annoying to find in single player since looking for them costs XP
- Mini quests are unbalanced, some trivial and some nearly impossible
- Some spells are weaker than others, feedback on damage and friendly fire is unclear, and only two final bosses with uneven difficulty
- Fourth spell choice feels useless and muddies the upgrade pool; wants a choice of spell count and more rerolls

##### Unique Points

- Elemental infusion system where any element can combine with any spell, with distinct visuals
- Enemies slow slightly when near the player, making weaving between them feel badass
- Explosive-enemy chain reactions as a catch-up mechanic
- Designed primarily for multiplayer with friendly fire and generous revives

#### Best Things

- Online co-op for up to 4 is the standout; friendly fire, spell combos and accidentally wiping teammates generate the fun, and reviewers call it the game that makes or breaks the experience
- Art style, animation and cute wizard designs with cosmetics and skins are widely praised as polished and welcoming
- Easy to pick up, chaotic 'brain off' gameplay that works for chill sessions with friends; several groups report returning weekly
- Elemental infusions, augments and spell synergies allow varied, sometimes overpowered builds
- Team-based map objectives and quests add some structure beyond pure survival (Metacritic critics cite novel map design and quest systems)
- Regular developer updates and receptive, community-oriented devs during Early Access
- Runs well on Steam Deck and has cross-platform multiplayer, solo option and controller support
- Low price (about $13) and a long-lasting time sink for some players, with several reviewers logging 100-280 hours

#### Worst Things

- 1.0 grind spike: gold rewards reduced while prices of permanent upgrades, enchantments, prestige resets and covenant unlocks rose sharply; reviewers say unlocking everything would take hundreds of hours
- Wizard unlocks moved from achievement/challenge-based to gold purchases, removing a beloved mechanic and adding tedium
- Limited variety: same enemies on every map, only a couple of bosses, and about a third of spells described as weak
- Shallow progression: hour 30 plays like hour 1 with bigger stats, so runs feel repetitive after a dozen matches
- Netcode and connection problems: no reconnect after a disconnect (a dropped player cannot rejoin), and lag with players matched from distant regions due to no region lock
- Performance: endless mode gets very laggy as the screen fills up
- Music is seen as basic, repetitive and poorly fitted to the game
- Patch cycle perceived as fixing one thing while breaking another (e.g. controller problems after patch 1.1.4 reported on the Steam forum)

#### Improvements

- Reduce the gold/grind economy and restore cheaper or achievement-based ways to unlock wizards and covenants
- Add more enemy types, bosses, maps and spells, and better balance across augments and elements
- Add reconnect-to-session support and region locking or region filters for multiplayer matchmaking
- Optimise endless mode and late-run performance; players also ask for split-screen multiplayer (a popular forum thread)
- Add rerolls for build-defining traits and infusions, and clearer stat descriptions with exact numbers
- Better teammate communication tools and deeper multiplayer synergy; Metacritic critics say co-op can feel like playing next to each other
- More music and soundscape options

#### Unique Things

- Online co-op first design for 1-4 players in a survivors-like, with friendly fire so teammates can hurt each other
- Team-based map objectives and quests (e.g. football/sheep/torch-style minigames) layered on top of wave survival
- Elemental infusion system combined with spell augments for build crafting
- Covenants as unlockable run modifiers with prestige-style resets of wizards
- Full 3D camera with a colorful, cartoonish wizard aesthetic and cosmetic skins, unusual in a genre dominated by 2D pixel art
- Cross-platform multiplayer, with a console release (PS5) alongside Steam

#### Tedious Or Disliked

- Gold grind needed for permanent upgrades, wizard unlocks and covenants; a single 45-minute multiplayer match may only afford one upgrade
- Prestige resets and max-leveling a wizard repeatedly just to unlock one covenant
- Some objective minigames (especially the football one) described as boring or impossible to complete with a swarm around
- Quest/achievement difficulty is uneven; a third of achievements are pure time sinks, and some Early Access quests were RNG-locked (e.g. needing specific infusions without rerolls)
- Hardcore mode outcomes hinge on early trait rolls (lightning/shadow good, acid/dark bad), forcing restarts
- Endless-mode lag and the screen becoming unreadable as spells pile up

#### Divisive Points

- The 1.0 overhaul: some say the UI redesign, polish and added wizard/boss made it better, while the top negative reviews say it made progression worse and less fun
- Solo play: some find it fine, others say it became much less playable solo after the grind increase and that the game only really works with friends
- Difficulty and build variety: fans enjoy the chaos and scaling difficulty, while roguelike veterans find builds shallow and spells unbalanced
- Visual chaos: a feature for some ('unreadable screen adds to the fun') and a problem for others
