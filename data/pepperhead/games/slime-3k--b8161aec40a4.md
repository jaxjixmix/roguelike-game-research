### Community

- **One Liner:** A pixel-art roguelite bullet-heaven from Konfa Games and tinyBuild, set in the Despot's Game universe, where you play a murderous slime and build a deck of weapons and passive-tag synergies to survive escalating hordes across about 20 levels.
- **Consensus Summary:** Steam players rate it Mostly Positive (about 80% of roughly 1,700 reviews) and it is a modest, cheap ($4.99) indie hit that has not broken out; third-party estimates put it at 50-100k owners and roughly $120k gross. Fans love the art, animation, weapon variety and the deckbuilding and tag-synergy layer, which they say makes it more strategic than a typical Vampire Survivors clone. Detractors say the meta-progression is slow and grindy, builds get narrow, and later levels feel like stat checks or difficulty spikes, so the game runs out of surprises around the mid-game. It left Early Access (launched Nov 2023) with a 1.0 release on Oct 31, 2024, and recent reviews are slightly better than the all-time average (86% positive on a small sample). I found no professional critic coverage: Metacritic lists the game but has no Metascore and only a couple of user ratings. #### Best Things

- Pixel art, character design and animation are widely called masterful and are the most consistent praise, including from players who otherwise bounce off the game
- The deckbuilding layer (choosing which weapons can appear in a run) plus a tag-synergy system gives builds real strategic weight, unlike idle-feeling survivor-likes
- Weapons are varied, funny and have satisfying sound design (one reviewer singles out the shotgun audio)
- Upgrades, both in-run and meta, are described as large and intuitive rather than many tiny percentage bonuses, so there is little 'number soup'
- Enemy hordes flowing around obstacles and the general action/effects feel polished and fast
- Every level differs and pushes you to adapt your deck, which keeps the campaign feeling fresh and gives achievement and challenge hunting to do
- A soundtrack that some reviewers call top-tier, and a low price that many consider good value, especially on sale
- Decent playtime for the price: a top-voted review reports nearly 30 hours and full completion

#### Worst Things

- Grindy meta-progression: upgrades and new cards are expensive and gated behind very specific unlock conditions (e.g. finish a level with several level-3 weapons of one type)
- Difficulty spikes and stat-check levels, with level 10 and the later levels called out repeatedly for chaos, tanky mini-bosses and slowdown
- Performance: frame drops and lag in late levels, particularly when many enemies and projectiles are on screen; a Steam discussion complains of freezes at the end of level 10
- Builds feel limited: players report being pushed to a single exact deck per level, and many unlockable cards or tags feel cosmetic or weak
- Weak-feeling weapons early and long stretches of repeating the same loop; one reviewer says hour 20 has little more than hour 2
- Bugs and platform issues: a level-10 crash report, a macOS launch failure thread, mouse/controller input conflicts, and poor Steam Deck input handling
- Boss-kill loot spawns on top of the player, which can break the 'No Hands' achievement, and boss rewards are seen as near worthless because the run ends right after
- Some find the loop repetitive and unfun compared with Vampire Survivors, calling it 'mid' or boring after a few hours

#### Improvements

- Raise the five-deck save limit; players say it forces constant rebuilding and discourages experimentation
- Better in-game explanation of mechanics (tags, synergies, why you are dying), since many negative reviews come from players who never discovered these systems
- Rebalance difficulty spikes and the tankiness of late-level enemies and mini-bosses so not every hard level requires one specific build
- Speed up or flatten the meta-progression and relax narrow unlock conditions so diverse weapons arrive before the campaign ends
- Fix late-game performance, boss-loot placement, and controller/mouse/Steam Deck input options (including a setting to replace the hold-to-exit-shop button)
- More powerups, loot and variety in level content (some levels feel like empty arenas with nothing to do)

#### Unique Things

- Deckbuilding bolted onto a bullet-heaven: you build a deck of weapons pre-run and levelling up opens a shop instead of offering random picks
- Tag-synergy passives that reward stacking weapons of the same tag, tied to meta-upgrades that change mechanics (such as contact damage)
- Weapons merge in tiers (two tier-1 weapons give a tier-2 weapon, up to A-tier), per a Metacritic user review
- Roughly 20 hand-designed levels with distinct enemy waves and bosses instead of one repeating arena, and predetermined waves so skill rather than RNG decides outcomes
- Twin-stick aiming with manual shooting, rather than fully auto-attack, plus optional map activities that make you roam
- Dark, edgy humour in the Despot's Game universe with a murderous jelly protagonist

#### Tedious Or Disliked

- Grinding DNA/XP and resources to unlock upgrades and cards, with players repeating a farm level such as level 10
- Hold-button prompts (especially to leave the shop on a gamepad) that are used very often with no option to change
- Screens crowded with projectiles and enemies, making evasion hard and triggering lag
- Replaying short runs against special levels (e.g. slowing-bullet level) that can instakill you
- Rebuilding decks repeatedly due to the limited number of save slots
- Shop-centric economy: money comes mainly from shops and drops, so weapons feel weak until you build up

#### Divisive Points

- Difficulty: fans call the bottleneck levels good forced experimentation; critics call them unfair stat checks
- Deckbuilding: some see it as the game's best feature and the source of strategic depth; others find it constricts variety or simply dislike it, and one left after seeing the deckbuilder tag
- Depth vs. repetition: some say every system has a purpose and rewards learning; others see the same loop executed repeatedly with shallow meta-progression
- Comparison with Vampire Survivors: some call it their favourite survivor-like, others say the older game does everything better apart from the visuals
