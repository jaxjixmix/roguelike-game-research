##### Praise

- Very nice, immersive visuals and environment that give a real sense of adventure
- Village meta progression: new buildings and NPCs unlock new abilities after each death
- Persistent character levelling with freedom to pick or switch classes at any time
- Subclasses change playstyle drastically and make you much more powerful
- Run-only abilities and items have cool effects and are enjoyable
- Boss fights feel epic, killing huge monsters as a tiny character
- Strong drive to upgrade and see the next area or boss; a solid sequel

##### Criticism

- Gameplay is extremely simple and gets repetitive, doing the same ability the same way
- Levels are repetitive, mostly rooms of enemies despite good visuals
- Characters and monsters are hard to see
- Subclass unlocks arrive very late (about 70% through playtime)
- Little reason to continue after the final boss; no hidden worlds or biomes like the first game's DLC

##### Unique Points

- Village-building meta progression gives an almost RuneScape-like feel of a persistent character
- Fixed biome and boss order makes it about full exploration from village to deep final layer
- Subclass system dramatically reshapes play, though gated late

#### Best Things

- Snappy, satisfying dual-stick combat with kiting; auto-fire skills and big lightning or piercing attacks that clear hallways of enemies feel great
- Seven classes with three specialisations each; classes play distinctly and encourage experimenting
- Account-wide buffs from levelling every class mean alternate characters make real progress, so you always keep something after a run
- Town building and upgrades give a sticky long-term progression loop; the town is shared across characters and you keep most of your resources
- Co-op for up to four players is a highlight, with easy drop-in and drop-out and the ability to leave and resume a run later; a Gauntlet-like slow-burn feel
- Gorgeous pixel art, good lighting and effects, strong sound design; a solid sequel improvement over the first game's pacing
- Runs are quittable and resumable at area boundaries, which suits short sessions and Steam Deck play
- Responsive dev support on save problems and free post-launch updates that added missions, weapons and mode variety

#### Worst Things

- Roguelite elements are thin: the main run goes through the same zones in the same order with only tile and enemy shuffling, so runs feel alike
- Late-game and New Game+ scaling breaks: enemy HP grows faster than gear and trinkets, some subclasses become too weak, and you eventually get one-shot with nothing to counter it
- Content is limited after 1.0 (few bosses, maps, legendary gear); reviewers call it barebones or like an early-access game and feel 2025 roadmap promises were underdelivered
- Character builds lack depth: no skill trees, upgrades are mostly flat stat increases, and loot is many tiny numerical bonuses with little identity
- Save-file loss and character-deletion bugs reported by multiple players, including after updates, with some needing support tickets to restore backups
- Balance issues: non-piercing weapons and single-hit defensive skills are weak at high difficulty, making some classes (e.g. Ranger) worse
- Slow health and mana regeneration leaves you waiting between fights, and the screen can get cluttered by effects so you cannot read what is happening
- Paid DLC (Celestial Ruins) drew anger from some players who say it should have been base-game content and that updates are scarce

#### Improvements

- More variety in tilesets, zone order, bosses and maps so runs differ more, which is the most common request
- More build-defining depth: talent or skill trees, more abilities earlier, and loot with distinctive effects instead of small flat stats
- Rebalance late-game and NG+ scaling so gear, trinkets and weak subclasses keep pace, and fix useless defensive skills and non-piercing weapons
- Reliable cloud saves and save-loss fixes, plus an easy manual backup and copy option
- Quality-of-life: faster out-of-combat regen, less visual clutter, local co-op, and more language translations (e.g. Spanish) requested on Steam forums
- Deliver a larger post-launch roadmap with more maps, bosses and legendary gear

#### Unique Things

- Blends a Diablo-style ARPG with a roguelite meta-loop, built around a persistent town with NPCs and building upgrades
- Account-wide class-level bonuses apply to every character, so alternate classes keep pushing your main roster forward
- Dual-stick shooter-style combat with cooldown abilities, reminiscent of Gauntlet and Children of Morta
- You can leave a run mid-dungeon at a level boundary and resume it later, including in multiplayer
- Endless New Game+ tiers and one-time special maps unlocked through the town
- Keep most resources on death (with a penalty of roughly 40% of carried gold and materials), so failure still yields progress

#### Tedious Or Disliked

- The early hours are a grind of the first zones; dozens of hours to build out the town and vendors in single-player
- Re-fighting identical maps and enemies in the same positions and order across runs, including late game
- Runs that turn into kiting huge mobs for long stretches once you are outscaled, with hundreds of enemies per floor on high difficulty
- Waiting for health or mana to regenerate between encounters, sometimes alt-tabbing while you wait
- Some permanent upgrades are per-character, so starting another class means regrinding
- Control complexity for some: aiming, moving, dashing and managing several cooldowns at once (a reviewer refunded after 36 minutes)

#### Divisive Points

- Difficulty and pacing: some find it never unfair and even a bit easy, others call the early game a gear-check grind and the late game a slog
- Whether it is really a roguelite: fans enjoy the slow-burn RPG progression, others say the roguelike mechanics barely matter
- Value: many say it is worth the $20 and hundreds of hours, especially in co-op, while others say content runs out after 15-20 hours
- Post-launch support and DLC: praised for free updates and dev responsiveness, but criticised as too sparse and monetised
