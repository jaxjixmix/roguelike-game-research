### Community

- **One Liner:** Klei Entertainment's hand-drawn, Tim Burton-flavoured wilderness survival game with permadeath, procedurally generated worlds, and no tutorial.
- **Consensus Summary:** Don't Starve is regarded as a modern survival classic: Steam shows 'Overwhelmingly Positive' at about 96.5% across roughly 113k reviews, and critics landed in the high 70s (Metacritic 79 on PC, Generally Favorable). Praise centres on the gothic art style, the discovery-driven learning loop and the tension of permadeath. Both players and critics flag the lack of guidance and punishing early hours as the main barrier, and several say the loop turns tedious once survival is mastered. The original single-player game is effectively in maintenance mode, since Klei's content updates moved to the multiplayer Don't Starve Together. Many recent Steam reviewers, even positive ones, tell newcomers to buy DST instead, even for solo play. It is not early access; it released in full in April 2013 after an alpha/beta period.
- **Reception Notes:** Launched to Generally Favorable reviews (Metacritic high 70s), with critics praising the art and addictive loop and criticising the steep, poorly explained learning curve. It became a long-running Steam hit (about 96.5% positive, Steam Spy owners 2-5M, a revenue estimate of roughly 13.9M USD gross, which is a modeled figure and not audited). Klei focused later development on Don't Starve Together and released the Reign of Giants, Shipwrecked and Hamlet expansions for the original, but the base game now gets little or no new content, which feeds recent negative reviews complaining of abandonment. It is on PC, Mac, Linux, consoles, Switch and iOS. #### Best Things

- Distinctive hand-drawn, Burton-esque gothic art with fluid animation, plus a gramophone-style soundtrack and strong atmosphere
- Permadeath loop where every death teaches something specific (bees, darkness, hunger), making learning itself the reward
- Survival systems that interlock: hunger, sanity, health, seasons, day/night cycle and creature behaviour create emergent stories
- No hand-holding; players respect that Klei kept a cohesive identity instead of chasing mainstream accessibility
- Huge replayability from randomly generated worlds, many unlockable characters with different perks, and adjustable world settings
- Everything has multiple uses (monsters can be turned against each other, even death can be exploited), which rewards experimentation
- Strong value, often very cheap on sale; expansions like Reign of Giants, Shipwrecked and Hamlet are described as near full games

#### Worst Things

- Almost no tutorial or in-game explanation; many mechanics (bird cage for food, bunnymen hostility to meat carriers, final Adventure level in darkness) are only learnable via the wiki or trial and error
- Early hours are brutal and feel unfair because death often comes from not knowing rules rather than player error
- Losing a long run to a hidden mechanic feels especially demoralising after dozens of hours
- Adventure mode is seen as poorly designed: fetch-quest errands across levels, then a surprise difficulty spike
- Once you can survive indefinitely the game loses purpose and becomes chores; a common critique is weak long-term goals
- Clunky real-time combat, especially clicking on moving targets with mouse and keyboard
- Some reports of crashes (e.g. loading worlds with caves via DST) and a black-screen lockup bug in recent negative Steam reviews
- Some players feel abandoned: the single-player game receives no new content while DST keeps being updated
- Base game feels dated next to Don't Starve Together (e.g. weaker crafting menu and quality of life), per reviewers who played both

#### Improvements

- Better in-game teaching or hinting (food values, creature behaviour) so players do not need a wiki or mods like craft pot and minimap
- Clearer feedback on why a run ended so failure teaches specific lessons instead of feeling like repetition
- New content and updates for the original game instead of leaving it behind DST
- More mid and late-game goals beyond surviving longer, and a redesigned Adventure mode
- Quality-of-life features built into the base game that players currently get via mods (geometric placement, minimap, displayed food values)
- Stability fixes for crashes and the black-screen bug

#### Unique Things

- Survival-crafting game with roguelike permadeath and procedural worlds but no levelling meta-progression; the only thing you carry over is knowledge
- Sanity as a third resource alongside health and hunger, with hallucinated monsters and shifting behaviour
- Darkness is lethal: night without light means death, which defines the pacing of every day
- Seasonal cycle (autumn, winter, spring, summer) each with its own threats, forcing long-term planning
- Art direction of a storybook with gothic undertones, with a cast of oddly distinct playable characters
- Emergent, systemic monster ecosystem where hostile creatures can be baited to fight each other

#### Tedious Or Disliked

- Constant resource gathering and base upkeep (chopping, mining, food, fuel) that becomes chores once mastered
- Learning by dying repeatedly with little carried over between runs, which some find repetitive rather than empowering
- Needing a second monitor with a wiki open to progress efficiently
- Adventure mode fetch-quest grind across multiple levels
- Solo play can feel repetitive and unstructured; several reviewers say the game works better in multiplayer

#### Divisive Points

- Deliberate obtuseness: fans call it rewarding discovery, detractors call it artificial difficulty (even a 7/10 PopMatters-style review called the learning process laborious)
- Original vs Don't Starve Together: some prefer the simpler, less bloated original, while many recommend DST even for solo play because of more content and ongoing updates
- Whether it is really a roguelike: permadeath and procedural worlds say yes, but the open-ended sandbox survival structure says otherwise
- Punishing permadeath: thrilling for some, demoralising for others as runs get longer
