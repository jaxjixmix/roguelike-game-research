### Commentary


#### Entry 1

- **Part:** 2
- **Label:** Tier list Part 2 (2024)
- **Summary:** Astrea is a dice-based deck-building roguelike with six playable characters. He admires how it solves the usual strategy-versus-randomness problem of dice games: every die face has a usable effect, and the game is built around deliberately swinging your health up and down. It is very complex, with long fights and heavy keyword load, so he recommends it mainly to experienced deck-builder fans. Audiovisuals are excellent, and he places it in A tier.
- **Quote:** this is going to be a solid a tier uh for Astria
- **Rerank Note:**

##### Praise

- Dice design keeps strategy meaningful: every face (including self-damage) can be exploited, so no roll is wasted
- Health-bar mechanic where low/high health thresholds unlock abilities, encouraging a constant take-damage/heal loop
- Six characters, each with roughly ten unique, complicated keywords
- Great music, amazing art and strong visual effects
- Secret final boss unlocked by beating the game with all six characters gives long-term progression motivation
- Unique events and companion 'friends' with their own mechanics add depth

##### Criticism

- Extremely complicated, possibly one of the most complex deck builders he has played
- Keywords are numerous and dice show only small icons, making it hard to remember what each does (harder to read than cards)
- Fights are very long (only about three fights per floor); final boss took him about an hour
- Both worlds look too similar (mostly blue), so it is hard to tell which world you are in and it is less immersive
- The default starting character is the least interesting one

##### Unique Points

- Designed around wanting to take damage, turning randomness into something you can always benefit from
- Health thresholds act as the activation system for abilities, creating a low-high-low-high health rhythm
- Solves the common dice deck-builder issue of diluted strategy without removing the RNG

##### Comparisons

- Slay the Spire

##### Design Criteria

- Strategy vs randomness: dice with drastically different faces that are all usable keeps planning meaningful
- Complexity/readability: many keywords and icon-only dice hurt accessibility
- Run/fight length: long, strategic fights with few encounters per floor
- Character diversity: six characters with unique keyword sets
- Meta-progression: secret boss gated behind winning with all characters
- Audio/visuals: great music, art and effects, but repetitive world colors
- Difficulty: challenging, aimed at veterans of the genre

#### Entry 2

- **Part:** top10-2024
- **Label:** Top 10 of 2024 (\#5)
- **Summary:** Astrea is a turn-based roguelike deckbuilder that uses dice instead of cards, with a Slay the Spire-style map of shops, events and elites. He calls it one of the most complicated deckbuilders he has played. Its core is a dual purification/corruption system where dice can heal or damage either side, and a virtue bar that only unlocks when your health is low. He finds it very rewarding when everything clicks, but would not recommend it to casual players. He ranks it fifth of the year.
- **Quote:** it's nice to find a more complex deck building game done well
- **Rerank Note:**

##### Praise

- Multi-purpose dice effects and self-damage make every roll usable, which reduces the luck factor
- Each character plays very differently, with roughly 30 unique mechanics apiece
- Turns and fights are puzzle-like, and winning with efficient use of technical mechanics feels very satisfying
- Sentinels, dice editing/destroying/duplicating and blessings (passives) add depth to builds
- Beautiful visuals and a fantastic soundtrack
- Meta-progression via unlocking characters and a teased mystery locked door

##### Criticism

- Very complex and not suited to casual players, and hard for newcomers
- Dice symbols are small and harder to remember than card text
- Luck still matters, and he wants more rerolling options to cut its impact
- The first available character is probably his least favorite
- One misplay can cost all your health given the low max-health cap of seven

##### Unique Points

- Corruption self-damage feeds the virtue bar, then purification heals you back in a cycle
- Dice serve dual purposes, used on self or enemy, which turns RNG into decisions
- Complex mechanics paired with complex strategy, the opposite of his usual preference for simple mechanics with deep strategy

##### Comparisons

- Into the Breach
- Slice &amp; Dice

##### Design Criteria

- Mechanic complexity vs strategic depth: usually prefers simple mechanics, but here the complexity works
- Luck/RNG mitigation: multi-purpose dice reduce reliance on rolls
- Character diversity: each character has many unique mechanics
- Risk vs reward: self-damage to fuel virtues versus dying to a misplay
- Meta-progression: character unlocks and a mystery door
- Audio/visuals: beautiful art and fantastic soundtrack
- Accessibility/readability: small dice symbols are hard to remember

### Community

- **One Liner:** A Slay the Spire-style roguelike deckbuilder by Little Leo Games where you build a pool of six-sided dice instead of cards, with a dual Purification/Corruption damage-and-healing system.
- **Consensus Summary:** Astrea is well regarded: Steam shows about 4,561 reviews at roughly 92% positive (Very Positive), Metacritic critic score is 85 and OpenCritic top-critic average is 87 (all critic reviews recommend it). Critics and players praise the fresh dice-building spin, the Purification/Corruption health-as-resource idea, six distinct Oracles and the striking art. Complaints cluster around heavy RNG, information overload and unclear iconography, weak damage previews, and (from some) runs that are either too easy or too samey. It is a finished game, not early access: PC launched Sep 2023, consoles Sep 2024, mobile Jul 2025, with post-release balance passes and content still appearing on Steam into 2026. Metacritic user score (6.9 on only 14 ratings) is far lower than Steam and is too small a sample to trust.
- **Reception Notes:** Launched on Steam Sep 21, 2023 at $24.99 from Little Leo Games, published by Akupara Games; released on PS4/PS5, Switch and Xbox Sep 26, 2024 and on iOS/Android Jul 17, 2025. Critics were consistently positive (OpenCritic 10 reviews, 100% recommend; Metacritic only 6 critic reviews, all positive). The Steam score is high in aggregate (about 92%), though the vaporlens aggregator shows 72% positive, implying lower sentiment in some recent or platform-specific reviews; recent Steam reviews from 2025-2026 are still mostly positive and mention updates. #### Best Things

- Purification vs Corruption turns your health bar into a resource: players deliberately take damage to unlock health-tied Virtues, and the risk/reward balancing is called addictive
- Safe / Balanced / Risky dice tiers let you choose your own volatility, and die faces can be rerolled, inverted, forged or permanently converted, giving real control over the randomness
- Six Oracles with very different playstyles, each with several viable archetypes; critics say all feel viable
- Huge content pool for replay: 350-750+ dice (store-page vs GameGrin counts differ), 170+ Blessings, Sentinel companions and ascension-style Anomaly difficulty levels
- Distinctive art direction with crystalline character designs and a blue/red palette; soundtrack also praised by some critics
- Three regenerating lives forgive mathematical mistakes and encourage greedy builds
- Fresh feel in a crowded Slay the Spire-like genre; many reviewers call it one of the better recent deckbuilder variants

#### Worst Things

- Iconography and UX overload: each Oracle has its own keywords and symbols, and every die has six faces to read, so evaluating choices is mentally exhausting
- RNG is heavy and not fully seeded (random targets, values, crits and the rolls themselves), so losses can feel undeserved and first turns differ even with identical choices
- No damage or outcome preview, and some corruption values are hidden, so players resort to pen and paper to avoid being blindsided
- Runs can feel samey: fewer random encounter types and less enemy variety than Slay the Spire, with the first act reportedly identical in demo and full game
- Balance: some critics and players say it is too easy or breakable once you find each character's best approach, while others hit brutal bosses and luck spikes at high Anomaly levels
- Safe dice and some Blessings are called too weak or too keyword-dependent, pushing builds toward Risky dice
- Later Oracles are very micro-heavy and slow to play, and some reviewers say too many systems make turns play themselves
- Short runs and price: several top negative reviews say a roughly 1-hour run feels shallow for $24.99 (would be fine at a discount); Steam listing is Windows only

#### Improvements

- Add a damage/effect preview (e.g. like Monster Train) and clearer surfacing of hidden corruption and damage sources
- Simplify or clarify iconography and die-face readability; some players shelved the game waiting for a UX fix or mod
- Fully seeded or more deterministic RNG and more consistent balance across Oracles and Anomaly levels
- More event and encounter variety plus more enemy variety to reduce repetition between runs
- Rebalance Safe dice and underpowered Blessings, and tighten keyword clutter across characters
- Reviewers and players wished for a richer story (the narrative is short and generic) and better Japanese/Korean localization

#### Unique Things

- Dice instead of cards: each die has six faces with different effects and you can edit those faces over the run
- Dual-direction Purification/Corruption damage: purification damages enemies or heals you, corruption the reverse, so the same effect can help or hurt depending on target
- Health-linked Virtues: repeatable passive abilities unlocked by your current health level reward playing near the edge
- Black Hole Blessings (powerful with notable downsides) and Star Blessings (smaller, no downside), plus Sentinels as companions
- Risk-tiered dice (Safe/Balanced/Risky/Epic) as the core build axis, with self-inflicted corruption dice you must play or swap

#### Tedious Or Disliked

- Reading six faces on each of several dice every turn and every reward screen
- Micro-management on later characters that makes early turns painfully slow until a build forms
- Saving-scumming or restarts felt necessary at high Anomaly levels because of dice variance
- Short runs (often about 1 to 1.5 hours) that some find shallow, while others say they get repetitive quickly
- Each character starting from the same difficulty with separate progression, which some found grindy
- Performance and UI issues reported on handheld devices (freezes, long autosaves)

#### Divisive Points

- Difficulty: a few players won on their first run in under an hour and called it too easy, while others find high Anomaly levels unpredictable and luck-driven
- RNG: praised as controllable thanks to reroll, convert and invert tools by many, yet a core reason for negative reviews from others
- Dice vs cards: some feel dice add novel tension; others say face-editing is less interesting than unique cards and the dual-damage system is partly marketing
- Visuals: widely praised, but a few players find the blue/red palette and stylized icons bland or confusing
