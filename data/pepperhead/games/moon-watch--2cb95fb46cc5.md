### Commentary


#### Entry 1

- **Part:** 3
- **Label:** Tier list Part 3 (2025)
- **Summary:** Moonwatch is an early-access deckbuilding, turn-based real-time action roguelike from the Backpack Hero developers, where time only moves when you move, in a Superhot-like fashion. You play abilities from a hand built over the run, spending mana that regenerates faster the less you move. He loves the core concept and the space zombies-and-vampires vibe, but finds it unbalanced, thin on content and with unsatisfying bosses. He places it in high C, with real potential to climb to B or even A as it gets tweaked and expanded.
- **Quote:** I'm going to put this as a high C
- **Rerank Note:** Early access evaluation; he says it could move to B or potentially A as content and tweaks arrive.

##### Praise

- Very unique concept: a Superhot-style time-stop mechanic fused with deckbuilding
- Mana regenerating faster when you stand still creates a cool gameplay loop of move, cast, wait
- Characters have different starting decks and feel drastically different from each other
- Runs feel unique between characters and builds
- Funny overall vibe of fighting zombies and vampires in space
- Developers (Backpack Hero) have a track record of unique concepts, and he is excited about where it goes

##### Criticism

- Currently unbalanced and finicky, feels early even for early access, with exploitable mechanics and underwhelming ones
- Each character has only about three builds that do not fuse well, so runs feel similar once tried
- Few abilities and items, so duplicates show up often and chaos or breakage results
- Polarizing boss items can make you overpowered or instantly dead after a bad start
- No good way to discard or reshuffle a bad hand
- Most bosses cannot be fought directly (for example activating towers) and none has a DPS check, a wasted opportunity
- Visuals feel minimalistic and he is not a fan

##### Unique Points

- Time only advances when you move, yet you can trigger hand abilities while time is stopped
- Mana regeneration tied to standing still forces a tension between positioning and casting
- Boss items act like cursed items, e.g. no mana gain from moving but mana from kills, making it all-or-nothing

##### Comparisons

- Superhot
- Backpack Hero
- Slay the Spire

##### Design Criteria

- Concept uniqueness: rewards a novel time-stop deckbuilding hook
- Balance: currently exploitable, with some mechanics overpowered and others weak
- Build diversity: only about three builds per character, limited cross-fusing
- Content depth: too few abilities and items, leading to duplicates
- Boss design: bosses lack DPS checks and feel unsatisfying
- Risk vs reward: polarizing boss items swing between dominant and dead
- Visuals/charm: minimalistic visuals but funny space-horror vibe
- Character variety: distinct starting decks make each character feel different

### Community

#### Best Things

- Core hook of pausing/stopping time inside a bullet-heaven format, described by players as Superhot plus Vampire Survivors in one
- Fun card and relic synergies that reward combo-building
- Runs are replayable in short bursts; some players keep looping back for a run or two
- Multiple modes (Classic, Horde, Wave, Challenge Runs, Infinite Loop/Endless) and progressive unlocks of cards, relics and starting decks
- Nice, fun little game with a few good synergies despite short length (recurring sentiment in positive reviews)

#### Worst Things

- Perceived abandonment: no meaningful updates since Dec 2024 and zero communication from Jaspel or Pretty Soon, which drives the most-upvoted negative reviews (33, 22, 15, 11 upvotes)
- Early Access timeline of 8-10 months was blown far past; roadmap items like the tech-tree meta-progression have not appeared
- Thin content for $9.99: 3 characters, 3 acts, 80 cards, 43 relics, 23 enemies and mini-bosses; one reviewer valued it at about $5
- Gameplay described by some as basic and repetitive
- Bugs: starter decks reportedly not working properly, unlock progress not persisting for some, card selection bugs, crashes with certain card combinations
- No Steam Cloud saves and Steam Deck achievement tracking problems

#### Improvements

- Communicate a roadmap or at least confirm whether development continues
- Add the promised content: more cards, characters, stages, enemies and the tech-tree meta-progression
- Fix the unlock/save persistence, starter deck and card selection bugs; add Steam Cloud
- Add windowed resolution options and more control customization (requested in Steam discussions)
- Rework build strategy depth: weapons and items are both temporary, pushing players toward just spamming as much as possible
- Better balance between movement and build power so dodging matters across the whole run, not only late

#### Unique Things

- Time-stop as the central weapon: stopping time lets you plan and set up combos in a horde-survival game
- Hybrid of three genres: bullet heaven, deckbuilder and tactics, which players say is doing something new
- From the creators of Backpack Hero, bringing a deckbuilder-flavored design sensibility to the survivors-like space
- Setting and tone: a moon in an alternate 19XX fighting vampires and cybernetically enhanced undead
- Modes including Wave, Horde, Challenge and Infinite Loop rather than a single run structure

#### Tedious Or Disliked

- Dominant strategy is to spew out as much as possible to offset everything being temporary, described as boring and time-consuming
- Movement can feel superfluous: when the build is strong you can stand still, when weak no dodging saves you, and few things need dodging until late
- Repetitiveness and short length drain replay value after a few runs
- Lack of meaningful new content means players hit the same ceiling quickly
- Unlock progression and bugs that interrupt runs

#### Divisive Points

- Whether the concept and itch.io-demo-level quality justify buying now: itch.io players rate it 4.7/5 while Steam reviews are Mixed
- Whether the time-stop plus deckbuilding mix is a fresh hybrid or two genres that clash (one reviewer says Loop Hero does the deck/auto-battler blend better)
- Whether the game is truly dead or just quiet: some supportive posts say keep going, devs, while many call it abandoned
