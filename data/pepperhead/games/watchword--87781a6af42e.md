### Commentary


#### Entry 1

- **Summary:** Watchword is a Scrabble-style roguelike built on Balatro's scoring and shop loop, with books standing in for jokers. It was sent to his channel and featured earlier in a small-roguelikes video. He calls it a solid word-based roguelike that blends word skill with ordinary video-game mechanics. He especially enjoyed playing it cooperatively with his girlfriend. Niche books and build-restricting rewards hold it back, so it lands in high B.
- **Quote:** I'm going to put Watch word like a high mid B tier.
- **Rerank Note:**

##### Praise

- Good mix of word skill and general video-game skill, with difficulty depending on both
- Balatro-style scoring and shop system where books replace jokers, enabling huge score moments
- Figuring out what to do with the offered letters is engaging
- Works well as a cooperative game, which is rare for roguelikes
- Tools like discard, hold and draw give room to customise runs
- Peaceful, relaxing music; sound design escalates well while scoring
- Pacing is nice, with a small 4x4 board that grows through the run

##### Criticism

- Slow start, and runs begin with similar short words because there is little to vary at first
- Many books are very niche and he rarely used them
- Some build strategies, like boosting four-letter words, lock you in and stop you chasing longer words, which he finds more rewarding
- Reward mechanics are a mixed bag
- Visuals are very basic

##### Unique Points

- Scrabble plus Balatro structure with books in place of jokers
- Board always starts 4x4 and grows over the run
- Naturally suited to co-op play, with one person finding words and the other handling the roguelike decisions

##### Comparisons

- Scrabble

##### Design Criteria

- Synergies/build variety: books enable combos but some are too niche or lock you into one strategy
- Reward design: incentivises short words over bigger words in some builds
- Pacing/run progression: slow start, small 4x4 board that builds up
- Audio: relaxing music and score-counting sound design that feels big
- Visuals: basic but pleasant
- Difficulty: depends on word skill plus general game skill

#### Best Things

- Fresh take on the Balatro-style formula: word tiles plus rule-changing books instead of poker hands
- 80+ book relics and tile variants (double-letter, metal, glitched, gold) produce synergy-driven builds
- Hand-drawn art and music are repeatedly called great
- Accessible and approachable, with accessibility settings praised; works for mixed ages and skill levels
- Allowing made-up words and swearing (but not slurs) gives strategic flexibility and feels fun
- Watchguard boss cycle keeps individual runs varied
- Relaxing, brain-exercising 'edu-tainment' feel; some say it improves vocabulary
- Runs well on Steam Deck, with trackpad input described as near flawless despite only partial touch support

#### Worst Things

- Short: the top positive-but-critical review says the game is 'REALLY short', feels like a proof of concept, and they ran out of content after a few runs
- Barely any difficulty scaling; a Steam discussion thread calls it too easy
- Different starting bags do not change playstyle enough to drive replayability
- At least one reviewer reports UI that is confusing and poor
- Crash reports: a recent negative review says the game crashes on launch, and a Steam thread documents frequent crashes
- Reports of perceived developer silence or lack of support in recent months
- Bugs raised in discussions: palindrome behavior, a book being discardable mid-run, and a tutorial softlock in the demo

#### Improvements

- More content and a fuller endgame or sequel-scale variety
- Stronger difficulty scaling and harder tiers
- More meaningful differences between starting bags
- Clearer UI, including seeing the projected score before submitting a word
- Custom dictionaries, localization, more books and achievements (the developer's stated roadmap)
- Seed selection and book editing were requested by players

#### Unique Things

- Anagram-roguelike framing: spell words rather than play poker hands
- Made-up words are allowed, so dictionary strictness is part of the strategy
- A 'watchword' mechanic expands the board
- Endless mode with billion-point scoring potential
- Magical book relics (80+) rewrite rules, like Balatro jokers but built around language
- Tile piece modifiers (metal, glitched, gold, double-letter) deck-build the letter pool

#### Tedious Or Disliked

- Runs can feel samey once you learn the bosses because difficulty barely climbs
- Dictionary questions and edge cases (words accepted or rejected) frustrated some players
- Confusing UI elements slow down reading what a play will score
- Crashes and softlocks interrupt runs for some users
- Having to tolerate low late-game challenge before unlocks pay off (progression unlocks bags, difficulties and books)

#### Divisive Points

- Length and value: some reviewers are still getting lots of play while others wish there was more content at $13.99 (specific hour counts could not be confirmed in the review data)
- Complexity: praised as approachable by some, called confusing by others
- Whether the game is 'complete': some treat it as early access or a proof of concept, others as a finished, polished game
