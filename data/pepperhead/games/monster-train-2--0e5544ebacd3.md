##### Praise

- Deployment phase: drawing all units at the start of each fight and placing them freely cuts RNG, while energy limits keep fights from feeling identical
- Huge build diversity from the clan system (two clans per run, two champions each), massively expanded by the spoiler reveal that restores the first game's clans, giving roughly 180 combinations
- New additions like equipment cards, room cards, unit abilities and a selectable Pyre feel like simple, well-judged layers of customization
- Ascension-style difficulty scaling makes early runs easy to jump into and have fun with crazy combos, without overwhelming the player
- Visuals, animation, art style and soundtrack are standout and instantly recognizable
- Rewind and restart-battle options help with mistakes and reveal how strategic and mathematical positioning and spell choices are

##### Criticism

- Spell-focused clan is the weakest: gimmicky and poor at fusing with other clans, even worse than in the first game
- Story element and joke dialogue on units feel unnecessary, though minor
- Pyre of Dominion is slightly overpowered and some balance tweaks are still wanted
- Deployment phase can make fights a bit consistent, and the rewind button can be used to cheat or feels slow and cheap
- Wants more boss variety and more content; community challenges don't interest him

##### Unique Points

- The hidden reveal that unlocks all clans from the first game inside the sequel, doubling build diversity, is one of the coolest things he has seen in a roguelike
- Deployment phase as a design fix for unit-draw RNG in an auto-battler deck builder
- Restart battle option teaches how much tiny positioning and spell choices matter

#### Best Things

- Huge replay value: 10 clans with a main-plus-ally pairing, plus covenant ranks, challenges, daily runs and Soul Savior mode; reviewers report hundreds of hours without boredom.
- Snowballing combat feels explosive and satisfying; assembling a broken build and steamrolling bosses is the recurring highlight.
- New systems (equipment, room cards, Pyre Hearts, the deployment phase) add build depth; IGN and Metacritic critics singled out equipment cards.
- Clan reworks and new clans are widely liked; original clans were rebuilt and are unlockable without extra cost, and the mushroom-themed Underlegion is a fan favourite.
- Strong soundtrack that keeps getting called out by both critics and players.
- Visual clarity aids such as the damage preview and improved quality-of-life over the first game.
- Good value and respectful monetisation: $24.99 base game, a free demo, and players note first-game content was not locked behind DLC.
- Accessible enough for newcomers to deckbuilders while still deep; some players who bounced off Slay the Spire say this one clicked.

#### Worst Things

- Heavy RNG and boom-or-bust runs: several reviewers say fights are either trivial or unwinnable, with few tight, close matches.
- Enemy and boss design can hard-counter a build (incant enemies, floor silence, sweep, cleave, magic shield, resurrection), punishing experimental builds.
- Post-launch bosses were criticised by some long-time players as overtuned and too dominant.
- UI is busy and oversized: players say it feels designed for mobile, is hard to read on PC, and everything flashes and shakes.
- Story, dialogue and cutscenes are widely regarded as weak (described as laughably or charmingly bad depending on the reviewer).
- Some feel it is expansion-sized rather than a true second game, and that the first game has better bosses, writing or polish.
- Status effects and card interactions pile up to the point where units carry too many effects to track.
- Some long-time players argue it lacks the difficulty and challenge options of Monster Train 1 and is too easy at the top if you know the tricks.

#### Improvements

- Reduce hard-counter encounters or let players see or influence upcoming enemy types so experimental builds are not blindsided.
- Add a UI or graphics simplification option and a less cluttered, more PC-friendly layout (whole train visible without scrolling).
- Restore first-game features some miss: a bigger boss pool, the Divine Temple shard system, and a mid-fight restart option.
- Rebalance or add toggles for overtuned post-launch bosses and weak clans (Awoken, Luna, Stygian and Gilda are mentioned as struggling).
- Improve the story and writing, or make cutscenes skippable or removable.
- Expand challenge and difficulty options and strengthen endless mode, which a Metacritic user called poorly designed.

#### Unique Things

- Three-floor vertical train with a Pyre: you position units on floors while enemies advance floor by floor, unlike Slay the Spire's single lane.
- Main clan plus allied clan pairing across 10 clans creates very many combinations, each with alternate starting champions.
- Deployment phase lets you place units across floors, making decks more spell-heavy and adding positioning decisions.
- Room cards, equipment and selectable Pyre Hearts layer extra scaling systems on top of the deck.
- Destiny of the Railforged adds the Railforged clan with a Forge mechanic plus a Soul Savior mode with upgradeable soul enhancements.
- Daily challenges with leaderboards and handcrafted Dimensional Challenges, plus a free unlimited demo.

#### Tedious Or Disliked

- Long unlock grind: levelling every clan to 10 and unlocking all cards and relics reportedly takes around 100 hours before the full game opens up.
- Having to repeat the same answer to recurring enemy mechanics (silence, sweep, incant) reduces build variety.
- Covenant (difficulty) climbing can feel like a loop of chasing a scaling multistriker or other high-roll engine.

#### Divisive Points

- Whether it is a perfect evolution of the first game or merely a big expansion; some players prefer Monster Train 1's polish and boss design.
- Difficulty and balance: some find it too easy with broken events and builds, while others find bosses and enemy counters punishing.
- Art direction and presentation: many love the art style and mushroom or cat designs, while some users describe the art as inconsistent or bad.
- Destiny of the Railforged is liked (83% positive on Steam, about 325 reviews) but notably less loved than the base game, with some complaining about balance of its new mode.
