### Community

- **One Liner:** A turn-based solitaire-meets-RPG roguelite where you clear cards from a tri-peaks-style tableau to charge four resources (Attack, Defense, Agility, Willpower) and cast spells against enemies, climbing to defeat Emperor Stuck.
- **Consensus Summary:** Solitairica is received as a clever, addictive mobile-born hybrid that critics scored well (Metacritic 82 from six mostly iOS reviews, 70 to 100) and Steam players rate Mostly Positive (about 76-78%, roughly 574 reviews). Fans love the twist on solitaire and the class/deck variety; detractors, who dominate the most-upvoted negative reviews, say the RNG feels unfair, unlock grinding is slow, and animations drag. It is a small-scale niche title (Boxleiter-style estimates put it at 100k-200k owners and low six-figure gross), not abandoned: the Steam forum has pinned threads for an 'Anniversary Update' and a '2025 FAQ' with activity through December 2025, and reviewers mention an update after years of silence. The mobile version originally launched in 2016 and PC came later.
- **Reception Notes:** Metacritic's 82 is based on six critic reviews (AppAdvice 100, TouchArcade 90, Apple'N'Apps 80, Multiplayer.it 80, Pocket Tactics 80, Gamezebo 70) that mostly reviewed the 2016 iOS version; Metacritic lists PC and iOS. #### Best Things

- Fresh hook: solitaire card-clearing drives RPG combat, described as 'solitaire where you can actually lose and cast spells'
- Four-resource system (Attack, Defense, Agility, Willpower) fuels spells, and synergy between spells, relics and enemy modifiers is praised as satisfying to figure out
- Many classes/decks to unlock, each with different starting resource generation and spell sets, giving variety between runs
- Relaxing, flow-state play that suits short bursts, podcasts or background TV; works well on Steam Deck
- Strong 'one more try' pull and high replay value for solitaire fans and returning mobile players; one reviewer calls the replay value 'insane'
- Accessible and easy to pick up; one reviewer plays it with their son, and the difficulty curve is described as easy to understand
- Critics called it a good fit for touch platforms and praised its addictive loop (AppAdvice 100, TouchArcade 90)
- Developer returned with an anniversary update years later, which some long-time players say improved the game

#### Worst Things

- RNG is the dominant complaint: stuns, poison, fire and card-altering effects are perceived as stacking unfairly, and the last needed card often fails to appear
- Late-game and final boss damage or card-flooding can exceed what a run can handle; some runs feel unwinnable regardless of build
- Progression grind: upgrades and decks cost 40-60 crystals while a failed run yields roughly 6-18, so unlocking everything takes many runs
- Animations and card flips feel slow even with the speed-up setting on
- Runs can feel samey: enemies are roughly the same each playthrough with only shuffled order and modifiers
- Little player agency over draws (no real tools to control card selection), which undercuts the 'strategy' tag for some
- Launch-era critic note that depth is limited and players repeat favourite spells after unlocking classes (Gamezebo 70)
- No cross-save or cross-play with the mobile version; PC release is Windows only

#### Improvements

- Add a middle difficulty option or softer RNG/difficulty curve (requested by several negative reviewers)
- Speed up animations further or allow skipping them
- Reduce the crystal grind or make early upgrade costs cheaper relative to run payouts
- Give players more tools to mitigate bad draws (mulligans, wildcard/joker-style effects) and review RNG bias, a topic raised on the Steam forum
- Controller support is a recurring forum request
- Cross-platform progress sync with the mobile version, and Mac/Linux builds

#### Unique Things

- Solitaire movement rule where you can play a card one rank above or below the exposed card, used as the core turn action
- Clearing specific card types charges resources that power spells, and boss/enemy attack timers force you to clear cards quickly
- Class-specific decks (warrior, rogue, wizard, bard and others) that each change how resources accrue
- Comically stuck-in-solitaire fantasy theme with Emperor Stuck as final boss, plus Aces/Kings/Queens as unlockable powerful deck cards
- Bridges mobile casual solitaire and roguelike deckbuilding, with Steam tags spanning Roguelike Deckbuilder, Relaxing and Addictive

#### Tedious Or Disliked

- Repeating the same early levels to farm crystals for each upgrade on each deck
- Slow card-flip and enemy-turn animations, cited as a reason to quit within minutes
- Turns spent passing while enemies add cards, poison, stun or drain abilities
- Achievement hunting: beating the final boss with every deck and a near-pure-RNG 'go from 1 health to full' achievement
- Bugs reported on the forum around the Bard class (spell purchasing, crashes on repeated Inspiration) and stacking effects

#### Divisive Points

- RNG: fans see it as the source of variety and say good builds smooth it out; others call it rigged and unfair
- Grind versus progression: some enjoy slowly unlocking slots and decks, while many call it painfully slow
- Mood: seen as a relaxing time-killer by some and as frustrating or boringly slow by others, with comparisons to Balatro as the more 'mature' alternative
