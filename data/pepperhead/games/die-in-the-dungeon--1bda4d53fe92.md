##### Praise

- Every die has a unique effect and adjustable numbers, giving deep customization of dice and board
- Three characters with different boards, starting dice and passives make each run feel like a new adventure
- Lots of content and regular updates keep it fresh on return visits
- Passive relics and newly added potions add variety and distinct playstyles
- Events have unique sprites and animations, immersing the player in the dungeon crawl
- Enemies interact with your board by placing things on it, adding strategic depth
- Dice design works around the luck handicap of dice games in an interesting way

##### Criticism

- Overwhelming at start due to many keywords and specific mechanics
- Art style is great but small, scattered text makes dice icons and descriptions hard to read and track
- Finding new dice later in a run is less satisfying than finding rare cards in Slay the Spire
- Defensive or weak builds do not die but make runs extremely long, e.g. 15-minute boss fights
- Some mechanics like terrain dice are inherently overpowered, showing balance difficulty
- Elites are just multiple waves of normal enemies, which wastes time versus one bigger, scarier enemy

##### Unique Points

- Dice as the core deckbuilder-like resource, placed on a board with enemy-interacting board effects
- Every die has its own effect and numbers rather than standard d4/d6/d8
- Events presented with bespoke sprites and animations rather than plain text

##### Comparisons

- Slay the Spire

##### Design Criteria

- Luck vs strategy: how a dice game manages randomness in a turn-based format
- Customization/build variety: unique dice, board, passives, relics
- Character diversity: different boards, dice and passives per character
- Readability/UI: art style and small text hinder understanding
- Balance: certain dice like terrain dice are too strong
- Run length/pacing: defensive builds drag, multi-wave elites waste time
- Reward satisfaction: new dice feel less exciting than rare cards
- Content and updates: amount of content and freshness
- Events: visual and thematic effort in events

### Community

- **One Liner:** A turn-based roguelike deckbuilder by ATICO where you use dice instead of cards, slotting attack, defense, healing and booster dice onto a character-specific board and boosting them with relics, starring cute pixel-art frogs fighting insects.
- **Consensus Summary:** Players like it a lot: Steam shows Very Positive (about 92% of roughly 3,600 reviews) and the developers and publisher (HypeTrain Digital, Sidekick Publishing) got it through Early Access (started Feb 21, 2025) to a 1.0 launch on May 1, 2026, with Switch also mentioned. Critics coverage is thin (two OpenCritic reviews, both very positive, and no Metacritic score). Praise centres on the dice-on-a-grid puzzle, pixel art and music, and an approachable on-ramp for genre newcomers. The recurring complaints are slow, long runs, builds converging on the same few strategies, enemies that do not scale so stalling or full-blocking works, and a lack of character and build variety. Reviewers who stay long (endgame players) are markedly more positive than those who bounce in the first hours.

#### Best Things

- Swapping cards for dice placed on a shaped board turns each turn into a spatial optimisation puzzle; reviewers call it a fresh take that changes how you plan, not just a reskin
- Pixel art and character/enemy design (cute frogs vs. bugs) are the most universally praised element, along with a well-liked soundtrack
- Combos can get very deep, for example once you can change faulty dice; long-time players say the synergy space is expansive
- Approachable: tooltips get new players going quickly, and it is described as a good gateway into the genre
- Four characters with different board shapes and sizes, plus artifacts and curses that alter how dice, board and enemies interact
- Events can be played around with your dice and many can be skipped if your dice are strong, which players find more engaging than event screens in peers
- Active, community-minded dev team: enemy design contests and community lore writing, regular Early Access updates, free Origins demo
- Good value at $14.99 with lots of hours of playtime reported by fans (some with several hundred hours)

#### Worst Things

- Runs and individual fights drag; many reviewers report runs of 60-90+ minutes, with enemies that have very high HP and low damage and no pressure to end fights
- Board readability degrades late: stacked effects on one tile and huge boosted numbers become hard to parse, to the point of not knowing what happened in some fights
- Builds converge on one or a few viable strategies, often stacking block and infinite or near-infinite combos; many dice offered in early acts feel like they make your deck worse
- Balance swings between trivial and brutal: some players finish with block in the hundreds and lose almost no runs, while others hit a wall at the higher ascension levels (D3 to D8)
- Characters feel too similar to some players, especially the two block-focused ones, and there are few non-attack/block dice types in practice
- Relics are seen as either underwhelming or game-solving, with weak payoffs for build themes such as glass or gold dice
- Accessibility gaps: constant looping animations that cannot be turned off triggered migraines/motion sickness for some, and the pixel font is hard to read for some; Steam Deck controller support was reported as poor
- Lore is thin in game with much of it reportedly living in the Discord
- Several negative reviewers say it lacks staying power or 'sauce' compared with Slay the Spire, Peglin or Astrea: numbers just go up, decisions repeat each turn, and progression feels flat

#### Improvements

- Add a fast mode or option to skip or speed animations to shorten long runs
- Improve readability: clearer display when several effects share a tile, and a breakdown view showing how relics and modifiers change each die's numbers
- Rebalance so more builds are viable, and so blocking every turn or rejecting all dice rewards is not the dominant approach; make enemies scale or add pressure to long fights
- More dice types, characters, relics with bigger build-around payoffs, and more run-to-run variety
- Add an accessibility mode (toggle for wiggling animations, readable font) and proper Steam Deck controller support
- Reduce the grind of levelling every character for unlocks and 100% completion, and make higher difficulties less reliant on cheesy infinite combos

#### Unique Things

- Dice rather than cards, placed into a per-character grid board where position and adjacency matter
- Attack, defense, healing and booster dice colour-coded, with boosters and copy effects that feed off neighbours
- Starting gear and dice that are worth keeping all run, unlike the disposable starter cards in many Slay the Spire-likes
- Began as a 2021 game jam entry and itch.io demo and grew into a Steam release with a community-involved development style
- Frogs-versus-bugs presentation with a gambling-flavoured risk/reward layer on events

#### Tedious Or Disliked

- Long, slow combats and runs with simple decisions repeated every turn
- Levelling characters to unlock content and pursue completion, which fans themselves call grindy
- Having to re-read dice descriptions and edge cases each turn, which makes it feel like homework to some
- Late-game number inflation and high-difficulty negative modifiers that push toward infinite-combo builds
- Persistent animations and small pixel text for motion- or migraine-sensitive players

#### Divisive Points

- Difficulty: some say it is far too easy and easy to break, others find the ascension levels brutal
- Run pace: some enjoy the calm, strategic tempo; others compare it unfavourably to 30-minute Slay the Spire runs
- Build variety: fans praise the combo depth while critics say choices are an illusion and everything converges
- Longevity: players with hundreds of hours love it, while a notable minority lose interest within 5-10 hours
