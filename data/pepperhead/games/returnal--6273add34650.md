### Community

- **One Liner:** A third-person sci-fi bullet-hell shooter by Housemarque in which astronaut Selene relives death on the alien planet Atropos, with a roguelike run structure, Metroidvania-style traversal unlocks and a cosmic-horror time-loop story.
- **Consensus Summary:** Critics received Returnal very well: Metacritic is 86 on both PS5 (115 reviews) and PC (Climax Studios port, Feb 2023), and it won Best Game at the British Academy Games Awards. Players are more split: Metacritic user score is 7.4 (68% positive, 12% mixed, 20% negative), and Steam sits at Very Positive (about 81%, roughly 11,100 reviews). Nearly everyone loves the gunplay, movement, sound and atmosphere; the split comes from the brutal difficulty, long runs with thin meta-progression, and (on PC) technical problems. Sony has effectively finished updating it, so PC issues that appeared after the last patch are still open. The game is fully released, not early access.
- **Reception Notes:** PS5 launch (Apr 2021) was critically acclaimed, with debate over the no-save design; Update 2.0 (Oct 2021) added suspend cycle saves, and the free Ascension update (Mar 2022) added co-op and the Tower of Sisyphus. It sold about 866,000 copies by Feb 2022 and won Best Game at BAFTA. The PC version released Feb 15, 2023 (Climax Studios, PlayStation Publishing) with the same Metacritic 86; many current Steam negatives concern a VRAM leak introduced in a late patch, Epic Online Services and PSN linking, and co-op bugs. #### Best Things

- Fluid, responsive third-person shooting and movement (dash, jump, melee, adrenaline mechanic) that feels arcade-tight and addictive
- Bullet-hell enemy and boss design that is readable and rewards pattern mastery; players talk about getting noticeably better over time
- Best-in-class sound design, music and lighting; the oppressive, eerie alien atmosphere (Giger/Beksinski-style visuals) is widely praised
- Strong voice performance by Jane Perry and a mysterious psychological-sci-fi story that rewards digging into scout logs and secret endings
- Weapon traits, parasites and artifacts create risk/reward decisions inside a run; Metroidvania-style tool unlocks open up traversal
- Post-launch content: Ascension update added online co-op and the Tower of Sisyphus endless mode, praised by fans who want pure gameplay with no story
- Runs acceptably on modest PCs and supports suspend-cycle saves since Update 2.0, making long runs less punishing
- High feel of mastery: many Steam reviewers with 50-180+ hours call it a favourite game of the decade and a Hades alternative for action fans

#### Worst Things

- PC port stability: widely reported VRAM/memory leak and texture-streaming degradation after a late patch, forcing restarts every hour or two and never fixed
- Crashes on launch or after the logos, black screens and poor frametimes for some players despite high fps
- Unwanted Epic Online Services install and optional PlayStation account linking (with region-locked prompts) on Steam drew a wave of negative reviews
- Co-op is buggy: lost runs after disconnect as guest, locked shops, reticle/ammo UI bugs, curses carrying over, loading-screen joins, and story progress only counting for the host
- Very punishing difficulty with no accessibility or easier mode; a single early hit can cripple a run because healing only raises max health when already full
- Environments are repetitive across biomes (same structures, palette swaps) and the loop feels samey after many runs
- Sony effectively abandoned PC support; full price on PC years later is criticised, and the next Housemarque game (Saros) is not coming to PC

#### Improvements

- Add an easier or accessibility/narrative difficulty so story-focused players can progress
- Stronger permanent meta-progression so failed runs feel like they move the needle
- Fix the PC VRAM leak and crash bugs and keep patching the port
- More build variety: larger pool of weapons, artifacts and non-stat-boost upgrades, plus more biome/environment variety
- Shorter runs or more checkpoints beyond suspend-cycle for players with limited time
- Fix co-op reliability (reconnect handling, shared story progress) and drop the forced extra launcher/online services

#### Unique Things

- Third-person bullet-hell shooter hybrid, rare in a genre dominated by top-down/2D roguelikes
- Runs are long (often 1-3 hours) and open-ended with persistent tools and Metroidvania-style gating rather than short quick runs
- Procedurally shuffled rooms paired with hand-built biome bosses and handcrafted story progression
- Haptic/adaptive trigger and 3D audio design built around the DualSense, carried into a cosmic-horror time-loop narrative
- Adrenaline, overload and malfunction systems (parasites, cursed items, weapon proficiency that persists) make every pickup a risk decision
- Tower of Sisyphus endless mode and 2-player online co-op added after launch

#### Tedious Or Disliked

- Losing a long run to a single mistake, with minimal carry-over; runs feel like they do not respect player time
- Having to replay early biomes repeatedly to reach where you died, seeing the same sequences dozens of times
- Random trap rooms, laser-heavy rooms and spikes felt cheap or cause lag on some PCs
- Incoming attacks are hard to track in chaotic fights, making hits feel unavoidable to some
- Needing to restart the PC game every hour because of the memory leak
- Unlocks and permanent progression feel too minor to help performance, so the grind feels unrewarding (a top-voted negative review)

#### Divisive Points

- Permadeath and no-checkpoint structure: fans see it as thematically perfect and the source of mastery, detractors call it exhausting and unfair
- Story: some love the ambiguity and secret endings, others cannot follow it or find the heroine sparsely written
- Roguelike credentials: judged as a roguelike, some say it lacks build variety and quick runs; others say it reinvents the genre
- PC port: some report flawless performance on cheap hardware, others unplayable instability or VRAM leaks
