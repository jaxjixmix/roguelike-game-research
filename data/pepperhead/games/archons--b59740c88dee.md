- **Rerank Note:**

##### Praise

- Two-character control is a unique twist on the horde-like genre and feels great once it clicks
- Abilities tie into the two-character movement, so each run feels different
- Everything is synced to the electronic music beat, and this can be used strategically (e.g. timed grenades)
- Gentle progression: starts simple, introduces more, slow early gameplay lets players adjust to the controls
- Good damage feedback and readability, so he usually knows why he took damage even with characters far apart
- Solid meta progression and enemy design; attractive look and feel

##### Criticism

- Game is short: only three levels, each ending in a boss
- Bosses are identical every run and the difficulty meter adds little new
- Wants more content and enemy variation in levels
- On-death and bounce-type abilities are useless against lone bosses with no adds, discouraging those boons
- The two-character control may not click for everyone

##### Unique Points

- Controlling two characters at once in a horde-like is a novel mechanic combined with a fitting genre
- Beat-synced damage ticks and ability triggers merge music and sound design, and enable rhythm-based strategy
- Design lesson: give boss fights small adds so on-kill and bounce effects still trigger

##### Comparisons

- Vampire Survivors
- other horde-likes / bullet heaven games

##### Design Criteria

- Novelty/unique twist: two-character control is the core hook
- Audio/music integration: beat-synced effects and sound design
- Meta-progression: substantial unlocks that suit the learning curve
- Onboarding and difficulty curve: slow, forgiving start that builds comfort
- Readability/feedback: clear cause of damage
- Content volume: only three levels and repeated bosses
- Boss design and build synergy: lone bosses invalidate some ability types
- Enemy design: liked, but wants more variety
- Replayability: abilities plus movement make runs feel different

### Community

- **One Liner:** A short roguelite/survivor bullet-hell from BulletHell Studios in which one player controls two characters at once, one per hand/stick, with weapons that fire in time to the music.
- **Consensus Summary:** Archons is a small, cheap indie (Steam, Windows only, Dec 6 2024) that sits at Very Positive on Steam (about 92-94% of roughly 430 reviews). Players love the two-character control hook, the tight positioning-focused combat and the music that syncs with weapon attacks. The recurring complaints are that it is short (around 3 hours for the main story, 3 bosses/levels), that content and enemy variety are thin, and that solo play is very mentally taxing while the game is clearly tuned for a controller or local co-op. It has no Metacritic or OpenCritic entry, so there is no critic consensus; reception is entirely player-driven. A few reviewers feel it is under-supported, and one said it seems abandoned, but I found no public patch notes confirming or refuting that. It is frequently discounted to about $1 (90% off the $9.99 base).

#### Best Things

- The dual-character mechanic is fresh: controlling two Archons at once is hectic but 'just works' and creates a flow state
- Weapons and attack animations are synced to the soundtrack, praised as a standout (composer Oliver Orand is named in a review)
- Movement and positioning matter more than spamming abilities; you cannot stand still or circle-strafe and win
- Polished and respects your time: a short run-based experience, with endless mode and a hard mode for replay
- Spectacular boss fights and satisfying build combos when rolls line up
- Great value at its frequent steep discounts; StarCraft references delight some players
- Full controller support and local split-screen co-op

#### Worst Things

- Very short: main story is only 3 stages/bosses, and reviewers call it short or a bit short
- Limited enemy variety; reviewers say many enemies use the same attack pattern and there are few maps
- Maps are too dark and hard to read; some players had to raise brightness
- No camera control and a rotating camera that adds to the stress
- Mouse and keyboard play is poor or problematic; a controller is strongly recommended
- Weak ability synergies and lackluster ability evolutions; the Archon ultimate is a static barrage
- Polish and optimization issues reported, plus poor translation and UI complaints in one negative review
- Ultimate is hard-coded to F even after rebinding (one reviewer)

#### Improvements

- Add a real single-character or AI-companion mode so solo players are not forced to control two characters
- More content: more bosses/stages, maps and enemy types with distinct attacks
- Give abilities real synergies with the Archon ultimate and stronger ability evolutions
- Improve map brightness/visibility and add camera options
- Fix mouse and keyboard support, rebinding bugs (ultimate on F) and UI/translation rough spots
- Add online co-op (requested in Steam discussions) and New Game+ / quality-of-life features
- Differentiate the two characters visually (color suggestion in discussions)

#### Unique Things

- You control two characters simultaneously, one per hand, as a survivor-style bullet hell, compared by one reviewer to It Takes Two meets Furi
- Attacks and weapons are rhythm-synced to the music
- Bloodrunes let you sacrifice health for power, and there are multiple currencies to spend
- Local split-screen co-op where each player pilots one Archon, versus solo where one player pilots both
- Positioning-first design rather than screen-filling particle chaos or auto-fire snowballing

#### Tedious Or Disliked

- Solo play demands a lot of mental juice and is described as stressful; one reviewer called it the most stressful roguelike they had tried
- Some unlocks/research feel grindy ('after a few grind sessions') and one reviewer said difficulty collapsed once a certain research was unlocked
- Healing pickups are time-limited and released by breaking containers, so they get wasted when damage breaks them at random
- Dark maps and lack of camera control are frustrating
- Reported bugs: freezing after reroll, an ice-ground mechanic problem in Endless Hard mode, and an achievement (Heartache) not unlocking

#### Divisive Points

- Solo vs co-op: some say the game is meant to be played solo (that is the challenge), others say it is made for co-op and solo is too hectic
- Price and length: some call it great only on discount while others say even full price is fair for the polish
- Difficulty: challenging until the two-character skill clicks, then some find builds too easy or some weapons unviable on hard mode
