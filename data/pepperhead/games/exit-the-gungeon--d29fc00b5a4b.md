### Community

- **One Liner:** A 2D vertical bullet-hell platformer spin-off of Enter the Gungeon where the Gungeoneers ride a collapsing elevator upward, with a blessed gun that keeps morphing into random weapons.
- **Consensus Summary:** Exit the Gungeon is received as a competent but clearly lesser spin-off: critics land in mixed-to-decent territory (Metacritic 68 Switch / 67 PC, about 45% of OpenCritic critics recommending) and Steam players are Mostly Positive at about 74% across roughly 3,800 reviews. Most praise the chaotic arcade energy, art, humor and bosses, while criticising the forced random gun-swapping, cramped arenas and reduced depth versus Enter the Gungeon. It began life as an Apple Arcade mobile title (Sept 2019) before reaching Switch/PC (Mar 2020), and many negative reviews are really 'this is not Enter the Gungeon' complaints. A late content update (Hello to Arms, adding an Arsenal Mode with normal weapon pickups) softened the biggest complaint, but a Meowitzer boss softlock bug is still reported in recent Steam reviews. It is a cheap, often-discounted short-session game rather than a long-term roguelite.
- **Reception Notes:** Launched Sept 2019 on Apple Arcade, then Mac/Windows/Switch in March 2020, PS4/Xbox One in Nov 2020, and separate iOS/Android releases in Aug 2025 per Wikipedia. Critics were mixed (71% of Metacritic Switch reviews rated Mixed, none Negative); the random-gun mechanic and loss of depth were the main sticking points. Steam is Mostly Positive (about 74%), with negative reviews heavily focused on the Meowitzer softlock, forced blessed-style runs and disappointment versus Enter the Gungeon. Final update Hello to Arms added Arsenal Mode for normal weapon pickups per a Steam reviewer. Steam has roughly 200k-500k owners (SteamSpy) and an estimated $1.17M gross / $345k net revenue (steam-revenue-calculator.com, Boxleiter-method estimate, not audited). Price $9.99, often on deep sale. Windows only on Steam, no Mac/Linux listing there. Steam recent reviews continue through 2026, indicating an ongoing trickle of players and a still-present boss bug. #### Best Things

- Frantic, nonstop arcade bullet-hell action with very little downtime and short runs that suit quick sessions
- Same Gungeon charm: humor, weapon designs, pixel art and animation, soundtrack and sound design
- Boss fights that remix the original's bosses into fusion bosses with new attack patterns
- Per-character elevator routes and a new vertical structure give it a distinct feel from the first game
- Unlockable hats, new costumes and small extras (Winchester and Loblina minigames, robot hearts now useful, Cultist playable without co-op) that completionists enjoy
- Cheap price and frequent sales make it decent value as a pickup-and-play game

#### Worst Things

- Constantly changing blessed gun means you rarely build toward anything; combo-based weapon quality still delivers junk guns at bad moments
- Meowitzer boss bug (boss turns invisible, softlocks or kills the run) is reported by many reviewers and persists years later
- Elevator arenas are small, flat and cramped, leaving little room for dodging or platforming; enemies spawn constantly
- Much less content and depth than Enter the Gungeon: no synergies, little new enemy/gun/music content, runs feel repetitive quickly
- Jump-immunity and dodge-roll spam can trivialise bullets and some bosses instead of rewarding skillful movement
- Stiff/odd controls and sometimes disorienting camera, cluttered visuals that hide hazards, and moving shooting platforms in later stages
- Feels like a simplified, mobile-origin spin-off rather than a true sequel; the most upvoted negative reviews are fans saying it is not Enter the Gungeon and forces blessed-style runs on them

#### Improvements

- Fix the Meowitzer softlock and other audio/visual bugs
- Offer more freedom over weapons by default (normal pickups/synergies) instead of relying on blessed guns; Arsenal Mode only partially addresses this
- Larger, more varied arenas with real platforming space and less spawn spam
- More new content (guns, enemies, characters, music) rather than recycled assets from Enter the Gungeon
- Tuning of blanks, hazards and weapon rolls so randomness feels less unfair

#### Unique Things

- Vertical elevator-climb structure instead of exploring a branching dungeon
- Weapon auto-morphs every ~30 seconds, with quality tied to a hitless kill combo (0-20)
- Jumping grants airborne invulnerability, making jump a core defensive tool alongside rolling
- Bosses are fusions of Enter the Gungeon bosses
- Hub (Underbreach) with NPCs and small minigames like Angry-Birds-style and pachinko-style games
- Began as an Apple Arcade exclusive and was designed as an arcade-style bite-sized spin-off

#### Tedious Or Disliked

- Forced elevator waves in early levels feel tedious and repetitive
- Random weapon swaps at bad moments, and combo reset on any hit, feel punishing rather than fun
- 100%-ing the game becomes grindy; reviewers describe it as dragging after the main content is done
- Repeated boss and enemy reuse and small gun pool make runs feel samey after a few characters
- Getting Meowitzer repeatedly and needing to restart runs because of the bug
- Levels feel flat and compact, with little platforming space, making later runs a grind of dodging spawn waves

#### Divisive Points

- Blessed/random weapon system: some find it chaotic fun, many find it the game's biggest flaw
- Comparison to Enter the Gungeon: defenders say it should be judged as a different arcade game, detractors call it a creative failure and letdown for fans
- Difficulty: some say it is very hard (bosses, tiny arenas), others find it easy once jump-dodging is discovered
- Value: cheap sale price seen as fair for what it is, but others feel even that is too much
