### Commentary


#### Entry 1

- **Part:** 3
- **Label:** Tier list Part 3 (2025)
- **Summary:** Bing in Wonderland is a traditional 2D action roguelike with a watercolor look and a goofy, flash-game vibe. He enjoys the fast, fun combat and the hat-throw dash, but finds the roguelike layer (boons, build variety) minimal and replayability low. Runs are short, about 15-20 minutes, ending in a boss, across 15+ levels. He says he is placing it above Baloner, with no tier letter stated in this segment.
- **Quote:** I think I'm going to put Bing above Baloner.
- **Rerank Note:**

##### Praise

- Watercolor-style visuals and good-looking, varied biomes
- Weapons (bow, axe, harp) play very differently from each other
- Unique hat-throw dash that gives invincibility frames for the whole travel, longer if the hat is farther
- Shield-break mechanic: stunned enemies can be jumped on for a big area-of-effect with a dramatic animation
- Fast-paced, satisfying combat that feels powerful from the start of a run
- Goofy charm (for example the food/pooping mechanic) and nostalgic flash-game feel
- Bosses have enjoyable mechanics

##### Criticism

- Roguelike elements are minimal; boons only add to what you were already doing and do not change how you play
- You do not feel very different from start to end of a run
- Enemies can feel dull, similar to each other, and sometimes confusing; some bosses are just bigger enemies
- Low replayability; the draw is finishing the levels rather than replaying runs
- Some character unlocks are gated behind beating a specific boss with a specific weapon/item, which is easy to miss

##### Unique Points

- Hat-throw-then-dash as an invulnerable dash mechanic he has rarely seen elsewhere
- Universal shield-break stun that feeds a jump-on AoE, central to the core loop
- Meta-progression includes unlocking and fusing characters with minor effects

##### Comparisons

- Baloner
- Flash games

##### Design Criteria

- Gameplay feel: fast, fun action with strong moment-to-moment combat
- Weapon diversity: distinct weapon move sets
- Roguelike depth: boons should meaningfully change play, and here they do not
- Replayability: low, driven by completing levels
- Run length: short 15-20 minute runs suit the flash-game style
- Visuals/charm: watercolor style and goofy enemies
- Enemy/boss design: similar enemies, bosses partly upscaled enemies
- Meta-progression: simple stat unlocks and character fusion, with some annoying unlock conditions

#### Best Things

- Simple, satisfying, addictive combat loop that is easy to pick up and hard to put down
- The butt slam ('Big Sit') and hat-throw mechanics add a distinctive, fun, strategic layer
- Wide variety of boons, weapons (bow, spear, cannon, axe, harp) and items enabling different builds
- Charming ink-wash / hand-drawn art style and quirky, absurd enemy designs
- Strong sense of humour, including funny achievements and deliberately wacky translation
- Has a clear end goal rather than endless runs, with a rewarding progression loop
- Fast stages (around 10 minutes each) and good controller and Steam Deck support
- Very cheap price for the amount of playtime (low single-digit to ~$9)

#### Worst Things

- Content runs thin and gameplay becomes repetitive after several hours; levels and visuals (e.g. the desert stage) blur together
- Sudden, sharp difficulty spikes in the mid-to-late game
- The Big Sit mechanic becomes overpowered and can trivialize other weapons and options
- Meta-progression feels long and grindy, with resources consumed quickly or poorly explained
- Humour (toilet and fart jokes) and broken-English style text read as childish or hard to understand for some
- Some indie jankiness in assets and overall polish
- Short, looping menu/shop music noted as grating in the Switch review
- Basic button-mashing combat feels shallow or plain to some; the most-upvoted negative reviews call it 'kinda boring' or just a thin action-platformer

#### Improvements

- More content: new weapons, stages, enemies and DLC beyond cosmetics
- Higher difficulty modes or an infinite mode to extend late-game appeal
- Clearer map and progression hints, and better explanation of upgrade resources
- A more casual or less grindy play option / rebalanced permanent upgrades
- Rebalance Big Sit so other builds stay competitive
- More varied music and stage palettes

#### Unique Things

- The butt slam (Big Sit) stomp as a central combat and damage mechanic
- Hat-throwing attack alongside charge shots and dashes
- Spending 'wisdom points' as an alternative currency, which one reviewer had never seen before
- Deliberately absurd Chinese-ink-painting world with surreal enemies (e.g. headbanging fish) and intentionally odd translation humour
- Wave-based platform brawler structure with no exploration, unlike most roguelites
- Self-aware 'pointless features and useless details' marketing tone

#### Tedious Or Disliked

- Grinding talent points and achievements between runs interrupts short-session roguelike play
- Repetitive, monotonous battles after a few hours as variety plateaus
- Looping shop/menu music during vendor stops
- Permanent stat upgrades make it feel like grind-until-strong rather than a pure roguelite (one negative reviewer's complaint)
- Difficulty spikes forcing reliance on grinding or a specific strategy like Big Sit
- Plain button-mashing combat that feels boring or shallow to some players, especially fans of the developer's earlier, more inventive Wanba Warriors

#### Divisive Points

- Art style and humour: charming and quirky to many, childish or off-putting to others
- Big Sit dominance: celebrated as the signature feature by some, criticised as a balance problem by others
- Thin content versus low price: some see great value, others say to just play Brotato or Hades-likes instead
