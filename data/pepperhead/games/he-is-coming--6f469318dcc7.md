#### Entry 1

- **Part:** 4
- **Label:** Tier list Part 4 (2025)
- **Summary:** He is Coming is an early-access auto-battler roguelike where you walk a forest gathering items and events across a 3-day cycle, then fight a boss, repeating until a final boss. The moment-to-moment gameplay is deliberately simple, but the item pool is full of mechanics-changing pieces that enable many wild build paths. He also loves the asynchronous PvP mode, where you face other players' saved builds and chase king status. He calls it one of his favorite games of the genre and places it in an easy A tier, above Clover Pit.
- **Quote:** I love simple games with crazy strategies.
- **Rerank Note:**

##### Praise

- Simple, low-effort gameplay paired with deep, complex roguelike strategy
- Huge variety of items that alter mechanics rather than giving flat stat bumps, enabling many build paths (bomb chains, acid/armor conversion)
- Forest map is full of events, fusion, shopkeepers and unique weapons/pendants, so he still finds new things after long play
- Speed options, skippable trivial fights and teleporting keep pacing fast and never boring
- Bosses each have puzzle-like mechanics and you can adapt your build to the announced upcoming boss
- Asynchronous PvP against other players' builds, with king progression, creates a meta and strong replayability
- Clear, non-overwhelming pixel art and great classic eight-bit music
- Low barrier to entry and selectable item bundles in PvP add flexibility; achievements unlock more items

##### Criticism

- Gameplay itself has almost nothing to do; the depth comes entirely from the roguelike elements
- The second map is less simple and in his view weaker than the first map
- Some build complexity gets very intricate, though he frames this as a positive

##### Unique Points

- Uses a very simple exploration/auto-battler loop and puts nearly all the depth into items and builds
- PvP is asynchronous: your dead build becomes an opponent for others, with beat-five-to-become-king and unbeaten-king goals
- Players can pick item bundles for PvP instead of having every item, shaping build diversity

- Clover Pit

- Build diversity and synergies: many mechanically distinct items enabling varied builds
- Simplicity vs depth: simple gameplay is fine if roguelike elements are strong
- Replayability and discovery: events and items in the forest keep surprising him
- Boss design: each boss is a unique puzzle requiring adaptation
- Pacing and run speed: speed options and skippable fights keep it fast
- Multiplayer/meta: async PvP adds strategic layer and a meta
- Visuals and audio: clear pixel art and good eight-bit music
- Accessibility: low commitment to get into runs
- Progression: achievements unlock new items

#### Best Things

- Tight, addictive core loop with short runs (roughly 10-15 minutes), so a bad-RNG run costs little
- Deep build theorycrafting: 350+ items, set bonuses, pairing items to merge them and save inventory slots; compared by players to MtG-style triggers and to Rummy
- Second biome (swamp) brings new mechanics, status effects and items rather than a reskin
- Kingmaker asynchronous PvP uses ghosts of other players' builds as bosses, and a daily hall of fame drives experimentation
- Strong retro pixel art, sound design and catchy chiptune music with a nostalgic NES feel
- Active developer: patches weekly or sometimes daily, listens to feedback
- Large compendium with 100+ unlockable challenges and many achievements giving long-term goals; players report 100-300+ hours
- Player agency in movement and item choices feels more direct than in Loop Hero, which some reviewers prefer

#### Worst Things

- Heavy RNG: synergies often require several specific pieces, and runs can be dead on day one if a key piece never appears
- Unlocking items via the compendium dilutes the pool, so the game feels harder and less synergistic the more you unlock
- Content is thin for some players: two biomes and a PvP mode, no final Demon King fight yet, two levels cleared in 3-4 hours by some
- Auto-battle means you feel like a spectator; decisions mostly reduce to which item to take
- Kingmaker PvP is accused of being meta-driven, sweaty and unfriendly to off-meta builds, with balance changes tilted toward PvP
- Screen shake causes headaches for some players with no option to disable it (most-upvoted negative review)
- Combat lacks animation, so fights look static and repetitive (critic and players)
- Price ($19.99 / about 9.99 euro in some regions) judged too high by some for an Early Access title with limited content
- Runs and campaigns feel short and easy: several reviewers say combos end before you get to enjoy them, and both maps can be cleared in about 3 hours
- Game play feels shallow to some: mostly walking around picking up good items, with little strategy beyond matching items to the boss

#### Improvements

- Add more luck mitigation: rerolls, stronger banishing, more reliable ways to shape the item pool, and more useful transmutation
- Add an undo or confirmation for accidentally swapping weapons, and let oils and edge upgrades carry over when changing weapon
- Add more roguelite meta progression (permanent upgrades, better starts) to offset the item-dilution problem
- Add an accessibility option to disable or reduce screen shake
- Deliver the promised third map and Demon King final boss, and more single-player content so it is not dominated by PvP
- Rework Gauntlet (real-time PvP) mode, whose path generation can be lopsided, and rebalance Kingmaker rewards and the crown system
- Quality-of-life requests such as a paper-doll avatar showing equipment, who-killed-you info on the death screen, and a skippable intro

#### Unique Things

- Day-and-step limited exploration with darkness, watchtowers and campfires, so routing and scouting are a core skill
- Boss every three days that you can challenge early to gain inventory slots, turning timing into a risk/reward decision
- Items are the only progression (no levelling), with pair-merging to compress inventory
- Asynchronous Kingmaker mode where bosses are other players' saved builds, plus a separate real-time duel (Gauntlet) mode
- Hidden objectives in the compendium that unlock items, with biome-specific item sets
- Often compared to Loop Hero, Backpack Battles and Slay the Spire, but mixes a map-exploration layer with auto-battle

#### Tedious Or Disliked

- Restarting repeatedly until RNG cooperates, especially on Hard and Very Hard difficulties
- Opening chests and discarding them because you cannot store, craft or manipulate items between days
- Filler fights against ordinary enemies that pose no challenge while you wait for a build to form
- Grinding the compendium and achievements, after which motivation drops sharply for some
- Keeping up with the PvP meta and heavy amount of item data to learn
- Unskippable intro and overtuned bosses mentioned in community threads

#### Divisive Points

- RNG: some see it as the intended loop that you learn to work around, others call it slot-machine gameplay with too little control
- Auto-battler format: a turn-off for some, the point for others who like pure build theorycrafting
- Kingmaker PvP: praised as smart asynchronous multiplayer by some, called unfun, meta-bound and a drag on single-player balance by others
- Value: worth $20 for 100+ hours to many, overpriced for an Early Access game with two maps to others
