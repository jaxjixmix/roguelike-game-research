### Community

- **One Liner:** A fast, Hades-inspired action roguelike from Striking Distance Studios (The Callisto Protocol) where a low-ranking guard races rival survivors and infected inmates to the last escape pod out of Black Iron Prison.
- **Consensus Summary:** Critics and players land in the same place: a confident, stylish, punchy Hades-like with a distinctive twist (rival survivors and a run clock), held back by grind, repetition and some design friction. Metacritic shows a 77 Metascore from 11 critics (9 positive, 2 mixed, 0 negative) and a 7.9 user score; Steam sits at Very Positive (about 82% of ~360 reviews). It launched Oct 27, 2024 on PC (Steam and Epic), PS5 and Xbox Series X as a full release, not early access, and Steam reviews show the game still being played and patched into 2026. Commercially it looks modest: a third-party Steam estimate puts gross revenue at only about $142k, though that tool is an order-of-magnitude guess and ignores console and Epic sales. The YouTuber whose ranking prompted this research put it in a fairly high B tier.
- **Reception Notes:** Launched Oct 27, 2024 (PC, PS5, Xbox Series X) at $24.99 from KRAFTON and also released on Epic Games Store. Launch criticism on Steam forums targeted the marketing and trailers, and the Epic version has been called unsupported. Critic reception was solid and consistent (only GameSpot at 60 and GAMES.CH at 76 fell below 80), while Steam user sentiment is Very Positive but with a small sample. SteamSpy lists 0-20k owners and very low concurrent users, and a Boxleiter-based estimate gives about $142k gross / $42k net on Steam alone, so treat the commercial picture as unverified and probably understated for multi-platform.

#### Best Things

- Combat feels smooth and punchy, with a mix of melee, gun and kick/push, and environmental hazards and destructible areas to exploit
- The Rivals system (competing survivors racing you to the escape pod, who you can sabotage via prison systems) is called a fresh take on the genre by several critics and players
- Strong comic-book / cel-shaded art style and dark humor that gives the Callisto Protocol universe a punk, arcade tone
- Fast-paced soundtrack (Mark Mothersbaugh is credited by a Steam reviewer) that matches the pace of the action
- Wide variety of suits, weapons and experiments to build around; specific weapons (e.g. broken bottles, shankers) are fan favorites
- Good level design and pacing once the game clicks; after the first win, many difficulty modifiers add replay value
- Plays well on Steam Deck and with a controller, per Steam reviewers
- Fighting your own corpse from previous runs and the do-or-die final run are memorable ideas

#### Worst Things

- Progression is very grindy: around six separate currencies, each unlocking one specific thing, and runs often yield only a handful of the one you need
- The first roughly 8 hours are described as a slog because difficulty starts high and upgrades arrive slowly
- Quick-time-event style button-combo minigames for attacking rivals get repetitive, often 10+ times per run (QTE can be turned off in settings)
- Persistent bugs reported, including a long-standing screen-blocking bug that ruined a run late in the game, a locked-door exploit, and crashes entering a rare computer room
- Difficulty scaling feels artificial to some, ramping as you play better rather than feeling earned
- Very little synergy between upgrades: players say pure single-target damage is the only real strategy and status effects are not worth taking because key enemies are immune
- Melee combat is considered weaker than the gun play by some critics, and level/room design gets repetitive
- Epic Games version reportedly has support problems, e.g. achievement sync and a broken launcher screen
- Constant choice between a temporary upgrade and currency for permanent unlocks: taking the currency leaves you underpowered and dying fast

#### Improvements

- Add in-game health bar / clearer health readout and a slot or list view of boons and which are mutually exclusive
- Allow full controller button remapping (asked for on Steam discussions and in reviews)
- Better explain the multiple currencies and accessibility options such as Guard Mode, and reduce the currency bottlenecks
- Make the rival system less punishing early on; some players asked for an option to remove the rival entirely
- Fix the recurring screen-blocking and softlock bugs, and improve Epic version support
- Broaden build variety and synergies so non-damage upgrades matter; critics also wanted more variety in rooms and enemies

#### Unique Things

- Rival survivors race you to the single escape pod and you can attack them remotely through the prison's systems during the run
- You fight the corpse of your previous self / fallen guards, tying death into the loop
- Runs are on a clock, so speed matters more than in most Hades-style games; a top Steam review describes it as Hades on speed
- Final-run gimmick: after clearing objectives, a high-stakes do-or-die run can wipe your unlock progression if you fail
- Twin-stick shooter plus melee blend in the Callisto Protocol sci-fi prison setting with a punk graphic-novel look

#### Tedious Or Disliked

- Multi-currency grind and slow unlocks, especially early
- Repeated button-combo minigames to hit or hinder rivals
- The end-game reset: failing the final do-or-die run wipes unlocks, which several Steam reviewers call the worst design choice (others later came to like it)
- Bosses and rooms that throw a lot of stuff at you and feel unfair or badly designed to some users
- Keeping track of several rivals while fighting hordes under a time limit feels stressful and cluttered

#### Divisive Points

- Rivals system: praised as the standout idea by critics, but disliked by players who find it a hindrance and time pressure in the early game
- Comparison to Hades: some say it borrows heavily but has its own personality, others call it a worse clone while a few say it beats Hades in places
- The do-or-die reset: loved by some as real stakes, hated by others as deleting progress
- Difficulty: rewarding once the rhythm clicks versus unforgiving from the start
