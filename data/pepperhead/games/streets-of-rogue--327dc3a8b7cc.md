### Community

- **One Liner:** A top-down, procedurally generated action roguelite/immersive-sim hybrid by Matt Dabrowski (published by tinyBuild) where you pick from 20+ wildly different character classes and complete city-block missions by fighting, sneaking, hacking, bribing or causing chaos.
- **Consensus Summary:** Streets of Rogue is a player-beloved cult hit: Steam shows 'Overwhelmingly Positive' at about 96% of roughly 23,100 reviews, with sandbox freedom, character variety and co-op chaos cited over and over. Critics were also favourable but the critic sample is small (Metacritic lists 82 on Xbox One from 5 reviews and 75 on Switch from 4; PC Gamer gave 84). The game spent roughly two years in Early Access from 2017 and reached 1.0 on 12 July 2019 on PC, then Switch, PS4 and Xbox One. Criticism clusters around clunky melee and gun aim, RNG and unfair late-floor modifiers, weak build synergy compared with deeper roguelites, and a solo experience that some find repetitive. A sequel, Streets of Rogue 2, is still listed as 'coming soon' on Steam with no release date, and much of the recent review traffic is fans waiting for it.

#### Best Things

- Huge sandbox freedom: every mission has many solutions (stealth, hacking, bribery, violence, distraction, using gang rivalries) and the game lets you pick your own approach
- 20+ character classes that change how the game plays (cop gets XP for arrests, zombie turns kills into zombies, shapeshifter possesses NPCs, gorilla, scientist, politician, etc.), driving replayability
- Emergent chaos from interacting systems: rival gangs, scientists vs gorillas, explosions, fridges and generators as weapons, NPCs reacting to each other and to you
- Excellent co-op (local and online up to 4 players); widely described as better with friends
- Humour and tone: absurd gags and parody factions are frequently called out by reviewers and players
- Strong long-term content: big quests, mutators, DLC characters, Steam Workshop support, sandbox/custom levels and characters (one reviewer logged thousands of hours)
- Responsive developer and unlocks earned in play rather than paywalled (Steam and Metacritic user reviews)
- Music praised as fitting, though some want more tracks

#### Worst Things

- Clunky combat: melee described as flailing, gun shots sometimes lock onto unintended targets, and knockback when shot from several directions can be deadly
- RNG-driven deaths: bad trait offers, unlucky items or a random NPC fight can end multi-hour runs
- Late-floor modifiers (falling bombs, spreading goo, everyone armed with rocket launchers) are seen by some as unfair rather than fun
- Heavy menuing to interact with things, and stealth and combat feel stiff to some players
- Limited build depth/synergy compared with Gungeon-style roguelites, per a co-op reviewer
- Technical gripes: Switch version lag (Metacritic user), Steam Deck unplayable per top negative review (controller inputs not recognised, sub-20 fps docked), occasional save-data loss and online multiplayer breakage reported in negative Steam reviews
- Thin narrative; one player wanted story between levels and critics noted an abrupt, anticlimactic ending (DarkStation)

#### Improvements

- Tighter melee and aimed-gun handling, plus less knockback chaos
- More build variety and stronger trait/item synergies so runs feel less like the same pool of offers
- Better balance on later floors, with fewer arbitrary run-killers and unavoidable modifiers
- Fixes and support for Steam Deck, controllers and online multiplayer stability
- More side objectives and less downtime in co-op when one player finishes early; a cleaner split-screen UI with four players
- More narrative or structure around missions and a more satisfying ending

#### Unique Things

- Roguelite structure (procedural floors, permadeath, unlocks) fused with immersive-sim style problem solving, often pitched as Nuclear Throne meets Deus Ex with GTA anarchy
- Class-specific playstyles, objectives and roleplay hooks, so the 'who am I and why am I here' feel changes each run
- Living simulated city where factions and NPCs fight each other independent of the player
- Arcade-GTA-like top-down view with procedural city blocks, with a custom level, character and sandbox toolset
- Drop-in local and online co-op and PvP in a game that otherwise feels like a solo sandbox

#### Tedious Or Disliked

- Waiting for useful traits to appear; some players feel the same weak offers recur
- Long floors, especially in co-op, where finished players idle waiting for teammates
- Repetition after the first dozen hours for players who don't chase new characters or big quests
- Menu-heavy interaction and the death-count tracker (some find it demoralising)
- Humour and writing that some find childish or annoying

#### Divisive Points

- Chaos vs fairness: fans love systems going haywire, detractors feel random NPC violence and modifiers make runs feel unfair
- Best with friends vs solo: co-op is widely loved while some solo reviewers find it repetitive or shallow (mixed Switch critic score of 60)
- Roguelite depth: many praise replayability via characters, others say build variety is thin
- Sequel direction: Streets of Rogue 2 is more open-world, and some who tried its playtest felt it was going in a different direction
