##### Praise

- Wildly chaotic, visually pleasing spectacle that goes well beyond what the trailer shows
- Very amusing and entertaining to watch; hectic but casual and fun
- Super satisfying to play without thinking, just to see things explode
- Spells are found during the run and can be shared across characters, adding some variety

##### Criticism

- Gets old very quickly
- Little customization at the start beyond character choice
- After you find your spells, upgrades are just number adjustments (area, defense, speed) that feel boring
- Gameplay barely changes as chaos increases; you just run in circles
- Single level layout with no variety in setting

- Spells are offered during the run and can come from other characters, so character choice matters less
- Chaos scales far beyond what marketing shows

### Community

- **One Liner:** A 3D-styled single-player action roguelite / survivors-like from Game Smithing Limited where you pick one of ~22 characters, stack 350+ skills, runes and weapons, and kill Void Lords and Titans across kill-based stages and endless modes.
- **Consensus Summary:** Soulstone Survivors is well liked: Steam shows Very Positive at about 91% across roughly 26,900 reviews, and the six critic reviews aggregated on Metacritic (all from the PS5 version) average 82. Players and critics praise the sheer amount of content, the power-fantasy escalation, the colourful visuals and the steady free updates. The recurring complaint from players is that builds, characters and maps feel too similar once you understand the tag/debuff stacking, and that the grind for meta materials and achievements is heavy. It spent about 2.5 years in Early Access (Nov 7, 2022 to full 1.0 on Jun 17, 2025), launched on PS5 and Xbox Series X around the 1.0 date, and is still receiving updates. Many reviewers give a positive vote with caveats, describing it as very solid but shallow in practice.

#### Best Things

- Huge amount of content: about 22 characters with 4-5 weapons each, 350+ skills, 100+ craftable weapons, a large passive/meta tree and nearly 500 achievements
- Fast, satisfying power escalation: players say you reach absurd damage quickly, which is what they play the genre for
- Kill-count based stage completion instead of a fixed timer, so strong builds can finish and leave early, with optional endless continuation
- Colourful, eye-catching 3D cartoon visuals and very good music; critics liken the look to a World of Warcraft-style aesthetic
- Meta-progression with the option to toggle parts of it off, plus difficulty and curse modifiers that raise rewards
- Ongoing free content updates after 1.0 (new characters, weapons, maps), praised even by negative reviewers
- Accessible and easy to pick up, with real full controller support; several reviewers call it a more fleshed-out step up from Brotato or Vampire Survivors
- Story framing with cutscenes and a Void King final goal, unusual for the genre, noted in the PlayStation Universe review

#### Worst Things

- Builds, characters, weapons and runes feel fundamentally the same: one review says you just stack debuffs on a target, with a dominant Doom-style damage tag available to everyone
- Gameplay at higher difficulty reduces to dashing in circles around bosses while dodging red zones and projectiles
- Little variety between stages and modes: maps have few gimmicks, and critics note arenas are small, walled areas
- Content feels inflated by reuse; one long review says character mechanics are the same system repackaged with different visuals and stats
- Late-game screen clutter and power creep make the screen an unreadable mess, and the combat feels floaty or like hitting damage sponges to some players
- Crashes on long runs (an hour-long run lost to a crash with no mid-run save) and performance dips on PS5 when the screen fills up
- Loud, constant XP-pickup audio and GPU-heavy effects are mentioned as annoyances
- Balance is uneven: some characters' skills, artifacts and ascension passives are much stronger, so players fall back to a few proven builds

#### Improvements

- Add a pause-and-resume or save between waves/rounds for long runs, so a crash does not waste up to an hour
- Make builds, characters and stages mechanically distinct (real stage hazards, character-specific mechanics) so choices matter
- Add co-op, including local co-op: several positive reviewers explicitly wish for it
- Reduce the material grind and rebalance dominant tags like Doom so more build archetypes are viable
- Improve performance and visual clarity when the screen is full, and offer more audio tuning (players ask for XP pickup sound options)
- A roadmap and Workshop/mod support are recurring community requests on the Steam forum

#### Unique Things

- Stage completion is based on kills rather than surviving a timer, which changes pacing versus Vampire Survivors-style games
- Skill tags and debuff stacking systems with runes, plus the ability to swap attacks/abilities during a run
- Real 3D fantasy-ARPG presentation with ~90 enemy types, Titan bosses and cutscenes, rather than pixel art
- Very large persistent progression: character levelling, passive grids, craftable weapons, ascension and optional meta toggles
- Multiple modes (clear-and-boss, arenas, endless) with curse/difficulty modifiers that scale reward
- Steam listing includes Mac and Linux builds as well as Windows, plus PS5 and Xbox Series X versions

#### Tedious Or Disliked

- Grinding crafting materials and meta currencies; one positive reviewer calls the material grind borderline excessive
- Maxing every character and chasing achievements becomes a grind after roughly 38 to 40 hours of genuine progression
- Very long endless runs, where low-DPS builds need minutes to kill bosses and some players burn out not knowing when to stop
- Repetitive bosses and basic environments (a point raised in the Critical Hits review) and lack of map variety
- Some players feel they see everything within a few hours, with the rest of the unlocks not changing decisions in a run
- Matches feel long and brainless once the build is settled; negative reviewers say unlocks and achievements never change the actual gameplay (top negative-review theme, 100+ helpful votes)

#### Divisive Points

- Depth versus illusion of choice: fans see deep build theorycrafting, while many negative reviews say every build plays the same
- Difficulty and feel: GameSpew found it forgiving and easy, while others find late-game chaos and dodging demanding or unreadable
- Value: commonly recommended at a discount (one reviewer says under 10 euros), with many calling it good value even while criticising the design
- Endless modes and long runs are either a feature (power fantasy, optional) or a burnout trap
