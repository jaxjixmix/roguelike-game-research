### Community

- **One Liner:** A pixel-art roguelike deckbuilder by solo dev Dan DiIorio (TrampolineTales) in which you fill a slot machine with symbols and items to earn enough coins to pay ever-rising rent.
- **Consensus Summary:** Very well received by players: Steam sits at roughly 93% positive (about 11,500 reviews, Very Positive). It is widely described as a short, addictive engine-building game and is best known as the game LocalThunk has called the largest influence on Balatro. Critics who covered it (mostly on the mobile ports) were favourable, with a few saying the design is rigid. Complaints concentrate on a bare-bones UI, heavy luck, thin long-term progression, and the 777-wins achievement. It left early access in Jan 2023 after starting in Jan 2021, and later shipped on mobile and consoles. Steam reviews in the local data show it still gets content updates, and some players say those updates caused symbol bloat.
- **Reception Notes:** Early access began January 2021 and the PC 1.0 release was January 6, 2023. Mobile versions followed in July 2023, and consoles (Switch, PS4/5, Xbox) in February 2025, per Wikipedia. Wikipedia quotes Rock Paper Shotgun calling it a compulsive celebration of silliness, TouchArcade calling it a perfect blend of deckbuilding and gambling, and Pocket Gamer finding depth under a simple surface, while Push Square was mixed and called it inflexible. The game was reportedly banned in 13 countries on Google Play over simulated-gambling policy, which the dev disputed. The Metacritic figures (critic 80, user 6.3) come from the iOS listing with very few ratings, so treat them as weak signals; OpenCritic could not be confirmed. #### Best Things

- The core loop of spinning, adding symbols and relics, and building adjacency synergies to outrun rent is called simple, quick and addictive
- Runs are short and easy to start; reviewers call it a fun 10-20 hour game (one finished in ~13 hours) and say a single run is quick
- Multiple viable builds: players describe broken engine runs (for example suns plus flowers) alongside disastrous bricks, which keeps runs surprising
- Symbol picking works like deckbuilding, so players feel they have more agency than in a pure slot machine, and some rate it above Cloverpit for that reason
- Charming retro pixel art, music and humor, including anti-capitalist theming and quirky symbol interactions
- Low price and no real-money gambling or microtransactions, which suits people who like gambling mechanics without the predatory side
- Steam lists Linux and Mac builds, Workshop support, full controller support and accessibility options (adjustable text size, colour alternatives, contrast controls)
- Historical significance as the main inspiration for Balatro, which makes many players curious enough to try it

#### Worst Things

- UI and menus are described as messy: tooltips can be walls of text, the library view is ugly, and there is little at-a-glance information about what you already own while drafting
- Very high RNG: a number of reviewers feel the outcome depends more on luck than on skill and that upgrades matter little
- Shallow replayability with no meta-progression: new and veteran players have identical starting content, and a run gives you knowledge only
- Weak or non-synergising draws can sink a run; some reviewers say upgrades barely matter and they only get past rent 4 in about 1 of 4 runs
- Some players call it passive, because much of a run is watching the reels spin
- Price complaints from a minority, who say $10 is steep for a game with one core mode and that free browser games do similar things
- Some reviews report crashes (for example around the 4th rent payment), although these are isolated

#### Improvements

- A clearer, more readable UI with better item and symbol tooltips and a nicer library view
- More information or control while drafting, such as seeing a summary of your current symbols and synergies
- Some form of meta-progression or additional modes and variance after the first win, since players want more content beyond one level
- More reliable drafting: fewer dead draws and more ways to steer toward a build, so runs depend less on luck
- Curbing symbol bloat from updates, which some long-time players say makes early rent payments fail due to weak, non-synergising draws
- A less grindy achievement set, with the 777-wins and RNG-dependent achievements repeatedly named as unnecessary

#### Unique Things

- The slot machine is the board: symbols occupy a 5x4 grid and adjacent symbols trigger each other, which makes it feel like a spatial deckbuilder
- The win condition is paying escalating rent to a landlord, a satirical anti-capitalist framing rather than fighting enemies
- Explicit design goal of a gambling-style game without real-money gambling or microtransactions
- Acknowledged direct influence on Balatro, endorsed by its creator, plus a positive nod from Magic: The Gathering designer Richard Garfield
- Built and updated by a single developer, who started in early access in Jan 2021
- Symbols have cross-interactions (ghosts, dwarves, pinatas etc.) that produce absurd emergent stories

#### Tedious Or Disliked

- The win 777 games achievement is widely called insanely grindy, especially since a win takes 30+ minutes and cannot be guaranteed
- Luck-based achievements can require hundreds of resets, according to one reviewer with 300+ resets on one
- Symbol bloat and sheer number of symbols can make getting the synergies you want a matter of luck
- Dense, cluttered UI and menus (one reviewer calls the drafting UI a mess and the graphics indecipherable)
- Repeating the same loop with limited variance after the first tens of hours

#### Divisive Points

- Skill versus luck: fans say you steer builds through drafting, detractors say it is mostly RNG
- Whether later additions enriched the game or caused symbol bloat that hurts run consistency
- Value: many call it a bargain 10-20 hour game, others feel it is too thin at full price
- Comparison with Balatro and Cloverpit: some say it is the charming raw original, others say it is less polished and less deep than newer games it inspired
- Presentation: some love the barebones retro graphics and UI as charming, others find them hard to read
